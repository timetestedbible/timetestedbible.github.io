# Why Jasher, Jubilees, and Enoch Are Not Scripture

**Thesis:** The pseudepigraphal books (Jasher, Jubilees, 1 Enoch, etc.) contain useful historical and cultural details but fail the internal consistency test that canonical Scripture passes. They introduce theological claims that contradict the canonical text — not on peripheral details, but on foundational doctrines like the state of the dead, the nature of angels, and the mechanics of sin. A book that contradicts the Torah on fundamentals cannot be Torah-level authority.

**Principle:** Scripture can *reference* a text without *endorsing* it as divinely inspired. Joshua 10:13 cites "the book of Jasher" the same way Paul quotes Epimenides (Titus 1:12) and Aratus (Acts 17:28) — the citation validates a specific datum, not the entire source. A historian can cite a newspaper article for a date without endorsing the newspaper's editorial page.

## Test 1: The State of the Dead — Jasher's Rachel Speaks from the Grave

### The canonical position (established exhaustively in `research/heaven-vs-resurrection-notes.md`):

The dead are unconscious. Nine OT passages state this explicitly:

| Ref | Key phrase |
|---|---|
| Eccl 9:5 | "the dead know not any thing" |
| Eccl 9:10 | "no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave" |
| Ps 146:4 | "in that very day his thoughts perish" |
| Ps 6:5 | "in death there is no remembrance of thee" |
| Ps 88:10-12 | "the land of forgetfulness" |
| Ps 115:17 | "The dead praise not the LORD" |
| Isa 38:18-19 | "the grave cannot praise thee, death cannot celebrate thee" |
| Job 14:10-12 | man "lieth down, and riseth not: till the heavens be no more, they shall not awake" |
| Eccl 12:7 + Ps 146:4 | spirit returns to God, thoughts perish — the person ceases |

Two NT statements confirm no one has ascended to heaven:

- Jn 3:13 — "No man hath ascended up to heaven" (categorical)
- Acts 2:34 — "David is NOT ascended into the heavens" (1000+ years after death)

### What Jasher claims (Jasher 42):

While Ishmaelite/Midianite traders carry the newly sold Joseph toward Egypt, they pass Rachel's tomb near Ephrath (cf. Gen 35:19-20). Joseph runs to his mother's grave, falls on it, and weeps. He cries out to her:

> "O my mother, my mother, thou who didst give me birth, awake now, and rise and see thy son, how he has been sold for a slave, and no one to pity him... Arise and see thy son, weep with me... see the heart of my brethren... comfort my father..."

**Rachel responds from the grave:**

> "My son Joseph... I have heard the voice of thy weeping... I know thy troubles... it grieves me for thy sake... Now therefore... hope to the Lord... do not fear, for the Lord is with thee... Rise my son, go down unto Egypt with thy masters..."

### The contradiction:

Rachel carries on a full conversation — hearing, knowing, grieving, advising, even prophesying ("the Lord is with thee"). This directly violates:

- **Eccl 9:5** — "the dead *know not any thing*" → Rachel "knows" Joseph's troubles
- **Ps 146:4** — "his thoughts perish" → Rachel thinks, reasons, gives advice
- **Ps 6:5** — "no remembrance" → Rachel remembers Joseph, his brothers, Jacob
- **Isa 38:18** — "the grave cannot praise thee" → Rachel invokes God's faithfulness
- **Eccl 9:10** — "no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom" → Rachel displays all four

This is not a minor inconsistency. It is a direct, irreconcilable contradiction of one of the Torah's clearest teachings about mortality. The canonical account of Joseph's sale (Gen 37:25-36) mentions no stop at Rachel's grave and no conversation with the dead — because the canonical authors understood that the dead cannot speak.

### Why this matters:

If Jasher is Scripture, then either Rachel can speak from the dead (contradicting Ecclesiastes, Psalms, Isaiah, and Job) or Scripture contradicts itself. If Scripture doesn't contradict itself, Jasher is not Scripture.

The Jasher scene is emotionally powerful — that's why it persists. But emotional power is not the test of canonicity. Internal consistency with the Torah is.

## Test 2: The "Watched" Reference — Biblical Citations of Jasher

Two canonical books cite "the book of Jasher":

- **Josh 10:13** — "Is not this written in the book of Jasher?" (the long day)
- **2 Sam 1:18** — "it is written in the book of Jasher" (David's lament for Saul/Jonathan)

Both citations reference *specific historical or poetic data* — not theological doctrine. Joshua cites Jasher for the fact that the sun delayed. David's lament is catalogued in Jasher as a song. Neither citation says "thus saith the LORD through Jasher" or treats Jasher as prophetically authoritative.

Compare: Paul cites the Cretan poet Epimenides — "Cretians are alway liars, evil beasts, slow bellies" (Titus 1:12) — and affirms the specific observation ("this witness is true," v.13). This does not make Epimenides Scripture.

**The surviving "Book of Jasher" may not even be the same book.** The text referenced by Joshua and Samuel was likely a collection of heroic songs (H3477 *yashar* = "upright" — "the book of the upright"). The Jasher text we have today is a medieval compilation with legendary expansions, some of which (like the Rachel scene) introduce theology incompatible with the canonical text. Even if the original Jasher was a legitimate historical chronicle, the surviving version has been expanded beyond recognition.

## Test 3: Jubilees — Adding To and Taking From the Torah

Jubilees (also called "Little Genesis") claims to be an angelic revelation to Moses on Sinai — dictated by the "Angel of the Presence" from heavenly tablets while Moses was receiving the Torah (Jubilees 1:1-4, 26-29; 2:1). It presents itself as an authoritative expansion of Genesis-Exodus, claiming the Torah you have is incomplete and needs this secret angelic supplement. Deuteronomy 4:2 and 12:32 were written to prevent exactly this kind of claim: "You shall not add to the word that I command you, nor take from it."

Five specific failures:

### 3a. Invents commandments and penalties the Torah never gave

Jubilees repeatedly inserts laws, rituals, and death penalties that Moses never gave:

- **Sabbath marital relations** — forbidden under penalty of death (Jubilees 50:7-8). The Torah's Sabbath commands (Exod 20:8-11, Deut 5:12-15) say nothing about this.
- **Sabbath fasting** — a capital offense (Jubilees 50:12-13). Yet Moses himself fasted forty days including Sabbaths (Exod 34:28).
- **Extra offering rules** — specific wood types for altar use (Jubilees 21:12-16), not found in Leviticus.
- **Sukkot wreaths** — wearing wreaths on heads during Sukkot (Jubilees 16:29-31), not commanded in Lev 23:40-42.
- **Intermarriage penalty** — stoning and burning for a father who gives his daughter to a Gentile (Jubilees 30:7-17), a penalty the Torah does not prescribe.

These are textbook violations of Deuteronomy 4:2. The Torah presents its penal code as complete. A book that invents new capital offenses is adding to the word.

### 3b. Removes the patriarchs' sins — hagiography

The Torah candidly records the patriarchs' failures, and those failures are theologically essential — they demonstrate that God's covenant faithfulness does not depend on human perfection. Jubilees edits this out:

- **Abraham's deception** about Sarah being his sister (Gen 12:10-20) — completely omitted in Jubilees.
- **Jacob's deception** of Isaac to obtain the blessing (Gen 27) — rewritten so Jacob is blameless.
- **Jacob's favoritism** toward Rachel — softened; Isaac is said to love Leah best in old age.

This is subtracting the Torah's theological point. The raw Genesis accounts are cited in the NT precisely because they show flawed humans receiving unmerited grace (Rom 4:1-5, Rom 9:10-13, Heb 11:8-12). Jubilees replaces history with propaganda — making the patriarchs flawless Torah-keepers, which undermines the entire grace-through-faith framework that Paul builds on the *actual* Genesis record.

### 3c. Replaces the Torah's calendar with a sectarian 364-day solar calendar

The Torah ties festivals to the agricultural/lunar cycle (Exod 12-13, Lev 23, Deut 16). The canonical text makes both luminaries authoritative:

- **Gen 1:14-16** — "And God made the two great lights — the greater light to govern the day, and the lesser light to govern the night with the stars." Both luminaries are "for signs, and for seasons (*moedim*), and for days, and years."
- **Ps 104:19** — "He appointed the **moon** for seasons (*moedim*)." The moon — not the sun — is specifically appointed for the appointed times.

The creation mandate establishes a non-negotiable condition: the moon must be a "great light" that "governs the night with the stars." Only the **full moon** is visible from dusk to dawn, ruling the entire night alongside the visible constellations. The sliver moon vanishes shortly after sunset, is barely visible, and cannot co-rule the night with the stars. The dark moon is not a light at all — it cannot serve as a sign. (See chapter 7, "When Does the Month Start?" for the full canonical argument for full-moon month-start.)

Jubilees mandates a fixed 364-day solar year (12 months of 30 days + 4 intercalary days) and explicitly condemns anyone who observes festivals "by the moon" as being in error, saying they will "corrupt the seasons" (Jubilees 6:36-38). 1 Enoch's astronomical sections (chs. 72-82) support this same solar-only framework.

**The pattern across the pseudepigrapha:** Every major extrabiblical calendar tradition lands on the wrong side of the moon question. Jubilees and 1 Enoch eliminate the moon entirely with a solar-only 364-day calendar. The rabbinic tradition (Talmud, Josephus, Philo) uses the dark moon or sliver moon. The ancient nations — Egypt, Babylon, China — all started months at or near the dark moon (see Ezek 20:18 — "Do not walk in the statutes of your fathers"). None of them arrive at the full moon, which is the only phase that fulfills Gen 1:16's requirement of a "great light" governing the night "with the stars."

A book that eliminates the moon from the calendar contradicts the Torah's creation ordinance. This is not a matter of calculation method — it is a fundamental disagreement about which luminaries govern the *moedim*. And a fixed 364-day calendar drifts against the actual harvest seasons within decades, contradicting the Torah's own agricultural timing instructions (Lev 23:39, Deut 16:9). The Dead Sea Scrolls version requires inserting an undocumented week every 7 years to stay synchronized — an addition with no scriptural basis (see chapter 15, "Solar Only Calendars").

### 3d. Backdates specific rituals the Torah presents as new revelation

**The nuance:** Scripture itself hints at pre-Sinai observance of Torah principles. Gen 26:5 says Abraham "obeyed my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws" — using the full Mosaic vocabulary (*mitsvah*, *chuqqah*, *torah*) centuries before Sinai. The seventh-day pattern is established at creation (Gen 2:2-3). Abel and Noah offer sacrifices. The Torah may well be eternal in its principles.

The problem with Jubilees is not that it shows patriarchs following God's ways. The problem is that it backdates *specific ritual details* that the Torah *explicitly presents as new at the time they were given*:

- **"This month shall be unto you the beginning of months"** (Exod 12:2) — presented as a new instruction to Israel. Jubilees claims the patriarchs already kept Passover with the same details.
- **Abraham inventing Sukkot rituals** — circling the altar seven times with palm branches and fruits (Jubilees 16:20-31). Lev 23:33-43 presents the Sukkot instructions to Israel through Moses; the text does not say "as your father Abraham already did."
- **Noah keeping Shavuot** with the rainbow covenant sign (Jubilees 6). The Torah presents the firstfruits/weeks observance as part of the Sinai legislation (Lev 23:15-21).

The test is simple: where the Torah itself says "this is new" or frames a command as first-given, Jubilees contradicts it by backdating. Where the Torah leaves room for eternal principles (Gen 26:5, Gen 2:2-3), that is a different question. Jubilees doesn't just affirm eternal Torah — it rewrites the Torah's own presentation of when specific rituals were revealed.

### 3e. Claims to be a parallel Sinai revelation

The ultimate "adding to the word": Jubilees opens by claiming the Angel of the Presence dictated the entire book to Moses from heavenly tablets *while he was on the mountain receiving the Torah* (Jubilees 1:1-4, 26-29; 2:1). It tells readers that the Torah they have is incomplete without this secret angelic supplement.

This would require us to believe Moses received two conflicting books on Sinai — one plain (our Torah) and one with hundreds of additions, subtractions, and corrections. The consistent testimony of Jewish and Christian canons for 2,000+ years (outside the Ethiopian tradition) is that the Torah stands alone as God's sufficient word. Deuteronomy 4:2 was written to prevent exactly this scenario: another book claiming equal Sinai authority while adding new commandments and removing inconvenient narratives.

## Test 4: 1 Enoch — Enoch Is the Messiah

This is the most disqualifying claim in the entire pseudepigraphal corpus.

In the *Parables of Enoch* (1 Enoch 37-71, also called the *Similitudes*), the text builds toward a climactic revelation. Throughout chapters 46-62, a figure called "the Son of Man," "the Elect One," and "the Anointed One" is described in unmistakably messianic terms — seated on the throne of glory, judging the kings and the mighty, possessing righteousness, chosen before creation. The reader naturally assumes this figure is a prophecy of the coming Messiah.

Then in 1 Enoch 71:14, the angel reveals the identity of this Son of Man to Enoch:

> "Thou art the Son of Man who art born unto righteousness, and righteousness abides over thee."

Enoch himself is identified as the messianic Son of Man. The entire Parables section has been building toward this: Enoch is the pre-existent, enthroned, anointed judge of the nations.

### The canonical contradiction:

The canon is unambiguous about who the Messiah is:

- **Isa 9:6-7** — "Unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder." The Messiah is born of the line of David, ruling on David's throne.
- **Mic 5:2** — "Out of thee shall he come forth unto me that is to be ruler in Israel; whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting." The Messiah comes from Bethlehem — the line of David, not the line of Seth/Enoch.
- **2 Sam 7:12-13** — "I will set up thy seed after thee, which shall proceed out of thy bowels... He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever." Davidic covenant — the Messiah is David's descendant.
- **Jer 23:5** — "I will raise unto David a righteous Branch, and a King shall reign and prosper." The Branch is from David.
- **Dan 7:13-14** — "One like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven." Daniel's Son of Man receives dominion from the Ancient of Days — the same title 1 Enoch appropriates for Enoch himself.
- **Jn 4:25-26** — "I know that Messiah cometh... Jesus saith unto her, I that speak unto thee am he." Jesus claims the title directly.
- **Acts 2:36** — "God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye have crucified, both Lord and Christ."

Enoch is from the antediluvian line of Seth (Gen 5:18-24). He is not from Judah. He is not from David. He has no claim to the throne. The canonical Enoch is described in one verse — "Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him" (Gen 5:24) — and in one NT commentary — "By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death" (Heb 11:5). Nothing in the canon even hints at a messianic role for Enoch.

### Why this is disqualifying:

The Rachel-speaks test (Test 1) shows Jasher contradicting a *doctrine* — the state of the dead. The Enoch-is-Messiah claim contradicts the *identity of the Messiah himself.* This is not a peripheral error. It is a competing gospel. A book that identifies the wrong person as the Son of Man cannot be divinely inspired Scripture — because the Holy Spirit does not contradict Himself about who the Anointed One is.

### The premise fails too — Enoch is not in heaven:

Beyond the messianic claim, 1 Enoch's entire framework assumes Enoch is alive, conscious, and touring the cosmos — receiving visions, speaking with angels, sitting on thrones. This rests on the popular assumption that "God took him" (Gen 5:24) means Enoch ascended to heaven and lives there now.

The canon says otherwise:

- **Jn 3:13** — "And **no man hath ascended up to heaven**, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man." Categorical. No man — not Enoch, not Elijah, not anyone.
- **Heb 11:5** — Enoch was "translated (G3346 *metatithemi* — to transfer, transpose, transport) that he should not see death." Scripture is explicit: Enoch did not die. But "translated" does not mean "currently alive in heaven." He was *transported*. He "was not found" — he vanished from his own time period.
- **Heb 11:39-40** — "These all... **received not the promise**: God having provided some better thing for us, that **they without us should not be made perfect**." None of them are made perfect *without us*. It is a collective event — the resurrection.

If Enoch didn't die and didn't go to heaven, where did he go? The simplest resolution consistent with all the texts: he was transported through time and space *to the resurrection event itself* — arriving at the moment when all the faithful are made perfect together. He vanished from his generation. He arrives at ours. No death, no heaven, no contradiction. (Compare Philip's transport in Acts 8:39-40 — same verb root, physical relocation by the Spirit.)

Either way — whether transported forward or by some other mechanism — Enoch is **not currently alive and conscious in heaven** receiving cosmic tours and being enthroned as the Son of Man. That is 1 Enoch's premise, and it fails. 1 Enoch's entire framework contradicts Jn 3:13 and Heb 11:39-40.

### The Jude citation:

The Jude 14-15 citation of 1 Enoch follows the same pattern as Josh 10:13 citing Jasher: Jude quotes a specific prophecy ("the Lord cometh with ten thousands of his saints, to execute judgment") that aligns with canonical teaching (Deut 33:2, Zech 14:5). He does not endorse 1 Enoch's messianic claim for Enoch, its elaborate Watcher mythology, or its astronomical cosmology. Citation of one datum is not canonization of the entire source.

### The calendar contradiction — same error as Jubilees:

1 Enoch's astronomical sections (chapters 72-82, the *Astronomical Book*) lay out a solar-dominated cosmology. Some interpreters read it as supporting a strict 364-day solar-only calendar (the same framework Jubilees and the Dead Sea Scrolls adopt). Others read its lunar sections as supporting a sliver-moon month-start. The text is ambiguous enough to sustain both readings — and that ambiguity is itself a problem: canonical Scripture is not ambiguous about the moon's role.

The canonical creation mandate is clear:

- **Gen 1:16** — God made "two **great** lights" — the lesser light to "govern the night with the stars." The moon is a *great* light and a *governor*. Whether 1 Enoch eliminates the moon entirely (solar-only reading) or reduces it to a sliver (lunar reading), it fails to arrive at the only phase that fulfills this requirement: the full moon — visible from dusk to dawn, ruling the entire night alongside the stars.
- **Ps 104:19** — "He appointed the **moon** for seasons (*moedim*)." The moon — not the sun — is specifically appointed for the appointed times.

No matter which way 1 Enoch is read, it lands on the wrong answer:

| Reading | Month starts on | Fulfills "great light governing the night with the stars"? |
|---|---|---|
| Solar-only (364-day) | No lunar month at all | **No** — eliminates the moon entirely |
| Sliver-moon | Barely visible crescent | **No** — vanishes after sunset, not visible with stars all night |
| Canon (Gen 1:16, Ps 104:19) | Full moon | **Yes** — visible dusk to dawn, co-ruling with the constellations |

See `chapters/07_When_Does_the_Month_Start.md` for the full canonical argument. See `chapters/15_Solar_Only_Calendars.md` for the detailed refutation of the 364-day system.

1 Enoch is not merely influenced by the wrong calendar — under the solar-only reading, it is the *source text* that Jubilees and the Dead Sea Scrolls sectarian calendars build upon. The calendar error originates here.

### Additional areas to examine:

- The Watchers narrative (1 Enoch 6-16) introduces an elaborate angel-fall mythology that goes far beyond Gen 6:1-4
- The judgment sections introduce categories and mechanisms not found in the Torah or Prophets

## The Principle: Historical Witness vs. Doctrinal Authority

These books can be useful the same way Josephus, the Talmud, or archaeological records are useful — as historical witnesses that illuminate context, preserve details, and corroborate timelines. The codebase already uses Jasher this way:

- Jasher 82:6 — witness to "same day" meaning in the Sinai arrival chronology
- Jasher 88:63-64 — "36 moments" detail for Joshua's long day

In both cases, Jasher provides a data point that is tested against the canonical text and accepted only where it aligns. This is the correct posture: **cite for data, test against canon, reject where it contradicts.**

The error is treating these books as *equal authority* with the canonical text — importing their theology wholesale rather than extracting their history selectively. When Jasher's Rachel speaks from the grave, Jasher has moved from historical chronicle to theological claim — and the claim fails the canonical consistency test.
