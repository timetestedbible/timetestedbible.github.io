# "Death Is Too Good for Them" — Rough Draft

## The Instinct

There's a feeling most people won't say out loud: death is too good for some people. Hitler. Child molesters. The man who tortured your family. Death feels like getting off easy. The human heart wants MORE — wants the wicked to SUFFER, not just stop existing. And if God's justice only offers death, it feels inadequate. Unsatisfying. Too merciful.

This instinct is real. And it may be the deepest root of the doctrine of eternal torment.

## The Roman Imagination

Tertullian — the first church father to systematize eternal conscious torment as doctrine — wrote explicitly about looking forward to watching the damned suffer. In *De Spectaculis* (On the Shows):

> *"How shall I admire, how laugh, how rejoice, how exult, when I behold so many proud monarchs... groaning in the lowest abyss of darkness!"*

This is not theology. This is the Colosseum projected into eternity.

The Roman world normalized prolonged, maximally painful, public suffering as ENTERTAINMENT. Gladiators, crucifixion, burning alive — these were spectacles. The crowd cheered. Christianity grew up in this world, and when it needed a doctrine of final punishment, it reached for the imagination it knew: the arena, but forever. Dante's *Inferno* is the literary version — nine circles of increasingly creative suffering, described with the same relish Tertullian felt.

The doctrine of eternal torment didn't come from Moses. It came from the Colosseum.

## What Torah Says

Torah anticipated this exact impulse — the desire to punish beyond the limit — and prohibited it:

> *"Forty stripes he may give him, **and not exceed**: lest thy brother should **seem vile** unto thee."* — Deut 25:3

God says: the danger is not that punishment is too lenient. The danger is that YOU become vile by exceeding the limit. The one who wants more-than-death punishment is the one Torah warns about — not the criminal, but the judge who can't stop.

Death is the ceiling. God's entire punishment system — proportionality (Ex 21:24), maximum stripes (Deut 25:3), capital punishment as the ultimate sentence, karet (cutting off), Jubilee (releasing all debts) — is built on LIMITS. Nothing in Torah is permanent punishment. Everything resolves. Everything ends. Death is the maximum.

The human heart says "not enough." Torah says: the human heart is the one in the wrong.

## Incompatible with What Jesus Taught

The desire for the wicked to suffer beyond death is incompatible with the clearest commands Jesus gave:

> *"Love your **enemies**, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you."* — Matt 5:44

> *"For if you **forgive** men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: But if you **forgive not** men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses."* — Matt 6:14-15

> *"Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what **judgment** ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what **measure** ye mete, it shall be **measured to you** again."* — Matt 7:1-2

> *"Then came Peter to him, and said, Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times? Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until **seventy times seven**."* — Matt 18:21-22

You cannot love your enemies AND want them tortured forever. You cannot forgive seventy times seven AND believe God forgives zero times for those who die wrong. You cannot pray "forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors" AND believe God's version of "not forgiving" is eternal conscious torment.

The desire for the wicked to suffer more than death is the opposite of what Jesus commanded. It is vengeance — and vengeance is God's, not ours (Rom 12:19). And God's vengeance, according to Torah, has a limit.

## The Tree Again

The desire for punishment beyond what God authorized is the tree of knowledge of good and evil in another form. God says: death is the ceiling. The human heart says: I know better — death isn't enough, they deserve MORE. That is deciding for yourself what good and evil, justice and injustice look like — independent of God's declared word.

> *"Ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil."* — Gen 3:5

The serpent's promise was moral autonomy — the right to determine for yourself what justice requires. The person who says "death is too good for them" is exercising that autonomy. They are the judge who exceeds the forty stripes. They are the one Torah warns will make their brother "seem vile."

## What Death Actually Is

Perhaps the instinct that death is "too easy" comes from misunderstanding what death IS.

Death is not a release. It is not rest. It is not peace. It is cessation — the complete un-making of a person. "As though they had not been" (Obad 1:16). Every memory, every experience, every relationship, every moment of consciousness — gone. Not sleeping. Not existing somewhere else. GONE. The person is un-created — returned to the state before God spoke them into being.

That is not "getting off easy." That is the permanent, total, irrevocable end of a person's existence. The wicked don't escape to oblivion. They are unmade. Everything they were — every ambition, every cruelty, every moment of defiance — is reduced to nothing. They do not remember. They do not experience. They are not.

Is that really "too good"? Or is the desire for more actually the desire to keep the wicked ALIVE — conscious, existing, persisting — just so they can feel pain? And doesn't THAT give them something they don't deserve: continued existence?

## The Irony

Eternal torment requires eternal existence. The wicked must be kept alive forever in order to suffer forever. Which means eternal torment is actually a form of IMMORTALITY — the very thing Scripture says God alone possesses (1 Tim 6:16) and the righteous must seek (Rom 2:7).

The doctrine of eternal torment grants the wicked what they never earned and God never gave: eternal existence. It keeps Hitler alive forever. It sustains the child molester's consciousness for all eternity. It gives the worst humans who ever lived the one thing reserved for the righteous: they never stop existing.

Death — real death, the second death, permanent cessation — denies them that. It takes everything. Including existence itself.
