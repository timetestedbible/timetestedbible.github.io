# The Tree That Wakes First

**Blog post notes — spoken delivery to non-academic audience**

---

## The Setup

This is almond season. Right now, across Israel, the almond tree is blooming — white blossoms on bare branches while every other tree still sleeps through winter. It's the first tree to wake up every year, sometimes as early as late January.

And its Hebrew name tells you why it matters. The word for "almond" and the word for "watching" are the same word. Same letters. The tree is literally named "the watcher" — because it wakes before everything else.

When God called the prophet Jeremiah, the very first thing He showed him was a branch of an almond tree. And then He said: "You have seen well — for I am watching over my word to perform it" (Jer 1:11-12). The watcher-tree, held up by the Watcher-God. Before any prophecy is delivered, God establishes the foundation: I do not sleep. What I speak, I stay awake to fulfill.

---

## Part 1: The Golden Almond Trees

The lampstand that burned day and night in God's temple — the menorah — was designed as an almond tree. Every single cup on every branch was shaped like an almond blossom (Exod 25:33-34). The light that never went out was shaped like the tree that wakes first. Light and watching, fused into one object.

In Revelation, Jesus walks among seven golden lampstands (Rev 1:12-13). He tells us what they are: "The seven lampstands are the seven churches" (Rev 1:20).

The churches are golden almond trees. Watcher-trees. Each one supposed to be the first to wake, the first to see what's coming, the first to shine light while others sleep.

But to one of those churches — Sardis — He says something devastating:

> "You have a name that you are alive, but you are dead. Do not sleep, and strengthen that which wants to die." (Rev 3:1-2, from the Hebrew)

An almond tree that has fallen asleep. A watcher-tree contradicting its own nature. Dead wood.

And yet — there's hope in that. Because when God wanted to prove which priest He had chosen, He took twelve dead sticks, put them in the tabernacle overnight, and in the morning, one of them had budded, bloomed, and produced ripe almonds. All three stages of life on dead wood, in a single night (Num 17:8).

A dead rod bore the watcher-tree's fruit. Dead wood can wake up. A church that looks dead can bear almonds again — if God wakes it.

We are in almond season. The question is whether we are awake.

## Part 2: The 144 Clans — A Nation Born in a Day

Everyone argues about the 144,000 in Revelation 7 — is it literal or symbolic? But that argument assumes we know what the number says. What if we've been reading it wrong?

There is a strong case that Revelation was originally written in Hebrew (hebrewgospels.com). The Hebrew text of Revelation 7:4 reads:

> "I heard the number of those who were sealed: 144 **alaphim**, sealed from the **mishpechot** (families) of the children of Israel."

Two words sit side by side: **alaphim** and **mishpechot**. Both mean "clans" or "families." They reinforce each other — the way Hebrew poetry always works, saying the same thing in two parallel ways.

Here's what matters: the word **alaphim** has two well-known meanings in Hebrew. It can mean "thousands." But it also means "clans" — family units within a tribe. Same word. Same letters. The only difference is the vowel marks that scribes added centuries later, which force you to pick one meaning. The original text holds both.

When a Greek translator encountered this word, he would have defaulted to "thousands" — because that's the more common meaning. And that's exactly what the Greek text of Revelation has. But the clan meaning was lost in translation. You can go from Hebrew to Greek and lose "clans." You can never go from Greek back to Hebrew and recover it.

And here's what a back-translator would never produce: the **alaphim** / **mishpechot** parallel. If someone were just converting Greek into Hebrew mechanically, they'd use the standard word for "tribes." Instead, this text uses "families" — a word that echoes and reinforces the clan meaning of alaphim. That's natural Hebrew composition, not reverse-engineering.

So what does it mean?

12 tribes times 12 clans = **144 clans**. Not 144,000 individuals. The number 12 — the number of covenant completeness — multiplied by itself. Every tribe, every clan, fully represented. Not a headcount. A complete family structure.

Scripture calls them **firstfruits** (Rev 14:4). The first portion of the harvest. They stand before the throne and they are the only ones who can learn the new song (Rev 14:3).

Trees in Scripture represent nations (Daniel 4, Ezekiel 31). The almond is the first tree to wake — the first nation to rise. And Isaiah asks: "Shall a nation be born at once? For as soon as Zion travailed, she brought forth her children" (Isa 66:8).

Aaron's dead rod bore almonds overnight. One night — bud, bloom, fruit. A nation born in a day. The 144 clans are not a cap on who gets saved. They are the structure of a nation — a royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:9), a kingdom of priests (Rev 5:10) — that wakes first, like the almond, while the rest of the forest still sleeps.

## Part 3: One From a City, Two From a Thousand

If the 144 describes the structure, how many people are actually in it?

Jeremiah 3:14 gives us the ratio:

> "I will take you — one from a city, and two from a clan — and bring you to Zion."

This is Hebrew poetry — two parallel lines saying the same thing. A typical city in ancient Israel held about 500-1,000 people. A "thousand" (the other meaning of the same clan-word) is... about a thousand. Both lines converge on the same number:

**About 1-2 people out of every thousand. Roughly 0.2%.**

That's the remnant ratio. And it's consistent with everything else Scripture says about how many are saved:

- "Narrow is the way... **few** there be that find it" (Matt 7:14)
- "Many are called, but **few** are chosen" (Matt 22:14)
- "Lord, are there **few** that be saved?" (Luke 13:23)

Now consider: the gathering doesn't just include people alive today. "The dead in Christ shall rise first — then we who are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them" (1 Thess 4:16-17). The multitude spans all generations.

About 117 billion people have ever been born. About 61 billion since the cross. At 0.2%:

- **Total multitude across all generations: roughly 60-230 million**
- **Living at the time of gathering: roughly 8-16 million** out of 8 billion
- **The raised dead: 87-97% of the total** — the vast majority

That's why Paul emphasizes the order: the dead rise **first**. They're the main event. The living are added to them.

And it matches what happened at Sodom. Four cities destroyed. Only 4 people extracted — Lot, his wife, two daughters. His wife didn't make it. Three survived:

- Lot = **one from a city**
- Two daughters = **two from a clan**
- 1 + 2 = 3. The Jeremiah formula, confirmed.

Out of roughly 4,000 people across the four cities of the plain, 3 survived. That's 0.075%. Right in the range.

And Abraham had already established the principle: God will not destroy a city if 10 righteous remain in it (Gen 18:32). The angel told Lot explicitly: "I **cannot** do anything until you arrive there" (Gen 19:22). Not "I prefer not to." **I cannot.** Extraction is a prerequisite for judgment.

At 0.2%, every modern city on earth has far more than 10 righteous scattered through it. God is covenantally bound — by His own promise to Abraham — to extract His people before destruction falls. The rapture isn't optional. It's required. "Come out of her, my people" (Rev 18:4) must happen before "she shall be burned with fire" (Rev 18:8).

Same pattern, every time: Lot out, then Sodom burns. Rahab out, then Jericho burns. "Come out," then Babylon burns. Never reversed. Never simultaneous. Jesus named it explicitly: "As it was in the days of Lot... even so shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed" (Luke 17:28-30).

One in every 500-1,000 people you pass on the street. A city of a million yields about 2,000. A town of 10,000 yields about 20. A village of 500 yields one.

One from a city. Two from a thousand.

Revelation 7 shows the same group from two perspectives. John **hears** the number — 144 clans, structured, countable, a complete covenant architecture. Then he **looks** and **sees** "a great multitude which no man could number, of all nations and kindreds and people and tongues" (Rev 7:9). Same group. The structured view and the demographic view. The covenant blueprint and the living crowd.

## Part 4: Be the Tree

So what do you do?

When Jesus gave His final warning about the end — the Olivet Discourse in Mark 13 — He closed with one word. Not in Greek. The Hebrew text gives us the actual word He used:

**עורו**

It means: **AWAKE.**

Not "watch" in the sense of observing. Not "be careful." The Hebrew word for "awake" comes from the same root as "to raise up" — the resurrection word. In Greek, the connection is the same: the word translated "watch" (gregoreo) comes from the word for "raise from the dead" (egeiro).

To watch IS to be raised. To sleep IS to be dead. Scripture uses this equation everywhere:

- "Many who **sleep** in the dust shall **awake**" (Daniel 12:2)
- "Them which are **asleep**... the **dead** in Christ shall **rise** first" (1 Thess 4:13-16)
- "**Awake** thou that **sleepest**, and **arise from the dead**, and Christ shall give thee **light**" (Eph 5:14)

That last verse even adds: Christ gives you **light**. And the light burns in almond-shaped cups on the golden lampstand. The resurrected receive the light of the watcher-tree.

The Hebrew of Revelation 16:15 makes it even sharper than the English:

> "**Holy** are those who **do not sleep**, and guard themselves."

Not "blessed" — **holy**. Set apart. Consecrated. And not "he that watches" — "those who **do not sleep**." Defined by the absence of death. The non-sleepers are the holy ones.

And Revelation 3:10 gives the covenant promise — in Hebrew, the same guardian-word appears twice:

> "Because you have **guarded** my word, so I will **guard** you from the hour of testing that is coming upon the whole earth."

You guard His word. He guards you from the hour. Same verb. Reciprocal.

The remnant in Revelation 12:17 is identified by two things: they **do** the commandments of YHWH, and they **believe** in the testimony of His Messiah. Not just knowing. Doing and believing.

So here is what the almond season says:

1. **Wake up.** The Hebrew command is "AWAKE" — be on the resurrection side, not the death side. If you're spiritually asleep, you're in the same category as the dead.

2. **Guard His word.** The promise is reciprocal — you guard it, He guards you. This is the mechanism of preservation through the hour of testing.

3. **Come out.** Jeremiah 50:8 says to come out of Babylon and "be as the he-goats before the flocks" — the prepared ones who lead. The word for those he-goats comes from a root meaning "prepared, ready, the future, treasure." The ones who go first are the prepared ones. The firstfruits. The future itself.

4. **Be the tree.** You are a royal priesthood. Aaron's almond rod proves it. The churches are golden almond trees. Your calling is to be the tree that wakes first — to bloom while others sleep, to bear fruit on what looked like dead wood, to be the light shaped like watching.

The almond is blooming. The season of the watcher-tree is here.

**עורו** — Awake.
