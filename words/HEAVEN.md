# HEAVEN — Word Study

**Hebrew:** שָׁמַיִם (*shamayim*, H8064)
**Greek:** οὐρανός (*ouranos*, G3772)
**Occurrences:** ~395 (OT) + ~264 (NT) = ~659 total

---

## Summary

The English word "heaven" translates two original words. Neither one means what modern Christianity typically assumes — a disembodied afterlife destination for the saved. The Hebrew *shamayim* means **the sky** (from a root meaning "to be lofty"). The Greek *ouranos* means **the sky** (possibly derived from *oros*, "mountain," through the idea of elevation). In both Testaments, "heaven" primarily refers to (1) the physical sky, (2) the cosmic expanse, or (3) God's domain/throne — the seat of His governing authority. It is never used in Scripture as a destination where dead humans go.

---

## The Hebrew: שָׁמַיִם (H8064)

**Derivation:** Dual form of an unused singular שָׁמֶה, from a root meaning "to be lofty."
**Strong's definition:** "The sky (as aloft; the dual perhaps alluding to the visible arch in which the clouds move, as well as to the higher ether where the celestial bodies revolve)."
**KJV translations:** heaven(s), air, astrologer

The dual form (*-ayim*) may indicate two visible layers: the atmospheric sky (clouds, birds, rain) and the cosmic sky (stars, sun, moon). This is a Hebrew morphological feature, not a statement about "multiple heavens" as a theological concept.

### Categorized Usage (~395 occurrences)

| Category | Count | Description | Representative Verses |
|----------|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| **God's domain** | ~120–140 | God's throne, God "of heaven," voice/action "from heaven" | Gen 24:3; Exod 20:22; Deut 4:36; Isa 66:1; Lam 3:50 |
| **Merism** | ~90–100 | "Heaven and earth" = all creation | Gen 1:1; 2:1; 14:19; Exod 20:11; Deut 4:26 |
| **Directional/spatial** | ~100–110 | "Under heaven," "midst of heaven," spatial reference | Gen 1:9; 6:17; 7:19; Deut 4:32; 30:4 |
| **Physical sky** | ~85–95 | Birds, rain, dew, clouds, wind, visible sky | Gen 1:26; 7:11; 27:28; Exod 16:4; Deut 11:11 |
| **Cosmic expanse** | ~60–70 | Stars, sun/moon, host of heaven, firmament | Gen 1:8, 14; 15:5; 22:17; Deut 4:19 |
| **Judgment from** | ~15–20 | Fire, brimstone, destructive action from heaven | Gen 19:24; 2 Kgs 1:10, 12; Job 1:16 |
| **Human aspiration toward** | ~10–15 | Tower to heaven, "ascend into heaven" | Gen 11:4; 28:12; Isa 14:13; Ps 139:8 |

### Critical Finding

**H8064 is never used as a destination for the dead.** Not once in 395 occurrences does *shamayim* describe a place where dead humans go after death. The Hebrew Bible's word for the realm of the dead is שְׁאוֹל (*sheol*, H7585) — "the world of the dead, a subterranean retreat" — which is DOWN, not up.

## The Greek: οὐρανός (G3772)

**Derivation:** "Perhaps from the same as G3735 (ὄρος, *oros*) through the idea of elevation." G3735 means "mountain — a mountain, as lifting itself above the plain."
**Strong's definition:** "The sky; by extension, heaven (as the abode of God); by implication, happiness, power, eternity."
**KJV translations:** heaven(s), heavenly, sky, air

The etymological connection to *oros* (mountain) is significant. In Scripture, MOUNTAIN = Kingdom/Ruling Authority (Rev 17:9; Dan 2:35, 44; Jer 51:25). If *ouranos* derives from *oros*, then "heaven" carries the semantic field of **elevated ruling authority** — the highest seat of government.

### Categorized Usage (~264 occurrences)

| Category | Count | Description | Representative Verses |
|----------|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| **God's domain** | ~45 | Father/God/throne/angels in heaven | Matt 5:16; 6:9; 18:10; 24:36 |
| **"Kingdom of heaven"** | ~32 | Matthew-specific phrase (= "kingdom of God") | Matt 3:2; 5:3, 10; 7:21; 13:11 |
| **Directional down** | ~35 | Voice, bread, fire, angels, Christ coming FROM heaven | Matt 3:17; John 6:31–33; 1 Thess 4:16; Rev 21:2 |
| **Cosmic/eschatological** | ~25 | Heavens pass away, new heavens, signs, powers shaken | Matt 5:18; 24:29; 2 Pet 3:10, 13; Rev 21:1 |
| **Physical sky** | ~15 | Birds of heaven, weather signs, clouds | Matt 6:26; 8:20; 16:2–3 |
| **Directional up** | ~12 | Jesus' ascension, looking up to heaven, prayer gestures | Mark 16:19; Luke 24:51; Acts 1:9–11; John 17:1 |
| **Stored in** | ~8 | Treasure, reward, names, inheritance reserved in heaven | Matt 5:12; 6:20; Luke 10:20; 1 Pet 1:4 |
| **Other** | ~92 | Various uses | — |

### Critical Finding

Of the ~12 "directional up" occurrences, only two involve humans going to heaven: **Elijah** (2 Kgs 2:11) and **the two witnesses** (Rev 11:12). Both are exceptions, not deaths — Elijah was taken alive; the witnesses are resurrected and summoned. **Jesus' ascension** (Acts 1:9–11) is post-resurrection, not death. No ordinary dead human is ever described as going to heaven.

## What Scripture Does NOT Say

### 1. "Go to heaven" — Zero occurrences

The phrase "go to heaven," "went to heaven," "going to heaven," or "gone to heaven" does not appear anywhere in the KJV Bible. The statement that defines the hope of popular Christianity — "when you die, you go to heaven" — is absent from its own text.

### 2. "Reign in heaven" — Zero occurrences

Scripture never says the saved will "reign in heaven." What it says:

> And hast made us unto our God kings and priests: and **we shall reign on the earth.**
> — Revelation 5:10

### 3. No dead person goes to heaven

> And **no man** hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven, even the Son of man which is in heaven.
> — John 3:13

> For **David is not ascended** into the heavens.
> — Acts 2:34

David — the man whose confidence about his dead child (2 Sam 12:23) is cited as the primary proof text for children going to heaven — has not himself ascended to heaven. Peter says so explicitly at Pentecost, after the resurrection, speaking under the Holy Spirit.

## The Key Proof Texts Examined

Every verse commonly cited to support "heaven as destination" falls into one of these categories: (A) doesn't use the word "heaven," (B) describes something stored in heaven, not people going there, or (C) describes the opposite direction.

### Verses that do NOT use the word "heaven"

| Verse | What it actually says | Word used |
|-------|----------------------|-----------|
| **John 14:2–3** | "In my Father's house are many mansions... I will come again, and receive you unto myself" | "Father's house" — no *ouranos*. And He comes to THEM. |
| **Philippians 1:23** | "To depart, and to be with Christ" | "With Christ" — no *ouranos*. Location unspecified. |
| **Luke 23:43** | "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise" | "Paradise" (G3857 *paradeisos*) — not *ouranos*. |
| **Luke 16:22** | "Carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom" | "Abraham's bosom" — not *ouranos*. A parable. |
| **Hebrews 11:10, 16** | "A city which hath foundations... a better country, that is, an heavenly" | "Heavenly" is an adjective (ἐπουράνιος, *epouranios*) — of heavenly origin/quality. The noun is "city" and "country." And this city comes DOWN (Rev 21:2). |
| **2 Corinthians 5:8** | "Absent from the body, present with the Lord" | "With the Lord" — no *ouranos*. Where is the Lord going to be? On earth (1 Thess 4:16; Zech 14:4). |

### Verses that describe things STORED in heaven (not people going there)

| Verse | What is stored | Key preposition |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Matthew 5:12** | "Your reward" | "in heaven" — reward is stored there |
| **Matthew 6:20** | "Treasures" | "in heaven" — treasures are stored there |
| **Luke 10:20** | "Your names" | "written in heaven" — registry is there |
| **Colossians 1:5** | "Hope" | "laid up for you in heaven" |
| **1 Peter 1:4** | "Inheritance" | "reserved in heaven for you" |
| **Hebrews 10:34** | "A better and enduring substance" | "in heaven" |

The preposition is "in" — stored there, safe there, held there. Not: "you will go there to collect it." A bank vault in London can hold your inheritance; you don't live in the vault. And the city where it's all stored comes DOWN to earth (Rev 21:2).

### Verses that describe the OPPOSITE direction

| Verse | Direction | What happens |
|-------|-----------|-------------|
| **Revelation 21:2** | DOWN | "New Jerusalem, coming **down** from God out of heaven" |
| **Revelation 21:10** | DOWN | "Holy Jerusalem, **descending** out of heaven from God" |
| **1 Thessalonians 4:16** | DOWN | "The Lord himself shall **descend from** heaven" |
| **2 Thessalonians 1:7** | DOWN | "Lord Jesus shall be revealed **from** heaven" |
| **Matthew 6:10** | DOWN | "Thy kingdom come... **on earth** as it is in heaven" |
| **Acts 1:11** | DOWN | "This same Jesus... shall so **come** in like manner" |

The consistent pattern: heaven comes DOWN to earth, not people going UP to heaven. The Lord descends. The city descends. The kingdom comes to earth.

## "Kingdom of Heaven"

This phrase appears 32 times — all in Matthew. Mark and Luke, describing the same events and teachings, use "kingdom of God." The phrases are interchangeable (compare Matt 19:23–24 where Matthew uses both in consecutive verses).

"Kingdom of heaven" does not mean "a kingdom located in heaven." The genitive indicates origin or character, not location — like "kingdom of England" means the kingdom ruled by England, not a kingdom inside England. It is the kingdom that originates FROM heaven and is established ON earth:

> Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done **in earth**, as it is in heaven.
> — Matthew 6:10

The prayer asks for heaven's kingdom to come TO earth — not for people to go TO heaven.

---

## "Heaven of Heavens"

Five occurrences (Deut 10:14; 1 Kgs 8:27; 2 Chr 2:6; 6:18; Neh 9:6). A Hebrew superlative construction (like "Song of Songs" = greatest song, "Holy of Holies" = holiest place). It means the highest/ultimate heavens — the fullest extent of the sky and beyond. Used to emphasize that even the entire cosmos cannot contain God:

> Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee.
> — 1 Kings 8:27

## "Third Heaven" and "Paradise"

Paul describes being "caught up to the third heaven" (2 Cor 12:2), then equates this with "paradise" (2 Cor 12:4). "Paradise" (G3857 *paradeisos*) is "of Oriental origin" — a Persian loanword meaning "a park, an enclosed garden." It appears only 3 times in the NT:

| Verse | Context |
|-------|---------|
| Luke 23:43 | "Today shalt thou be with me in paradise" — spoken to a dying man |
| 2 Corinthians 12:4 | "Caught up into paradise" — equated with "third heaven" |
| Revelation 2:7 | "The tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God" |

The Revelation passage places the tree of life IN paradise — and the tree of life appears in the New Jerusalem (Rev 22:2), which comes DOWN to earth (Rev 21:2). Paradise is not a separate heavenly realm but a feature of the city that descends.

The rabbinic concept of three heavens: (1) atmospheric sky, (2) cosmic sky/stars, (3) God's throne-room. Paul's experience was a vision of God's throne — not a description of where the dead reside.

## The Actual Destination in Scripture

Scripture is consistent about where the saved will be:

| Promise | Verse |
|---------|-------|
| Inherit the **earth** | Matthew 5:5; Psalm 37:9, 11, 22, 29 |
| Inherit the **land** | Genesis 15:18; Deuteronomy 1:39; Isaiah 60:21; Ezekiel 47:13–14 |
| Reign on the **earth** | Revelation 5:10 |
| Live in a city on a renewed **earth** | Revelation 21:1–2 (new heaven AND new earth; city comes down) |
| Resurrection of the **body** | 1 Thessalonians 4:16; 1 Corinthians 15:42–44 |
| Dwell in the **land** forever | Psalm 37:29; Isaiah 60:21 |

**Heaven** is where God currently reigns, where rewards are stored, and where the city is being prepared. But the destination for the redeemed is **earth** — a renewed earth, with the heavenly city descended upon it, and God dwelling among men:

> Behold, the tabernacle of God is **with men**, and he will **dwell with them**, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them.
> — Revelation 21:3

God comes DOWN. The city comes DOWN. The kingdom comes to EARTH. The inheritance is LAND. The dead RISE from the ground. The direction in every case is **down and out**, not up and away.

## The Heaven ↔ Mountain ↔ Kingdom Connection

The etymological chain is striking:

1. **G3772** (*ouranos*, heaven) — "perhaps from G3735 through the idea of elevation"
2. **G3735** (*oros*, mountain) — "a mountain, as lifting itself above the plain"
3. **Mountain** in Scripture = Kingdom/Ruling Authority (Rev 17:9; Dan 2:35, 44; Jer 51:25)

Heaven → Mountain → Kingdom. The word itself may encode the concept: heaven is the highest seat of ruling authority — God's kingdom, elevated above all earthly kingdoms.

This explains why "the mountain of the LORD's house shall be established in the top of the mountains" (Isa 2:2) — God's kingdom above all kingdoms. And why Hebrews calls the destination "mount Sion, the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem" (Heb 12:22) — not a cloud-realm, but a kingdom-city on a mountain.

## What Christianity Replaced

The biblical framework is:

| Element | Scripture says | Popular teaching says |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Destination** | Renewed earth (Rev 21:1) | Disembodied heaven |
| **Direction** | God comes DOWN (Rev 21:3) | People go UP |
| **Inheritance** | Land (Gen 15:18; Matt 5:5) | Clouds/spiritual realm |
| **Body** | Resurrection of the body (1 Cor 15) | Soul leaves body |
| **City** | New Jerusalem descends (Rev 21:2) | Believers ascend to it |
| **Duration** | "Dwell therein forever" (Ps 37:29) | Eternal in the heavens |
| **Kingdom** | "On earth as in heaven" (Matt 6:10) | In heaven, not on earth |

The shift from "land inheritance + resurrection + city descends" to "souls go to heaven when they die" represents a fundamental reversal of the biblical direction. Scripture's arrow points DOWN. Popular theology's arrow points UP.

## "New Heaven and New Earth" — Renewed, Not Replaced

Every passage describing the "new heaven and new earth" uses vocabulary that means **renewal/restoration**, not creation from scratch.

### The Greek: καινός, not νέος

| Passage | Greek word for "new" | Strong's | Meaning |
|---------|---------------------|----------|---------|
| Revelation 21:1 | καινός (G2537) | "New, especially in **freshness**" | Qualitative newness — made fresh |
| 2 Peter 3:13 | καινός (G2537) | Same | Same |
| Revelation 21:5 | καινός (G2537) | Same | "I make all things **new**" |

Strong's explicitly contrasts the two Greek words for "new": G2537 καινός is "new (especially in **freshness**; while G3501 (νέος) is properly so with respect to **age**)."

If the earth were being annihilated and replaced with a brand-new one that never existed before, νέος (*neos*) would be the natural word. Instead, every passage uses καινός (*kainos*) — the existing thing made fresh, restored, renewed in quality.

Revelation 21:5 is decisive: "I make all things **new** [*kainos*]." The grammar matters. He does not say "I make all **new** things" (replacing the old with different things). He says He takes the existing things (*panta*, all things) and makes them *kainos* — fresh, restored.

### The Hebrew: חָדָשׁ — from the root meaning "restore to completeness"

Isaiah 65:17 uses **H2319 חָדָשׁ** (*chadash*) — the adjective form of the verb **H2318 חָדַשׁ** (*chadash*), "to renew, restore to completeness."

Every use of this verb root in the Hebrew Bible describes **restoration to a former fullness**, not creation ex nihilo:

| Verse | Object | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| 1 Samuel 11:14 | The kingdom | Restored to proper function |
| 2 Chronicles 15:8 | The altar | Restored to proper function |
| 2 Chronicles 24:4, 12 | The temple | Physical restoration to completeness |
| Psalm 51:10 | A right spirit | Restored to proper state |
| Psalm 103:5 | Youth | Restored to fullness/vigor |
| Psalm 104:30 | Face of the earth | Seasonal restoration to fullness |
| Isaiah 61:4 | Waste cities | Rebuilt to completeness |
| Lamentations 5:21 | Our days | Return to former fullness |

(See the full analysis in the [H2320 word study](/words/H2320.md).)

The Hebrew word behind "new heavens and new earth" literally carries the meaning: **heavens and earth restored to their complete, proper state.**

### Peter's Flood Parallel Confirms It

Peter explicitly parallels the coming fire-renewal with Noah's flood:

> The world that then was, being overflowed with water, **perished**.
> — 2 Peter 3:6

> The heavens and the earth, which are now... **reserved unto fire** against the day of judgment.
> — 2 Peter 3:7

The flood "destroyed" the world (3:6) — but it's the same earth. It was purified by water, not annihilated and replaced. Peter draws a direct parallel: as water purified the earth then, fire will purify it next. Then comes verse 13: "new [*kainos*] heavens and a new [*kainos*] earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness."

Same earth. Different purification. Restored to completeness. The destination isn't a different planet — it's THIS earth, renewed.

### The Hebrew Revelation Text: Three Layers the Greek Loses

The Hebrew text of Revelation 21:1 (from the Hebrew Gospels tradition) reads:

> וְרָאִיתִי שָׁמַיִם חֲדָשִׁים וַאֲדָמָה חֲדָשָׁה כִּי הַשָּׁמַיִם וְהָאָרֶץ הָרִאשׁוֹנִים יִגְוַע וְהַיָּם לֹא יֵשׁ עוֹד

The Greek collapses at least three distinctions that the Hebrew preserves:

**1. יִגְוַע (*yigva*) — "expired / gave up the ghost" (H1478 גָּוַע)**

The Greek uses the generic G3928 παρέρχομαι — "passed away." The Hebrew uses **the personal death verb** — the word for a patriarch breathing his last:

| Verse | Who expires |
|-------|-----------|
| Genesis 25:8 | Abraham gave up the ghost (*gava*) |
| Genesis 25:17 | Ishmael gave up the ghost (*gava*) |
| Genesis 35:29 | Isaac gave up the ghost (*gava*) |
| Genesis 49:33 | Jacob yielded up the ghost (*gava*) |
| Genesis 6:17; 7:21 | All flesh *died* (*gava*) in the flood |

The earth doesn't generically "pass away." It **expires** — breathes out — the way Abraham did. And Abraham will be resurrected. The flood parallel is precise: Genesis 6:17 uses the same verb (*gava*) for what happened to all living things in the flood — but the earth survived and was renewed. The earth *gava* then, and it will *gava* again. Both times, what follows is restoration (*chadash*), not annihilation.

**2. אֲדָמָה (*adamah*) vs. אֶרֶץ (*erets*) — two different "earths"**

The Greek uses γῆ (*ge*) for both instances of "earth," flattening the distinction. The Hebrew makes a deliberate switch:

| Term | Hebrew | Meaning | Used for |
|------|--------|---------|----------|
| The **renewed** earth | **אֲדָמָה** (H127) | Soil, ground — from H119 "to be red/ruddy" | The ground from which אָדָם (*adam*, man) was formed (Gen 2:7). The ground cursed for man's sake (Gen 3:17). The ground that receives the blood of Abel and the dead. |
| The **first** earth | **אֶרֶץ** (H776) | Land, territory — "from a root meaning to be firm" | The geopolitical earth, the territorial system, the land as a cosmic entity. |

The first *erets* (the territorial system) expires. What emerges renewed is *adamah* — humanity's very soil, the ground of human existence. The word connects the renewed earth directly to Adam — to humanity itself. Renewed ground (*adamah chadashah*) for renewed humans. Earth-resurrection mirrors man-resurrection.

No back-translator working from the single Greek word γῆ (used twice) would produce two different Hebrew words with a theological distinction between them. The Hebrew → Greek direction is the only one that makes sense.

**Revelation 21:5 — "I have made everything restored"**

The Hebrew of verse 5 adds a further distinction:

> עָשִׂיתִי הַכֹּל חָדָשׁ — "I have made everything renewed/restored"

The verb **עָשִׂיתִי** (*asiti*) is Qal **perfect** — completed action, already done. The Greek ποιῶ (G4160, present active) says "I **make**" — ongoing. The Hebrew says "I **have made**" — finished, declared with prophetic certainty from the throne. Combined with חָדָשׁ (*chadash*), the full force is: "I have restored everything to completeness." Not a work in progress, but an accomplished fact.

**Revelation 21:6 — "It has all crossed over"**

The Hebrew of verse 6:

> עָבַר הַכֹּל אֲנִי הָרִאשׁוֹן וְהָאַחֲרוֹן תְּחִילַּת וְהַסּוֹף וַאֲנִי אֶתֵּן לְהַצָּמֵא מַיִם חַיִּים בְּחִנָּם

The Greek says γέγονεν (G1096) — "it is done." The Hebrew says **עָבַר הַכֹּל** — "the all has **crossed over**." The verb עָבַר (*avar*, H5674) means "to cross over, pass through, transition." This is the verb for **crossing the Jordan** into the Promised Land. It is the root behind עִבְרִי (*ivri*, "Hebrew" — "the one who crossed over"). The new creation is framed as the final entry into the inheritance — the Jordan has been passed, the transition is complete.

Further, the Greek identifies the speaker as "Alpha and Omega" — Greek letters. The Hebrew says **הָרִאשׁוֹן וְהָאַחֲרוֹן** — "the first and the last" — Hebrew words, not foreign letters. And the promise to the thirsty is **מַיִם חַיִּים** (*mayim chayyim*) — "living waters." This is a Torah technical term for flowing spring water used in purification rituals (Lev 14:5–6, 50–52; 15:13; Num 19:17). The renewed creation is inaugurated with purification language, and it is given **בְּחִנָּם** (*b'chinnam*) — "for nothing, without cost."

**Revelation 21:7 — The land-possession verb**

> מִי שֶׁמְּנַצֵּחַ יִהְיֶה יוֹרֵשׁ מֵהַכֹּל וַאֲנִי אֶהְיֶה לוֹ לֵאלֹהִים וְהוּא יִהְיֶה לִּי לְבֵן

The Greek says κληρονομήσει πάντα — "shall inherit all things." The Hebrew says the overcomer **יִהְיֶה יוֹרֵשׁ מֵהַכֹּל** — "will be a **possessor** of everything." The word **יוֹרֵשׁ** (*yoresh*) is a participle of **יָרַשׁ** (*yarash*, H3423), whose Strong's definition reads: "to occupy **by driving out previous tenants and possessing in their place**; to seize, to inherit, to dispossess." This is THE land-possession verb — the word Joshua used for taking Canaan, the word Deuteronomy repeats for entering the Promised Land. The overcomer is not abstractly "inheriting" — he is taking territorial possession.

The covenant formula uses **אֶהְיֶה** (*ehyeh*) — "I will be" — the divine self-identification from the burning bush (Exod 3:14, אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה). God identifies Himself by His covenant name within the promise to the overcomer.

**Revelation 21:8 — Wages from the fire of Sheol**

> אֲבָל לְהָאֵינוֹ מַאֲמִינִים וְלָרַצְחָנִים וְלַזּוֹנִים וְלַמְכַשְּׁפִים וְלַכַּזְבָנִים אֶתֵּן אֶת שְׂכָרָם מֵאֵשׁ הַשְּׁאוֹל הַשּׂוֹרֵף בָּאֵשׁ וְגָפְרִית וְזֹאת הִיא הַמִּיתָה הַשְּׁנִיָּה

Three divergences from the Greek:

| Feature | Greek | Hebrew |
|---------|-------|--------|
| **First in the list** | δειλοῖς — "the **cowardly**" | הָאֵינוֹ מַאֲמִינִים — "those who **do not believe**" (from אָמַן, the root of "amen" and "faith") |
| **Punishment** | λίμνη πυρός — "**lake** of fire" (no OT precedent) | מֵאֵשׁ **הַשְּׁאוֹל** — "fire of **Sheol**" (H7585, the established OT realm of the dead, 65+ occurrences) |
| **Their portion** | μέρος — "their **part/portion**" | שְׂכָרָם — "their **wages**" (H7939 שָׂכָר, "payment of contract, salary") — echoing Rom 6:23 "the wages of sin is death" |

The Hebrew also has a shorter list — five categories (unbelieving, murderers, fornicators, sorcerers, liars) versus the Greek's eight (adding fearful, abominable, idolaters). The primary category in the Hebrew is not cowardice but **unbelief** — those who do not trust (*aman*).

**The directionality pattern holds:** "Lake of fire" has no Hebrew source; no back-translator would replace it with "fire of Sheol," an OT term from a different semantic domain. "Wages" and "portion" are not synonyms; no translator would swap one for the other. The Hebrew grounds every concept in established OT vocabulary. The Greek introduces novel images (lake of fire) and shifts categories (cowardly for unbelieving). The translation arrow points Hebrew → Greek.

**3. "The sea was no more" — symbolic resonance**

> וְהַיָּם לֹא יֵשׁ עוֹד — "and the sea was no more"

In Scripture, SEA symbolizes chaotic peoples — "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Rev 17:15). It is the opposite of MOUNTAIN (kingdom/ruling authority) and DRY LAND (stability/sanctified). "No more sea" means no more chaos, no more ungoverned nations, no more turbulence. The kingdom (mountain) has fully replaced the chaos (sea). The renewed *adamah* has no sea because there is no more human rebellion — only the kingdom of God, descended from heaven to earth.

---

## Psalm 115:16

> The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD's: but **the earth hath he given to the children of men.**

This single verse states the division plainly. Heaven is God's domain. Earth is man's inheritance. The promise was never to relocate humans to God's domain. The promise is that God relocates His throne to man's domain:

> The tabernacle of God is **with men**. — Revelation 21:3

---

## Conclusion

"Heaven" (שָׁמַיִם / οὐρανός) means the sky — the physical atmosphere, the cosmic expanse, and by extension, God's throne and governing authority. It is where God dwells, where rewards are stored, and where the city is prepared. It is NOT a destination for dead humans. No one in Scripture "goes to heaven" after death. The dead await resurrection. The inheritance is the earth. The city comes down. God comes down. The kingdom comes to earth.

The phrase "go to heaven" — the defining hope of popular Christianity — does not appear in the Bible. Not once.
