Jesus gave one imperative in the Olivet Discourse that is often overlooked: learn. "Learn a parable of the fig tree." Not "decode the fig tree" or "identify the fig tree." Learn the parable - understand what it teaches. The fig tree is not the subject. The season is the subject. And in Hebrew, summer and the end are nearly the same word.

I. "Learn This Parable"

Now learn a parable of the fig tree; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh: So likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors. Matthew 24:32-33

The parable is not about the identity of the tree. It is about the leaves - and what they announce. When a fig tree puts forth leaves, every farmer in Israel knows: summer is coming. The leaves are the signal. The question Jesus poses is not what is the fig tree? - it is what season is it?

That question has a Hebrew answer most English readers never hear.

II. The Untimely Fig - What Falls When Spring Arrives

And the stars of heaven fell unto the earth, even as a fig tree casteth her untimely figs, when she is shaken of a mighty wind. Revelation 6:13

The Greek word translated "untimely figs" is G3653 olynthos - a winter fig. In autumn, small figs form on the current year's wood and persist through winter on bare branches. They cling for months but never ripen. They are fruit of the wrong season - formed too late to mature before cold, too early to be part of the spring crop.

The Biological Mechanism

As long as a fruit is developing, a growth hormone (auxin) flows from it through the attachment zone, keeping it connected to the branch. When the fruit stops developing - as with winter figs that never ripen - auxin flow decreases. In spring, the tree redirects its life to new buds, new leaves, new fruit. The old unripe figs receive less and less of the tree's vitality. The attachment weakens. And then the wind comes and dislodges what was already internally disconnected.

The tree's own new growth causes the old fruit to fall. The arrival of the new season severs the connection. The untimely fig is not torn off by external violence - it is shed because the tree's life has moved on.

The Spirit/Wind Connection

In Hebrew, H7307 ruach means wind AND spirit AND breath. The "mighty wind" that shakes the untimely figs loose in Revelation 6:13 is the same ruach that gives life to the dead:

Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live. Ezekiel 37:9

The Spirit that raises the dead is the same Spirit that dislodges the unripe. The biological mechanism maps exactly: the tree's own life force - redirected to new growth - is what causes the old fruit to detach. The force that gives life and the force that sheds the old are one and the same.

In the same late-winter landscape, two trees stand side by side. The almond: dead wood blooming into life - the resurrection tree (Numbers 17:8, Aaron's rod budded and bore almonds overnight on dead wood). The olynthos: potential that clings but never ripens - the anti-resurrection. One is waking. The other is about to fall. The Watcher announces the season; the untimely fig is what the Watcher finds when He inspects.

The triumphal entry falls on Nisan 10 — lamb selection day (Exodus 12:3). The people came out to greet Jesus with palm branches (John 12:12-13). The fig tree parable says the leaves signal when he comes again. The first entry and the second are both announced by the fig tree: at the first, the cursed fig tree (Nisan 11, the morning after the entry) revealed a tree with leaves but no fruit. At the second, the fig tree putting forth leaves signals that summer — the end — is near. The untimely figs that fall in Revelation 6:13 are the counterpart to the cursed fig tree of the first entry: what never matured is shed as the King arrives.

IV. "Summer Is Near" = "The End Is Near"

God shows Amos a basket of summer fruit and then declares judgment. The pun is enacted - spoken aloud as a prophetic act:

And he said, Amos, what seest thou? And I said, A basket of summer fruit [H7019 qayits]. Then said the LORD unto me, The end [H7093 qets] is come upon my people of Israel; I will not again pass by them any more. Amos 8:1-2

Qayits (summer) and qets (end) are near-homophones. God holds up summer fruit and says: the end has come. Summer IS the end. When Jesus tells a Hebrew-speaking audience "summer is near," they hear: the end is near.

Greek confirms the same link. G2330 theros (summer) shares its root with G2325 therizo (to reap) and G2326 therismos (harvest). In both languages, summer and harvest and ending converge in a single word-family.

The Hebrew word behind "the end" deepens this further. H7093 qets comes from H7112 qatsats — "to cut off." The same cutting action as covenant-cutting (H3772 karath). The "end" in Hebrew is not a fade-out — it is a cut, the decisive boundary where covenant terms are executed. When Amos says qayits/qets (summer/end), the "end" he invokes is a covenant cut. And in Greek, G5056 telos — the word Jesus uses for "end" in Matthew 24:6,14 — means the purpose arriving, not termination but fulfillment. Summer is near = the goal is arriving = the covenant cut is approaching. (See the full study: The End.)

Each of these timelines originates from a different patriarch, a different covenant, a different book of Scripture. Genesis, Exodus, Numbers, Daniel, the Gospels, the Epistles. Different starting points, different counting methods - yet they arrive at the same doorstep.

Consider the anchor points. 120 Jubilees: "My spirit shall not always strive with man (H120 adam — mankind, the species), for that he also is flesh: yet his days shall be an hundred and twenty years" (Genesis 6:3). This cannot mean individual lifespan — people after the flood lived well past 120 (Abraham 175, Isaac 180, Jacob 147). The 120 years is how long God's spirit will contend with mankind. Read as 120 Jubilee cycles of 49 years each, this yields 5,880 years — a span that, from the traditional reckoning of Adam, terminates around 2025. Moses' 120 embodies the same number in a single life — the one man in Scripture who reached exactly 120: he died on his birthday (Deuteronomy 31:2, Deuteronomy 34:7), his body taken by God (Jude 9).

70 Jubilees from the Jordan: Israel crossed into the promised land around 1406 BC. Seventy cycles of 49 years plus the Jubilee year itself (the first year of the next cycle) = 3,431 years, landing at 2025/2026. Two days from the cross: Peter writes "one day is with the Lord as a thousand years" (2 Peter 3:8). Two thousand years from the crucifixion (~32 AD), minus seven, brings us to ~2025.

70 × 70 from Abraham: The covenant with Abraham (~1876 BC) plus 4,900 years (70 × 70), minus the 1,000-year reign, converges on the same window. Jacob at 77: Jacob left for Haran and found his bride at age 77. The modern state of Israel, born in 1948, turns 77 in 2025.

Daniel's 1290 + 1335: The KJV renders Daniel 12:11 as future tense throughout: "From the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that maketh desolate set up..." But the Hebrew verb forms distinguish two different tenses:

הוּסַר (husar) — Hophal Perfect: "was removed." Completed past action, passive voice. The daily offering was taken away — already done.

וְלָתֵת (ve-latet) — Qal Infinitive Construct with lamed: "and to the placing of." A purpose/direction infinitive — pointing forward to a future event.

שִׁקּוּץ (shiqquts) — H8251: "disgusting thing, filthy idol" — from H8262 shaqats "to loathe, detest."

שֹׁמֵם (shomem) — H8074: The Qal participle of shamem can be active ("the one that desolates") OR stative ("the one to be desolated"). The consonants שׁמם support both readings. The stative reading yields: the detestable thing destined for desolation — not the idol that causes destruction, but the idol that will itself be destroyed.

A literal rendering with the stative reading of שׁמם:

And from the time the daily offering was taken away, and to the setting up of the detestable thing to be desolated, there shall be 1,290 days. Daniel 12:11 (literal from Hebrew)

The taking away is past. The setting up is the destination. And the thing set up is destined for desolation — it will itself be destroyed. The 1,290 spans between a completed event and a future one. Jerusalem fell and the daily sacrifices ceased around 598 BC (the 12th month of Nebuchadnezzar's 6th year). Add 1,290 years: ~692 AD — the Dome of the Rock built on the temple mount. A detestable thing to be desolated.

Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days. Daniel 12:12

Blessed is he who waits until the detestable thing is itself made desolate. From the abomination (~692 AD) plus 1,335 years = ~2028. Two sequential periods — 1,290 + 1,335 = 2,625 years from 598 BC to ~2028. If the abomination is made desolate at the midpoint of the seven-year tribulation, then ~2028 minus 3.5 years points back to ~2025 — the same convergence window.

None of these calculations alone would be compelling. Together, the convergence is the signal. Seven leaves on the same branch, all appearing in the same season.

VI. The Moon Becomes Blood - 47 Thrones

The untimely figs of Revelation 6:13 fall in the same verse sequence as a detail that deserves its own attention:

And I beheld when he had opened the sixth seal, and, lo, there was a great earthquake; and the sun became black as sackcloth of hair, and the moon became as blood; And the stars of heaven fell unto the earth, even as a fig tree casteth her untimely figs, when she is shaken of a mighty wind. Revelation 6:12-13

The moon becoming blood and the untimely figs falling - in the same breath. The Hebrew word for "full moon" is H3677 keseh, and the word for "throne" is H3678 kisse. Both derive from the root H3680 kasah - "to cover, to fill up." Remove the Masoretic vowel points and the consonants are identical. Throne and full moon are the same word in unpointed Hebrew.

All 47 occurrences of this word in Revelation carry both readings. A Greek reader sees thronos. A Hebrew reader hears the full moon. When "clouds and darkness are round about him: righteousness and judgment are the habitation of his throne" (Psalm 97:2), clouds and darkness surrounding the full moon looks like a lunar eclipse - a blood moon.

VII. The "Never Again" Curse

The curse Jesus speaks over the fig tree uses a specific formula:

Let no fruit grow on thee henceforward for ever. Matthew 21:19

Permanent cessation. Never again. Now compare the curse over Babylon in Revelation:

And the fruits that thy soul lusted after are departed from thee... thou shalt find them no more at all. Revelation 18:14
The voice of harpers... shall be heard no more at all in thee; and no craftsman... shall be found any more in thee; and the sound of a millstone shall be heard no more at all in thee; And the light of a candle shall shine no more at all in thee; and the voice of the bridegroom and of the bride shall be heard no more at all in thee. Revelation 18:22-23

The same formula - never again - applied six times to Babylon and once to the fig tree. Both describe something with the appearance of life but no substance. Leaves on the tree, commerce in the city. Both receive the same sentence: permanent cessation with no restoration.

The Hebrew binds them tighter. The word "untimely birth" is H5309 nephel - literally "the fallen thing," from H5307 naphal, "to fall." Naphal is the ordinary Hebrew word for "fall" - it appears hundreds of times. But the derived noun nephel is rare (only 3 times in the Old Testament), and Isaiah's declaration over Babylon uses the same root, doubled:

Babylon is fallen [H5307 naphal], is fallen [H5307 naphal]; and all the graven images of her gods he hath broken unto the ground. Isaiah 21:9

In Hebrew, a word doubled intensifies. "Holy, holy, holy" is the supreme degree of holiness. And every instance of the doubled naphal as a prophetic declaration in Scripture points to the same subject: Babylon by name (Isaiah 21:9, Jeremiah 51:49), Egypt as the original model of the enslaving system (Ezekiel 30:6), Haman who decrees destruction of God's people from within the empire (Esther 6:13), and Sisera the military commander of the oppressing kingdom — tripled (Judges 5:27). The doubled naphal is not a general sentence on empires. It is the sentence reserved for Babylon the Great and its agents. The nephel — the fallen thing — derives from this root. Like the untimely fig that detaches when the new season arrives, the doubling seals what the "no more at all" enacts. No return. No reattachment.

VIII. What the Parable Says to Do

The passage following the fig tree parable (Matthew 24:32-51) contains four illustrations and one imperative. Each adds a layer:

1. Recognize the season (Fig tree, vv. 32-33). The leaves are the signal. The season does not wait for you to notice it.

2. Understand the pattern (Days of Noah, vv. 37-41). "They were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage." Normalcy masks danger. The flood did not arrive because people were sinning - it arrived while people were living as though nothing would change.

3. Watch constantly (Thief in the night, vv. 42-44). "If the goodman of the house had known in what watch the thief would come, he would have watched." The exact timing is unknowable - therefore watch always.

4. Serve faithfully (Faithful servant, vv. 45-51). "Who then is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath made ruler over his household, to give them meat in due season [G2540 kairos]?" The same word kairos appears in Mark 11:13 - "the kairos of figs was not yet" - linking the cursed fig tree to the faithful servant. Both are about producing at the appointed time.

He that gathereth in summer is a wise son: but he that sleepeth in harvest is a son that causeth shame. Proverbs 10:5

IX. The Almond Has Already Bloomed

The almond tree is blooming right now in Israel - white blossoms on bare wood, the first tree awake while everything else still sleeps. Its Hebrew name, H8247 shaqed, comes from H8245 shaqad - "to watch, to be wakeful." The tree is literally named "the watcher." God held it up before Jeremiah and said:

Thou hast well seen: for I will hasten [H8245 shaqad - watch over] my word to perform it. Jeremiah 1:12

God's watchfulness is not waiting for the season. He has already declared it. The almond is blooming. The winter figs are about to fall. The leaves are appearing on the branch. Seven independent timelines point to the same doorstep. The Hebrew word for summer sounds like the Hebrew word for end.

The question is not whether summer is coming. It is whether we recognize the leaves.
