We've published four new verse studies that connect in ways we didn't expect when we started. What began as a word study on "new" in Hebrews 10:20 pulled a thread that ran through Isaiah 14, Daniel 7, and Malachi 2 — and the thread is the calendar.

The Freshly-Slain Way (Hebrews 10:20)

Every major English translation renders Hebrews 10:20 as "a new and living way." The Greek word is G4372 prosphatos — a word that appears only here in the entire New Testament. Its etymology is unambiguous: pro (recently) + sphazo (to slaughter). It literally means "freshly slain."

"New" in English implies replacement — something that didn't exist before. "Freshly slain" implies access — the same door, freshly opened by blood. The way into God's presence was not abolished. It was freshly opened.

The same word for "living" (zōsan) appears in Romans 12:1 — "present your bodies a living sacrifice." The way is simultaneously freshly slaughtered and alive. So are we.

Son of Darkness (Isaiah 14:12–15)

Everyone knows Isaiah 14. The proud rebel who wanted to be God, cast down from heaven. But look up every Hebrew word — trace each one to its root — and the passage stops being about generic pride.

"Throne" and "full moon" are the same consonants (כסא), from the same root meaning "to cover." "Congregation" is moed — whose primary meaning is "appointed time." "North" is tsaphon — from a root meaning "to hide, to conceal." "Most High" is elyon — used as a plain adjective ("uppermost/greatest") in nearly half its biblical occurrences. And "be like" (damah) shares its consonants with a completely different root meaning "to silence, to destroy."

When the primary meanings are used:

"I will rise into the sky. I will raise my covered moon above the stars of God. I will establish the high place of the appointed times from the farthest extent of the darkness [of the moon]. I will rise at the height of the thick covering. I will silence the greatest [light]."

A blueprint for calendar corruption. The same agenda Daniel 7:25 names plainly: "He shall think to change times and laws."

The study walks through every word, argues why "full moon" fits better than "throne" in this context (the setting is the sky, the comparison is against stars, every surrounding word is calendrical), and traces the consequences Scripture attaches to calendar violations — from exile (Leviticus 26) to the burning of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 17:24–27) to dung on the priests' faces (Malachi 2:3).

The Foremost of Days (Daniel 7)

Daniel 7 is usually read as a prophecy about empires. It is. But the chapter's climactic conflict is calendrical: the adversary's defining act is changing times and laws (7:25), and the resolution comes when the "Ancient of Days" takes His throne and "the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom" (7:22).

The Aramaic word for "Ancient" comes from a root meaning "to advance, to proceed forward" — the same word used when Abraham advanced to his next camp. The "Ancient of Days" is not merely the old one. He is the one who advances the days — the Foremost of Days, the one from whom the calendar proceeds. And His throne (korse) is the Aramaic cognate of the Hebrew kisse/kese — the same word as Isaiah 14:13.

Isaiah 14 describes the usurpation. Daniel 7:25 describes the execution. Daniel 7:9 and 7:22 describe the restoration — the Foremost of Days reclaims His throne, and time is set right.

The Offal of Your Feasts (Malachi 2:1–9)

The KJV renders Malachi 2:3 as "I will corrupt your seed, and spread dung upon your faces." Two problems:

"Corrupt" translates H1605 gaar. This word appears 13 times in Scripture. In 11 of those, the KJV itself translates it "rebuke" — including Malachi 3:11, one chapter later. There is no lexical reason to render it "corrupt" here.

"Dung" translates H6569 peresh. It appears only 6 times. The other five are all the intestinal waste of a sin offering — the offal burned outside the camp (Exodus 29:14, Leviticus 4:11, 8:17, 16:27, Numbers 19:5). This isn't barnyard manure. It's the unclean refuse of a sacrifice. God will smear the sacrificial offal of their corrupt feasts on the priests' faces — and they will be carried away with it, like the waste of a sin offering removed from the camp.

The corrected reading: "I will rebuke your seed, and scatter the offal on your faces — the offal of your feasts — and you shall be carried away with it."

The Thread

These four studies connect:

  • Isaiah 14 describes the ambition — raising a counterfeit covered moon, seizing authority over the appointed times from darkness, silencing the greatest light.
  • Daniel 7 describes the execution — "he shall think to change times and laws" — and the restoration when the Foremost of Days reclaims His throne.
  • Malachi 2 describes the consequence for the priests who let it happen — the offal of their corrupt feasts smeared on their faces.
  • Hebrews 10:20 describes the resolution — the way into God's presence was not replaced. It was freshly opened by sacrifice. The same way. The same door. Fresh blood.

The calendar fell first. The temple followed. And the way back is not a new way — it is the freshly-slain and living way. The same path, freshly opened.
