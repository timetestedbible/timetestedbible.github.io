The Atzeret — A Deeper Look

The word behind "solemn assembly" is the word behind "the restrainer." This section is the detailed study behind the atzeret concept referenced throughout this post.

The Root: H6113 atsar — "to hold back, restrain, enclose"

H6113 atsar (עָצָר) is a primitive root meaning "to inclose; to hold back; to maintain, rule, assemble." Its noun form is H6116 atzeret (עֲצֶרֶת) — the "solemn assembly" that marks the closing day of a feast cycle:

  • Closing the womb — "The LORD hath restrained me from bearing" (Genesis 16:2). The very first use of the root is about life held back
  • Closing all wombs in judgment — "The LORD had fast closed up all the wombs of the house of Abimelech, because of Sarah" (Genesis 20:18)
  • God will not hold back at delivery — "Shall I cause to bring forth, and shut the womb?" (Isaiah 66:9). He who opens will not atsar at the moment of birth
  • The Shunammite woman — the same scene where כעת חיה ("time of life") appears. When her promised son dies, she tells her servant: "Slack not" (H6113 atsar) thy riding for me (2 Kings 4:24). She refuses to be held back

The Noun: H6116 atzeret — Closing Assembly

Every occurrence of atzeret in the Torah marks a terminal boundary:

  • Day 7 of Unleavened Bread — atzeret (Deuteronomy 16:8)
  • Day 8 of Sukkot — atzeret (Leviticus 23:36; Numbers 29:35)
  • Solomon's dedication — eighth day atzeret (2 Chronicles 7:9)
  • Ezra's Sukkot — eighth day atzeret (Nehemiah 8:18)

The atzeret is the day that holds everything in. The closure. The final enclosure before release.

The Greek: G2722 katechō = H6113 atsar

Paul's word in 2 Thessalonians 2:6–7 is G2722 katechō (κατέχω) — "to hold down, hold back, restrain, detain, hold fast." The semantic overlap with H6113 atsar is exact:
