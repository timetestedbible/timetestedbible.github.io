# Genesis 2:9

> *"And out of the ground made the LORD God to grow every $[tree] that is pleasant to the sight, and good for food; the $[tree] of life also in the midst of the $[garden], and the $[tree] of knowledge of good and evil."*

## The Symbolic Reading

> **In the center of God's defended sanctuary stand two nations: the living covenant community, and the mortal condition that experiences both desirability and ruin.**

---

## How Each Word Transforms

### "Tree" → "Nation"

H6086 *ets* — the $tree study establishes: tree = nation or people group. Five explicit identifications across different authors and centuries: Israel = vineyard (Isa 5:7), Babylon = tree (Dan 4:22), Assyria = cedar (Ezek 31:3), Israel = vine (Hos 10:1), Israel = olive tree (Rom 11:17–24). "All the trees of the field shall know that I the LORD have brought down the high tree, have exalted the low tree" (Ezek 17:24) — the trees of the field are the nations.

### "Life" → "Living"

H2416 *chay* — alive, living. The same word as in *mayim chayim* (living $water). *Ets chayim* = the living nation. Proverbs confirms: "She [wisdom/Torah] **is** a tree of life" (Prov 3:18). "The $[fruit] of the righteous is a tree of life" (Prov 11:30). "A wholesome tongue is a tree of life" (Prov 15:4). The tree of life is the covenant community sustained by Torah-faithfulness — characterized by life only.

### "Knowledge" → "Experience"

H1847 *da'ath* — from H3045 *yada'*: "to ascertain **by seeing**; used in a great variety of senses... observation, care, recognition." This is not information but intimate, firsthand knowing. The same root as "Adam *knew* (*yada'*) Eve" (Gen 4:1). *Da'ath* is the kind of knowing that comes from union with the thing known — experience, not awareness.

### "Good" → "Desirability"

H2896 *towb* — "good in the widest sense." KJV ranges across: "beautiful, bountiful, cheerful, at ease, fair, favour, glad, joyful, pleasant, pleasure, prosperity, sweet, wealth, welfare." Not merely moral good — the full spectrum of what is desirable, attractive, and beneficial.

### "Evil" → "Brokenness"

H7451 *ra'* — from H7489 *ra'a'*: "to **spoil** (literally, by **breaking to pieces**)." At root, evil = ruin, breakage, shattering. KJV also renders it as adversity, affliction, calamity, distress, harm, hurt, misery, wretchedness. The opposite of *towb*: everything that is ruinous, harmful, and destructive.

### "In the Midst" → "At the Center"

H8432 *tavek* — midst, center, among. Both trees stand at the center of the $garden (H1588 *gan* — defended enclosure, from H1598 *ganan*: "to hedge about, defend"). Two conditions, side by side at the heart of the sanctuary.

---

## The Two Trees

Gen 2:9 introduces both trees together. They are not opposites — they are **asymmetric**. One is pure; the other is mixed.

- **Tree of life** (*ets chayim*) = the nation characterized by **life only**. Unmixed. No death.
- **Tree of knowledge of good and evil** (*ets ha-da'ath towb wa-ra'*) = the condition of **experiencing both** — desirability AND brokenness, life AND death, mixed together.

The tree of knowledge of good and evil does not exclude life — it *contains* life alongside death. It is the **mixed** condition. The tree of life is the **unmixed** one.

Deuteronomy 30:15 presents the same choice at Sinai:

> *"See, I have set before thee this day **life and good**, and **death and evil**."* — Deut 30:15

Two paths. **Life and good** = the tree of life — the unmixed condition, covenant faithfulness leading to life. The other path introduces **death and evil** into the experience. This is not the tree of evil — it is the tree of experiencing *both*. But the cost of entering the mixed condition is that death comes with it. You cannot experience *ra'* without mortality. The tree of life offers life without death; the tree of knowledge of good and evil offers the full range — and the price of the full range is that death is part of it.

---

## The Mixed Element in the Holy Place

Both trees stand "in the midst" of the $garden — inside the defended sanctuary. The tree of life belongs there: pure life, unmixed. But the tree of knowledge of good and evil — the mixed condition, *towb* and *ra'* together — also stands at the center. Something impure inside the holy space.

And it is there by God's design. "Out of the ground made the LORD God to grow **every tree**" (Gen 2:9). He placed it. He did not hide it or wall it off. He gave one instruction: do not eat from it.

This is the same pattern as the adversary before the throne:

> *"The sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and **Satan came also among them**."* — Job 1:6

> *"He shewed me Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD, and **Satan standing at his right hand** to resist him."* — Zech 3:1

> *"The **accuser** of our brethren is cast down, which accused them **before our God** day and night."* — Rev 12:10

The adversary does not break in. He has access. He stands in the court, among the sons of God, before the throne. His access has boundaries — he can only operate within what God permits (Job 1:12, 2:6) — but he is **inside** the holy space.

The $serpent in the $garden is the same pattern: the deceiver operating from within the sanctuary. Not storming the gate — already present. And the priest's job was to guard against it.

### Adam's Failure to Guard

Gen 2:15 gave Adam the priestly mandate: H5647 *abad* (to serve) and H8104 *shamar* (to guard) the sanctuary. The same word-pair used for Levitical duty: *"They shall **keep** (shamar) his charge... to **do the service** (abad) of the tabernacle"* (Num 3:7). Adam was the first priest. His charge was to guard the holy space.

The tree of knowledge of good and evil was inside the garden — the mixed element in the holy place. The $serpent was inside the garden — the adversary with access to the court. Adam's mandate was not to remove them (God placed them there) but to keep the boundary: do not eat, do not consume, do not let the mixed thing become part of you.

He did not guard. He did not keep. The priest failed his charge. And Ezekiel echoes the indictment: *"Ye have **not kept the charge** of mine holy things"* (Ezek 44:8).

### Why the Mixed Element Is Permitted

The tree is there because the choice is the point. You cannot choose life unless death is present to choose. You cannot demonstrate faithfulness unless unfaithfulness is available. The presence of the mixed element inside the sanctuary is what makes Adam's obedience meaningful.

> *"I will rain $[bread] from heaven for you... **that I may prove them**, whether they will walk in my law, or no."* — Exod 16:4

The manna had rules. The tree has a rule. The alternative — the mixed thing inside the holy space — is how covenant faithfulness is tested. Jesus' parables confirm that the mixing is temporary and deliberate:

- **Wheat and tares** — *"Let both grow **together** until the $[harvest]"* (Mat 13:30). The enemy sows; the master says wait. Separation comes at the appointed time.
- **The net** — Gathers "of every kind" (Mat 13:47). Good and bad sorted on the shore, not in the water.
- **The man without a wedding garment** — Inside the feast, but exposed at inspection (Mat 22:11–13).

The mixed condition is not permanent. It persists until the $harvest — the appointed separation — when the $fire sorts what belongs from what does not.

## "Ye Shall Be as Gods"

The $serpent reframes the tree's promise:

> *"For God doth know that in the day ye eat thereof, then your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods (H430 *elohim*), **knowing** (H3045 *yada'*) good and evil."* — Gen 3:5

The promise is *da'ath* — experiential knowing of both sides. And God confirms the result:

> *"The man **is become as one of us**, to know good and evil."* — Gen 3:22

The $serpent did not lie about the *outcome* — only about the *cost*. The experience of both sides is real. The death is also real.

The "knowing good and evil" formula appears across Scripture as a marker of capacity:

- **Deut 1:39** — Children who "had no knowledge between good and evil" could enter the land. They had not yet entered the mortal experience of both sides.
- **2 Sam 14:17** — The woman of Tekoa compares the king's discernment to "an angel of God (*mal'ak elohim*), to discern good and bad."
- **1 Ki 3:9** — Solomon asks for a heart "to discern between good and bad" — the capacity to judge.
- **Isa 7:15–16** — Immanuel will eat butter and honey "that he may know to **refuse** (H3988 *ma'as*) the evil, and **choose** (H977 *bachar*) the good." Mature *da'ath* = the ability to reject *ra'* and elect *towb*.

## The First *Da'ath*

> *"And the eyes of them both were opened, and they **knew** (H3045 *yada'*) that they were **$[naked]**; and they sewed $[fig] leaves together, and made themselves aprons."* — Gen 3:7

The very first experience gained from the tree of knowledge of good and evil is the awareness of **$nakedness** — the state of being without covenant covering. The $naked study establishes the equation: naked = without covenant covering (Ezek 16:8 — "I covered thy nakedness... and entered into a covenant with thee"). The first taste of *ra'* (brokenness) is not pain or punishment but *exposure*.

And the root connection hides in the Hebrew: H5903 *'eyrom* ($naked) shares the root H6191 *'aram* with H6175 *'arum* (the $serpent's craftiness, Gen 3:1). The one who strips them of covering bears the same root as the condition it produces.

---

## Leaves Without Fruit

Their response to nakedness is $fig leaves — and the $fig-tree study traces what that means. The fig tree is the visible indicator of covenant fruitfulness. Its $fruit reveals covenant standing. But the fig tree has two modes: **leaves with fruit** (covenant health) and **leaves without fruit** (appearance without substance).

When Jesus approaches the fig tree outside Jerusalem, He finds "nothing but leaves" (Mk 11:13) — and curses it. No $fruit. No covenant works. Only the outward display.

In Gen 3:7, the first human response to covenant-loss is to reach for **leaves** — the appearance of covenant standing — without any $fruit (works of obedience) to back it up. They sew together the visible indicator of the thing they no longer possess.

The covering fails. Leaves cannot cover nakedness. Appearance cannot replace substance.

## The Lamb

> *"Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats (H3801 *kethoneth*) of skins (H5785 *'owr*), and **clothed** (H3847 *labash*) them."* — Gen 3:21

God replaces the fig leaves with skins. An animal must die. The first blood shed in Scripture is shed to provide covenant covering. This is the first $lamb — innocent life given to cover covenant breach.

The H3801 *kethoneth* is the same word used for the priestly garments (Exod 28:4, 39–40). God does not merely hide their nakedness — He clothes them in the garment of covenant service.

The contrast is precise:

- **Fig leaves** — human-made, no death required, appearance without $fruit
- **Skins from a $lamb** — God-provided, requires innocent life, actual covenant covering

The line runs from Gen 3:21 → the Passover $lamb (Exod 12) → "Behold the $[lamb] of God" (Jn 1:29) → "the $[lamb] slain from the foundation of the world" (Rev 13:8). The covering for $nakedness was always the $lamb.

## Driven Out — The Way Preserved

> *"And the LORD God said, Behold, the man is become as one of us, to know good and evil: and now, lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the $[tree] of life, and eat, and live for ever."* — Gen 3:22

God states the concern plainly: the man now experiences both *towb* and *ra'*, and must not also take hold of the tree of life. The text does not explain why — it simply records the action that follows.

> *"So he **drove out** (H1644 *garash*) the man."* — Gen 3:24

H1644 *garash* = to expel, cast out, drive away. This is not gentle. It is forcible removal from the sanctuary. The priest who failed to guard is expelled from the temple he was charged to keep.

But the tree of life is not destroyed — it is **guarded**. The $sword at the gate preserves (H8104 *shamar*) the $way (H1870 *derek*). The Gen 3:24 study traces what this means: *shamar* = to hedge about, preserve, guard — the same priestly mandate Adam was given in Gen 2:15. The *derek* = covenant-conduct, the pattern of life. And the $sword (*chereb*) shares its root with Horeb, the $mountain of the $fire-law.

The $way to the tree of life is not closed. It is *kept* — preserved by the $fire-word. And the $sword that guards it is the $fire-word itself: *chereb* shares its root with Horeb, the $mountain where God spoke Torah from $fire (Deut 33:2 — "from his right hand went a fiery law for them"). The $fire at the gate of Eden and the $fire at Sinai are the same word doing the same thing.

H2015 *haphak* — the sword's action — means "to **transform, convert, overturn**." Every Pentateuch use describes change of nature: water turned to $blood, $curse turned to blessing, Sodom overthrown. The sword does not rotate — it **converts what it contacts**.

The way back from the $thorns (Gen 3:18 — the cursed ground, the world outside the sanctuary) passes through the $fire-word. The covenant itself is $fire passing between pieces (Gen 15:17 — "a smoking furnace, and a burning lamp that passed between those pieces"). To re-enter the sanctuary is to pass through the $fire-covenant — the same Torah that is a tree of life to those who lay hold of it (Prov 3:18).

## Testing Against Scripture

**Deut 30:15–19 — Life-and-good vs death-and-evil.** Moses presents the identical choice at Sinai: *"I have set before you life and good, death and evil... therefore choose life."* Two trees restated as two covenant paths. **Supports.**

**Prov 3:18 — Wisdom IS a tree of life.** Torah-obedience is the tree of life. The unmixed condition — life through covenant faithfulness. The *da'ath* gained God's way (Prov 1:7: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge"). **Supports.**

**Prov 1:7 + Dan 12:4 — True da'ath vs multiplied da'ath.** True knowledge begins with the fear of the LORD (Prov 1:7). In the end-times, human *da'ath* (H1847) increases (Dan 12:4) while God's word cannot be found (Amos 8:12). Human experience multiplies while people stop choosing the tree that was always offered. **Supports.**

**Isa 5:20 — Calling evil good and good evil.** The end-state of experiencing both sides without Torah as the standard: the categories reverse. What is *ra'* (broken) gets called *towb* (desirable). What is *towb* gets called *ra'*. **Supports.**

**Rev 21:4, 22:2 — No more death; the tree of life remains.** "There shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain." The *ra'* is gone. The mixed condition dissolves because there is no more death to experience. Only the tree of life stands — leaves "for the healing of the nations." **Supports.**

**Rev 2:7 — "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life."** The tree was never taken away — it was offered again and again. Torah IS a tree of life (Prov 3:18). The $fruit of the righteous IS a tree of life (Prov 11:30). A wholesome tongue IS a tree of life (Prov 15:4). God set the choice before every generation: *"choose life"* (Deut 30:19). **Supports.**

No passage tested contradicts the reading. Six confirm it independently.
