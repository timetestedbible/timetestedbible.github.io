# Malachi 2:1–9 — The Offal of Your Feasts

> *"Behold, I will **corrupt** your **seed**, and spread **dung** upon your faces, even the **dung** of your solemn **feasts**; and one shall **take you away** with it."* — Mal 2:3 KJV

This verse is often quoted but rarely examined. Two translation choices obscure what God is actually saying to the priests — and why.

---

## "Corrupt" or "Rebuke"?

The KJV translates H1605 *gaar* as "corrupt" — *"I will **corrupt** your seed."* But *gaar* appears 13 times in Scripture, and in 11 of those occurrences the KJV itself translates it **"rebuke"**:

- *"His father **rebuked** him"* — Gen 37:10
- *"You have **rebuked** the nations"* — Ps 9:5
- *"He **rebuked** the Red Sea"* — Ps 106:9
- *"You have **rebuked** the proud"* — Ps 119:21
- *"God shall **rebuke** them"* — Isa 17:13
- *"The LORD **rebuke** you, O Satan"* — Zech 3:2
- *"I will **rebuke** the devourer for your sakes"* — Mal 3:11

That last one is one chapter later — same book, same author, same word, translated "rebuke." There is no lexical reason to render *gaar* as "corrupt" in 2:3. The word means **rebuke**. God is rebuking the priests' offspring, not corrupting them.

*"I will **rebuke** your seed."*

---

## "Dung" — or the Offal of a Sacrifice?

The word translated "dung" is H6569 *peresh*. It appears only 6 times in all of Scripture. The other five:

- **Exodus 29:14** — the *peresh* of the **sin offering** bullock, burned **outside the camp**
- **Leviticus 4:11** — the *peresh* of the sin offering, carried **forth outside the camp**
- **Leviticus 8:17** — the *peresh*, burned with fire **outside the camp**
- **Leviticus 16:27** — the *peresh* of the **Day of Atonement** sin offering, burned **outside the camp**
- **Numbers 19:5** — the *peresh* of the red heifer, burned entirely

Every single occurrence outside Malachi is the **intestinal contents of a sacrificial animal** — the unclean waste that must be removed from the camp and burned. This is not barnyard manure. It is a technical sacrificial term: the offal of the offering, the part that is rejected, carried out, destroyed.

So "the *peresh* of your feasts" is not "dung associated with your holidays." It is **the sacrificial offal produced by your corrupt feasts** — the unclean waste of offerings made at the wrong time, in the wrong way, under a corrupted calendar. And God will smear it on the priests' faces.

---

## "Taken Away with It"

*"And you shall be **taken away** (H5375 *nasa*) with it."*

H5375 *nasa* means to lift up, carry, bear away. The same word used for carrying the offal outside the camp. The priests will be treated like the *peresh* itself — carried out and removed. They become the refuse of their own corrupt sacrifices.

This is not a metaphor about embarrassment. It is a sentence: you will be treated as the unclean waste of a sin offering. Carried outside. Burned.

---

## Why This Matters

The context (Mal 2:1–9) makes the offense clear:

- **v.5–6** — The covenant with Levi was life and peace. *"The **law of truth** was in his mouth ... he walked with me in peace and equity, and did **turn many** away from iniquity."*
- **v.7** — *"The priest's lips should keep **knowledge**, and they should seek the **law** at his mouth: for he is the **messenger** of the LORD of hosts."*
- **v.8** — *"But **you** are departed out of **the $way**; you have caused many to **stumble at the law**; you have **corrupted the covenant** of Levi."*

The priests were supposed to guard Torah and turn people toward it. Instead they departed from the $way, caused people to stumble at the law, and corrupted the covenant. The feasts they administer produce offal — unclean waste — because the feasts themselves are wrong.

Malachi 2:3 is not about generic unfaithfulness. It is addressed specifically to priests who corrupted the calendar and caused the people to stumble. The punishment fits the crime: the sacrificial waste of their corrupt feasts will be their legacy.

---

## The Corrected Reading

> *"Behold, I will **rebuke** your seed, and **scatter** the **offal** on your faces — the **offal** of your **feasts** — and you shall be **carried away** with it."*

Every word uses an attested meaning. "Rebuke" is how *gaar* is translated 11 of 13 times. "Offal" is what *peresh* refers to in every other occurrence. "Carried away" is the standard meaning of *nasa*. The KJV's "corrupt" and "dung" flatten a precise indictment into a vague insult.
