# Daniel 12:11-12

> *"And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that maketh desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days. Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days."*

## The Symbolic Reading

> **From the time the daily offering was removed and to the setting up of the detestable thing destined for destruction — 1,290 days.**

The key insight is in the Hebrew verb tenses, which the KJV flattens into English.

---

## The Verb Tenses

### "Was Taken Away" — Completed Past

**Hebrew:** הוּסַר (*husar*)
**Form:** Hophal Perfect — passive, completed action

The Hophal is the passive of the causative (Hiphil). The Perfect aspect marks the action as **completed** — something that was done, not something yet to happen. "The daily offering **was removed**" — past tense, accomplished fact.

This matters because it places the removal of the daily offering in the **past** relative to the prophecy's fulfillment — a completed historical event, not a prediction. Three biblical sources and two cuneiform tablets converge on the same moment: the capture of Jerusalem on **2 Adar in Nebuchadnezzar's 7th year** (~598 BC), when the temple vessels were taken to Babylon and the daily offerings ceased (see [The Historical Anchor](#the-historical-anchor-2-adar-nebuchadnezzars-7th-year) below).

### "To the Setting Up" — Future Direction

**Hebrew:** וְלָתֵת (*velatet*)
**Form:** Qal Infinitive Construct with lamed prefix

The lamed (ל) prefix on the infinitive indicates **purpose or direction** — "to the setting up of" or "for the giving of." This points **forward** — from the past removal toward a future event.

### "To Be Desolated" — Destined for Destruction

**Hebrew:** שֹׁמֵם (*shomem*)
**Strong's:** H8074 — שָׁמֵם (*shamem*)

The KJV renders this "that maketh desolate" — the abomination is the active agent of desolation. But H8074 in the Qal can also be **stative**: "to be desolated, to be appalled." The detestable thing is not the thing that CAUSES desolation — it is the thing **destined TO BE desolated**.

This inverts the traditional reading: the "abomination" is not the destroyer. It is the thing that will itself be destroyed.

---

## The Two Readings

| Traditional | Alternative |
|-------------|-------------|
| "the daily sacrifice **shall be** taken away" (future) | "the daily offering **was** removed" (Hophal Perfect: completed past) |
| "the abomination **that maketh** desolate" (active agent) | "the detestable thing **to be** desolated" (stative: destined for destruction) |
| "set up" (passive, vague timing) | "**to** the setting up of" (directional: pointing toward a future event) |

## The Historical Anchor: 2 Adar, Nebuchadnezzar's 7th Year

The Hebrew verb tenses demand a **past event** as the starting point. What event? Three independent witnesses — Jeremiah, 2 Kings, and a Babylonian cuneiform tablet — converge on the same moment.

### Jeremiah's Date

> *"This is the people whom Nebuchadrezzar carried away captive: **in the seventh year** three thousand Jews and three and twenty."*
> — <a href="/reader/bible/akjv/Jeremiah/52#v28" onclick="event.preventDefault();AppStore.dispatch({type:'SET_VIEW',view:'reader',params:{contentType:'bible',translation:'akjv',book:'Jeremiah',chapter:52,verse:28}})">Jeremiah 52:28</a>

Jeremiah dates the first captivity to Nebuchadnezzar's **7th year** using regnal (non-accession) counting. The parallel account in 2 Kings calls this the **8th year**:

> *"And the king of Babylon took him **in the eighth year of his reign**."*
> — <a href="/reader/bible/akjv/2+Kings/24#v12" onclick="event.preventDefault();AppStore.dispatch({type:'SET_VIEW',view:'reader',params:{contentType:'bible',translation:'akjv',book:'2 Kings',chapter:24,verse:12}})">2 Kings 24:12</a>

Same event, one-year difference. This is not a contradiction — it proves Jeremiah uses **regnal** year counting (counting from year 1), while Kings uses **accession-year** counting (the first partial year = year 0). This distinction matters for all prophetic chronology.

### The Babylonian Chronicle: An Exact Date

The cuneiform tablet BM 21946 (the "Jerusalem Chronicle"), published by D.J. Wiseman in 1956, records the **exact day**:

> *"In the seventh year, in the month of Kislev, the king of Akkad mustered his troops, marched to Hatti-land, and encamped against the city of Judah. **On the second day of the month of Adar** he seized the city and captured the king."*
> — Babylonian Chronicle (BM 21946 / ABC 5)

**2 Adar** — the 2nd day of the 12th month — in Nebuchadnezzar's 7th year. Jerusalem fell. Jehoiachin surrendered. The temple vessels were carried to Babylon. The daily offerings ceased.

### "When the Year Was Expired"

2 Chronicles confirms the timing with a Hebrew idiom:

> *"**When the year was expired**, king Nebuchadnezzar sent, and brought him to Babylon, with the goodly vessels of the house of the LORD."*
> — <a href="/reader/bible/akjv/2+Chronicles/36#v10" onclick="event.preventDefault();AppStore.dispatch({type:'SET_VIEW',view:'reader',params:{contentType:'bible',translation:'akjv',book:'2 Chronicles',chapter:36,verse:10}})">2 Chronicles 36:10</a>

"When the year was expired" (לִתְשׁוּבַת הַשָּׁנָה, *litshuvat hashanah*) means the **turn of the year** — the end of Adar, the last month. This places the captivity in Adar, exactly as the Babylonian Chronicle records.

Five independent sources — three biblical, two cuneiform — converge on the same event in Adar of 598/597 BC. The daily offering **was removed** (Hophal Perfect) on a date we can identify to the exact day.

## The 1,290 Days

The count of 1,290 days runs **from** the removal of the daily offering **to** the setting up of the detestable thing that is destined for destruction.

If "days" function as prophetic **years** (as in <a href="/reader/bible/akjv/Ezekiel/4#v6" onclick="event.preventDefault();AppStore.dispatch({type:'SET_VIEW',view:'reader',params:{contentType:'bible',translation:'akjv',book:'Ezekiel',chapter:4,verse:6}})">Ezekiel 4:6</a> — "I have appointed thee each day for a year"), then 1,290 years from the captivity in 598 BC reach **692 AD** — and the precision is striking:

- **AH 72** (Islamic year 72) runs from ~21 June 691 to ~10 June 692. The mosaic inscription inside the Dome of the Rock dates its construction to this year: *"Built this dome the servant of God Abd al-Malik... in the year two and seventy."*
- **The 1,290th year** from 598 BC = year 692, running from ~Nisan 692 (April) to ~Adar 693 (March).
- **The two periods overlap** from approximately **April to June 692** — a window of about three months. The Dome of the Rock was completed within this overlap: the intersection of the Islamic construction year and the prophetic endpoint.

A detestable thing (*shiqquts*) — set up on the site of the holy of holies — **to be desolated** (*shomem*). Not the cause of desolation, but the thing destined for destruction.

### The 1,335 Years: "Blessed Is He That Waiteth"

> *"Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days."*
> — <a href="/reader/bible/akjv/Daniel/12#v12" onclick="event.preventDefault();AppStore.dispatch({type:'SET_VIEW',view:'reader',params:{contentType:'bible',translation:'akjv',book:'Daniel',chapter:12,verse:12}})">Daniel 12:12</a>

From the abomination's setup (~692 AD) plus 1,335 lunar years = **~2028**. Blessed is the one who waits until the detestable thing is itself made desolate.

Two sequential periods — 1,290 + 1,335 = 2,625 years from ~598 BC to ~2028.
