# Isaiah 22:14

> *"Surely this **iniquity** shall not be **purged** from you till ye **die**, saith the Lord GOD of hosts."* — KJV

---

## Why This Verse Matters

God declares this sin will not be H3722 *kaphar*-ed — covered, atoned for, expiated — "until you die." But ordinary death does not atone for sin. If it did, everyone who dies would be forgiven. The Targum Jonathan recognized the problem:

> *"This your iniquity shall not be forgiven you till you **die the second death**, said the Lord, the God of hosts."*

The Hebrew signals a death beyond atonement's reach. H3722 *kaphar* — the Day of Atonement word, the word for the mercy seat's covering — fails here. This iniquity is not covered until a death occurs that is itself the final reckoning. Not a death that leads to resurrection, but a death that IS the sentence.

This is one of six OT passages where the Targums use "second death," always meaning permanent, final death.
