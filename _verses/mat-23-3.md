# The Seat of Moses — Do As *He* Says

> *"The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses' seat: All therefore whatsoever they bid you observe, that observe and do; but do not ye after their works: for they say, and do not."* — Mat 23:2-3 (KJV)

Read that way, the verse commands obedience to the Pharisees' teaching — and thirteen verses later Jesus is overturning their oath-rulings (Mat 23:16-22), and one chapter earlier He warned against "the leaven of the Pharisees… their doctrine" (Mat 16:12), and He had already convicted them of "making the word of God of none effect through your tradition" (Mark 7:13; Mat 15:3-9). The Greek reading contradicts its own chapter.

## What the Hebrew Says

The oldest Hebrew text of Matthew — the Shem Tov text preserved in the 1380 *Even Bochan* — reads:

> על כסא משה ישבו הפירושים והחכמים. ועתה כל אשר **יאמר** לכם שמרו ועשו **ובתקנותיהם** ומעשיהם אל תעשו שהם אומרים והם אינם עושים

> *"Upon the seat of Moses sit the Pharisees and the sages. And now, all that **he says** to you, keep and do; but by their **takkanot** and their deeds do not do — for they say, and do not."*

Two words change everything:

- **יאמר — "he says," singular.** The antecedent is Moses — the seat's Torah, read aloud from it — not the men sitting on it. The Greek reads plural, "whatever *they* say."
- **תקנותיהם — "their takkanot."** Not a loose word for "works": *takkanah* is the technical rabbinic term for an enacted ordinance — the man-made legislative layer, the "tradition of the elders" itself.

The verse then reads as one consistent command: **do Moses' law; do not do their tradition.** The very next verse describes the takkanot — "they bind heavy burdens… on men's shoulders" (Mat 23:4) — and the oath-rulings Jesus strikes down in vv. 16-22 are worked examples.

## One Vav

The entire difference between "he says" and "they say" is a single letter: יאמר vs יאמרו. The same jot-level variance that separates "they pierced" from "like a lion" in Psalm 22:16. A Greek translator normalizing the verb to the nearer plural noun — the Pharisees — produces the Greek reading; the technical pair "their takkanot and their deeds" flattens to "their works" (ἔργα).

And the direction of drift is documented: the later Hebrew copies (du Tillet 1555, Münster) were *revised toward* the Greek. Where this text differs from the Greek, it is the earlier stratum. The custodians of this text were rabbinic Jews who lived by the takkanot — "do what Moses says, not their takkanot" is a reading *against the transmitters' own interest*, the kind a copyist softens but does not invent.
