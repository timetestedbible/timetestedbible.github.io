# From That Day, No One Knows

> *"Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only."* — Mat 24:35-36 (KJV)

"No one can know the day" may be the most-quoted line in prophecy — quoted to end every conversation about timing. The Hebrew Matthew (Shem Tov, *Even Bochan*) reads the two verses this way:

> והשמים והארץ יעברו. **ומהיום ההוא ומהעת ההיא** אין מי שיודע ולא מלאכי השמים אלא האב בלבד

> *"And the heavens and the earth shall pass away. And **from that day and from that time**, there is none who knows — not even the messengers of the heavens — but the Father alone."*

Three observations, all in the letters:

1. **The מ prefix sits on both nouns** — *u-me-ha-yom ha-hu u-me-ha-et ha-hi*, "from that day and from that time." Not the Greek's "concerning (περὶ) that day." The verse states *from when* the not-knowing runs — and "that day" points backward to the clause just spoken: the day heaven and earth pass away. From that day forward, none knows.
2. **There is no "neither the Son."** The Hebrew names the angels and the Father only.
3. **The text's own structure agrees.** Shem Tov's manuscript section break falls *after* verse 36 — binding "from that day" to the heaven-and-earth clause of v. 35, not to the Noah comparison that follows (v. 37, which in the Hebrew reads "the **days** of the Son of Man," not "the coming").

Verse 35 is also shorter than the Greek: "heaven and earth shall pass away" — full stop.
