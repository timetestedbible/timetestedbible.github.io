# The Bridegroom — and the Bride

> *"Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom."* — Mat 25:1 (KJV)

The Hebrew Matthew (Shem Tov, *Even Bochan*) ends the verse with two more words:

> מלכות שמים דומה לעשר בתולות שלקחו נרותיהן ויצאו **לקראת חתן וכלה**

> *"The kingdom of heaven is like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet **a bridegroom and a bride**."*

The same reading stands in Codex Bezae — the oldest Greek–Latin codex — the Old Latin, and the Syriac. The virgins are not the bride; they go out to *meet* her. Scripture painted the scene long before, in the royal wedding psalm: "the **virgins her companions** that follow her… shall enter into the king's palace" (Ps 45:14-15). The $virgin is the espoused — covenant standing, not attainment — and the ten are the bride's company awaiting the wedding.

## The Readings Down the Parable

- **v. 2** — five were עצלות כסילות, "**lazy** fools," and five זריזות וחכמות, "**diligent** and wise." The fool's disease named outright: hearing without rising to do.
- **v. 5** — the bridegroom ויתמהמה, "**tarried**" — Habakkuk 2:3's exact verb, where the vision is "for the appointed time… though it tarry, wait for it."
- **v. 6** — the midnight cry opens ויהי בחצי הלילה — the Passover-stroke formula of Exodus 12:29, word for word.
- **v. 8** — the foolish confess שנרותינו **נדעכו**, "our lamps are **put out**" — *nid'akhu*, the verb Scripture keeps for one lamp only: "the $lamp of the **wicked** shall be **put out**" (Prov 13:9; 24:20; Job 18:5-6). The wise virgin's portrait is the opposite verse: "her candle **goeth not out** by night" (Prov 31:18).
- **v. 10** — the ready go in with him **לחופה**, "into the *chuppah*" — the bridegroom's canopy of Psalm 19:5 — and **ונסגר השער**, "the **gate** was shut": *sha'ar*, the sky-gate word, not a house door.
- **v. 13** — "you do not know the day nor the hour **when the bridegroom will come**" (שיבא החתן) — no "wherein the Son of man cometh" (a clause the oldest Greek also lacks). The watch is for a wedding procession.

The $oil that divides the ten cannot be the Spirit — it is bought ("go to them that sell, and **buy**," v. 9; "**buy** the truth," Prov 23:23), stored in one's own vessel, and no one can lend it: proven works, the doing that turns the commandment-$lamp into light.
