# After Those Days

> *"Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken."* — Mat 24:29 (KJV)

Whole prophetic systems hang on the Greek's εὐθέως — "immediately" — and on the phrase "the tribulation of those days." The Hebrew Matthew (Shem Tov, *Even Bochan*) carries neither:

> ובאותה שעה אחרי הימים ההם יחשך השמש והירח לא יגיה אורו והכוכבים יפלו מהשמים וחיל השמים יתנדדו

> *"And **in that hour, after those days**, the sun shall darken, and the moon shall not give forth its light, and the stars shall fall from the heavens, and the host of the heavens shall be shaken."*

No "immediately." No "tribulation" — George Howard's English of the Shem Tov text prints "(the tribulation of)" in parentheses precisely because the Hebrew does not carry it. The signs are pinned to *a time* — "in that hour, after those days" — not to the far side of a tribulation clock.

Two verses later the gathering comes "with a **shofar** and a great voice… from the **four winds** of the heavens" (v. 31), and the fig-tree lesson says "he is near, **at the gates**" — לשערים (v. 33), the same gate word the sky's architecture uses throughout Scripture.
