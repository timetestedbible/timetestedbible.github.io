## Context Bag

### Strong's Data
H7585 *sheol* — "Hades or the world of the dead (as if a subterranean retreat), including its accessories and inmates." From H7592 *sha'al* = "to inquire, to request, to DEMAND." KJV: "grave" (31×), "hell" (31×), "pit" (3×). 65× OT.
G86 *hades* — "properly, UNSEEN (from alpha-privative + G1492 *eido* = to see); Hades or the place/state of departed souls." KJV: "hell" (10×), "grave" (1×). 11× NT.
H4194 *maveth* — "death; concretely, the dead, their place or state." From H4191 *muth* (to die). Often paired with sheol.
H6913 *qeber* — "a sepulchre" — the physical burial site. Distinct from sheol (the state/realm).
G1067 *gehenna* — "valley of Hinnom; figuratively, everlasting punishment." DISTINCT from hades — gehenna is judgment, hades is the interim state.
G2288 *thanatos* — "death" — NT equivalent of H4194.

### Etymology Is the Key
H7585 *sheol* derives from H7592 *sha'al* = "to ask, inquire, request, DEMAND." Sheol is literally "the demanding place" or "the place that asks/requires." This matches its character across Scripture: sheol demands, is never satisfied, always opens its mouth for more.

Prov 27:20 — "Hell (sheol) and destruction are NEVER FULL; so the eyes of man are never satisfied."
Prov 30:16 — "The grave (sheol); and the barren womb; the earth that is not filled with water; and the fire that saith not, It is enough." Four insatiable things — sheol is the first.
Isa 5:14 — "hell (sheol) hath ENLARGED herself, and OPENED HER MOUTH without measure."
Hab 2:5 — "enlargeth his desire as hell (sheol), and is as death, and CANNOT be satisfied."

### Sheol Is Personified as a Power
Not just a location — an active entity:
- Has a MOUTH (Isa 5:14, Prov 1:12)
- Has a BELLY (Jonah 2:2)
- Has GATES (Isa 38:10, Mat 16:18)
- Has BARS (Job 17:16)
- Has a HAND/POWER (Ps 49:15, Ps 89:48, Hos 13:14)
- FOLLOWS Death as a companion (Rev 6:8)
- DELIVERS UP its dead (Rev 20:13)
- Is ultimately DESTROYED — cast into lake of fire (Rev 20:14)

### Both Righteous and Wicked Go to Sheol
Jacob expects to go to sheol mourning (Gen 37:35). Job asks to be hidden in sheol (Job 14:13). David, Hezekiah, psalmists all face sheol. But also: "the wicked shall be turned into hell" (Ps 9:17). Sheol is the universal destination — its demand is on ALL flesh. It is not punishment. It is mortality's claim.

### What Sheol Is NOT
Sheol ≠ qeber (physical tomb). Sheol ≠ gehenna (judgment/punishment). Sheol ≠ the lake of fire (final destruction). Sheol ≠ the pit/abyss (temporary containment of the living). These are distinct in Scripture though they overlap.

### God's Power Over Sheol
1 Sam 2:6 — "The LORD killeth, and maketh alive: he bringeth down to the grave (sheol), and bringeth up."
Ps 16:10 / Acts 2:27,31 — "thou wilt not leave my soul in hell (sheol/hades)" — messianic, fulfilled in resurrection.
Ps 49:15 — "God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave (sheol)."
Hos 13:14 — "I will ransom them from the power of the grave (sheol); I will redeem them from death: O death, I will be thy plagues; O grave (sheol), I will be thy destruction."
Rev 1:18 — "I have the keys of hell (hades) and of death."
1 Cor 15:55 — "O death, where is thy sting? O grave (hades), where is thy victory?"
Rev 20:14 — "death and hell (hades) were cast into the lake of fire."

### Sheol Parallels Pit (Bowr) — But Differs
Ps 30:3 — sheol ‖ bowr. Isa 14:15 — sheol ‖ bowr. Isa 38:18 — sheol ‖ bowr. The parallelism shows overlap. But the pit-abyss study argues the pit is temporary containment (everything comes out). Sheol holds the dead. Both involve descent and potential release. The difference: the pit restrains INFLUENCE; sheol claims LIFE.

### Connection to Water Study
The cistern (H877/H953 bor/bowr) = grave in Hebrew parallelism. A cistern with no water is a death-container. To forsake the fountain of living water (Jer 2:13) for cisterns is to choose sheol — the demanding place — over the source of life.

### Pattern Summary
1. Sheol from sha'al (to demand) = the demanding place, never satisfied
2. Personified as a power — mouth, belly, gates, bars, hand
3. Universal — both righteous and wicked descend
4. Distinct from gehenna (punishment) and the lake of fire (final)
5. God has power over it — ransom, keys, resurrection
6. Christ is the answer to sheol's demand — Ps 16:10, Acts 2:27-31
7. Sheol itself is destroyed — cast into lake of fire (Rev 20:14)
8. Death and hades are a PAIR — always together (Rev 6:8, 20:13-14)
