## Roots

**G1577 ekklesia** = from G1537 ek (out of) + derivative of G2564 kaleo (to call). "A calling out." Strong's: "a popular meeting, especially a religious congregation (Jewish SYNAGOGUE, or Christian community of members on earth or saints in heaven or both)." KJV: "church" (111×), "assembly" (3×). 115× NT total. NOTE: Strong's own definition includes JEWISH SYNAGOGUE under this word.

**G2564 kaleo** = to call (aloud). The root verb. Related: G2821 klesis = calling/invitation. G2822 kletos = called/invited.

**G4864 synagoge** = from G4863 synago (to bring together). "An assemblage of persons." KJV: "assembly, congregation, synagogue." Strong's: "specially a Jewish synagogue; by analogy, a CHRISTIAN CHURCH." Both ekklesia and synagoge cover both Jewish and Christian assemblies in Strong's definitions.

**H6951 qahal** = from H6950 qahal (to convoke, assemble). "Assemblage." KJV: "assembly, company, congregation, multitude." 116× OT. This is the word the LXX renders as ekklesia.

**H6950 qahal** = root verb: to convoke, assemble together. H6953 qoheleth = "assembler" — the title of Ecclesiastes. The Preacher/Assembler.

**H5712 edah** = from H5707 ed (witness). "Stated assemblage, concourse, family, crowd." KJV: "assembly, company, congregation." Often rendered synagoge in LXX. Derives from WITNESS — the assembly is a witness-body.

**H4150 moed** = from H3259 yaad (to appoint). "Appointment, fixed time, festival, assembly, congregation, place of meeting." KJV: "appointed time, assembly, congregation, feast, season, synagogue." The tabernacle is called "tent of moed" (tent of meeting/appointment). The feasts are "moedim" (appointed times). co: SHADOW(Col 2:17 appointed times as shadow).

## Context Bag — H6951 qahal (OT)

Gen 28:3 — Isaac to Jacob: "be fruitful and multiply, that thou mayest be a QAHAL of people." co: FRUIT, ISRAEL. Assembly-concept applied to Jacob's offspring from patriarchal era — before Sinai, before the law.

Gen 35:11 — God to Jacob: "a nation and a QAHAL of nations shall be of thee." co: ISRAEL, NATIONS. Compare Gen 48:19 "melo ha-goyim" (Ephraim study). Assembly of nations promised to Jacob.

Gen 48:4 — Jacob recounts: "I will make of thee a QAHAL of people." Same promise, now connected to Joseph/Ephraim's blessing. co: EPHRAIM.

Exo 12:6 — "the whole QAHAL of the EDAH (H5712) of Israel shall kill it in the evening." FIRST PASSOVER. Both assembly-words together. The qahal acts corporately at the founding event — the same event that includes the mixed multitude (Exo 12:38) and provides for strangers (Exo 12:48). co: ISRAEL, COVENANT.

Deut 5:22 — "These words the LORD spake unto all your QAHAL in the mount out of the midst of the fire." SINAI. God addresses the qahal directly. Acts 7:38 renders this same assembly as "the ekklesia in the wilderness." co: FIRE, TRUMPET(implied — Exo 19:16).

Deut 9:10 — "the day of the QAHAL." Sinai is called "the day of the assembly." co: FIRE.

Num 10:7 — "when the QAHAL is to be gathered together, ye shall blow, but ye shall not sound an alarm." TRUMPET summons the qahal. co: TRUMPET. The assembly is convoked by trumpet — same pattern as 1Th 4:16.

Num 15:15 — "one ordinance for the QAHAL, and for the stranger that sojourneth." co: ISRAEL(inclusion), COVENANT. The assembly operates under one law for native and stranger.

Num 16:3 — "the QAHAL of the LORD." The assembly belongs to God. Possessive — same as Mat 16:18 "MY ekklesia."

Deut 23:1-3 — qahal of the LORD has entry requirements: excludes Ammonite/Moabite. BUT: Ruth (Moabitess) joins Israel and enters the messianic line. Isa 56:3-7 explicitly opens the qahal to strangers who keep covenant. The text presents both the restriction and its overcoming.

Ps 22:22 — "I will declare thy NAME unto my brethren: in the midst of the QAHAL will I praise (H1984 halal) thee." co: NAME, PRAISE. **→ Heb 2:12 quotes this as: "in the midst of the EKKLESIA (G1577) will I sing praise."** Direct H6951→G1577 chain. The "church" in Hebrews IS the "congregation" of the Psalms. Same word.

Ps 89:5 — "thy faithfulness in the QAHAL of the saints (H6918)." co: PRAISE(v5 H3034 yadah — Judah's root), SAINTS.

## Context Bag — G1577 ekklesia (NT)

**JESUS' USAGE (2×):**

Mat 16:18 — "upon this ROCK I will build MY ekklesia; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it." co: ROCK. Jesus says "MY ekklesia" — possessive, same as "qahal of the LORD" (Num 16:3). His Jewish audience would hear: the assembly of God's people. The word is not explained as new — it's assumed to be understood.

Mat 18:17 — "tell it unto the ekklesia: if he neglect to hear the ekklesia, let him be unto thee as a heathen man and a publican." DISPUTE RESOLUTION function. Same function as the qahal in OT judicial contexts. co: COVENANT.

**ACTS 7:38 — THE DEFINING VERSE:**

Acts 7:38 — Stephen: "This is he that was in the EKKLESIA (G1577) in the wilderness with the angel which spake to him in mount Sina, and with our fathers." Stephen calls ISRAEL AT SINAI "the ekklesia." Same G1577 used for the NT communities. The word spans Sinai to the present without a break. co: ISRAEL, FIRE, TRUMPET.

**ACTS — LOCAL ASSEMBLIES:**

Acts 2:47 — "the Lord added to the ekklesia daily such as should be saved." co: ISRAEL(Jerusalem context).
Acts 8:1 — "great persecution against the ekklesia at Jerusalem; all scattered." co: JERUSALEM.
Acts 9:31 — "the ekklesiai... throughout Judaea and Galilee and Samaria had rest." Plural — multiple local assemblies. co: PEACE.
Acts 14:23 — "ordained elders in every ekklesia." Each assembly has its own leadership.
Acts 14:27 — "gathered the ekklesia together, rehearsed... he had opened the door of faith unto the GENTILES." co: NATIONS, FAITH.

**ACTS 19 — SECULAR ASSEMBLY (SAME WORD):**

Acts 19:32 — pagan riot at Ephesus: "the ekklesia was confused." KJV translates as "assembly."
Acts 19:39 — "lawful ekklesia." KJV: "lawful assembly." Civic/legal assembly.
Acts 19:41 — "dismissed the ekklesia." KJV: "dismissed the assembly."
SAME G1577. KJV translates as "assembly" here, "church" elsewhere. The word itself = called-out gathering. Not inherently religious.

**JAMES 2:2 — SYNAGOGE FOR BELIEVERS:**

Jas 2:2 — "if there come unto your SYNAGOGE (G4864) a man with a gold ring." James uses synagoge (not ekklesia) for the believers' gathering. Both words cover the same reality. The distinction between "synagogue" and "church" is a later tradition, not in the text.

**PAUL — BODY THEOLOGY:**

Eph 5:23-32 — ekklesia = body of Christ; Christ = head; husband-wife metaphor. v25: "Christ loved the ekklesia and gave himself for it." v27: "glorious ekklesia, not having spot or wrinkle." v32: "great mystery: I speak concerning Christ and the ekklesia." co: BODY, MARRIAGE.

Col 1:18 — "he is the head of the body, the ekklesia: who is the beginning, the FIRSTBORN from the dead." co: FIRSTBORN, BODY.

1Tim 3:15 — "the ekklesia of the living God, the pillar and ground of the TRUTH." co: TRUTH.

Heb 12:23 — "the ekklesia of the FIRSTBORN, which are written in heaven." FIRSTBORN plural (G4416 = enrolled firstborn ones). co: FIRSTBORN, JERUSALEM(v22 "heavenly Jerusalem"), NAME(written/enrolled).

**REVELATION — 7 EKKLESIAI:**

Rev 1:4 — "to the seven ekklesiai which are in Asia." Seven specific assemblies. co: SEVEN.
Rev 2-3 — letters to seven ekklesiai. Each addressed individually with specific commendation/warning.
Rev 22:16 — "I Jesus have sent mine angel to testify unto you these things in the ekklesiai." co: NAME.

## Patterns

1. **SAME WORD, SINAI TO REVELATION.** H6951 qahal → LXX ekklesia → G1577 ekklesia. Acts 7:38 explicitly calls Israel at Sinai "the ekklesia." Heb 2:12 quotes Ps 22:22 qahal as ekklesia. The word spans the entire Scripture without the text marking a break or a new entity. Whether the NT ekklesia is a continuation or a new creation — the text uses the same word and does not announce a distinction.

2. **KJV TRANSLATION CREATES THE DIVIDE.** Same G1577: "church" (111×) and "assembly" (3×). The English word "church" creates the impression of a new institution. The Greek word is simply "called-out gathering." Strong's own definition covers Jewish synagogue AND Christian community under the same entry.

3. **"MY EKKLESIA" = "QAHAL OF THE LORD."** Mat 16:18 ("my ekklesia") mirrors Num 16:3 ("qahal of the LORD"). Possessive — the assembly belongs to God/Christ. Same ownership language.

4. **TRUMPET CONVOKES.** Num 10:7 — trumpet gathers the qahal. 1Th 4:16 — trumpet of God at the Lord's descent. The assembly is called together by trumpet in both testaments. co: TRUMPET.

5. **ONE LAW, STRANGER INCLUDED.** Num 15:15 — qahal and stranger under one ordinance. Same inclusion pattern as Exo 12:48 (Israel study). The assembly was never genetically exclusive.

6. **DISPUTE RESOLUTION FUNCTION.** Mat 18:17 uses ekklesia for community judicial process — same function as the qahal in OT (Num 35:12,24-25; Josh 20:6). Not worship-only — the assembly has legal/judicial authority.

7. **SYNAGOGE AND EKKLESIA OVERLAP.** James 2:2 uses synagoge for the believers' assembly. Strong's defines synagoge as including "Christian church." The sharp synagogue/church distinction is post-biblical — the text uses both words for the same reality.

8. **EDAH FROM WITNESS (H5707).** H5712 edah derives from H5707 ed = witness. The assembly is a WITNESS-body. The qahal is convoked; the edah testifies. co: TRUTH.

9. **DEUT 23 vs RUTH vs ISA 56.** The qahal has entry restrictions (Deut 23:1-3). Ruth (Moabitess) joins anyway and enters the messianic line. Isa 56:3-7 opens the qahal to strangers who keep covenant. The text presents restriction AND its overcoming without resolving the tension systematically. Same pattern as the Israel study — the boundary is real AND permeable.

10. **BODY + FIRSTBORN + HEAD.** Eph 5:23 (head of body = ekklesia). Col 1:18 (head, firstborn from dead). Heb 12:23 (ekklesia of the firstborn ones). The assembly is described as a body with Christ as head, populated by firstborn-enrolled ones. co: FIRSTBORN, BODY.

11. **SILENCE = CONTINUITY, NOT AMBIGUITY.** Ekklesia already had a defined meaning: qahal = Israel's assembly (116× OT, LXX rendering, Acts 7:38 confirmation). Using an established word without redefining it means the established meaning holds. A change in meaning would require the text to announce it — "ekklesia now means X instead of qahal" — and no such announcement exists. The burden of proof is on the claim of new meaning, not on continuity. Silence is not neutral here: it is evidence that the meaning was not altered.

## Framework Audit

Each major framework requires a specific textual claim. The audit checks whether the text makes that claim.

**1. Dispensationalism** — Requires: the ekklesia is a NEW entity distinct from Israel, beginning at Pentecost (Acts 2). Textual problem: Acts 7:38 calls Israel at Sinai "the ekklesia" using the same G1577. The word pre-exists Pentecost by ~1,500 years. No text announces a new ekklesia replacing or alongside the old. Mat 16:18 "I will build" could mean build up / strengthen (G3618 oikodomeo = build/edify, used for building up what exists, e.g. 1Cor 14:4), not necessarily create from nothing. The framework requires ekklesia to mean something different from qahal — the text does not make that move.

**2. Replacement Theology (Supersessionism)** — Requires: the church replaced Israel as God's covenant people. Textual problem: Rom 11:1-2 "God hath NOT cast away his people." The tree is Israel's (Rom 11:17-18 "thou bearest not the root, but the root thee"). Jer 31:36-37 ties Israel's permanence to the sun and moon. Gentiles are grafted INTO Israel's tree, not planted in a new one. The ekklesia is the qahal — you cannot replace yourself. The framework requires a transfer of identity; the text describes an expansion through joining (Exo 12:48 pattern).

**3. Dual Covenant Theology** — Requires: Jews and Gentiles have separate covenants / paths to God. Textual problem: Exo 12:49, Num 15:15 — "ONE law for homeborn and stranger." Eph 2:14 — "he is our peace, who hath made BOTH ONE, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition." One law, one tree, one body (Eph 2:16), one ekklesia. The framework requires two tracks; the text describes one assembly with one law.

**4. Catholic/Orthodox Ecclesiology** — Requires: the church is a visible institution with apostolic succession as its defining structure. Textual problem: ekklesia = "called-out assembly" — the word describes a gathered people, not an institutional hierarchy. The qahal had elders and judges, but was defined by being convoked by God (trumpet, Num 10:7), not by its organizational structure. Acts 14:23 ordains elders in each ekklesia, showing local structure — but the word itself describes the assembly, not the institution. The framework builds from the word more than the word contains.

**5. Protestant "Invisible Church"** — Requires: the true church is invisible, consisting of all believers regardless of visible assembly. Textual problem: ekklesia and qahal both describe CONCRETE ASSEMBLIES — visible, gathered, local (Acts 8:1 "ekklesia at Jerusalem"; Acts 14:23 "every ekklesia"). The word means a called-out gathering that actually gathers. Heb 10:25 "not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together" — the assembly is something you attend, not merely belong to invisibly. The framework spiritualizes a word that is concretely physical.

**6. Messianic Judaism / One Law** — Claims: the ekklesia is Israel's assembly; Gentiles join Israel; one law for all. Textual support: ekklesia = qahal (Acts 7:38). Gentiles graft in (Rom 11:17). One law for native and stranger (Num 15:15). The framework aligns with the word-data.

**SUMMARY:** The identity question (what is the ekklesia?) is answered by the text: it is the qahal. Frameworks 1-3 require the text to say something it doesn't say. Frameworks 4-5 build institutional/spiritual categories from a word that means "gathered assembly." Framework 6 aligns with the word-data.
