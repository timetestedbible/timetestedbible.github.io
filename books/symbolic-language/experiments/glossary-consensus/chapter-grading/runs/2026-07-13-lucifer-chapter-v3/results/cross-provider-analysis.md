## Material disagreements

### Shachar / blackness

Claude and Grok accept this as an explicitly labeled same-consonant wordplay.
GPT treats the displayed phrase “son and bearer of blackness” as a grammatical
translation and fails it because the verb alone does not establish a nominal
construction. The footnote and body therefore send different signals: the
footnote says wordplay, while the displayed paraphrase can be read as a direct
translation.

### Kisse / keseh

All three accept the consonantal identity and the independent full-moon
spellings. They disagree on comparison: Grok says the book's hearing is better
supported, GPT says the choice is underdetermined, and Claude says the ordinary
“my throne” reading remains better supported because the full-moon noun is not
attested with this suffix. The shared narrow gap is disclosure of the
productive-but-unattested suffixed form, not rejection of the wordplay.

### Adamma / silence

GPT requires a directly comparable Hithpael with causative cessation semantics
and an external target. Claude and Grok apply Rule 3's express allowance for a
productive grammatical rule and therefore admit the form, but neither finds
the silence hearing better supported than “make myself like.” Thus no grader
establishes the silence hearing as the probable primary vocalization. The real
dispute is whether productive formation establishes admissibility or whether a
root-and-stem duplicate is required.

### Integrated eclipse reading

Grok finds the book's joint model better because it unifies the appointed
assembly, lunar seat, spatial ascent, dark covering, Daniel 7:25, and the noon
darkness. GPT prefers the ordinary cosmic-usurpation model because it preserves
the immediate grammar with fewer disputed steps. Claude's middle judgment is
the most explicit: the integrated reading is admissible and gains genuine
unification weight, but its superiority is underdetermined until the chapter
compares the complete rival model and states reversal conditions.
