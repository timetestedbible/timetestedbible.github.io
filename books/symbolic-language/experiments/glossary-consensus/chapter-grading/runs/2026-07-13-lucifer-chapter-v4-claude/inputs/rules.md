# Human-approved rules — working document

This file records only rules expressly approved by the user. It is not yet a
complete ruleset and must not be submitted to providers until the user approves
the completed document.

## Rule 1 — Masoretic pointing is evidence, not a presumption

The Masoretic vowel points and accents count once as an important historical
reading-tradition witness. They receive no automatic evidentiary head start and
impose no additional burden merely because another admissible reading differs
from them.

If the comparison remains underdetermined, the Masoretic reading may remain the
practical citation default, but it must be labeled "traditional reading;
comparison underdetermined," not original, inspired, demonstrated, or
confirmed.

Dependent manuscripts, accents, translations, lexicons, and commentaries do
not multiply the Masoretic tradition into additional independent witnesses.

## Rule 2 — Status-based appeals do not prove a reading

A reading does not become correct because it is traditional, held by a
majority, or endorsed by an authority. The following are invalid as proofs of
the reading itself:

- appeal to tradition: "this is the received or historic reading";
- appeal to majority: "most surviving manuscripts, translations, scholars,
  institutions, or evaluators accept this reading";
- appeal to authority: "this rabbi, church, scholar, critical edition,
  lexicon, council, or AI endorses this reading."

Traditions, manuscripts, and authorities may preserve relevant data or make
arguments. Their data and arguments are evaluated on their merits; their
status, title, institutional acceptance, antiquity, or popularity supplies no
additional proof.

Raw numerical majority establishes prevalence in the surviving record, not
originality. Agreement among demonstrably independent witnesses may carry
evidentiary weight only because of their independence, provenance, and
discriminating information—not because they form a majority. Dependent copies
or repetitions of one tradition count as one lineage.

The same rule applies to the experiment itself: agreement among several AI
providers does not make a rule or reading true. Their responses identify
arguments, evidence, ambiguities, and possible errors for human review.

## Rule 3 — Symmetrical admission gate

Before comparison, both readings must independently:

1. account for the same agreed consonants and matres lectionis;
2. use an independently attested or regularly derived Hebrew or Aramaic lexeme
   and sense;
3. be morphologically possible;
4. use an attested grammatical construction or a productive grammatical rule
   applicable to the language, period, and genre;
5. produce a coherent reading in the immediate clause.

A proposal requiring different consonants is a separate textual-variant claim
and cannot be smuggled into a vocalization comparison.

A meaning derived only from the disputed locus cannot validate itself through
a lexicon, translation, or commentary that merely repeats that interpretation.
The independent attestation or derivation must be identified.

Both the Masoretic reading and the alternate reading must pass the same gate.
Passing establishes admissibility or possibility, not probability, originality,
inspiration, or victory.

## Rule 4 — Reciprocal consilience without circularity

An admissible reading may receive comparative support from an independently
established background hypothesis when their conjunction makes multiple
independent textual facts more expected than rival conjunctions do.

The analysis must proceed in stages:

1. state and freeze the evidence for the background hypothesis without using
   the target passage;
2. establish that each candidate reading independently passes Rule 3;
3. state what the background hypothesis predicts or makes more likely in the
   target passage;
4. compare the complete joint models, including the strongest combination of
   each candidate reading with each serious rival background hypothesis;
5. identify which textual facts each joint model explains, which facts it
   leaves unexplained, and which additional assumptions it requires;
6. map dependencies so that the same fact, tradition, or inference is not
   counted once for the background hypothesis and again for the target reading;
7. state what evidence would weaken or reverse the claimed reciprocal support.

After the independent background case is frozen, the target reading may
reciprocally strengthen that hypothesis only through incremental evidence not
already used to establish it.

Mere compatibility, interpretive usefulness, theological attractiveness, or
the ability to accommodate a fact after seeing it supplies little or no weight.
Greater weight requires comparative expectedness, prediction, explanatory
scope, or unification of otherwise independent details.

The alternative reading is not disfavored merely because it contributes less
to a preferred theory. It is comparatively weaker only when the rival joint
model explains fewer material facts, requires more unsupported assumptions, or
makes the observed evidence less expected while remaining subject to the same
linguistic and canonical tests.

Reciprocal confirmation is not circular when both propositions possess
independent support and their combination supplies new, nonduplicative
explanatory gain. If either proposition depends entirely on the other, or if
the apparent gain reuses the same evidence, the argument is circular and must
be rejected.
