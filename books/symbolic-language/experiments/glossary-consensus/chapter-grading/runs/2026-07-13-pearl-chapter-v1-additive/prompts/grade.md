## Required evaluation

1. Extract the chapter's material claims. Classify each before judging it.
   Consolidate them into 8–12 claims rather than splitting every sentence.
   Apply the symmetrical linguistic admission gate only to a proposed
   vocalization, word division, morphology, syntax, or lexical sense. Do not
   reject a symbolic derivation, astronomical fact, background hypothesis, or
   theological synthesis for failing a category-inapplicable vocalization gate.
2. For every linguistic alternate, first determine whether the received or base
   reading and the proposed additive reading are contradictory or compatible.
   Test both symmetrically. Passing means admissible, not probable or victorious.
   When they are compatible, do not make the received reading the rival. Record
   whether both stand, then separately judge whether active additive intent is
   supported, possible but unestablished, or affirmatively excluded.
3. For every comparative claim, identify the evidence actually supplied by the
   target, the independently frozen background packet, or evaluator knowledge.
   Mark evaluator knowledge as requiring source verification; do not pretend it
   was printed in the chapter.
4. Apply reciprocal consilience exactly as Rules 4 and 5 state. Map dependencies,
   identify duplicated evidence, and say what would reverse the result. For a
   noncontradictory additive claim, compare the strongest received-only joint
   model with the received-plus-additive joint model. Do not require the
   additive hearing to defeat the received hearing it preserves.
5. Reject appeals to tradition, majority, and authority as proof. A lexicon,
   grammar, manuscript, version, or scholar may contribute data or an argument,
   but its status cannot decide the result.
6. Distinguish four possibilities: a genuine missing-evidence gap in the
   chapter, an overclaim by the chapter, uncertainty caused by the evaluator's
   own knowledge limit, and a decision blocked because the rubric itself lacks
   a rule.
7. Do not assign arbitrary numerical weights or a numerical score. Use the
   categorical outcomes in the response schema.
8. Return exactly one rule-audit entry for each numbered rule in the frozen rubric.
   Recommend at most five changes, and only if they could change a material
   claim's outcome. Prefer a citation, footnote, quotation, or narrowed sentence
   over new expository sections. The objective is rigor without book bloat.
9. Treat model agreement as no proof at all. Give your own evidence-based grade.
   In the summary, keep `BOTH_ADMISSIBLE_ADDITIVE` claims separate from claims
   whose additive intention remains underdetermined; the second status does not
   erase the first.

Overall verdicts:

- `MEETS_STANDARD`: the core additive readings pass admission, their integration
  has positive noncircular support, and no unresolved material gap blocks the
  additive claim. They need not displace compatible received readings.
- `MEETS_WITH_LIMITED_GAPS`: the integrated core survives, but a small number of
  claims need outcome-relevant documentation or narrowing.
- `UNDERDETERMINED_BY_PACKET`: a material core reading cannot pass or fail the
  admission gate, or the packet supplies no positive evidence for the claimed
  integration. Do not use this merely because compatible readings both stand.
- `DOES_NOT_MEET_STANDARD`: a core additive claim fails admission, depends on
  circular support, contradicts the preserved base reading, or is excluded by
  affirmative linguistic or contextual evidence.
