CLASSIFY THE RELATIONSHIP

First reduce both readings to their minimum core identifications. Do not let
extra detail create an artificial disagreement.

- MATCH: the core identifications are equivalent. Differences are merely
  wording or immaterial detail.
- REFINED: the book preserves the consensus's general referent or category but
  materially changes its extension, boundary, conditions, or precision. A
  narrowing may reject some cases the broader consensus admits; a broadening
  may admit cases it excludes. This is real disagreement at the boundary, but
  not replacement of the central referent.
- DIVERGENT: the cores materially contradict. Both claims cannot be true of the
  same referent in the same context and sense; accepting the book requires
  rejecting or replacing the consensus identification. A source and its
  product remain different referents when the book explicitly denies their
  identity, even if they are causally related.
- NOVEL: no recognizable consensus or listed alternative covers the book's
  core identification. Novelty is absence from consensus, not contradiction.

Use `core_relation` to expose the relationship at the center and
`extension_relation` to expose disagreement over the cases covered. A REFINED
verdict must state what cases the book adds, excludes, or reclassifies. A
DIVERGENT verdict must identify a replaced referent or material contradiction,
not merely a narrower boundary.

Also judge whether the citations printed with the glossary entry alone provide
strong, partial, or insufficient support. Do not yet decide whether a divergent
full chapter is persuasive; that is a separate stage.
