## Works (good works, evil works) — FULL

Source run: `2026-07-12-works-symbol-links-final`

Model: `claude-fable-5`

Book entry: Deeds — not a synonym for obedience. A work becomes obedience when it executes a legitimate command; it becomes disobedience when the command or deed conflicts with God's command. Romans 4 uses the laborer's work offered for wages and debt; James 2 uses Abraham's work as faith made visible. Complete obedience still earns no wage: the servant has only done his duty.

Adjudicated core identification:
- A 'work' is a deed or action as such — not a synonym for obedience and not an inherently negative cipher for self-righteous effort. The deed becomes obedience when it executes a legitimate command and disobedience when the deed or its commanding authority conflicts with God's command; and no work, not even complete obedience, creates a wage-claim on God (Luke 17:10), which is the specific claim Paul excludes in Romans 4:4-5 while James 2 uses the same word for faith's visible enactment.

## Works of the law — FULL

Source run: `2026-07-12-works-symbol-links-final`

Model: `claude-fable-5`

Book entry: Paul's phrase for deeds considered in relation to law. It is not a synonym for obedience and does not identify one unchanging class of deeds. The surrounding passage may identify God's law, a circumcision-party application, or leave the particular acts unnamed. What remains consistent is the rejected claim made for those deeds: they cannot purchase justification, produce the Spirit, establish covenant standing, or place God in debt. The context identifies the work; the phrase denies it as the purchase price.

Adjudicated core identification:
- 'Works of the law' (erga nomou) is not a code-word for one fixed class of deeds (neither legalism-in-general nor Jewish boundary markers specifically); the surrounding context identifies which deeds are in view (God's law in Rom 3:20, 28 and Gal 3:10; the circumcision-party's demand in Gal 2:16; unnamed acts in Gal 3:2, 5), and what Paul uniformly denies is the claim made for those deeds — that they can purchase justification, produce the Spirit, establish covenant standing, or place God in debt. The phrase attacks the deed's asserted purchase-power, not the deeds or the law as such.
