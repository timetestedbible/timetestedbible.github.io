The week of the cross, in the temple, the chief priests and elders demanded of Jesus by what authority He did these things (Matthew 21:23).
Part of His answer was a vineyard parable Isaiah had already keyed:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 21:33]
____
Hear another parable: There was a certain householder, which planted a *vineyard*, and *hedged* it round about, and digged a *winepress* in it, and built a *tower*, and let it out to *husbandmen*, and went into a far country:
____

Every man in that courtyard knew the property.
Isaiah had sung it seven centuries earlier — a vineyard planted with the choicest vine, fenced, a tower built in the midst of it, a winepress made therein (Isaiah 5:1-2) — and Isaiah closed his song by handing over the deed:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 5:7]
____
For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts *is the house of Israel*, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for sym:sym-judgment[judgment], but behold oppression; for sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], but behold a cry.
____

The vineyard is decoded by the prophet who planted it.
The remaining symbols are decoded too — the wall, the tower, the winepress, the tenants, and the owner's strange, repeated verb — each one defined elsewhere in Scripture, waiting to be carried in.
Walk the parable with its own vocabulary and it stops being a fable about ingratitude; it becomes an indictment with every party named, a sentence with a date on it, and the blueprint of the building that rose from the rubble.

[discrete]
=== The Wall Called Salvation

Isaiah names the first symbol in the act of removing it.
The song of the vineyard ends with the owner's sentence: __“I will take away the *hedge* thereof, and it shall be eaten up; and break down the *wall* thereof, and it shall be *trodden down*”__ (Isaiah 5:5).
What a wall is, Scripture states outright — the same prophet, promising Zion restored:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 60:18]
____
Violence shall no more be heard in thy land, wasting nor destruction within thy borders; but thou shalt call thy sym:sym-wall[walls] *Salvation*, and thy gates Praise.
____

Isaiah says it twice — __“*salvation* will God appoint for walls and bulwarks”__ (Isaiah 26:1) — and Zechariah shows the substance behind the masonry: Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls, __“for I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a *wall of fire* round about”__ (Zechariah 2:4-5).
A sym:sym-wall[wall] is salvation — the covenant's enclosure: God Himself around His people, the boundary that divides inside from outside.
Read the vineyard's sentence with the definition installed and it turns exact: to break down the wall is to withdraw salvation, and what stood inside is trodden down.

[discrete]
=== The Tower in the Midst

Isaiah set the tower __“in the midst”__ of the vineyard (Isaiah 5:2), and the Hebrew Matthew keeps his word — _migdal_, the same word that stands over Scripture's two defining towers.
Solomon built the first into a proverb: __“The sym:sym-name[name] of the LORD is a *strong tower*: the righteous *runneth into it*, and is safe”__ (Proverbs 18:10) — the Name carrying the Head's Word, covenant law, and fruitful priesthood, sheltering the house that bears it.
Babel raised the second: __“let us build us a city and a tower… and let us *make us a name*”__ (Genesis 11:4) — the counterfeit, masonry stacked to house a self-made name.
A sym:sym-tower[tower] is what a name builds.

The question then answers itself: what stood in the midst of the house of Israel bearing a Name?
God's word to David about his son settles it: __“He shall build an house *for my name*”__ (2 Samuel 7:13).
The vineyard's tower is the sanctuary — the Name's stronghold planted among the vines.
Redraw the parable's real estate with the labels on: the house of Israel, enclosed in salvation, the temple standing in the middle — let out to tenant farmers, with the owner away and the fruit coming due.

[discrete]
=== The Winepress

Isaiah's inventory ends on the piece the estate was built to serve.
The owner fenced the vineyard, cleared its stones, planted the choicest vine, raised the tower — __“and also *hewed out* a sym:sym-winepress[winepress] therein: and he *looked* that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth *wild grapes*”__ (Isaiah 5:2, ASV).
A press is hewn into rock and cannot be moved; the whole property is built around the day its produce is rendered.
Isaiah repeats his verb five verses later — he *looked* that it should bring forth grapes, and he *looked* for sym:sym-judgment[judgment] and sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] (Isaiah 5:2, 7 — _qavah_ both times) — and the repetition names the crop: the grapes this press was hewed to catch are the sym:sym-fruit[fruit] by which a nation is judged, its works.

The crop takes one step more: when God comes looking for sym:sym-grapes[grapes], what He finds — or misses — is men.
__“I found Israel like *grapes* in the wilderness”__ (Hosea 9:10); and when Micah walks the stripped vineyard — __“there is no *cluster* to eat”__ — he names the loss in the next breath: __“the *good man* is perished out of the earth”__ — and the men who remain __“all lie in wait for *blood*”__ (Micah 7:1-2).
The blessing side holds the same identification: __“As the new wine is found in the *cluster*, and one saith, Destroy it not; for a *blessing* is in it: so will I do for my servants' sakes”__ (Isaiah 65:8) — the sym:sym-remnant[remnant] spared as good grapes left on the vine.
The works ripen into the people who bear them; the vineyard's yield is persons — which is why what runs from this press, when the vintage is wild, is *blood*.

Scripture keeps the press's ledger on both sides, and Solomon writes the credit column:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:9-10]
____
Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the *firstfruits* of all thine increase:
So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy *presses* shall *burst out* with new wine.
____

The bursting press is the blessing itself — Joel promises the same overflow, __“the floors shall be full of wheat, and the *fats* shall *overflow* with wine and oil”__ (Joel 2:24) — and the tithe is __“*reckoned*… as the fulness of the *winepress*”__ (Numbers 18:27), for the press is where a year's increase is counted and rendered.
Judah's own blessing wears it: __“he washed his *garments* in *wine*, and his clothes in the *blood of grapes*”__ (Genesis 49:11).
Treading even had its own cry, the _heydad_, the vintage-shout — grapes trodden and merriment made (Judges 9:27) — and when God sentences Moab, the sentence is a silenced press: __“the treaders shall tread out no wine in their presses; I have made their *vintage shouting* to cease”__ (Isaiah 16:10).

Joel wrote the overflow clause twice, and the second time the vat holds something else.

[quote.scripture, Joel 3:13]
____
Put ye in the sickle, for the sym:sym-harvest[harvest] is *ripe*: come, get you down; for the press is *full*, the fats *overflow*; for their *wickedness* is great.
____

Same vat, same rare verb — overflowing with wine and oil in one chapter, overflowing __“for their wickedness is great”__ in the next.
One press, two vintages: what the vineyard bears decides what runs out of it.
The vintage-shout changes sides the same way — __“he shall give a *shout*, as they that *tread the grapes*, against all the inhabitants of the earth”__ (Jeremiah 25:30) — and the same chapter had already poured the result: __“Take the *wine cup* of this fury at my hand, and cause all the nations… to drink it”__ (Jeremiah 25:15).
What the press treads out, the cup serves.

Isaiah's sentence had already routed the vineyard into its own press — the wall broken down, __“and it shall be *trodden down*”__ (Isaiah 5:5) — and Lamentations records the execution: __“the Lord hath trodden the sym:sym-virgin[virgin], the daughter of Judah, as in a *winepress*”__ (Lamentations 1:15).
The growers went in as the vintage.
Isaiah saw the Treader coming back from that work, and staged the scene as a watchman's challenge and its answer:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 63:2-3]
____
Wherefore art thou *red* in thine apparel, and thy garments like him that treadeth in the *winefat*?
I have trodden the sym:sym-winepress[winepress] *alone*; and of the people there was none with me: for I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury; and their *blood* shall be sprinkled upon my sym:sym-garment[garments], and I will stain all my raiment.
____

Genesis dressed Judah in garments washed in the blood of grapes; the Treader wears the same wardrobe with the other vintage in it, and the stain reads the verdict.
John saw the last run of the press, at full scale:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:18-20]
____
…Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the *vine of the earth*; for her grapes are fully *ripe*.
And the angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, and gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great sym:sym-winepress[winepress] of the *wrath* of God.
And the winepress was trodden *without the city*, and *blood* came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles…
____

Isaiah's vineyard was one nation; the last vintage gathers the vine of the whole earth, held until its grapes are __“fully ripe”__ — Joel's press, full because the wickedness is great.
The Rider of Revelation 19 arrives already dressed for it, __“clothed with a vesture *dipped in blood*,”__ and __“he *treadeth the winepress* of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God”__ (Revelation 19:13, 15) — Bozrah's Treader, riding out to finish the treading.
The press completes the estate the way it completes the song: the Owner will have His vineyard's yield either way — wine from good grapes, blood from wild.
Everything that happens next in the parable happens because the fruit fell due.

[discrete]
=== The Sent

The plot moves on one drumbeat verb.
__“He *sent* his servants… again, he *sent* other servants… last of all he *sent* unto them his son”__ (Matthew 21:34-37) — _apostellō_ every time, the sending that makes an apostle.
Scripture turns that verb into a place in Jerusalem, and interprets the name on the spot:

[quote.scripture, John 9:7]
____
Go, wash in the pool of *Siloam*, (which is by interpretation, *Sent*.) He went his way therefore, and washed, and came *seeing*.
____

Siloam — Isaiah's Shiloah — is the city's gentle spring-channel, and its name is the parable's verb.
Isaiah had already ruled on the cost of refusing it: __“Forasmuch as this people *refuseth* the waters of Shiloah that go softly… now therefore, behold, the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the *king of Assyria*”__ (Isaiah 8:6-7).
A sym:sym-flood[flood] is an army; refuse the soft Sent water, and the violent water comes.

Jesus filed the same case, with a tower in it.
Told of Galileans slaughtered by Pilate, He answered with Jerusalem's own dead:

[quote.scripture, Luke 13:4-5]
____
Or those eighteen, upon whom the *tower in Siloam* fell, and slew them, think ye that they were sinners above all men that dwelt in Jerusalem?
I tell you, Nay: but, except ye *repent*, ye shall all *likewise* perish.
____

The tower of *Sent* fell on men of Jerusalem, and He said __“except ye repent”__ twice (Luke 13:3, 5).
The same chapter holds the name's two poles side by side: eighteen men crushed under the tower, and a daughter of Abraham whom Satan had bound __“lo, these eighteen years,”__ loosed (Luke 13:11, 16).
Wash in Sent and come seeing; refuse Sent and die under its stones.

Last of all the owner sent his Son — and the tenants __“caught him, and cast him *out of the vineyard*, and slew him”__ (Matthew 21:39), which is where history put the cross: He __“suffered *without the gate*”__ (Hebrews 13:12), outside the enclosure His own parable had drawn.
One Greek word carries the location through both texts and a third — _exō_, outside: cast out of the vineyard, suffering without the gate, and the winepress __“trodden *without the city*”__ (Revelation 14:20) — the blood runs outside the wall, every time.
Within the generation the __“likewise”__ was paid in full: __“thine enemies shall cast a trench about thee… and shall not leave in thee *one stone upon another*; because thou knewest not the time of thy *visitation*”__ (Luke 19:43-44).
AD 70 executed Isaiah's sentence clause by clause — the wall broken down, the tower in the midst brought down on the city's men, and the vineyard's own participle pronounced over the ruin: __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles”__ (Luke 21:24) — Luke's verb is _pateō_, the treading of the winepress of wrath (Revelation 14:20; 19:15).

[discrete]
=== The Builders

The hearers pronounced the verdict themselves — __“He will miserably destroy those wicked men”__ (Matthew 21:41) — and Jesus turned from the story to a psalm, and the turn names the tenants' trade:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 21:42]
____
Did ye never read in the scriptures, The sym:sym-rock[stone] which the *builders* rejected, the same is become the *head of the corner*: this is the Lord's doing, and it is marvellous in our eyes?
____

The husbandmen of the story stand as the builders of the psalm — He swapped the words in one breath, and the men in front of Him accepted delivery: __“when the chief priests and Pharisees had heard his parables, they perceived that *he spake of them*”__ (Matthew 21:45).
The identification is ratified by the accused.

Builders of what?
Scripture keeps a file on the structures Israel's leaders raised while refusing the tried stone.
Solomon set the first beside the Name-tower itself, one verse later: __“The rich man's wealth is his strong city, and as an *high wall* in his *own conceit*”__ (Proverbs 18:11) — and the two verses share their Hebrew verb, for the righteous man is set safely on high (_sagab_) in the Name, while the rich man is high (_sagab_) only in his own imagination.
Ezekiel watched the trade at work: prophets __“saying, sym:sym-peace[Peace]; and there was no peace,”__ building a wall and daubing it __“with untempered morter”__ — and God announcing its inspection: __“there shall be an *overflowing shower*; and ye, O great hailstones, shall fall; and a *stormy wind* shall rend it”__ (Ezekiel 13:10-11).

Isaiah heard the builders state their charter outright, and recorded the Owner's answer — a foundation and a surveyor's line:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 28:15-17]
____
Because ye have said, We have made a *covenant with death*… for we have made *lies our refuge*, and under falsehood have we hid ourselves:
Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a sym:sym-rock[stone], a *tried* stone, a precious *corner* stone, a sure foundation…
sym:sym-judgment[Judgment] also will I lay to the line, and sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] to the plummet: and the hail shall sweep away the *refuge of lies*, and the waters shall *overflow* the hiding place.
____

Amos saw the instrument in the Owner's hand: __“the Lord stood upon a wall made by a plumbline, with a plumbline in his hand… Behold, I will set a *plumbline* in the midst of my people Israel”__ (Amos 7:7-8).
One Hebrew verb of overflowing — _shataph_ — carries the same sym:sym-flood[flood] through every counterfeit in the file: the river over its banks when Shiloah is refused (Isaiah 8:8), the waters over the refuge of lies (Isaiah 28:17), the shower that breaks the whitewashed wall (Ezekiel 13:13).
Every structure the builders raised falls to one water.
They rejected the tried stone because it did not fit the building they had underway — a high wall of conceit, whitewashed, chartered by a covenant with death — and the plumbline hung against their wall read the verdict the storm executed.

[discrete]
=== The Hedge, Removed Twice

Matthew's word for the vineyard's hedge is _phragmos_ — and the Hebrew Matthew fences the vineyard with _gadar_, the very root of the wall Isaiah's sentence breaks down.
Paul reaches for the identical Greek word to name one more wall:

[quote.scripture, Ephesians 2:14]
____
For he is our sym:sym-peace[peace], who hath made both one, and hath broken down the *middle wall of partition* between us;
____

The partition — the _phragmos_ — stood between the citizen and the stranger, __“aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from *the covenants of promise*”__ (Ephesians 2:12).
The vineyard's enclosure came down twice, and the two demolitions face opposite directions.
In judgment the wall was removed so that the vineyard could be trodden down (Isaiah 5:5); in mercy the partition was dissolved so that the sym:sym-stranger[stranger] could come in: __“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but *fellowcitizens* with the saints, and of the household of God”__ (Ephesians 2:19).
Salvation's boundary was not abolished; it was opened, and the opening runs one way — inward.

[discrete]
=== The New Building

The rejected stone went straight to work.
Peter, writing to the scattered, lays the courses:

[quote.scripture, 1 Peter 2:4-5]
____
To whom coming, as unto a *living* sym:sym-rock[stone], disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious,
Ye also, as *lively stones*, are built up a *spiritual house*, an holy priesthood…
____

Two verses later he sets this chapter's two quarry-texts side by side: __“Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious”__ (1 Peter 2:6, from Isaiah 28:16), and __“the stone which the builders disallowed, the same is made the *head of the corner*”__ (1 Peter 2:7, from Psalm 118:22).
Paul draws the same elevation in the very paragraph where the partition falls: __“built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief *corner* stone; in whom all the building fitly framed together groweth unto an holy *temple*”__ (Ephesians 2:20-21).
The tower in the midst of the vineyard was a house for the Name built of quarried rock; the house now rising is built of people — every stone alive, set on the stone the builders threw away.

The blueprint's last page still shows a wall.
John measures the city that comes down from God:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:12, 14]
____
And had a sym:sym-wall[wall] great and high, and had twelve gates… and *names* written thereon, which are the names of the *twelve tribes* of the children of Israel…
And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the *twelve apostles* of the Lamb.
____

The wall, like the tower, is built of names — the tribes on the gates, the apostles, the *sent ones*, in the foundations — and Isaiah's definition was written for this city all along: walls called Salvation, gates called Praise (Isaiah 60:18).
One symbol of the parable is still in motion.
__“Whosoever shall fall on this stone shall be broken: but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder”__ (Matthew 21:44) — Daniel watched that sym:sym-stone[stone] strike, grind the empires to sym:sym-chaff[chaff], and then grow, until it __“became a great sym:sym-mountain[mountain], and filled the whole earth”__ (Daniel 2:35): the kingdom.
The kingdom is exactly what the Owner deeded away in the sentence: __“The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and given to a *nation* bringing forth the sym:sym-fruit[fruits] thereof”__ (Matthew 21:43).
Which nation bears the fruit, and by what name Scripture calls it, link:/books/symbolic-language/jacob-israel-and-ephraim/[Jacob, Israel, and Ephraim] will prove.
