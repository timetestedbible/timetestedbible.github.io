Two things almost everyone believes about __“the name”__ turn out to be wrong, and they are the two doors most people walk in by.
The first: that to __“take the LORD’s name in vain”__ is to curse — __“oh my God”__ as an idle breath — or to mangle the sacred syllables, YHWH, Yahweh, Yahuah.
The second is quieter.
We end our prayers __“in Jesus’ name,”__ as though the phrase were a key that turns the lock — and when nothing turns, we quietly wonder what went wrong.

Both mistakes treat the name as a _sound_: a word to pronounce correctly, or a formula to attach.
But Jesus made a promise about His name that no magic word could keep:

[quote.scripture, John 14:13]
____
And *whatsoever ye shall ask in my name*, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.
____

_Whatsoever_ — and yet how many prayers, sealed with the name, go unanswered?
Either the promise fails, or __“in my name”__ means something deeper than a phrase we append.
To find it we must stop asking how the name _sounds_ and ask what it _is_ — and Scripture builds the answer a piece at a time.

[discrete]
=== In My Name

Begin with that promise.
To ask __“in my name”__ is not to invoke a syllable but to ask _as one who belongs to Him_ — within His will, on His terms, inside the relationship.
Paul says the same of the whole of life: __“whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus”__ (Colossians 3:17).
You cannot do _everything_ by pronouncing a word over it; you can only do everything _within a relationship_.
To act __“in the name”__ is to act inside the bond.

The bond is something a person _carries_.
At Sinai, God named the guide who went before His people — one with the name itself _in him_:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 23:20-21]
____
Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to *keep thee in the* sym:sym-way[way], and to bring thee into *the place which I have prepared*… Beware of him, and obey his sym:sym-voice[voice], provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for *my name is in him*.
____

This is no created messenger.
He bears God’s own _name_ — His presence and authority — and Scripture, looking back, tells us plainly who he was: __“the angel of his presence saved them… and he bare them, and carried them all the days of old”__ (Isaiah 63:9).
The sym:sym-rock[Rock] that followed Israel through the wilderness, Paul says, __“was Christ”__ (1 Corinthians 10:4).
So Christ — the One in whom the name dwelt — led them by the pillar of cloud and fire; and the same One came in the flesh.
(One clause does reach ahead: this Angel __“will not pardon your transgressions”__ — and transgress they did, at the very foot of the mountain; the name-bearer who would at last _pardon_ comes only when He takes flesh.)

When Jesus says __“I have declared unto them thy name”__ (John 17:26), He is not reciting a sound He learned; He is that name-bearer, now walking among His people — the name _borne_ in a person, made visible in a faithful life, and kept the way it always was: He keeps them __“in the sym:sym-way[way]”__ (covenant-conduct); they must __“obey his voice.”__

The promise looked beyond that generation’s journey.
The Angel was sent __“to bring thee into the place which I have prepared”__ — the very words the name-bearer would take up as His own: __“I go to prepare a place for you… I will come again, and receive you unto myself”__ (John 14:2-3).
The One in whom the name dwells led Israel through the wilderness, came in the flesh, and will come again to gather His people into the place He has prepared — one name-bearer, from the wilderness to the end.

[discrete]
=== Lord, Lord

Which sets up the sharpest test Jesus ever gave.
Picture the people most certain of their standing:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:22-23]
____
Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy *name*? and in thy *name* have cast out devils? and in thy *name* done many *powers*?footnote:dynameis[The King James prints “wonderful works”; the Greek is _dynameis_ — powers, not _erga_, works.]
And then will I profess unto them, *I never* sym:sym-knowing[knew] *you*: depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*.
____

Every sentence has the name in it — prophesying, casting out devils, doing powers __“in thy name.”__
No one ever spoke it more.
He turns them away as __“workers of *lawlessness*”__ — _anomia_.
They had the sound and not the substance.

The name cannot be a word on the lips: a man may have it there constantly and remain a stranger to the One who bears it.
What, then, _is_ it?

[discrete]
=== The Name Is the Covenant

Here at last Scripture says it outright.
David, settled in his palace, wants to build a house for the _ark of the covenant_:

[quote.scripture, 2 Samuel 7:2]
____
…I dwell in an house of cedar, but the *ark of God* dwelleth within curtains.
____

God answers, and quietly trades one word for another:

[quote.scripture, 2 Samuel 7:13]
____
He shall build an house for my *name*, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.
____

David said _the ark of the covenant_; God said _my name_.
They are the same thing.
The name _is_ the **covenant**footnote:covenant[_Covenant_ — the renewed covenant of the kingdom; see the link:/books/symbolic-language/gospel/[Gospel] chapter.] — the binding relationship between God and His people.

Notice that God, answering, plays on David’s own word — for a __“house”__ in Scripture is a building _and_ a family line, and a family line is exactly what carries a name.
David offered to build God a house of stone; God replies, __“the LORD telleth thee that *he will make thee an house*”__ (2 Samuel 7:11) — not cedar but sons: the house _of David_.

The promise then stands established three ways at once, and every level holds.
In the flesh: the seed and throne endure — __“the Lord God shall give unto him the *throne of his father David*… and of his kingdom there shall be *no end*”__ (Luke 1:32-33).
In stone: Solomon raises the temple __“for my name.”__
And in covenant: the Son of David builds the house the first two only sketched — __“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up… he spake of the *temple of his body*”__ (John 2:19-21); __“ye also, as lively sym:sym-stone[stones], are *built up a spiritual house*”__ (1 Peter 2:5); __“Christ as a son over his own house; *whose house are we*”__ (Hebrews 3:6).

Zechariah compresses the whole promise into one oracle:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 6:12-13]
____
…Behold the man whose *name* is The BRANCH; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall *build the temple* of the LORD:
Even he shall build the temple of the LORD; and he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and *rule upon his throne*; and he shall be a priest upon his throne: and the counsel of peace shall be between them both.
____

Name, house, and throne in one Man.
The wordplay is the doctrine: a house exists to carry a name, and God’s answer to David binds His name and David’s house into a single building.

Now everything before falls into place: this is why asking __“in his name”__ is asking within the covenant; why the name is _borne_ by the obedient; why the man who only _speaks_ it is unknown.
And it is why __“there is none other name under heaven… whereby we must be saved”__ (Acts 4:12): no other name, because no other _covenant_.
To __“sym:sym-belief[believe] on his name”__ (John 1:12) is not to credit a title but to enter that covenant in trusting obedience.

It is also why the name can be a refuge:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 18:10]
____
The name of the LORD is a *strong tower*: the righteous *runneth into it*, and is safe.
____

No one takes shelter inside a sound.
A covenant can be run into — walls, a roof, and a door that shuts behind the one who keeps it.

It even untangles an old puzzle.
God tells Moses He was __“not known”__ to the patriarchs __“by my name JEHOVAH”__ (Exodus 6:3) — though Abraham plainly _heard_ the word, __“I am the LORD that brought thee out of Ur”__ (Genesis 15:7).
He knew the _sound_; he had not yet entered the _covenant_ that name would come to carry.

Which is exactly why the argument over the right syllables, earnest as it is, looks straight past the thing itself.
The name is not a sound to recover.
It is a covenant to keep.

[discrete]
=== Taking It in Vain

Now — and only now — the commandment opens:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 20:7]
____
Thou shalt not take the *name* of the LORD thy God *in vain*; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his *name* in vain.
____

__“Vain”__ is _shav_ — emptiness, _for nothing_.
To take the name in vain is to take the _covenant_ for nothing: to wear it, claim it, swear by it, while living as though it asks nothing of you.
It was never first a sin of the mouth; it is a sin of the life.
The cursing man breaks it, yes — but so does the one who cries __“Lord, Lord”__ and works lawlessness, and the one who seals empty prayers with a name he will not keep.

Solomon files that exact case:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 28:9]
____
He that turneth away his ear from *hearing the law*, even his *prayer* shall be *abomination*.
____

The mouth full of the name, the ear turned from the covenant that fills it: such prayer does not merely go unanswered, it offends.
There is the answer to the puzzle this chapter opened with: the prayer sealed __“in the name”__ fails because the ear has left the law.
Most who take His name in vain do it inside the church.

The prophets prosecute the same sin under another verb.
__“When they entered unto the heathen… they *profaned my holy name*, when they said to them, These are *the people of the LORD*, and are gone forth out of his land”__ (Ezekiel 36:20).
Israel did not profane the name by mispronouncing it; they profaned it by wearing it faithlessly in front of the nations — covenant-breakers introduced everywhere as the covenant people.
And God’s remedy is not a correction of speech: __“I will *sanctify my great name*, which was profaned among the heathen… when I shall be *sanctified in you* before their eyes”__ (Ezekiel 36:23) — He hallows His own name by making the people who wear it holy.

[discrete]
=== The Lord’s Prayer

The most familiar prayer in the world asks for exactly this — and once the name is understood, the whole prayer opens, line by line.
Its grammar carries more weight than the recitation lets on.
__“*Hallowed be thy name*”__ (Matthew 6:9) is not a description and not a compliment; the verb is an imperative: _let thy name be made holy_ — the one praying is demanding the very thing Ezekiel promised, that the name be hallowed in its people before the eyes of the world.

Hear the petitions that march out behind it, each in the same commanding mood: __“Thy *kingdom come*.
Thy *will be done* in earth, as it is in heaven”__ (Matthew 6:10).
Name hallowed, kingdom come, will _done_ — the covenant kept holy in a people, the King’s reign arriving, and His will obeyed on earth the way heaven already obeys it.
The second petition tells us how the first is granted: a name is hallowed when the will of its Bearer is done — beginning with the one praying.

The covenant runs through every petition that follows; walk them with the Bible’s symbolic language in hand.
__“Give us this day our daily sym:sym-bread[bread]”__ (Matthew 6:11) — and bread is a defined symbol: the *word* from the mouth of the LORD (Deuteronomy 8:3).
The petition even has a history, for the first daily bread was the manna — rained from heaven one day’s portion at a time, and God states His purpose in the giving: __“that I may *prove* them, whether they will *walk in my law*, or no”__ (Exodus 16:4).

Luke’s wording keeps the manna’s rhythm exactly — __“Give us *day by day* our daily sym:sym-bread[bread]”__ (Luke 11:3) — and when Luke’s Jesus finishes the prayer, He walks straight into a parable of a man begging *three loaves at midnight* and a Father who gives to them that ask (Luke 11:5-13): the petition, dramatized.
To ask for daily bread is to ask for the day’s portion of the word — and to take up the day’s test of walking in it.

__“And forgive us our *debts*, as *we* forgive our debtors”__ (Matthew 6:12).
The release of debts is the law’s own mercy — __“every creditor that lendeth ought unto his neighbour shall *release* it… because it is called *the LORD’S release*”__ (Deuteronomy 15:2) — and the petition asks God to run His release toward us as we run it toward our brothers.
Luke’s version glosses the symbol as it goes: __“And forgive us our sym:sym-sin[sins]; for we also forgive every one that is *indebted* to us”__ (Luke 11:4) — sins in the first clause, debtors in the second: the two gospels set the columns side by side, and the debt is sin.

It is the one line of the prayer Jesus stops to underline the moment He finishes teaching it — __“For *if ye forgive* men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: but *if ye forgive not*…”__ (Matthew 6:14-15) — and He later stages it as a full drama: the servant forgiven ten thousand talents who takes his brother by the throat, and is delivered back to the tormentors (Matthew 18:32-35).
The prayer must be practiced by the man praying it, or it testifies against him.

The last petition asks the covenant God for the covenant deliverance: __“lead us not into temptation, but *deliver us* from evil”__ (Matthew 6:13).
Both halves repay testing.

__“Temptation”__ translates a Greek word that means the _proving_, and James rules out the modern reading in one stroke: __“God cannot be tempted with evil, *neither tempteth he any man*”__ (James 1:13) — yet the same James calls the man blessed who _endures_ it: __“when he is *tried*, he shall receive the *crown of life*”__ (James 1:12).

Beneath the Greek stands a Hebrew word the King James renders both ways, and the wobble is our familiar signpost: __“God did *tempt* Abraham”__ (Genesis 22:1) — the first __“tempt”__ in the Bible, in the very scene that gives us the first __“worship”__ (Genesis 22:5, a discovery the link:/books/symbolic-language/worship/[Worship] chapter will unfold) — is the same word as __“that I may *prove* them”__ over the manna (Exodus 16:4), and __“to *prove* thee… whether thou wouldest *keep his commandments*, or no”__ (Deuteronomy 8:2); David even prays _for_ it: __“*prove* me”__ (Psalm 26:2).
The petition, then, does not imagine God dangling sin; it pleads about the furnace-hour of proving — __“the *hour of temptation*, which shall come upon all the world, to *try* them”__ (Revelation 3:10) — the same plea Jesus pressed on His sleeping disciples in Gethsemane: __“Watch and pray, that ye *enter not into temptation*”__ (Matthew 26:41).

The plea __“deliver us from evil”__ is personal in the Greek — _deliver us from the evil one_: the same __“wicked one”__ who snatches the sown word and fathers the sym:sym-tares[tares] (Matthew 13:19, 38).
Watch the Name-bearer pray this very petition over a man:

[quote.scripture, Luke 22:31-32]
____
…Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as sym:sym-wheat[wheat]:
But *I have prayed for thee*, that thy sym:sym-faith[faith] fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.
____

Trial itself is not prayed away — the manna was given to prove — the plea is to be brought _through_ the proving and out of the destroyer’s hand, with the promise standing behind it: God __“will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a *way to escape*”__ (1 Corinthians 10:13).

Read whole, the prayer Jesus taught is the covenant renewed in miniature: the name hallowed, the kingdom summoned, the will obeyed, the word received daily, the release practiced, the deliverance promised.
Every line of it is prayed __“in the name,”__ because every line of it lives inside the covenant — and its salvation is promised to those who practice what they pray.

[discrete]
=== Keeping the Name

How, then, is the name kept full rather than emptied?
By its terms — the law.
Moses binds the two in a single line:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 28:58]
____
If thou wilt not observe to do *all the words of this law* that are written in this book, that thou mayest sym:sym-fear[fear] *this glorious and fearful name*, THE LORD THY GOD…
____

Keep the law and you honor the name; despise the law and you empty it.
So at the very end of the story, the people who belong to God are marked by that exact pairing — __“they that keep the commandments of God, and the sym:sym-faith[faith] of Jesus”__ (Revelation 14:12) — with __“his Father’s name written in their foreheads”__ (Revelation 14:1): the covenant owned and worn where all can see — the sym:sym-seal[seal] set on God’s servants.
Scripture binds the name and the law together, and will not let them be pried apart.

[discrete]
=== The Counterfeit Name

In the Bible’s symbolic language, many symbols come paired with an opposite — and describing the opposite often sheds light on the meaning of its counterpart.
The name has such an opposite, and it is the oldest counterfeit on record.

On the plain of Shinar the builders said, __“let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and *let us make us a name*”__ (Genesis 11:4) — sym:sym-babel[Babel], the kingdom whose founding act is a self-made name: an identity manufactured from below, a covenant with no one above.
God’s answer stands in the very next chapter, spoken to one man called out of that world: __“I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and *make thy name great*”__ (Genesis 12:2).
A made name or a given one — the whole war of the Bible in two clauses.

The war ends on men’s foreheads.
The beast __“causeth all… to receive a mark in their right hand, or in their *foreheads*… the *name of the* sym:sym-beast[beast]”__ (Revelation 13:16-17); one chapter later, the Lamb’s company stands on mount Sion __“having his *Father’s name* written in their foreheads”__ (Revelation 14:1).
There is no unmarked forehead in that scene.
Everyone wears a name — the one men make for themselves, or the one God gives — and the commandment asks which one you will refuse to carry for nothing.

[discrete]
=== Called by His Name

One question remains: how does a person come to bear a name that is not his own?
Scripture answers with the oldest institution it has — a bride takes her husband’s — and Isaiah shows the formula in a single desperate verse:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 4:1]
____
And in that day seven women shall take hold of one man, saying, We will eat our own sym:sym-bread[bread], and wear our own sym:sym-garment[apparel]: only *let us be called by thy name*, to take away our *reproach*.
____

To be __“called by thy name”__ is to be sym:sym-marriage[married] — that is the whole request — and the women’s bargain proves it: the law obliges a husband to supply his wife’s __“food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage”__ (Exodus 21:10), and these seven offer to waive everything but the name itself.
Read in the Bible’s symbolic language, the bargain turns chilling.

Seven women — seven, the number Scripture gives the assemblies of God’s people (Revelation 1:20) — asking to wear the Bridegroom’s name while eating _their own_ bread and wearing _their own_ apparel: their own word in place of His, their own righteousness in place of His.
It is the __“Lord, Lord”__ of Matthew 7 spoken as a marriage proposal — the name, minus the covenant that fills it.
And the true Husband gives His sym:sym-widow[widowed] people exactly link:/books/symbolic-language/orphans-widows-and-the-fatherless/[the two things these women decline] — __“food and raiment”__ (Deuteronomy 10:18): the sym:sym-bread[bread] of His word and the sym:sym-garment[garment] of His righteousness.footnote:fn08foodraiment[The food and the raiment are opened fully in the link:/books/symbolic-language/orphans-widows-and-the-fatherless/[Orphans, Widows, and the Fatherless] chapter.]

The true marriage is enacted through the same covenant mechanics.
When Ruth asks Boaz for marriage, she asks him to spread the corner of his garment over her (Ruth 3:9) — and __“skirt”__ there is the Hebrew _kanaph_, the sym:sym-wings[wing]: the covenant edge.

For Ruth is everything the seven women are not, and her story keeps the ledger.
She brings no bread of her own: she eats at Boaz’s table until she __“was sufficed, and *left*”__ (Ruth 2:14), gleans his field by the law of the corner — with __“handfuls *of purpose*”__ dropped in her path (Ruth 2:16) — and is sent home under six measures of barley with the charge, __“Go not *empty*”__ (Ruth 3:17).
And the only raiment the story provides her is the one she asks for.
Boaz had blessed her, __“a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose sym:sym-wings[wings] thou art come to trust”__ (Ruth 2:12) — and on the threshing floor she asks him to make good his own blessing in person: the widow’s garment laid aside (Ruth 3:3), and one covering requested — his.

God describes His own wedding to Israel with the same gesture:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 16:8]
____
Now when I passed by thee, and looked upon thee, behold, thy time was the time of love; and I *spread my skirt* over thee, and covered thy sym:sym-naked[nakedness]: yea, I sware unto thee, and *entered into a covenant* with thee, saith the Lord GOD, and *thou becamest mine*.
____

There the raiment clause is written out in full, in the verses that follow the spread skirt: __“Then washed I thee… I anointed thee… I *clothed thee* also with broidered work… and I covered thee with silk… thy *raiment* was of fine linen… thou didst *eat* fine flour, and honey, and oil”__ (Ezekiel 16:9-13) — food and raiment, both from the Husband, exactly the provision the seven women waived.

To take the husband’s name is to come under the husband’s wing — and the wing’s corners carry the sym:sym-tzitzit[tassels], bound there so that the wearer remembers all the commandments of the LORD and does them (Numbers 15:38-39).
The covering a bride enters when she takes the name is stitched at every corner with the reminder of the law of the One who spreads it — and a bleeding woman once link:/books/symbolic-language/wings/[took hold of that corner] in sym:sym-faith[faith], and the name did what no sound can (Matthew 9:20-21).footnote:fn08wings[The wing, the fringe bound at its corner, and the healing found there are derived in the link:/books/symbolic-language/wings/[Wings] chapter.]
The name and the commandments arrive as one garment — which is why the name is kept by keeping the law (Deuteronomy 28:58), and emptied any other way.

This grasp is the gesture the nations finally learn.
Isaiah’s seven women __“take hold”__ of one man; Zechariah sees the same verb close on the right thing:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 8:23]
____
…ten men shall *take hold* out of all languages of the nations, even shall take hold of the *skirt* of him that is a Jew, saying, *We will go with you*: for we have heard that *God is with you*.
____

The hand closes on the _kanaph_ — the wing-corner where the commandments hang — and the words are Ruth’s own, the Moabite bride who said __“whither thou goest, I will go”__ (Ruth 1:16).

Ruth’s wedding under the wing leads directly into this chapter’s closing point.
The law states the purpose of a kinsman’s marriage in name-language: the son is raised __“that his *name* be not put out of Israel,”__ and the man who refuses is shamed as one who __“will not *build up his brother’s house*”__ (Deuteronomy 25:6, 9) — in the law’s own grammar, building a house and raising a name are one act.
So Boaz declares he has taken Ruth __“to *raise up the name of the dead* upon his inheritance”__ (Ruth 4:10); the elders bless her as one come to __“*build the house*”__ (Ruth 4:11); and the house that wedding built was the house of *David* (Ruth 4:17) — the very house God would answer with a house for His own name.

A bride under the wing, a name raised, a house built — and in the fulness of time, a Son of that house who is Himself the temple.
The symbols do not merely repeat; they compound.

The symbol gathers up the ones before it.
The sym:sym-gospel[gospel] is the covenant _announced_; the sym:sym-way[way], the sym:sym-truth[truth], and the sym:sym-life[life] are the covenant _embodied_; the name is the covenant _owned_ — taken upon yourself as a sym:sym-marriage[bride] takes her husband’s, to keep or to empty.
Most who cry __“Lord, Lord”__ wear it for nothing.
To bear it in truth is to keep the covenant of the One in whom the name dwells — and so to be sym:sym-knowing[known] by Him in that day.
And the name owned is also written — impressed on its bearers like a signet in wax.
Where God sets it, what it says, and what counterfeit stamps the same two places — that is the seal, and it is the link:/books/symbolic-language/the-seal/[Seal] chapter.
