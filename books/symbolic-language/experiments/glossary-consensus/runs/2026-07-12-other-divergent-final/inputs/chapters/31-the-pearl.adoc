The kingdom of heaven is likened to a *merchant man* seeking *goodly pearls*, who finds *one pearl of great price*, sells all that he has, and buys it.
Jesus left this parable unkeyed — no interpretation is recorded — so the derivation stands where the link:/books/symbolic-language/the-parables-of-the-kingdom/[Parables of the Kingdom] chapter left it: on *our* method alone, tested like everything else and held fast if it proves good.

The method begins with the parable's own key words: a *merchant*; *goodly* — simply *good*; and *one* sym:sym-pearl[pearl] — for *one* is no mere count.
Of all the goodly pearls the merchant finds, he sells everything for exactly one, and one signals what it signals everywhere in Scripture: *complete*, *whole*, undivided — a thing nothing can be added to.
Search those words through Scripture, and they gather at one address.

[discrete]
=== Three Words, Three Places

The *pearls* come first, and the King James buried them.
The link:/books/symbolic-language/the-parables-of-the-kingdom/[Parables of the Kingdom] chapter already dug them out from under the King James’ __“rubies”__: the Hebrew _peninim_ is, by Strong's own definition, __“probably *a pearl* (as round).”__
Recovered, the pearls keep exactly the parable's company: sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] is priced above *pearls* (Proverbs 3:15; 8:11; Job 28:18) — and the seeker who searches for her __“as for *hid treasures*”__ (Proverbs 2:4) is himself the merchant of the parable.
And the virtuous woman — the one sym:sym-marriage[bride] — is __“far above *pearls*”__ (Proverbs 31:10), drawn up out of the sym:sym-sea[sea] of the nations.

Now the *merchant* and his *good*.
Solomon prices wisdom in the merchant's own terms — __“For the *merchandise* of it is better than the merchandise of silver”__ (Proverbs 3:14) — and Scripture stamps that merchandise *good* in exactly two more places in all its pages.
The first is the woman of this same book — __“her *merchandise* is *good*”__ (Proverbs 31:18) — and in the Hebrew this is no echo but a *quotation*: letter for letter the clause of 3:14, כִּי־טוֹב סַחְרָהּ, __“for her merchandise is good.”__
The second is the parable itself: the kingdom's __“*merchant* man, seeking *goodly* pearls”__ (Matthew 13:45).
Three texts, one thread — sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom], the woman, the sym:sym-pearl[pearl]: the *good merchandise*.

Proverbs interchanges the subject in its own grammar: the find-verb of 3:13 returns exactly twice in the book — __“whoso findeth *me*”__ (8:35, Wisdom speaking) and __“whoso findeth *a wife*”__ (18:22).
Wisdom and the bride hold one slot in Solomon's sentences, and the parable's pearl prices them both.
The merchant's vocabulary has led to two poems — wisdom's and the bride's — and this book reads poems like these on the bare letters.

=== The Poems in Plain Language

The link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter read the lights: the sym:sym-sun[sun] the Father and the husband; the sym:sym-moon[moon] the sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness] — the Son toward His Father, the sym:sym-marriage[bride] toward her Bridegroom; the sym:sym-stars[stars] the righteous, gathered into the twelve; the sym:sym-elders[elders] seated among the host; and the gates of the sky's tabernacle, through which the lights come and go.
Lay the poems against that sky, line by line, and every line finds its station.

__“She riseth also while it is yet night… her lamp goeth not out in the night”__ (31:15, 18).
Of all the lights a reader has ever seen, exactly one rises as the dark falls and burns until morning: the *full moon*, which climbs the east gate at sunset and holds the sky the whole night through.
And what she serves her household is the calendar's own food — __“the covenant's meat… the appointed portion”__: the portion-word is _choq_, the __“ordinances of the sym:sym-moon[moon] and of the sym:sym-stars[stars]”__ by sym:sym-night[night] (Jeremiah 31:35).

__“She is more bright than pearls”__ (3:15).
__“Bright”__ is the King James’ own word for the moon — __“the *moon* walking in *brightness*”__ (Job 31:26) — the comparison priced in the moon-verse's own vocabulary.

__“Length and appropriateness of days is in her right hand; and in her left hand riches and shining”__ (3:16).
The month's own length — twenty-nine days or thirty — is declared at her fulness, and declared *fitting*: the appointed day, the day of meeting, sym:sym-meat[meat] given __“in *due season*”__ (Matthew 24:45).
And Strong's reads the right-hand word as __“locally, the south,”__ and the left-hand word as __“properly dark (as enveloped)”__ — the north: days in her bright hand, shining even in her dark one.

__“She worketh willingly with her palms… her palms hold fast the circuit… She spreadeth out her palm to the poor”__ (31:13, 19, 20).
The poem says *palm* — _kaph_ — where it could have said hand, and keeps the two words distinct inside single verses.
And _kaph_ is a shape before it is a limb: Strong's spends the word on every *curved, hollow container* — the palm, the sole, the pans of the tabernacle, the bowl of a spoon — a rounded vessel made to *hold* and to *cover*.
It is the palm God laid over Moses while the glory passed by (Exodus 33:22); and here it is the curved cup that receives the Bridegroom's light and *holds fast the circuit*, carrying it round the year — then opens toward the sym:sym-poor[poor], the King's own signature: __“He shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the *poor* also”__ (Psalm 72:12), in the very psalm where they fear Him __“with the sun and *before the face of* the moon”__ through all revolutions of time (72:5, as the letters run).

__“She considereth a field, and taketh it: with the fruit of her palms she planteth a vineyard. She girdeth her loins with strength”__ (31:16-17).
This is the Bridegroom's own record in parable: He *planted the vineyard* (Matthew 21:33) — and Isaiah names it, __“the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of *Israel*”__ (Isaiah 5:7) — and He *bought the field* for the treasure hidden in it, and __“the field is the *world*”__ (Matthew 13:44, 38).
And she *strengthens* what she plants, where the counterfeit of the last chapter's plan __“didst *weaken* the nations”__ (Isaiah 14:12) and wears out the saints (Daniel 7:25): one plants and strengthens, the other wears down.

__“She tasteth that it is good, her merchandise”__ (31:18).
The words first spoken over light itself — __“and God saw the light, that it was *good*”__ (Genesis 1:4) — tasted now over the *good merchandise* of the three texts.

__“She is not worried by the whiteness for her household: for all her household are clothed in double… her clothing is white linen and purple”__ (31:21-22).
Her household are the sym:sym-stars[stars], and they want for no light: they are clothed *in double* — the reading the oldest translations kept — and her own weave is _shesh_, *white linen* by definition.

__“Her husband is known in the gates, when he sitteth among the elders of the land”__ (31:23) — __“and let her own works make her shine in the gates”__ (31:31).
Her husband, the sun of this portrait, is *known in the gates* — the doors of his own circuit through the twelve — and he sits among the sym:sym-elders[*elders*], the bench the Sun, Moon, and Stars chapter seated among the host of heaven.
Her works shine at the same doors: the gates her fulness passes through, each one, the city says, a single pearl.

__“Her sons arise, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he maketh her shine”__ (31:28).
The verb is _halal_, whose first sense is *to shine*: he makes her shine, as the sun makes the moon.

__“Strength and splendour are her clothing; and she laugheth at the last day”__ (31:25).
Beneath the laugh, in the same letters, stands the _shachaq_ — the sky where the sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness] is established (Psalm 89:37) — and she can laugh at the day because she knows its date: sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] laughs at calamity (Proverbs 1:26) for the reason the psalm gives, __“he seeth that his *day is coming*”__ (Psalm 37:13).

__“She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness”__ (31:26).
That is the nightly speech of the nineteenth Psalm — __“no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard”__: the witness declares, and what she declares is torah, kindly.

Through all of it the poem never says *moon*; the Hebrew of the portrait contains no moon-word at all.
It paints a light that rises in the dark, shines the whole night with a shining her husband gives her, deals the appointed portions, holds the circuit — and withholds the name to the last line.
The Song of Songs supplies what the portrait withheld, singing the same two praise-verbs over the same one-above-the-daughters woman:

[quote.scripture, Song of Solomon 6:9-10]
____
My dove, my undefiled is but *one*; she is the *only one* of her mother, she is the choice one of her that bare her.
The daughters saw her, and *blessed* her; yea, the queens and the concubines, and they *praised* her.
Who is she that looketh forth as the morning, *fair as the* sym:sym-moon[moon], clear as the sym:sym-sun[sun], and terrible as an army with banners?
____

The poem paints the portrait; the Song signs it.

Wisdom's poem reads the same way, line by line.
__“She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her: and blessed is every one that holdeth her fast”__ (3:18) — the holding-verb is _tamak_, the verb of the palms holding the circuit, and the verb of Moses’ hands on the mountain, held up *steady* — _emunah_, the faithful-witness word — __“until the going down of the sun,”__ while Joshua *weakened* Amalek — _chalash_, the rare verb of Isaiah 14:12, three occurrences in all of Scripture (Exodus 17:12-13).

__“The LORD by wisdom hath founded the earth; by understanding hath he established the heavens”__ (3:19) — __“established”__ is _konen_, the verb of __“the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*”__ (Psalm 8:3); the wisdom priced above sym:sym-pearl[pearls] is the wisdom that built the sky — __“when he prepared the heavens, *I was there*”__ (Proverbs 8:27).
__“By his knowledge the depths were broken open, and the skies drop down the dew”__ (3:20) — the *broken-open* deeps are Genesis 7:11's, the flood's calendar-stamped ledger; and the __“skies”__ are the _shachaqim_, the witness's own sky, dropping down the dew.

=== The One Pearl of the Night Sky
Mark the pearl's properties as Scripture handles the figure.
It is *one*: the merchant found __“one pearl of great price”__ and stopped collecting, for the kingdom is not a collection but one bride, bought whole.
It is *round and complete*: the roundness sits inside Strong's definition itself.
It is *white* — a bearer of soft, borrowed light out of darkness — and it is born of the *waters*.
Then Revelation prices the city's entrances in it:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:21]
____
And the twelve sym:sym-door[gates] were twelve *pearls*; *every several gate was of one pearl*…
____

The Greek doubles the point — _each one_ of the gates, _of one_ pearl — and the Hebrew Revelation doubles it the same way: every *single* gate, of *one* pearl.
Not gates studded with pearls: each gate *is* one pearl — one, complete, whole.
So the pearl, wherever Scripture spends it, is the one complete thing of great price — and it is a *gate*.
Now ask the merchant's question: has the King hung such a thing anywhere in plain sight — one, round, white, complete, and standing at a door?

Look up.
Of all the moon's phases and all the lights of heaven, exactly one is white, round, and complete: the night sky owns precisely one pearl.
And Scripture had already assigned that light its office: the moon appointed for the _moedim_, the appointed times, from the fourth day (Psalm 104:19; Genesis 1:14).

The pairing was set at the pattern's founding: Joseph's dream read the moon as the *mother* (Genesis 37:9-10), and the sun comes out of his chamber __“as a *bridegroom*”__ (Psalm 19:5) — a bridegroom's answering light is his bride, the counterpart the link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter already seated facing him, moon toward her Bridegroom as the Son is moon toward His Father.

=== The Letters Confirm It

If the pearl has led truly, the letters should be waiting at the same spot — and they are.
Scripture keeps a __“fulness”__ overhead, and the King James buried it exactly as it buried the pearls.
The Hebrew is _keseh_ — Strong's: __“properly, *fulness* or the *full moon*”__ — and most translations render it as what it is:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 81:3, NKJV]
____
Blow the trumpet at the time of the New Moon, at the *full* sym:sym-moon[moon], on our solemn feast day.
____

Here the King James stands nearly alone with its __“in the time appointed”__; the NKJV, the ESV, the NASB, the Jewish Publication Society's version — __“at the full moon for our feast-day”__ — and Smith's Literal Translation all read *full moon*.
And the oldest translation of all leans the same way: the Septuagint, made centuries before Messiah, renders the day __“in the *glorious* day of your feast”__ — the Greek is _eusēmos_, *well-marked, conspicuous*, built on _sēma_, a *sign*, the very root the Greek of Genesis 1:14 hangs the lights on, __“for *signs*.”__
Whatever else those translators heard in _keseh_, they heard the day the sky cannot hide — and of the moon's phases exactly one is conspicuous, while the dark conjunction is the one day the sky shows no sign at all.

The verse does not say __“and”__ between the new moon and the full moon — one day, not two.
Some translations insert an __“and”__ to split them apart; the Hebrew, rendered literally, in its own order, runs the other way — _you shall blow with the new-moon trumpet in the full moon for the day of our festival sacrifice_ — the full moon *is* the head of the month, the feast-day of Numbers 10:10 and 28:11.

Solomon uses the same word for the day the traveling husband returns: __“he will come home at the *full moon*”__ (Proverbs 7:20) — _keseh_ again — and there it is spelled כסא, letter for letter the spelling of _kisse’_, __“throne.”__
The last chapter stood on that identity from the enemy's side; here is the LORD's side of it. Both words grow from one root, _kasah_, to cover, to fill — the throne is the covered seat, the full moon the filled light — and Job holds the two senses up in a single verse: __“He holdeth back the face of *his throne*, and spreadeth *his cloud* upon it”__ (Job 26:9), the veiled face of His full sym:sym-moon[moon].

If it is asked which way the covering runs — whether the throne-word is covered in darkness or covered in light — Scripture describes the throne itself.
Daniel saw the throne-room itself, and every detail belongs to this chapter:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9 — rendered from the Aramaic]
____
I beheld till the *thrones* were *set in place*, and the *Ancient of days* did sit, whose garment was *white as snow*, and the hair of his head like the *pure wool*: his throne was like the *fiery flame*, and his wheels as burning fire.
____

The thrones are *set* — placed like lights in their stations.footnote:dan79set[The King James prints __“the thrones were cast down”__; the Aramaic verb is one of placing, and the newer translations read it so — the thrones were set, not overthrown.]
And the title under __“Ancient of days”__ is built on a root of moving onward, going before — read once, He is the Ancient of Days; read again, He is the *Foremost of Days*: the One at the head of the days, seated where the days begin.
His garment white as snow, His hair like the pure wool, His throne flame, His wheels burning fire — and the plural on __“wheels”__ is the ending this book met on Daniel's sevens: an intensive, not a count — *the wheel complete*, the perfect circle of fire.

Whiteness enthroned on light; brightness round about it, __“as the appearance of the sym:sym-bow[bow] that is in the sym:sym-cloud[cloud] in the day of rain”__ (Ezekiel 1:26-28); and its Occupant __“coverest thyself with *light* as with a garment”__ (Psalm 104:2).
If the throne is the _keseh_, Daniel's vision draws what the letters spell: the full moons set in their stations, and the Head of Days taking His seat — white, complete, covered in light — where the month begins.

The psalm that gave this book the sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness] link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[already sets the throne] as the sym:sym-sun[sun] and as the sym:sym-moon[moon] — Psalm 89:36-37 — plain simile, not wordplay, and the throne is a throne of light.footnote:fn26thronepsalm[Derived in the link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter.]
Where darkness stands near it, it stands *outside*, as a veil: __“clouds and darkness are *round about* him”__ (Psalm 97:2) — round about, not within — the cloud of Job 26:9 spread *upon* the face, holding the light back.
The _keseh_ is covered in light; only the counterfeit is covered in dark.

=== The Gate That Opens

The sky is built like a sanctuary — the tabernacle Psalm 19 pitched for the sun — and its front, like the tabernacle's, faces the sunrise, the sym:sym-east[east].
Now hear Ezekiel on the gates of the house:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 46:1]
____
Thus saith the Lord GOD; The sym:sym-door[gate] of the inner court that looketh toward the sym:sym-east[east] shall be *shut the six working days*; but on the *sabbath* it shall be opened, and in the day of the *new* sym:sym-moon[moon] it shall be opened.
____

Three kinds of days — working days, sabbaths, new-moon days (Amos knew the third kind too: __“When will the *new moon* be gone, that we may sell corn?”__ Amos 8:5) — and on the day that begins the month, the east gate stands open.
A gate is opened because something is meant to come through it.
And exactly one phase enters by the east gate at the fall of night: the *full* moon enters in the east at the hour the portrait already fixed — through the front door of the sky's tabernacle, the faithful witness at maximum.

The sliver, by contrast, is hunted low in the *west*, at sunset, beside the sun — and the sheepfold has a rule about entrances:

[quote.scripture, John 10:1-2]
____
Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that entereth not by the sym:sym-door[door] into the sheepfold, but *climbeth up some other way*, the same is a *thief* and a robber.
But he that entereth in by the sym:sym-door[door] is the *shepherd* of the sheep.
____

The prince of Ezekiel's house comes and goes __“by the way of the gate that looketh toward the *east*”__ (Ezekiel 46:12).
The true light enters at the front sym:sym-door[gate]; the counterfeit climbs in from the dark side — which is precisely the address his heart chose in the last chapter.

=== One Pearl, One Gate

Now set the pieces together.
The pearl is the full moon; the city's pearl-gates are His appointed times — for the _moedim_ are, as the last chapter read the word, *meetings*: the fixed days on which the King receives.
Twelve gates; twelve months of full moons; the sym:sym-stars[stars] of God gathered into the twelve constellations, the __“Mazzaroth”__ brought forth __“in his season”__ (Job 38:32), through which the full moon makes its yearly round.
And there Solomon's woman collects what her portrait promised — praised in the gates her fulness passes through, her husband at home among the elders.

The merchant who sold all he had for *one pearl of great price* was not buying jewelry.
He was buying his entry at the King's gate — the appointed time of meeting — and Isaiah had already welded the paired parables into one sentence: __“sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom] and knowledge shall be the stability of thy *times*… the sym:sym-fear[fear] of the LORD is his *treasure*”__ (Isaiah 33:6).
The treasure and the times; the pearl and the gate.

__“It is the glory of God to *conceal* a thing: but the honour of kings is to *search out* a matter”__ (Proverbs 25:2) — He hid the pearl where only a merchant who would sell everything looks.

=== Pearls Before Swine

Jesus spent the pearl-word one other time, and it is a warning:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6]
____
Give not that which is *holy* unto the sym:sym-dog[dogs], neither cast ye your *pearls* before *swine*, lest they *trample them under their feet*, and turn again and rend you.
____

The dogs and the swine are one family — unclean sym:sym-beast[beasts], the nations outside the covenant table.
And what swine do to pearls is exactly what Scripture says the nations do to holy things: they tread them underfoot — __“it is given unto the *Gentiles*: and the holy city shall they *tread under foot*”__ (Revelation 11:2); the sanctuary and the host __“*trodden under foot*”__ (Daniel 8:13); __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles, until the *times* of the Gentiles be fulfilled”__ (Luke 21:24).

Mark the last word of that sentence: what the trampling runs until is a matter of *times*.
If the pearl is the appointed time of meeting, the warning reads whole: hand the holy times to the nations and they will not keep them — they will trample them, and turn and rend the hand that gave them.
Daniel named the plan — to change the times and the laws; this verse is the Lord's advance notice of how the theft would look on the ground — the pearl underfoot.

=== The Two Moons

Read this chapter and the last as the pair they are.
The enemy raises the moon covered in *darkness*; the LORD's sign is the same seat covered in *light* — the Father's whole face borne through the dark.
One moon reflects everything; the other reflects nothing, for its source produces only dark.
One enters by the door; the other climbs up some other way.

The sky itself stages the ambition, for the two moons stand in two different places: the full moon, standing always opposite, can never come between; only the moon covered in darkness can stand *over* the sun — slide before his face and put out the day at noon — and blot the sym:sym-stars[stars] from the same sky.
That is the plan of the last chapter drawn in one picture — __“Woe unto them… that put *darkness for light*, and *light for darkness*”__ (Isaiah 5:20).

The sym:sym-stars[stars] — the righteous, who keep time — follow one moon or the other.
That is why the parable's merchant sells *all*: the pearl is not an ornament on the kingdom; it is the gate into it, and there has only ever been one seller who could ask that price and one buyer who paid it gladly.

Grade this chapter the way the Parables of the Kingdom chapter graded everything, for the method matters more than any single finding.
Every link stands in the text: the pearl-word under the King James’ __“rubies”__; the city's gates each of *one pearl*; the moon set over the _moedim_ from the fourth day; the fulness-word under __“throne”__ and __“time appointed”__ confirming where the pearl had already led; the east gate opened on the new-moon day; and the two poems rendered whole on the bare letters, every departure weighed word by word.
The chain that joins the links is a reading — this book's reading, offered to be tested, not swallowed.

The reckoning that follows from it — where the month truly begins, how the sabbaths fall, what history did to the calendar and what the sky and the records of the nations still testify — is a book of its own.footnote:fn26ttt[The complete case is made in _Time Tested Tradition: The Renewed Biblical Calendar_.]
This chapter needs only the symbol.
The kingdom has a gate; the gate is one complete pearl; and the merchant who understands what is being sold does not haggle.

=== Every Word Weighed

The rule of the link:/books/symbolic-language/what-is-the-point/[What Is the Point?] chapter cuts both ways: the pointing gets a vote, not a veto — and neither does the re-reading, until the letters and the witnesses agree.
Here, then, is every departure, weighed; whatever is not listed is the King James untouched.

*In wisdom's poem (Proverbs 3:13-20):*

- __draweth out__ (3:13) — _puq_, Strong's “to issue, draw out”; the King James’ “getteth” flattens a verb of drawing forth.
- __going-round__ (3:14) — _sachar_, “to travel round (as a pedlar); traffic” — the merchant-word of 31:14, 18; rendered by its own root-sense.
- __harvest__ (3:14) — _tevuah_, “income, i.e. produce”; the King James renders it “increase” and “fruits” elsewhere — the field-word, kept agricultural.
- __bright__ (3:15) — _yaqar_: the King James’ own rendering at Job 31:26 — “the moon walking in *brightness*” — the one place it describes a light.
- __pearls__ (3:15; 31:10) — _peninim_, Strong's “probably a pearl (as round)”; the chapter's founding recovery.
- __length and appropriateness of days / shining__ (3:16) — the root under _orek_ keeps a second sense in the same letters: _arik_, __“it was not *meet* for us”__ (Ezra 4:14), Strong's __“reaching to a given point; to suit”__ — the days counted *and* fitted; and _kavod_ is glory as Scripture displays it, radiance — __“Arise, *shine*; for thy light is come, and the *glory* of the LORD is risen upon thee”__ (Isaiah 60:1).
- __splendour / wholeness__ (3:17) — _noam_, “agreeableness, i.e. delight, suitableness, splendor”; _shalom_, “safe, i.e. well, happy... completeness” — peace as the whole and unbroken.
- __holdeth her fast__ (3:18) — _tamak_, “to sustain; to obtain, keep fast” — the verb of 31:19.
- __established__ (3:19) — _konen_ (H3559), the verb of Psalm 8:3, “the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*.”
- __broken open / skies__ (3:20) — _baqa_, the verb of Genesis 7:11 (“were all the fountains of the great deep *broken up*”); _shachaqim_, the sky-word of Psalm 89:37.

*In the bride's portrait (Proverbs 31:10-31):*

- __never diminish__ (31:11) — _chaser_: Strong's King James column reads “decrease, abated, fail” — the gain that does not wane.
- __palms__ (31:13, 16, 19, 20) — _kaph_, “the hollow hand or palm,” kept visible because the poem itself sets _kaph_ beside _yad_ inside single verses (vv19-20): the palm and the hand are distinguished by the poet, not by us.
- __the one who travels round__ (31:14) — _sachar_ again, the same going-round as 3:14.
- __taketh it__ (31:16) — the written verb is _laqach_, to *take*; the King James prints __“buyeth it.”__
- __the covenant's meat / the appointed portion__ (31:15) — _teref_ is the noun of Psalm 111:5, “he hath given *meat* unto them that fear him: he will ever be mindful of his *covenant*”; _choq_ is “an enactment; an appointment (of time)” — the King James’ own “set time” (Job 14:13), and the word of Jeremiah 31:35's moon-and-stars ordinances.
- __tasteth that it is good / in the night__ (31:18) — the Hebrew order restored: טָעֲמָה כִּי־טוֹב — “she tasteth that *it is good*,” the _ki tov_ spoken over the light (Genesis 1:4); “in the night” is the written text (the kethib).
- __the circuit__ (31:19) — _pelekh_, “from an unused root meaning to be round; a circuit... also a spindle (as whirled)”; the King James itself renders it as a district (“part,” Nehemiah 3). Both spin: the housewife's whorl and the sky's round.
- __not worried by the whiteness / in double__ (31:21) — _yare_ is fear in the sense of anxious care, and _sheleg_, snow, is Scripture's standard of *whiteness* (__“white as snow,”__ Isaiah 1:18); and the letters שנים read as _shnayim_, “double”: so the Septuagint (“garments of double texture”), the Vulgate, and Smith's Literal Translation; the pointing reads _shanim_, “scarlet.” The elder witnesses carry the double; the same letters also spell “years” — a revolution of time.
- __white linen__ (31:22) — _shesh_, “bleached stuff, i.e. white linen” — the whiteness sits inside the plain word; the King James’ “silk” is the departure, not ours.
- __laugheth at the last day__ (31:25) — _sachaq_ + _le-yom acharon_: so Young's Literal (“she rejoiceth at a latter day”), the Douay-Rheims (“she shall laugh in the latter day”), and Smith's Literal; the identical phrase is “the *last day*” at Isaiah 30:8. And unpointed, שחק is also _shachaq_ — the witness's sky — a second reading standing in the letters.
- __keepeth the watch / walkings__ (31:27) — _tsofiyah_, the watchman's verb (“I have made thee a *watchman*,” Ezekiel 3:17); _halichot_, “a walking, a procession” — the family of Job 31:26's moon “*walking* in brightness.”
- __maketh her shine / she it is that shall shine / make her shine in the gates__ (31:28, 30, 31) — _halal_, whose base sense is “to *shine*” before it is “to praise” (the King James’ own “when his candle *shined*,” Job 29:3; “the moon walking in brightness” verse sits in the same field): to praise her is to make her shine, and both senses are true at once.
- __gone up above them all__ (31:29) — _alah_, “thou hast gone up”: so Young's and Smith's, literally.
- __vapour__ (31:30) — _hevel_, “breath, vapour” — Ecclesiastes’ word, rendered literally.

Weigh them all; the reading asks no favors.
Every changed word is an attested sense of the very letters the scribes counted, most of them the King James’ own renderings from other verses — and the elder witnesses, the Greek and the Latin, already read the doubled garments a thousand years before the points were fixed.
The pointing keeps the housewife; the letters keep the housewife *and* the light she was always painted from.
Both are true — that is how this language testifies.
