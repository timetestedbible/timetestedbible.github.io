# Proposed baseline rules v1

## 1. Scope and possible outcomes

These rules compare two proposed vocalizations or word divisions of the same
agreed unpointed Hebrew or Aramaic consonantal text. A proposal requiring a
different consonant is a textual-variant claim and must be evaluated separately.

The permitted outcomes are:

- `READING_A_BETTER_SUPPORTED`
- `READING_B_BETTER_SUPPORTED`
- `UNDERDETERMINED`
- `INADMISSIBLE_A` or `INADMISSIBLE_B`

The method must not force a winner.

## 2. Freeze both candidates before comparison

Before weighing evidence, state for each candidate:

- the consonantal division;
- proposed vocalization;
- root and lexeme;
- morphology and syntax;
- proposed sense in context;
- the evidence that would falsify or materially weaken it.

Neither candidate may change after comparison begins without restarting the
comparison and disclosing the change.

## 3. Symmetrical admission gate

Each candidate must independently:

1. account for every consonant and mater lectionis in the agreed text;
2. use an attested or regularly derived Hebrew or Aramaic lexeme and sense;
3. use a morphologically possible form;
4. yield an attested or defensible grammatical construction for the relevant
   language, period, and genre;
5. produce a coherent proposition in the immediate clause.

Dictionary inclusion establishes admissibility, not probability. A gloss
derived only from the disputed passage cannot independently validate itself.
A candidate that fails any gate is inadmissible rather than merely weak.

## 4. Status of the received pointing

The consonants are the written datum. Masoretic vowels and accents record a
later historical interpretation of that datum. They are neither binding nor
without value.

The received pointing counts as one historically important reading-tradition
witness. Its repetition in dependent manuscripts, accents, translations,
lexicons, or commentaries does not create additional independent witnesses.
It is the conventional citation and translation default when the evidence is
underdetermined, but that practical default is not evidence that it is the
original or best reading.

No candidate bears an increased evidentiary burden merely because it departs
from the received pointing. Both candidates bear the same burden of producing
positive evidence. Anyone alleging deliberate alteration, corruption, bias,
exceptional grammar, or a special transmission mechanism bears the burden of
establishing that additional claim.

## 5. Fixed evidence order

After both candidates pass the gate, examine the same evidence for both in this
order:

1. direct disambiguating textual evidence;
2. immediate morphology, syntax, discourse, and poetic parallelism;
3. repeated and unambiguous usage in the same book or identifiable authorial
   stratum, including diagnostic collocations;
4. progressively broader biblical usage and genuinely diagnostic canonical
   parallels;
5. independent early Hebrew or Aramaic manuscript evidence;
6. ancient translations that actually distinguish the candidates, adjusted
   for translation technique and retroversion uncertainty;
7. later reading traditions, including Masoretic pointing and accents;
8. remote cognates, analogies, and modern judgments.

A lower class may corroborate a higher class but does not overturn clear
higher-class evidence without a specific, independently supported explanation.
The evaluator must not count thematic resemblance as a linguistic parallel.

## 6. Whole-explanation comparison

For every material datum, record whether it favors A, favors B, or is neutral,
and why. Prefer the candidate that explains the most high-quality evidence,
requires the fewest unsupported exceptions, and also gives a documented
mechanism for the existence of its rival where such a mechanism is claimed.

Frequency alone does not decide. Rare but attested language is allowed when
the context supports it. Interpretive attractiveness, novelty, familiarity,
and theological usefulness are not evidence.

## 7. Independence and double-counting

Count evidentiary lineages, not copies or authorities. Collapse manuscripts,
translations, lexicons, and commentaries that depend on the same exemplar,
translation, database, or reading tradition. An ancient translation counts
only when a defensible retroversion distinguishes the readings. State all
known uncertainty about dependence rather than converting uncertainty into an
extra vote.

## 8. Objective-fact ledger

Every conclusion must separate:

- directly observable textual facts;
- lexical or grammatical facts supported by cited examples;
- historical facts supported independently of the disputed reading;
- inferences drawn from those facts;
- theological premises;
- unresolved assumptions.

All material contrary evidence must be recorded. A claim stated as fact must
be independently checkable. An evaluator may use a scholar's data or argument,
but not the scholar's status, institution, denomination, or popularity as
weight.

## 9. Logical-fallacy audit

The comparison must reject:

- appeal to tradition, authority, popularity, novelty, or incredulity;
- circular use of a translation, lexicon, or commentary whose conclusion
  derives from the disputed pointing;
- genetic dismissal of evidence solely because its source is Jewish,
  Christian, sectarian, traditional, or modern;
- guilt by association or claims about motive unsupported by documented
  conduct relevant to the source and locus;
- special pleading, moving burdens, or changing weights after seeing which
  candidate benefits;
- equivocation between "possible," "probable," "traditional," "original,"
  and "inspired";
- arguments from silence unless the missing evidence is shown to be evidence
  that should probably exist under the candidate;
- counting several dependent weak indicators as though their number could
  outweigh one direct, independent, stronger indicator;
- converting a criticism of one reading or witness into positive proof of the
  rival reading.

## 10. General source-risk rule

A source's weight may be adjusted only when objective evidence, established
independently of the disputed reading, demonstrates a systematic tendency or
institutional commitment relevant to that precise class of readings. The same
test applies to every source and either candidate.

The adjustment affects only the evidentiary weight of that source. It never
supplies positive evidence for its rival. Ethnicity, religious label, or later
association alone never triggers an adjustment.

Where a source has a demonstrated position at stake in the question being
decided, its reading remains evidence that the source held that interpretation
but requires independent corroboration before being used as evidence that the
interpretation is correct.

## 11. Predeclared heightened-scrutiny contexts

The following triggers are declared before any disputed word is selected and
operate only through Rule 10:

1. **Messianic testimony:** When a later reading tradition that rejects Jesus
   adjudicates whether an earlier text testifies concerning Jesus, its reading
   requires independent corroboration on that question.
2. **Appointed times:** When a reading tradition has an established calendar
   commitment and the disputed reading materially determines the biblical
   appointed times or their governing signs, its reading requires independent
   corroboration on that question.
3. **Expressly sealed passages:** When Scripture expressly says that particular
   words or a book are sealed until an appointed time, the absence of earlier
   recognition or traditional attestation cannot by itself disqualify a later
   reading. The seal does not make the later reading true; the candidate must
   still pass every gate and win by the same positive evidence.

These triggers do not authorize a presumption that the traditional reading is
false. They remove only an otherwise claimed source-based advantage or an
invalid argument from expected historical silence.

## 12. Decision threshold

A candidate is `BETTER_SUPPORTED` only when:

1. it has direct controlling evidence; or
2. it has a clear advantage at the highest evidence class that materially
   distinguishes the candidates, is supported by at least one additional
   independent class, and faces no equally strong unresolved counterevidence.

Several weak or dependent indicators do not satisfy the threshold. If neither
candidate satisfies it, the result is `UNDERDETERMINED`. The received pointing
may remain the citation default in that event, but must be labeled
"traditional reading; comparison underdetermined," not "confirmed."

## 13. Reproducibility and reversal audit

The final report must:

- apply the rules in the stated order;
- show the same evidence ledger for both candidates;
- identify every dependence or source-weight adjustment;
- state which evidence would reverse the result;
- repeat the classification with the labels A and B exchanged to verify that
  the procedure does not favor a label;
- report whether the conclusion survives removal of each disputed assumption;
- disclose every rule that materially affected the outcome.

No rule, exception, weight, or heightened-scrutiny category may be added after
the target reading is known. A genuinely missing rule requires
`UNDERDETERMINED` for that case and a prospective revision tested separately on
future cases.
