_Time Tested Tradition: The Renewed Biblical Calendar_ (July 2026) takes Scripture and the historical record together and tests the calendars we have inherited from our fathers.

The premise is simple: almost everyone assumes that the calendar and oral history of the modern Jews reflect the calendar actually in use at the temple in Jesus’ day and throughout the Old Testament.
But if we can definitively identify the years, the months, and the days of the week on which certain events occurred, then we can see what calendar — if any — matches the historical testimony.
The evidence is overwhelming, and it is damning to the assumed traditions.

_Time Tested Tradition_ shows, on the most direct reading of nearly all of the primary sources — from Josephus to Philo to Scripture itself, anchored by solar and lunar eclipses documented in stone — that the cross had to be AD 32.
On the known dates, none of the known weekly sabbaths align with Saturday; the year of the cross puts a *Monday* crucifixion — an impossibility for rising on the third day after three days and three nights.

This creates a dilemma: either accept errors in Scripture, or stretch the other historical evidence beyond what a disinterested student of history would accept.
The political, social, and career consequences of questioning the inherited Jewish calendar outweigh every other consideration — and so the question goes unasked.

=== Whose Testimony?

Before weighing the evidence, weigh the witnesses, for Scripture itself commands it.
The inherited calendar rests, in the end, on the word of a lineage — and Jesus rendered a verdict on that lineage to its face:

[quote.scripture, John 8:44]
____
Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do… When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a *liar, and the father of it*.
____

He said it to the very leadership whose successors fixed the calendar now defended; He warned His disciples to __“beware of the leaven of the Pharisees”__ — which, Matthew explains, __“is their *doctrine*”__ (Matthew 16:6, 12); Mark records the charge that reaches our question exactly — __“making the word of God of none effect through your *tradition*”__ (Mark 7:13); Paul instructs Titus to give no heed __“to Jewish fables, and commandments of men, that turn from the truth”__ (Titus 1:14), and warns the Colossians against being taken captive __“after the *tradition of men*… and not after Christ”__ (Colossians 2:8).
The Revelation names the same lineage in this book's symbols: the sym:sym-assembly[synagogue of Satan], __“which say they are Jews, and are not”__ (Revelation 2:9).
Most Christians miss the force of these verses because they imagine the Pharisees’ fault was keeping the law too strictly; Scripture's charge is the opposite — that their oral tradition *nullified* the written commands they claimed to guard.

The prophets had already given this displacement its legal name.
Micah's indictment of Israel names the crime as keeping — the covenant's own verb — sworn to the wrong legislator:

[quote.scripture, Micah 6:16]
____
For the *statutes of Omri* are *kept*, and all the *works of the house of Ahab*, and ye walk in their counsels; that I should make thee a desolation…
____

__“Kept”__ is _shamar_, the verb of keeping the commandments, here spent on a king's statutes; evil works are another legislator's statutes kept.
Jehoshaphat is the bright mirror: he __“walked in his *commandments*, and not after the *doings* of Israel”__ (2 Chronicles 17:4) — one nation, two legislators, and every deed executing one or the other.
Jesus prosecutes the same charge: __“Why do ye also *transgress the commandment of God* by your *tradition*?… in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the *commandments of men*”__ (Matthew 15:3, 9) — and the Hebrew Matthew writes the legal terms into the verse: your _taqqanot_, your *enactments*, and _mitzvat anashim_, a *commandment of men* learned by rote.
He even splits one set of men down the middle: __“The scribes and the Pharisees sit in *Moses' seat*: all therefore whatsoever they *bid you observe*, that observe and do; but do not ye after their *works*”__ (Matthew 23:2-3) — what they relay from Moses is commanded, and their own manufacture is refused in the same breath.

Moses legislated the audit in advance: the prophet whose sign comes to pass, but who teaches departure from the commandments, is refused — __“ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and *keep his commandments*”__ (Deuteronomy 13:4).
The judgment scene runs on that statute: the pleaders of __“many *powers*”__ claim, in the Greek, _dynameis_, and in the Hebrew Gospel _otot_, *signs*; the sentence answers with works — __“depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*”__ (Matthew 7:22-23), which is Psalm 6:8 word for word: __“depart from me, all ye *workers of iniquity*.”__
Signs prove power; works prove whose command.

And if Scripture declares a sect to be children of the father of lies, how can its testimony be received?
Shall we trust them on some things and not others — and by what rule would we know which?
Scripture gives the rule in this chapter's epigraph: __“prove all things; hold fast that which is good”__ — test the testimony, but do not receive it untested because it is old, or loud, or theirs.

When that testimony is put on the stand, weigh what it actually brings.
Its witnesses are dated, nearly to a man, *after the fall of the temple*: the Mishnah was compiled around AD 200, the Talmuds centuries later still, and the fixed, calculated calendar in use today is attributed to Hillel II in the fourth century — three hundred years after the altar went cold.
From the years when the temple stood, the defenders of the inherited calendar bring almost nothing; and the evidence that does survive from those years — the eclipse-dated regnal records, Josephus, Philo, the Scriptures themselves — reads against them.
So the argument runs backwards.

Where the record is silent, silence is pressed into service as proof: _no one recorded a change, therefore nothing changed_ — as though a hostile majority ever preserves the records of the minority it silenced; the writings of the losing side are not archived by the winners, they are neglected and destroyed.
That same argument from silence once dismissed the house of David as legend — until the Tel Dan stone surfaced in 1993 with __“House of David”__ cut into it; and Pontius Pilate — until his stone came out of the ground at Caesarea in 1961.
Where the record speaks, it is dismissed for contradicting the conclusion.
And when the accounting is finally demanded — a date, a source, a stone — the answer given is __“tradition,”__ said twice, and louder the second time.

Tradition is a map, and the map is not the terrain.
Maps drift with every generation of copyists who inherit them; the terrain does not move.
And God did not appoint the map — He appointed the terrain: __“let them be for sym:sym-sign[signs], and for seasons”__ — for _moedim_, the appointed times — __“and for days, and years”__ (Genesis 1:14), spoken of the lights in the firmament, which have kept perfect time through every century in which the maps were redrawn.
_Time Tested Tradition_ is a resurvey of the terrain: every claim dated, every source cited, every date checked against a sky that cannot be edited.

In it, effectively every piece of primary evidence is laid on the table, dated and cited, with enough detail that you can judge for yourself who is reading the facts objectively and who is bending their interpretation to protect the dark-or-sliver-moon calendar and the Saturday sabbath.
The book then makes the case for the sabbaths — as a sym:sym-sign[sign] and a _moed_ — to be governed by the sym:sym-moon[moon], and in particular by the *full moon*.
_MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language_ documents extensive new evidence that only further confirms the conclusions of _Time Tested Tradition_.
