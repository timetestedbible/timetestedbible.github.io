The flood account closes with a verse that tells two stories in one set of letters: pointed as the Masoretes left it, a ship's log; pointed again, the end of the age.
The log first:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 8:13]
____
And it came to pass in the six hundredth and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the sym:sym-sea[waters] were dried up from off the earth: and Noah *removed the covering* of the ark, and *looked, and, behold*, the face of the ground was dry.
____

This is the only event in Genesis dated to the first day of the first month — and every Nisan 1 that Scripture dates after it marks a renewal: a tabernacle raised (Exodus 40:2), a temple sanctified (2 Chronicles 29:17; Ezekiel 45:18), a going up from Babylon begun (Ezra 7:9).
The date phrase itself sets the *one* first — __“in the *one* and six hundredth year”__ — where Noah's own tallies run hundreds first: __“three hundred *and fifty* years”__ (Genesis 9:28) — the order most of Scripture keeps.
Jesus took this narrative, of all narratives, as the type of the end: __“as it was in the days of Noe, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man”__ (Luke 17:26).

[discrete]
=== The Letters Read Again

Remove the vowel points — the small signs added centuries after the letters were written — apply the Bible's own symbolic language, and the same verse can also be read as:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 8:13 — the letters read again]
____
At the completeness of six sym:sym-seal[seals] of the age — in the first month, the first day of the month — the *peoples* were desolated and laid waste *from above* the earth.
God *took up the righteous* of the Sabbath's ages *with the covering* of the covenant, and they *appeared* — and behold, the face of the earth was desolated.
____

Heard a third time, the letters open further — the sign, the full moon, the sword:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 8:13 — the letters heard a third time]
____
At the completeness of six sym:sym-seal[seals] of the age — *at the chief renewal, on the Whole-day of renewed moon* — the *peoples* were desolated and laid waste *from above* the earth.
God *took up the righteous* of the Sabbath's ages — *the sym:sym-sign[sign] at the full moon of the covenant* — and they *appeared*: and behold, His sym:sym-sword[sword] upon the face of *Admah*.
____

The table that follows shows how the same letters carry both stories — the received points beside this book's, with the Bible's own symbolic language supplying the senses, and beside every claimed sense its proof: the verse where the King James prints the same sense, the Scripture that defines the symbol, or the repointed word itself.
Where the second and third hearings differ, the row carries both, marked ^2^ and ^3^.
Notice what the second story tells: nothing that contradicts the prophecies of the end of the age written elsewhere — it confirms and clarifies what stands already established on independent ground.
The parallel reading does not replace the log, and need not: it lives alongside it, a testimony to the supernatural intelligence required to tell two very different stories with one set of letters.

For most rows the table takes nothing more than a different sense of the same word — a sense the translators themselves print in the verse cited beside it.
A few of its readings want more light.

*The date.* Hebrew has no separate word for _the_ in a phrase like this one — after the one-letter prefixes meaning _in_ and _of_, the word _the_ survives only as a vowel mark; in the bare letters, __“in the first”__ and __“in first”__ are written exactly alike, and whether this date says _the_ at all is the points' choice, not the letters'.
_Rishon_ — __“first”__ — never names its cycle: first of _what_, the verse does not say; “of the year” is carried in from other datings — and the King James itself prints this word __“chief”__: __“the sons of David were *chief* about the king”__ (1 Chronicles 18:17).
_Chodesh_ — __“month”__ — is the renewal, the KJV's own __“new moon,”__ at whatever scale the count is running: this verse has already stretched its year-word to the age, and the month-word stretches with it.
Run the cycle at full scale and the slot reads: *at the chief renewal* — the regeneration of all things at the end of the age, __“which God hath spoken by the mouth of all his holy prophets since the world began”__ (Acts 3:21).
The Greek Matthew names that day __“the *regeneration*, when the Son of man shall sit in the *throne* of his glory”__ (Matthew 19:28); the Hebrew Matthew reads the same clause __“the day of the *judgment*, when the Man sits on the *kisse* of his glory.”__
Renewal on one page, judgment on the other — and both seat the day on the throne, and the throne and the full moon are one spelling: the _kisse_ is the _keseh_.
_Echad_, the day's word, is the whole — __“the *whole* congregation *together*”__ (Ezra 2:64) — and the moon at her fullness is Scripture's whole moon: __“the *whole moon* became as blood”__ (Revelation 6:12, literal Greek).
Zechariah names the day by the same word and hands it the full moon's signature: __“*one day*… not day, nor night: but… at evening time it shall be *light*”__ (Zechariah 14:7).
By the count, too, the day was already the moon's: the head of the month that Part Four will seat at the moon's fulness puts the unveiling on the _keseh_'s own day before any letters are reheard at all.
The formula keeps this voice everywhere it is written: on this same date the tabernacle is raised (Exodus 40:2), the sanctuary is cleansed (Ezekiel 45:18), and the going up from Babylon begins (Ezra 7:9) — the dwelling raised, the house cleansed, the people called out, at the chief renewal.

*The taking.* The verb __“removed”__ is singular, and Noah — _nuach_, *rest* — is defined the first time Scripture introduces him: __“just and *perfect* in his *generations*”__ (Genesis 6:9), where __“perfect”__ is _tamim_, complete, and __“generations”__ is _dorot_, the ages.
Make Noah the righteous — a company — and the singular verb goes looking for a singular subject: *God took* the righteous, __“caught up together… to meet the Lord”__ (1 Thessalonians 4:17), and they __“*appear* with him in glory”__ (Colossians 3:4).

*The sign.* The little word _et_ — the untranslatable object-marker — is, in the same two letters, _ot_, the *sign*: the sign-word is written without its vowel-letter in the very verse that appoints the lights __“for *signs*, and for seasons”__ (Genesis 1:14, _le'otot_).
One chapter later the narrative says the phrase aloud — __“This is the *sign* of the *covenant*”__ — _ot ha-brit_, three times (Genesis 9:12, 13, 17) — spoken over the bow set in the cloud, in a sentence that itself opens with the bare _et_.
Paul rules the seal beside it: __“the sym:sym-sign[sign] of circumcision, a sym:sym-seal[seal] of the sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness]”__ (Romans 4:11) — a sign is a seal.
The covering itself — _mikhseh_ — is the same letters that, pointed otherwise, read _keseh_, the full moon, and _kisse_, the throne, a pair the link:/books/symbolic-language/the-pearl/[Pearls of Wisdom] chapter of Part Four will examine in full.

*The sword.* _Charvu_ — __“was dry”__ — is letter for letter _charbo_, *His sword*: __“If he turn not, he will whet his *sword*”__ (Psalm 7:12).
The sixth seal's sky is Isaiah's — __“the heavens shall be *rolled together as a scroll*”__ (Isaiah 34:4) — and Isaiah's next verse names what that sky sends down: __“For my *sword* shall be bathed in heaven: behold, it shall come down… to judgment”__ (Isaiah 34:5).

*The city.* _Ha'adamah_ — __“the ground”__ — keeps its article and repoints the name beneath it: _ha-Admah_, *the Admah*, the city God overthrew with Sodom: __“How shall I make thee *as Admah*?”__ (Hosea 11:8; Deuteronomy 29:23).
The town wears the ground's own word as its name — Hebrew sets the article on such names as readily as on the soil itself, _the_ Ai, _the_ Ramah (Joshua 7:2; Judges 4:5) — and the Lord's key had already set the two judgments side by side: the days of Noah, then __“likewise also… the days of *Lot*”__ (Luke 17:26, 28).
The flood's own verse carries Lot's city in its last word.
What that sign appoints, the link:/books/symbolic-language/return-on-the-full-moon/[Return on the Full Moon] chapter gathers.
What carries the sign, the link:/books/symbolic-language/clouds-of-heaven/[Clouds of Heaven] chapter will follow through the chariot, the throne, and the white horse after the calendar has established the moon's office.

=== The Sixth Seal Vouches

The second story is not a new oracle; it is one John saw.
Open the sixth seal: the sun black, the moon as blood, the heavens departing — and the kings of the earth crying to the sym:sym-mountain[mountains]:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 6:16]
____
And said to the mountains and rocks, Fall on us, and hide us from the *face* of him that sitteth on the *throne*, and from the wrath of the Lamb…
____

The face, and the throne — _kisse_, the covering-word's other pointing.
The very next scene is the taken-up company: __“a great multitude… clothed with white sym:sym-garment[robes]… These are they which came out of great tribulation”__ (Revelation 7:9, 14) — the covered ones.
The flood account and the sixth seal are the same picture, and the second was written inside the first, letter for letter, two thousand years before John opened his eyes on Patmos.

[discrete]
=== Held to the Same Law

Hold these readings to the same law as every reading in this book.
Each word must be attested in the lexicon — no invented senses; the assembled sentence must agree with Scripture written elsewhere — and here it is Revelation itself, seal by seal, that vouches; and the second reading must never unseat the first, and never touch a command.
The log of the flood still stands; the ship still dried on a real mountain; nothing here loosens a verse from its plain sense.
The warrant for hearing more than once is no scholar's technique — it is the practice these chapters opened from Scripture's own hand: God decoding His own pun for Jeremiah, David's *spoken once, twice have I heard*, Isaiah rhyming his verdict into the letters.

[discrete]
=== What It Would Cost to Write

Now weigh what this verse would cost to write.
A poet can rhyme a line; a careful author can plant a pun and land it.
But sit down and try to compose twelve consecutive words that read as a dated entry in a ship's log — grammar sound, narrative plain — while the same letters, pointed differently, read as the sixth seal of a book that would not be written for two and a half millennia: every alternate sense attested, every symbol matching the definitions Scripture gives elsewhere, the two readings never fouling each other from the first word to the last.

One pun is wit.
A sustained double reading the length of a sentence, hidden under the points and waiting while empires rose and translation after translation printed only the log — that is not wit.
This is the fingerprint the Introduction found in the web — the last chapter called the puns the same hand pressing deeper; here the hand writes an entire verse twice in a single stroke.
And the key that unlocks it was hung on the door by the Lord Himself: as it was in the days of Noah — the days of Noah were *written* as the days of the Son of man.

Genesis 8:13 is one example — Scripture holds many more, for whoever will dig deeper — and here the printed reading keeps the first seat: the log is primary, and the prophecy rides within it.
It is not always so.
The chapter on Lucifer's declared plan — the reading this book's own cover is painted from — waits in Part Four to show the other case: a verse whose primary reading is, by the letters’ own weight, the one lying hidden under the points — hidden by the very one the verse is warning about.

There is one dimension deeper still.
Before Israel wrote in the square letters of Babylon, the letters themselves were pictures — and the pictures preach.
