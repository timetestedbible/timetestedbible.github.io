A woman who had bled for twelve years pressed through a crowd to touch the edge of Jesus’ robe, and __“immediately her issue of blood stanched”__ (Luke 8:44). The Gospels are precise about what she touched — not the cloak, but its __“border”__ (Luke 8:44), its __“hem”__ (Matthew 14:36). She was reaching for a particular part of the garment, and to see why, you must follow one Hebrew word that English has scattered into six.

The word is _kanaph_. Translators render it _wing_, _skirt_, _border_, _corner_, _edge_, _uttermost part_ — and the single thread is lost in the spread. It is the eagle’s _wing_ on which God __“bare you… unto myself”__ (Exodus 19:4), the _skirt_ Boaz spread over Ruth, the _border_ of a garment, the four _corners_ of the earth (Isaiah 11:12). One word: the outermost edge of a thing, where it meets the world.

[discrete]
=== The Fringe on the Wing

God commanded something bound to that edge:

[quote.scripture, Numbers 15:38-39]
____
…bid them that they make them *fringes in the borders of their garments*… that ye may look upon it, and *remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them*; and that ye seek not after your own heart and your own eyes…
____

Those fringes — the _tzitzit_ — are the corners of the garment: God commands them bound on its __“four quarters”__ (Deuteronomy 22:12), so the law itself hangs at every corner. Their purpose is stated flatly — to _remember the commandments and do them_, and not to follow __“your own eyes”__ (there again is the man who is right in his own eyes, from the last chapter). The commandments are tied to the corners of the covenant garment; its very edges carry the law.

[discrete]
=== Healing in His Wings

Now the woman’s reach makes sense. She did not grab at random cloth; she took hold of the _tzitzit_ — the corner where the law hangs. The word the Gospels use for what she touched, the __“hem”__ or __“border”__ of His garment (Matthew 14:36; Luke 8:44), is _kraspedon_ — the very word the Greek Old Testament uses to translate _tzitzit_. She laid hold of His tzitzit, the corner-fringe that carries the commandments; and the prophet had foretold exactly where healing would be:

[quote.scripture, Malachi 4:2]
____
But unto you that fear my name shall the *Sun of righteousness* arise with *healing in his wings*…
____

Healing _in his wings_ — in the _kanaph_, the corner where the tzitzit hang. The healing is not loose in the air; it is at the fringe, where the commandments are. She reached for the law made flesh and laid hold of its corner: __“If I may but touch his garment, I shall be whole”__ (Matthew 9:21) — and she was.

[discrete]
=== Spread Your Wing Over Me

The edge that carries the law is the edge that makes covenant. To spread your _kanaph_ over someone is to take them into covenant — we saw it last chapter, where God __“spread [his] skirt”__ over Israel and __“entered into a covenant”__ (Ezekiel 16:8). Ruth asks it of Boaz in the same word:

[quote.scripture, Ruth 3:9]
____
…spread therefore thy *skirt* over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman.
____

So to dwell under the wing is to dwell under the covenant. Ruth had come __“under whose wings thou art come to trust”__ (Ruth 2:12); the Psalms make God’s _kanaph_ the place of refuge. And here is the grief of it: again and again He longed to gather His people under His wing, as a mother bird her young, and again and again they would not. Jesus laments it over Jerusalem in that very image — __“how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her wings, and ye would not!”__ (Matthew 23:37). The wing offered is the covenant offered; to refuse it is rebellion, and it leaves the house bare — __“Behold, your house is left unto you desolate”__ (Matthew 23:38).

[discrete]
=== The Corner of the Field

The same shape appears at the edge of a field — though the word now changes, for a field’s corner is _pe’ah_, not _kanaph_. That is the symbol working as it always does: by structure, not by spelling. The reserved edge may be a wing, a border, or a corner; what repeats is the shape — the boundary kept back, not reaped for oneself. And that edge God reserved:

[quote.scripture, Leviticus 19:9-10]
____
…thou shalt not wholly reap the *corners of thy field*… thou shalt leave them for the *poor and stranger*…
____

The forgotten sheaf and the field’s corner are not the owner’s; they belong __“for the stranger, for the fatherless, and for the widow”__ (Deuteronomy 24:19). A field is people — humanity is sown and reaped like grain — and the corner is only a thin border of the whole. Yet that thin edge is enough to live on: it is where Ruth gleaned (Ruth 2:3), and where she was redeemed. The remnant is fed at the edge.

And that remnant — the stranger, the fatherless, the widow who glean the corner — is the next symbol to define. The wing gathers this book to a single edge: the law is sewn into it, and there is healing in it; the covenant is made by spreading it, and refuge is found beneath it; and at the corner of the field, where it touches the world, the poor and the stranger glean enough to live.
