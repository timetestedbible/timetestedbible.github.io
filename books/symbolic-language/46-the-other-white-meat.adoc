For a generation the pork industry sold its product under one slogan: *the other white meat* — pork marketed as if it were poultry, clean-looking flesh for the health-conscious table.
The slogan was better theology than its writers knew.
The law's swine is exactly that: the meat that *presents* clean and is not — and this book, whose title is sym:sym-meat[MEAT], owes its readers the counterfeit before the vocabulary closes.

Start where the law starts.
Leviticus sorts the beasts by two tests: the parted hoof — the outward test, the walk anyone can see — and the chewed cud, the inward one: food brought back up and worked again.
The camel, the coney, and the hare all fail the same way, cud without hoof.
Then comes the one beast in either list that fails the other way:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 14:8]
____
And the swine, *because* it divideth the hoof, yet cheweth not the cud, it is unclean unto you: ye shall not eat of their flesh, nor touch their dead carcase.
____

Moses' _because_ is the definition: the visible sign is not merely insufficient — worn without the substance, it is the ground of the indictment.
The swine appears to walk the walk — the hoof is the look of obedience, not the life of it — and never chews the word.
The cud is this book's own mechanic in animal form: sym:sym-milk[milk] is the word received, meat is the will done, and between them stands rumination — the word brought back up and chewed again, __“in his law doth he meditate day and night”__ (Psalm 1:2).
The clean beast receives and re-works; the swine swallows once, swallows anything, and never brings it back.
That is why it cannot tell pearls from slop: what has no second chewing has no taste — and the law says so in its own vocabulary, for the __“jewel of gold in a swine's snout”__ hangs on __“a fair woman which is without *discretion*”__ (Proverbs 11:22), and the word under _discretion_ is *ta'am* — **taste**, the very word of __“O *taste* and see that the LORD is good”__ (Psalm 34:8).
Job states the equation the snout fails: __“Doth not the ear try words? and the mouth *taste* his meat?”__ (Job 12:11).

The New Testament has a one-word name for the hoof-without-cud structure.
__“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, *hypocrites*! For you are like *whitewashed tombs* which indeed appear beautiful outwardly, but inside are full of dead men's bones and all uncleanness”__ (Matthew 23:27 NKJV) — the parted hoof in architecture.
And Paul writes the beast's definition into covenant standing itself: __“he is not a Jew, which is one *outwardly*… But he is a Jew, which is one *inwardly*; and circumcision is that of the heart”__ (Romans 2:28-29).
The swine is the outward-only covenant member, cast in flesh and forbidden at the table.

[discrete]
=== The Boar at the Vine

Asaph shows the beast at work against the covenant.
God __“brought a vine out of Egypt”__ and planted it (Psalm 80:8); the hedges broke, and __“the *boar out of the wood* doth waste it”__ (Psalm 80:13).
The boar is _chazir_, the swine; the wood is the forest — and the sym:sym-tree[Trees] chapter has already surveyed that forest: the towering trees are the gentile powers.
The boar out of the wood is the unclean nation out of the gentile forest, gnawing the vine of Israel.
Mark its verb: _karsam_, __“doth waste”__ — it stands nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible, and it is about to reappear.

[discrete]
=== Pearls Under Feet

The Pearls of Wisdom chapter opened this verse; here stands the beast that dictated it:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6]
____
Give not that which is *holy* unto the sym:sym-dog[dogs], neither cast ye your *pearls* before *swine*, lest they *trample them under their feet*, and turn again and rend you.
____

The Hebrew Matthewfootnote:fn46hg[The Hebrew Matthew cited throughout this book is the Shem Tov text, preserved complete in Ibn Shaprut's _Even Bochan_ (c. 1385). The full text, alongside the book's other Hebrew witnesses, is at https://TimeTested.Bible[TimeTested.Bible].] tightens both halves.
__“That which is holy”__ is written there as *holy flesh* — _basar qodesh_, the sacrificial meat of Haggai 2:12, carried in the covenant hem — so the withheld thing is MEAT, holy meat.
And __“lest they trample”__ reads __“lest they *chew* them”__ — and mark that verb: _karsam_ stands exactly *once* in the whole Hebrew Bible, at the boar in Asaph's vine.
A translator working backward from the Greek would have reached for the everyday word for trampling; this text reaches past it for the rarest verb in the language — the boar's own.
One beast, one mouth, vine or pearls: the Hebrew Matthew does not construct the connection, it carries it.
The consonants even close the scene: the verse ends ויחזרו — __“and they *turn* to tear you”__ — _chazar_, the return-verb, written on the swine's own letters.
The _chazir_ chazars; the beast does what its name says.
Peter will need that pun shortly.

[discrete]
=== The Herd and the Tombs

In the country of the Gadarenes the symbol walks.
Isaiah had drawn the swine-eaters' whole address seven centuries before:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 65:4]
____
Which *remain among the graves*, and lodge in the monuments, which *eat swine's flesh*, and broth of abominable things is in their vessels;
____

Now hear Mark set the scene:

[quote.scripture, "Mark 5:3, 5, 11"]
____
Who had his *dwelling among the tombs*… And always, night and day, he was in the *mountains*, and in the tombs, crying, and cutting himself with stones… Now there was there nigh unto the *mountains* a great herd of *swine* feeding.
____

Graves, mountains, swine: the prophecy staged as an exorcism.
The unclean spirits' own plea names their preferred house — out of the man, into the swine — and granted it, the herd __“ran violently down a steep place into the sym:sym-sea[sea]… and were choked in the sea”__ (Mark 5:13): Legion, a Roman army word, drowned like Pharaoh's host, as the Sea & Waters chapter read it.
The city then begged Jesus to leave — the economy of the unclean preferred its herd to the healed man — and the healed man, clothed and in his right mind, was sent to publish in Decapolis what the LORD had done: the first herald planted in gentile ground, a man who had lived the swine's whole address and come home.

[discrete]
=== The Far Country's Trough

The prodigal lived it from the other side.
In the far country the younger son __“joined himself to a citizen of that country; and he sent him into his fields to feed swine. And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks that the swine did eat: and *no man gave unto him*”__ (Luke 15:15-16).
The lost house in exile, bonded to the unclean nation, tending its appetites, envying its slop — and discovering that the far country feeds its swine and not its servants.
His coming to himself is a bread-count — __“how many hired servants of my father's have bread enough and to spare”__ (Luke 15:17) — and his restoration is a robe and a table: __“Bring forth the best robe… bring hither the fatted calf… and let us eat”__ (Luke 15:22-23).
Feeding swine is what exile *is*; the feast is what return looks like.
Between the trough where no man gave and the table where the Father gives stands the whole gospel — and the swine-field is the one station with no giver in it.

[discrete]
=== The Sow That Was Washed

Peter closes the case on the beast, and he closes it on teachers:

[quote.scripture, 2 Peter 2:22]
____
But it is happened unto them according to the true proverb, The sym:sym-dog[dog] is turned to his own vomit again; and the *sow that was washed* to her wallowing in the mire.
____

The chapter is doctrinal end to end — it opens on __“false teachers… who privily shall bring in damnable heresies”__ (2 Peter 2:1) and calls them __“wells without water”__ (2 Peter 2:17) — and it ends on the pun the Hebrew Matthew wrote into the swine verse: both halves of the proverb turn on *returning*, and the _chazir_ is the returner.
The washing was real; the nature was not changed by it.
The sow's appetite survived her bath, because a bath is not a birth: __“by one Spirit are we all baptized *into one body*”__ (1 Corinthians 12:13) — washed *and* incorporated.
Washed only, she goes back under the feet that trample — into the mire, the trodden street-clay, the one place Ezekiel's healing river passes by: __“the miry places thereof… shall not be healed”__ (Ezekiel 47:11).

[discrete]
=== The Swine Inside the Gates

The Old Testament's last swine verses aim inward — not at Rome, but at covenant men wearing the beast's structure.
Isaiah's indictment builds across two chapters: they sacrifice in gardens, burn incense on altars of brick, __“remain among the graves… which eat *swine's flesh*, and broth of abominable things is in their vessels”__ — and then the boast that gives the beast away: __“which say, Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for *I am holier than thou*”__ (Isaiah 65:3-5).
The swine-eater claims *extra* holiness — hoof without cud, worn as a crown.
In the next chapter the offerings themselves invert: __“he that killeth an ox is as if he slew a man… he that offereth an oblation, as if he offered *swine's blood*”__ (Isaiah 66:3) — worship from the self-willed is the unclean offering, whatever is on the altar.
And the sequence ends at the LORD's coming: __“They that *sanctify themselves*, and purify themselves in the gardens… eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, shall be *consumed together*”__ (Isaiah 66:17) — a deliberate menu of the forbidden, eaten by the self-sanctified, judged at the end.
The food law stands in Scripture's final vision; the man who eats the counterfeit meat has made himself its emblem.

Now set Isaiah's swine-eaters beside a phrase of Revelation.
Twice the risen Lord speaks of __“them which *say they are Jews, and are not*, but are the *synagogue of Satan*”__ (Revelation 2:9; 3:9) — the assembly that claims the name without the truth of it.
That is the swine's structure stated as ecclesiology: the outward sign worn, the inward work absent — Romans 2:28-29's outward Jew, gathered into a body.
Isaiah had already drawn them: __“Your brethren that hated you, that *cast you out for my name's sake*, said, *Let the LORD be glorified*”__ (Isaiah 66:5) — persecution dressed in doxology, from inside the family, in the very chapter where the swine's flesh is eaten by the self-sanctified.
The parted hoof can incorporate; it can fill a room and call itself an assembly.
What it cannot do is chew the cud — and by that test, not by the nameplate, the two assemblies are told apart.

Under the flesh-as-teaching register this book keeps meeting, one more floor opens.
The consonants of _basar_, flesh, are the consonants of the herald's tidings — the root of _besorah_, the sym:sym-gospel[gospel]-word, as the Gospel chapter found.
To eat swine's flesh, at that depth, is to receive the swine's *tidings* — __“another gospel: which is not another”__ (Galatians 1:6-7) — the counterfeit good-news of the nation that never chews what it swallows.
The other white meat is the other gospel.

[discrete]
=== Peter's Sheet, and the Boundary

One vision guards this symbol from both abuses.
When God opened the door to the nations He showed Peter a sheet of __“all manner of fourfooted beasts”__ with the command, __“Rise, Peter; kill, and eat”__ — and Peter gives the vision's own interpretation, twice: __“God hath shewed me that I should not call *any man* common or unclean”__ (Acts 10:28).
The sym:sym-beast[beasts] are men; the vision is God taking nations into covenant — the Trees chapter walked it through.
It changes what the nations are, not what dinner is: years later Peter still writes of the washed sow, because the beast that stays a beast stays unclean.
A man may stop being pictured by the swine — the Gadarene was; the prodigal was — and the sheet declares that no nation is beyond the cleansing.
But the picture itself never becomes food.

[discrete]
=== Living the Similitude

Now weigh what the whole case makes of the command itself.
God says of the prophets, __“I have multiplied visions, and used *similitudes*, by the ministry of the prophets”__ (Hosea 12:10) — and the Signs & Similitudes chapter showed what that ministry cost: Hosea's marriage was the similitude, Ezekiel's siege-brick was the similitude; the prophets did not merely speak the pictures, they *lived* them, by obedience.
The food law casts every covenant household in the same theater.
The man who eats the clean and refuses the swine enacts, three meals a day, the picture the law drew: take in what walks and ruminates — the word received and worked — and take nothing that only appears clean.
His table is a lived similitude, as Hosea's house was.
That is not the reason the command binds — it binds because God spoke it — but it is the reason revealed: obedience is a role in the play, and the prophets teach us to want the part.

You have now watched the beast from every side — at the vine, under the pearls, among the tombs, at the trough, in the mire, and inside the gates claiming the name.
Keep the command and you fulfill the picture; explain the picture away and drop the command, and you have become the picture's subject — the parted hoof with nothing brought back up.
The command stands, the similitude stands on it, and the two assemblies stand before it: one brings the word back up and chews it toward doing; the other wears the hoof.
Taste is the whole difference — and the glossary ahead holds the vocabulary this book has assembled so that yours, by use, may be exercised to discern.
