A coin is the seal made money.
Jesus defined this chapter's symbol Himself, in a parable built for the purpose:

[quote.scripture, Luke 15:8-10, NKJV]
____
Or what woman, having ten silver *coins*, if she loses one coin, does not light a sym:sym-lamp[lamp], sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it?
And when she has found it, she calls her friends and neighbors together, saying, “Rejoice with me, for I have found the piece which I lost!”
Likewise, I say to you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over *one sinner who repents*.
____

The equation is stated in the last line: the found coin *is* the repenting sinner — the same celebration that met the prodigal son (Luke 15:23-24, 32), told with silver instead of a son.
Identify this symbol correctly and it unlocks a mystery woven through half a dozen parables — and, at the far end, the wisdom the last book calls for: the mark of the sym:sym-beast[beast], its *image*, its sym:sym-name[name], the *number of its name*, and why no man may buy or sell without them.

Decode the parable's own furniture first.
The woman sweeps *the house* — and the lost sheep of this parable's twin are __“the lost sheep of the *house of Israel*”__ (Matthew 10:6); her ten coins sit where the house's ten tribes were lost.
She searches by a lit sym:sym-lamp[lamp] — and the lamp is the commandment (Proverbs 6:23); its sym:sym-light[light] is the commandment lived, as the link:/books/symbolic-language/light-and-darkness/[Light and Darkness] chapter proves.
The lost are sought by the light of the law until the image comes back to the hand that struck it.

[discrete]
=== The Image and the Name

Metal is dug from the ground, refined in fire, and struck with two things: an image and a name.
From that moment the coin belongs to whoever's face and superscription it bears, wherever it happens to lie.

Jesus built an argument on exactly that.
Shown the tribute penny, He asked, __“Whose is this *image* and *superscription*?”__ (Matthew 22:20), and when they answered, __“Caesar's,”__ He divided the whole estate in a sentence: __“Render therefore unto Caesar the things which are Caesar's; and unto God the things that are God's”__ (Matthew 22:21).
The sym:sym-beast[beast] is a kingdom, and a kingdom stamps its money; the coin bears the beast's image and name — let the beast have its coin.

The question He asked answers itself a second time, one step higher: whose image and superscription do *you* bear?
__“God created man in his own *image*”__ (Genesis 1:27), and the sym:sym-seal[seal] writes His superscription on the forehead — so what is rendered unto God is the image-bearer himself: __“present your *bodies* a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your *reasonable service*”__ (Romans 12:1).
People are God's coinage.
This chapter follows that coin through Scripture — minted, counted, lost, found, spent, and counterfeited.

[discrete]
=== The Mint

His coin is minted the way all coin is: dug from a field, refined in fire, stamped with the name.
The sym:sym-field[field] is the world (Matthew 13:38), and the treasure hid in it was worth selling everything to buy (Matthew 13:44).
The refining is the trial — __“he *knoweth* the way that I take: when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as *gold*”__ (Job 23:10) — and hear what comes out of the furnace when the sym:sym-remnant[refined third] is brought through:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 13:9]
____
…I will *refine* them as silver is refined, and will try them as *gold* is tried: they shall *call on my name*, and I will hear them: I will say, It is *my people*: and they shall say, The LORD is *my God*.
____

Refined, then stamped: the metal comes through the fire calling on the Name, and the Name claiming the metal — the seal's inscription, spoken from both sides at once.

[discrete]
=== The Census and Its Ransom

The law prices persons in silver — not as a figure of speech, but as statute.
A man vowed to God is redeemed __“by thy estimation”__, in sanctuary shekels, by age and by ability to pay (Leviticus 27:2-8).
And whenever Israel is counted, every counted head owes a coin:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 30:12, 15]
____
When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel after their number, then shall they give every man *a ransom for his soul* unto the LORD, when thou numberest them; that there be *no plague* among them, when thou numberest them… The *rich* shall not give *more*, and the *poor* shall not give *less* than half a shekel… to make an *atonement* for your souls.
____

Every clause carries doctrine.
The ransom is owed *at the counting* — to stand in God's census is to stand ransomed.
The price is flat — no man's soul outbids another's at that window.
The money is called __“a *memorial*”__ (Exodus 30:16) — the same word the law uses for the sign bound between the eyes — and it is not banked but appointed __“for the service of the tabernacle.”__
Appointed how, Exodus records to the shekel: __“of the hundred talents of silver were *cast the sockets of the sanctuary*, and the sockets of the vail; … a talent for a socket”__ (Exodus 38:25-27) — a half-shekel a head for six hundred three thousand five hundred fifty counted men, melted into the foundations.
*God's house stood on the ransom-money of His counted people* — every board of the tabernacle socketed in redemption silver.

The census began at the door in Egypt.
The first ransom of the firstborn was the Passover lamb's blood — the firstborn under the marked doors were *bought* — and God claimed them on the spot: every firstborn thereafter __“shalt thou *redeem*”__ (Exodus 13:13), under a sign the law placed with care: __“a sign unto thee upon thine *hand*… a memorial between thine *eyes*, that the LORD's law may be in thy *mouth*”__ (Exodus 13:9).
Hand, eyes, mouth — hold the address of the sign; this chapter will need it.
And when the claimed were redeemed by silver, the count was a census of names: __“Number all the firstborn… take the *number of their names*”__ (Numbers 3:40) — twenty-two thousand two hundred seventy-three of them, exact to the man where the army musters are round, and the two hundred seventy-three above the Levites' count were ransomed at five shekels a head (Numbers 3:43-50).
God counts what He redeems by name — and the price attaches to the name counted.

The census rule is proven in narrative, at the cost of seventy thousand lives.
__“Satan stood up against Israel, and provoked David to *number* Israel”__ (1 Chronicles 21:1) — a census with no ransom taken — and the clause of Exodus 30 executed: the plague fell, from Dan to Beersheba, and halted at one threshingfloor, where David refused to make an offering of __“that which doth cost me nothing”__ and *bought* the ground (2 Samuel 24:24).
That purchased floor is mount Moriah — the site of the temple (2 Chronicles 3:1).
Satan's census kills; God builds His house where the ransom is paid.

[discrete]
=== The Fish That Pays It

The census coin surfaces once in the Gospels, and the scene runs on every symbol this book has built.
At Capernaum the collectors of the didrachma — the temple half-shekel, the very ransom of Exodus 30 — ask whether the Master pays it.
Jesus answers with the census's own logic: kings do not tax their sons; __“then are the *children free*”__ (Matthew 17:26) — the Son owes no ransom in His Father's house.
He pays anyway, and the payment is a parable in miniature:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 17:27]
____
…go thou to the sym:sym-sea[sea], and cast an hook, and take up the sym:sym-fish[fish] that *first cometh up*; and when thou hast opened his *mouth*, thou shalt find a piece of money: that take, and give unto them *for me and thee*.
____

The redemption sign's third address answers here: the law in the *mouth* (Exodus 13:9) — and the ransom coin is found in the fish's *mouth*: the sym:sym-fish[fish] is a covenant man, and what his mouth holds is his redemption.

The fish is a man in the sea of nations, drawn out by a fisher of men — and this is Peter the fisher's *first catch*.
The coin is in the fish's *mouth* — and the mouth is where the seal-texts put the word: the hand-sign and forehead-memorial exist __“that the LORD'S law may be *in thy mouth*”__ (Exodus 13:9); __“the word is very nigh unto thee, *in thy mouth*, and in thy heart”__ (Deuteronomy 30:14); __“*confess with thy mouth* the Lord Jesus”__ (Romans 10:9).

A man wears the seal on his forehead; a fish carries it in its mouth — on a fish they are the same place.
The man drawn up out of the sea comes up with the ransom-witness in his mouth: the caught man carries his confession, and the confession is coin enough.
And the coin itself is exact: a stater — one full shekel, precisely *two* half-shekel ransoms — __“for me and thee.”__
The Hebrew Gospel seals the grammar: the coin is given _be'ad_ us — “on behalf of” — the very preposition of the atonement, __“and Aaron shall make atonement *for* himself, and *for* his house”__ (Leviticus 16:6).
The substitution is in the preposition: one pays, another is covered.

[discrete]
=== The Widow's Whole Living

Whose gift weighs most at God's treasury, the census has already answered: the rich not more, the poor not less — a soul is a soul.
Now watch Him apply it.
Jesus sits __“over against the treasury”__ and watches the people cast in money; the rich cast in much; and a poor sym:sym-widow[widow] — the covenant people cut off from her Husband, the remnant in a single figure — throws in __“two mites, which make a farthing”__ (Mark 12:41-42):

[quote.scripture, Mark 12:43-44]
____
…this poor *widow* hath cast *more* in, than *all they* which have cast into the treasury: For all they did cast in of their *abundance*; but she of her want did cast in all that she had, even *all her living*.
____

__“All her living”__ — the Greek says her whole *life*.
Under this chapter's symbol the two mites are not a donation; they are the coin-self rendered entire — the living sacrifice of Romans 12:1, in currency.
And the frame around the scene is deliberate: Mark sets it immediately after the scribes __“which *devour widows' houses*, and for a pretence make long prayers”__ (Mark 12:40), and Luke sets it immediately before __“there shall not be left one stone upon another”__ (Luke 21:6).
A devoured widow feeding the treasury of a condemned house — and the Lord, watching the treasury, sees *her*.
Abundance gives of itself; the widow gives herself.

[discrete]
=== The Two Censuses

Now the piece this symbol unlocks.
Revelation holds two enrollments, and nearly every reader studies the dark one in isolation.
Set them side by side instead.
In chapter seven, God counts: __“I heard the *number* of them which were *sealed*: … an hundred and forty and four thousand of *all the tribes*”__ (Revelation 7:4) — a census, taken tribe by tribe like Sinai's, of servants sealed in their foreheads before the winds blow.
In chapter thirteen, the beast counts:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 13:16-18]
____
And he causeth *all*… to receive a *mark* in their right hand, or in their foreheads: And that no man might *buy or sell*, save he that had the mark, or the *name* of the beast, or the *number of his name*… Let him that hath understanding *count the number* of the beast: for it is the number of a man…
____

The mark of the beast is the counterfeit of the temple census, member for member.
Its *name* sits where the sym:sym-name[NAME] goes; its *number* answers the number of the sealed; its enrollment is universal where God's is __“of all the tribes”__; and where God's census collects a ransom-coin for the sanctuary, the beast's census issues its coin-mark for the market — __“no man might buy or sell”__ without it.
Babylon's seal is Babylon's money: commerce standing exactly where atonement stood.
A *number of a name* is a census figure — a count filed under the name that owns it — and Revelation keeps both ledgers in that grammar: __“I heard the *number* of them which were sealed”__ (Rev 7:4) stands against the number of *its* name, and the victors of the sea of glass overcome __“the beast… his mark, and the *number of his name*”__ (Rev 15:2) — the enrollment itself, refused.
The counterfeit even keeps the ransom's equality clause: where the census took the same half shekel of __“the *rich*”__ and __“the *poor*”__ (Ex 30:15), the mark falls on __“the wealthy and the poor”__ alike — the Hebrew Revelation's own wording.
Even the registers face each other in the same chapter — the beast's rolls against __“the *book of life* of the Lamb”__ (Revelation 13:8), the register of which the psalm says, __“The LORD shall *count*, when he *writeth up* the people”__ (Psalm 87:6).

The plague-clause inverts with the rest.
The ransom was paid __“that there be *no plague* among them, when thou numberest them”__ — and the tormentors of the trumpets touch __“only those men which have *not* the seal of God in their foreheads”__ (Revelation 9:4), while the first vial falls __“upon the men which had the *mark of the beast*”__ (Revelation 16:2).
God's counted take no plague; Babylon's counted are the plague's named address.
Nor can Babylon's market starve the sealed, for the other market never closed: __“he that hath *no money*; come ye, buy, and eat… *without money* and without price”__ (Isaiah 55:1).

Even the number keeps the pattern.
Six hundred sixty and six stands in the Old Testament at the counterfeit's two poles: it is a *money* figure — __“the weight of gold that came to Solomon in *one year* was six hundred threescore and six talents”__ (1 Kings 10:14), the revenue at the very turn where the king began multiplying gold against the law of the king (Deuteronomy 17:17) — and it is a *census* figure, an entry in the return rolls (Ezra 2:13).
The number of the beast is a man's gold-count, set against the God who keeps a ransom-count.
Every man ends in one census or the other: counted and ransomed, or counted and marked.

[discrete]
=== The Price of the Shepherd

Man's mint also priced God.
__“If ye think good, give me my price… So they weighed for my price *thirty pieces of silver*”__ — and the LORD's own comment drips with the irony: __“a goodly price that I was prised at of them”__ (Zechariah 11:12-13).
Thirty shekels is not a random sum; it is the law's damages for a *gored slave* (Exodus 21:32).
The Shepherd was appraised at the compensation owed for a dead servant — and the coins could not even rest where they were paid: returned to the temple, they were refused — __“it is not lawful for to put them into the treasury, because it is the *price of blood*”__ (Matthew 27:6) — the treasury that swallowed widows' houses gagged on this coin — so they bought __“the *potter's field*, to bury *strangers* in”__ (Matthew 27:7), the price cast __“to the potter”__ just as Zechariah said.

Now read the purchase with the symbols on.
The sym:sym-field[field] is the world, and the sym:sym-stranger[strangers] are the far-off — __“strangers from the covenants of promise”__ (Ephesians 2:12) — and Paul preaches this very transaction: __“ye who sometimes were far off are made nigh *by the blood* of Christ… Now therefore ye are *no more strangers* and foreigners, but fellowcitizens”__ (Ephesians 2:13, 19).
What the betrayers' coin bought in irony, His blood bought in earnest: the field-world as the strangers' resting place, where the far-off are __“*buried with him*”__ into His death and raised fellowcitizens (Romans 6:4).

Even the potter belongs to the picture: the price was cast __“to the potter *in the house of the LORD*”__ (Zechariah 11:13) — to the Former of clay, who takes the vessel marred in His hand __“and made it *again* another vessel”__ (Jeremiah 18:4).
The field where broken vessels are laid belongs to the Potter who makes again — bought whole by the Man who sold all He had for the treasure hidden in it.

For no coin of ours could have closed the account:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 49:7-8]
____
None of them can by any means *redeem* his brother, nor give to God a *ransom* for him: For the redemption of their soul is *precious*, and it ceaseth for ever:
____

The true price was named at the census — a ransom for the soul — and paid at the cross: __“ye were *not redeemed* with corruptible things, as *silver and gold*… but with the *precious blood* of Christ”__ (1 Peter 1:18-19).
__“Ye are *bought with a price*: therefore glorify God in your body”__ (1 Corinthians 6:20) — the purchase ends where the tribute question began: the image-bearer rendered to the One whose image he bears.

Hosea acted the whole chapter out in one verse: commanded to love an adulteress __“according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel,”__ he went to market for his own wife — __“So I *bought her* to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for an homer of barley”__ (Hosea 3:2).
The Husband paying coin to redeem the wife who left him.
And the sym:sym-marriage[marriage] itself — what joins it, what can break it, and how far the Husband went to keep it — is the next chapter.
