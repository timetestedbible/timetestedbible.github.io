This book is about the symbolic language of the Bible, and little is more symbolic than the speech the sky pours forth, day after day and night after night — knowledge shown without a word, in every tongue at once.
All of creation testifies, and Scripture provides the key that unlocks the symbolic meaning of the sun, the moon, and the stars; with their meaning unlocked, we can come to understand how they govern the sym:sym-day[day] and the sym:sym-night[night].

The method is the one this book has used from its first chapter: search the Scriptures for every reference to the sun, the moon, and the stars, decode their symbolic meaning, and watch that meaning reflected in the very structure of creation.
And the stakes are not academic: __“He appointed the *moon* for *seasons*”__ (Psalm 104:19) — the appointed times — and the announcement of the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] itself carries the command to keep them:

[quote.scripture, Nahum 1:15]
____
Behold upon the sym:sym-mountain[mountains] the *feet* of him that sym:sym-gospel[bringeth good tidings], that publisheth sym:sym-peace[peace]!
O Judah, *keep thy solemn feasts*, perform thy vows…
____

The bringer of the good tidings and the command to keep the feasts arrive in one breath; and it will take sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom] to discern the sym:sym-truth[truth] of the matter.

From the first page of Scripture the lights of heaven are given a job, and it is not merely to glow.
The founding versefootnote:gen116[The King James closes the verse “he made the stars also” — but the Hebrew has no second “made” and no “also”; the sentence runs straight on from the lesser light's commission to the stars, as rendered here.], in its own order:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 1:16 — rendered from the Hebrew]
____
And God made two great lights; the *greater light to rule the day*, and the *lesser light to rule the night with the stars*.
____

The Psalm that recounts the same making leaves no doubt:

[quote.scripture, Psalms 136:8-9]
____
The *sun to rule by day*… The *moon and stars to rule by night*: for his mercy endureth for ever.
____

The stars are no afterthought scattered in the dark; they rule the night together with the moon — __“the ordinances of the sym:sym-moon[moon] and of the sym:sym-stars[stars] for a sym:sym-light[light] by sym:sym-night[night]”__ (Jeremiah 31:35).
The greater light governs the day; the lesser light and the stars govern the night.

And the Hebrew names the two lights with the family's own words.
__“Greater”__ and __“lesser”__ are _ha-gadol_ and _ha-qaton_ — the very pair the King James itself renders __“her *eldest* son Esau… her *younger* son Jacob”__ (Genesis 27:15), and __“the name of the *elder* was Leah, and the name of the *younger* was Rachel”__ (Genesis 29:16).
Read the founding verse with the family's own vocabulary and it says what Joseph will dream: the *elder light* to rule the day, and the *younger light* to rule the night with the stars — a household hung in the sky from the fourth day.

And Scripture keeps a portrait of the younger.
When Samuel asks Jesse for his sons, __“There remaineth yet the *youngest*”__ — _ha-qaton_ — __“and, behold, he *keepeth the* sym:sym-sheep[sheep]”__ (1 Samuel 16:11); and David the _qaton_, the anointed shepherd, is the name the prophets give the Messiah centuries after David slept:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 34:23]
____
And I will set up *one shepherd* over them, and he shall feed them, even *my servant David*; he shall feed them, and he shall be their shepherd.
____

Two offices are written into the lights at the founding: they are set __“to rule”__ (Genesis 1:16-18), and to be __“for sym:sym-sign[signs], and for seasons”__ (Genesis 1:14) — to mark the appointed times.

=== Joseph's Dream

Scripture interprets its own symbol.
Joseph dreams of the heavens bowing, and his father reads the dream on the spot:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 37:9-10]
____
…behold, the *sun and the moon and the eleven stars* made obeisance to me… Shall *I and thy mother and thy brethren* indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee…?
____

Jacob does not hesitate: the _sun_ is the father, the _moon_ the mother, the _eleven stars_ the brothers — Jacob, his wife, and the twelve sons who become the tribes.
The lights are read as persons: a governing household.
Lift the dream from the one family to the whole, and each light tells what it is.

[discrete]
=== The Sun: the Father

The sun is the greatest light; the moon and the wandering stars shine only with what they borrow from it — so it stands for the source.
The dream named the sun the father, and the Psalm names Him outright:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 84:11]
____
For the *LORD God is a sun* and shield: the LORD will give grace and glory…
____

He alone shines of Himself; all other light is His light, handed on.

And the verse holds a second office beside the first — a *sun and shield* — and the psalm has already said who the shield is, two verses up:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 84:9]
____
Behold, O God our *shield*, and look upon the face of thine *anointed*.
____

The shield is the Anointed — the one who stands between the source and the man.
Abram heard the office in the first person: __“Fear not, Abram: I am thy *shield*”__ (Genesis 15:1).
And Moses felt it on the mountain: link:/books/symbolic-language/shadow/[God's own hand held over him] in the cleft while the glory passed by (Exodus 33:22).footnote:fn24cleft[The link:/books/symbolic-language/shadow/[Shadow] chapter watched that hand cover Moses — the shadow of God's hand set between a man and His glory.]
The sun, and beside it the shield: the source, and the curved covering that hands on what a man can bear of it.

And when the New Testament names Him by this office, it names the whole household with Him:

[quote.scripture, James 1:17]
____
Every good gift and every *perfect* gift is from above, and cometh down from the *Father of lights*, with whom is *no variableness, neither shadow of turning*.
____

The __“lights”__ He is Father *of* are the lights of this chapter — the lesser light and the stars, the children who shine with what He gives.

And mark where the verse sets the perfection: not on the Father alone, but on what stands *with* Him — the Greek is _par' hō_, *beside whom*.
The Hebrew text of James reads it the same way: every good gift comes down __“from the *light* of the Father”__ — the letters under __“from the light,”__ מאור, spell _ma'or_, the *luminary* of the fourth day — and __“*beside him*”__ (_etslo_, the close-company word) __“there is no *exchange* of light and darkness.”__

The light that stands beside the Father of lights with *no variableness, neither shadow of turning* is the Son — and of the moon's faces, exactly one carries no shadow of turning at all: the *full*, constant and complete the whole night it reigns.
And the Son of man *has* a face in the sky, by the Lord's own word:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:30]
____
And then shall appear the *sign of the Son of man in heaven*: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the sym:sym-cloud[clouds] of heaven with power and great glory.
____

A *sign* that *appears in heaven* — the fourth day's own vocabulary again, a sign of the lights — and the reader of this chapter now knows which face it is: the full, unshadowed witness.

And Scripture has already named the *good gift* the Father of lights sends down from above.
On the fourth day, __“God *gave* them in the firmament of the heaven”__ — the verb is _natan_, *to give*, under the King James' __“set”__footnote:gen117gave[Genesis 1:17 — וַיִּתֵּן, “and God *gave* them in the firmament”; the King James prints “set.”] — gave them __“for sym:sym-sign[signs], and for *seasons*”__, the _moedim_, the appointed times — __“and God saw that it was *good*”__ (Genesis 1:14-18).
Given from above, given for the appointed times, and pronounced *good*: the fourth day is James' sentence, written forward.
And Moses says the giving reaches every people: the sun, the moon, and the stars, __“which the LORD thy God hath *divided unto all nations* under the whole heaven”__ (Deuteronomy 4:19) — _chalaq_, to *apportion*, as lots are dealt — the appointed times are the portion the Father of lights deals to all under heaven.

Hold that mark: a perfect and complete gift bears *no shadow of turning* — it will divide the true sign from the counterfeit when the counterfeit appears.

[discrete]
=== The Moon: the Faithful Witness

The moon rules the night, yet it has no light of its own — all it gives is the sun's light turned back to the earth.
That is the Son exactly: __“The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do”__ (John 5:19); __“I am the light of the world”__ (John 8:12) — light reflected, not self-made, the Father's light carried faithfully into the dark.
It is David's covenant psalm that seats his throne among the lights:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 89:36-37]
____
His seed shall endure for ever, and his *throne* as the sym:sym-sun[sun] before me.
It shall be established for ever as the sym:sym-moon[moon], and as a sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness] in heaven.
____

The Hebrew under __“throne”__ is _kisse_ — the same three letters as _keseh_, the *full moon*, an identity the Pearls of Wisdom chapter weighs verse by verse — and the word under __“before me”__ is _neged_: *in front of, facing* — Strong's, __“a part opposite; a counterpart, or mate”__ — the very word of the helper made __“*meet* for him”__ (Genesis 2:18).

His throne as the sun *facing* me: exactly one light stands opposite the sun and returns his whole face, and so the psalm's next breath names the seat for what it is — the throne, the *full moon*, established for ever, the faithful witness in the sky.

And weigh what the word testifies: the throne stands to the sun as the *wife* stands to her *husband* — the _ezer ke-negdo_, the helper who is his counterpart and mate.
Joseph's dream read the moon as the wife and mother; the psalm has already seated her as the sun's _neged_ — two witnesses, and at the mouth of two witnesses the matter is established: the moon is the sun's symbolic *wife*, the counterpart who stands facing him.

And Revelation opens by handing the psalm's title to its Owner: __“Jesus Christ, who is the sym:sym-faithful-witness[*faithful witness*], and the first begotten of the dead”__ (Revelation 1:5) — the *Faithful Witness* is the Messiah's own name, and the moon is that name written in the sky.
The _qaton_ son who kept the sheep holds the _qaton_ light's throne.

The lesser light that governs the night is the Son, bearing the Father's light through it.

And the sym:sym-night[night] it shines in is this present age — __“the sym:sym-night[night] cometh, when no man can work”__ (John 9:4); __“the night is far spent, the day is at hand”__ (Romans 13:12).
Into that dark the Son came as its light:

[quote.scripture, John 1:5]
____
And the sym:sym-light[light] shineth in sym:sym-darkness[darkness]; and the darkness *comprehended it not*.
____

The Greek under __“comprehended”__ is _katalaben_ — to *seize, to take hold of* — and it carries two readings, both true.
The Hebrew text of John reads the taking-hold as power: the gloom __“does not have *power over* it”__ — the darkness cannot overpower the light.
And read as the mind's taking hold — sym:sym-understanding[understanding] — the measure is the one link:/books/symbolic-language/the-fool-and-the-wise/[the Fool and the Wise chapter fixed]: understanding is the commandments done (Psalm 111:10)footnote:fn24understanding[Derived in the link:/books/symbolic-language/the-fool-and-the-wise/[The Fool and the Wise] chapter.] — to understand a light is to walk by it.
The world's night could not put out its light, and would not obey it: the witness shines full in the dark, and the dark does not keep what it shows.

__“As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world”__ (John 9:5) — for a light that shines in the dark is the light of the night.
As the moon carries the sun's light while the sun is down and unseen, so the Son carries the Father's into the world's night, __“a light that shineth in a dark place, until the day dawn”__ (2 Peter 1:19) — until the righteous shine as the sun.
And one light keeps the verse's exact hours: the full moon rises as the sun sets and does not set until the day dawns — sunset to sunrise, the whole watch of the dark — the timetable of the promise hung in the sky.

And the two dominions have their peoples: the children of sym:sym-light[light] and of the sym:sym-day[day] — the households link:/books/symbolic-language/light-and-darkness/[the Light and Darkness chapter divided] (1 Thessalonians 5:5).footnote:fn24households[Derived in the link:/books/symbolic-language/light-and-darkness/[Light and Darkness] chapter.]
The day's children are the covenant righteous; the night is where the nations sit — __“Galilee of the *Gentiles*; the people which sat in sym:sym-darkness[darkness] saw great sym:sym-light[light]”__ (Matthew 4:15-16).
And that is why the night is given governors at all: the moon and the stars are set *over* the night, the Son and His righteous carrying His light to the people in the dark.

=== The Stars: the Righteous

The moon does not rule the night alone — __“the moon and stars to rule by night”__ (Psalm 136:9) — and the stars that rule beside Him are named:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:3]
____
And they that be sym:sym-wise[wise] shall *shine as the brightness of the firmament*; and they that turn many to sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] *as the stars* for ever and ever.
____

The wise — those who heard and did — are the stars; __“ye are the light of the world”__ (Matthew 5:14).

And mark how a star shines, for it is not the moon's way.
The moon gives back a light that strikes it from without; a star *burns* — each one a fire of its own, shining from within.
That too is exact.
His light is not bounced off the righteous; it is kindled *in* them — __“God… hath *shined in our hearts*”__ (2 Corinthians 4:6), __“Christ *in you*, the hope of glory”__ (Colossians 1:27) — treasure carried __“in *earthen vessels*”__ (4:7).
And this book has already handled the vessel and the fuel: the commandment is the sym:sym-lamp[lamp] and the law is the sym:sym-light[light] (Proverbs 6:23), and what burns in the lamp is sym:sym-oil[oil] — the pressed and tested sym:sym-fruit[fruit] of the tree — __“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your *good works*”__ (Matthew 5:16).
The saints shine as the stars shine: a received fire burning from within, fruit alight in the lamp of the commandment; and so they rule the night at His side, through the long night of this age, until the morning.

And that co-reign is stated as plainly as Scripture can state it:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 20:6]
____
Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be *priests* of God and of Christ, and shall *reign with him* a thousand years.
____

John saw the scene open with the bench being seated — __“I saw *thrones*, and they sat upon them, and *judgment was given unto them*… and they lived and *reigned with Christ* a thousand years”__ (Revelation 20:4).
The stars are not ornaments of the night; they reign in it, with the Light that burns in them.

=== The Mazzaroth: the Twelve Tribes

The stars are counted, and they come to twelve.
Joseph saw eleven, and himself the twelfth — the sons who became the tribes of Israel.
John sees them gathered into a crown:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 12:1 — rendered from the Hebrew text]
____
And there was seen a great *sign* in the heavens: a certain woman clothed in the sym:sym-sun[sun], and the sym:sym-moon[moon] under her feet; and from her head begins the *cycle*, the *first of the twelve* sym:sym-stars[stars].
____

The covenant nation wears the whole of heaven — clothed with the sun, the moon at her feet, the year's round beginning at her head.
And the verse announces itself in the fourth day's own vocabulary: a *great sign in the heavens* — the words of the two *great* lights set in the heavens __“for sym:sym-sign[signs], and for seasons”__ (Genesis 1:14-16); what John saw is one of the signs the lights were hung to give.
And every choice in that rendering is the Hebrew's own.footnote:hebrevinterlinear[The Hebrew Revelation — the Cochin manuscript tradition, _Ele Ha-Sodot_, c. 1700, which also carries the Gospels, James, and Jude — is the second witness this book consults for New Testament wording. A complete interlinear, and the evidence for the text's legitimacy as a witness, is presented at https://TimeTested.Bible[TimeTested.Bible].]
The sign is _nes_ — not a mere signal but a *standard*, raised high for every eye, the ensign-word of the __“root of Jesse”__ (Isaiah 11:10).
The crown is _kether_, the *royal* crown of Esther — a circle of *authority*, and the circle a *cycle*: rule that runs a round; her __“head”__ is _rosh_, the word every *beginning* opens with.
And _echad_ stands twice in the sentence — *one, a certain, first*, the word of __“the *first* day”__ (Genesis 1:5) — once counting the woman, and once, with _min_, ranking her: *first of the twelve*.

And the counted sign can be read in the sky itself.
The stars of God gather into constellations, the __“Mazzaroth”__ He brings forth in their season (Job 38:32), and His people gather into the twelve tribes, the constellations of His heaven — and of the twelve figures of that circuit, exactly *one is a woman*: Virgo, the virgin.
And mark how a bride wears the sun.
Not by the sun standing in her — a figure the sun stands in rides the sky by day, washed invisible in his glare, a sign no eye could ever see — but by the *full* sym:sym-moon[moon] standing in her, delivering the whole of the sun's light from directly opposite: the Bridegroom clothing his bride — a relay this chapter is about to draw whole.

And her feet are answered too, for the moon *under* them is not a footstool of contempt; it is the stone she *stands on*.
Scripture names the believer's footing — __“and that *Rock* was Christ”__ (1 Corinthians 10:4); __“upon this sym:sym-rock[rock] I will build my church; and the *gates* of hell shall not prevail against it”__ (Matthew 16:18); __“he… set my *feet* upon a *rock*, and established my goings”__ (Psalm 40:2) — the psalm this book's Introduction stood on.
The woman clothed by her Bridegroom stands on her Rock: the bride upon the moon, the assembly upon Christ.

And that clothing is not courtesy; it is *commandment*.

[quote.scripture, Exodus 21:10]
____
If he take him another wife; her *food*, her *raiment*, and her *duty of marriage*, shall he not *diminish*.
____

Torah binds a husband to three portions — food, raiment, and the appointed time of marriage — and the verse's Hebrew is this chapter's own vocabulary: __“her raiment”__ is _kesutah_, her *covering*, from _kasah_, to cover — and __“shall he not diminish”__ is _yigra_, to lessen, to withhold.
Read the sky by the statute: at her appointed time the Bridegroom clothes His bride with the whole of His light, her *covering undiminished* — and by this we know that the symbol of the Bridegroom clothes the symbol of the bride, because the Husband keeps His own law.

And if any doubt remains, Isaiah names the day that settles it:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 30:26]
____
Moreover the light of the sym:sym-moon[moon] shall be *as the light of the* sym:sym-sun[sun], and the light of the sun shall be *sevenfold*, as the light of seven days, in the day that the LORD *bindeth up the breach* of his people, and *healeth the stroke of their wound*.
____

On that day the portions are paid in the open sky: the moon wears the sun's own brightness — the bride clothed to the full — and the sun blazes sevenfold, and the day it happens is a *healing* day, the breach bound up.
The lights do not trade places; the Husband raises His wife's portion to His own.
From her the circuit begins — Virgo, the sign of Issachar, the tribe of __“men that had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do”__ (1 Chronicles 12:32) — and the full moon standing at her feet marks the head of the year.

So the lights are read straight through: the Father the sun, the Son the moon that bears His light through the night, the righteous the stars that rule the night with Him, gathered into the twelve tribes.
And the promise to the stars is the sun's own brightness: __“Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father”__ (Matthew 13:43).

=== The Relay of Light

Hold the offices together and a question surfaces that must be settled before the counterfeit: this chapter read the sym:sym-sun[sun] as the Father — yet Malachi calls the Son __“the *Sun* of righteousness”__ (Malachi 4:2); it read the sym:sym-moon[moon] as the Son — yet Joseph's dream read her as the *mother*, and the sym:sym-pearl[Pearls of Wisdom] chapter will price her as the pearl.
Do the symbols trade places?

They do not — they name *offices*, not persons: the source of light in a relation, and the faithful reflector in a relation.
And Scripture hangs the whole chain on one verse: __“the *head* of every man is Christ; and the head of the woman is the man; and the *head of Christ is God*”__ (1 Corinthians 11:3) — then restates it in light-language: the man is __“the image and *glory* of God: but the woman is the *glory* of the man”__ (11:7), the very word Paul hangs on the sky — __“one *glory* of the sun, and another *glory* of the moon”__ (1 Corinthians 15:41).
*Glory of* is reflection-language: each bearer in the chain is moon to the one above, and sun to the one below.

.The relay of light — each bearer is moon to the one above, sun to the one below
image::relay-of-light.svg["The relay of light: the Father, a rayed sun; the Son, moon toward the Father and sun toward the bride; the bride, moon toward the Son and sun toward the world; the world in darkness receiving a great light",pdfwidth=88%,align=center]

Toward the Father the Son stands in the moon's office: __“the *brightness of his glory*”__ (Hebrews 1:3), doing nothing of himself (John 5:19) — the faithful witness.
Toward the bride He stands in the sun's: the bridegroom of Psalm 19, __“the *Sun* of righteousness”__ arising with the day.

And the office keeps handing down the chain: __“*I* am the light of the world”__ (John 8:12) — __“*Ye* are the light of the world”__ (Matthew 5:14) — the same title, passed one link; and the Lord even time-stamped the transfer (John 9:5).
When the Sun left the sky, the moon and the stars carried His light through the night.

So when one verse sets Jesus as sun and another sets Him as moon, neither has moved Him: the two texts stand on opposite sides of the same Man — one looking up the chain, one looking down.
And the world's long night will end the way the sky rehearses it every morning: the faithful witness of the night rises at the day as the Sun of righteousness, __“with *healing* in his sym:sym-wings[wings].”__

=== The Elders Among the Host

If the stars are the righteous, the leaders of the righteous should stand among them by rank — and Scripture seats them there by name.
It calls the lights of the sky and the court around the throne by one name: Moses names __“the sym:sym-sun[sun], and the sym:sym-moon[moon], and the sym:sym-stars[stars], even *all the host of heaven*”__ (Deuteronomy 4:19); Micaiah sees the same host in session:

[quote.scripture, 1 Kings 22:19]
____
I saw the LORD sitting on his *throne*, and all the *host of heaven* standing by him on his right hand and on his left.
____

One phrase, both scenes: the stars are the host, and the host is the court.
Job heard them at the founding as a single choir — __“the *morning stars* sang together, and all the *sons of God* shouted for joy”__ (Job 38:7).

And the court has a seated bench.
Daniel watched __“*thrones* were placed”__ — plural — and __“the judgment was set”__ (Daniel 7:9-10).
Jesus assigned twelve of the seats — __“ye also shall sit upon *twelve thrones*, judging the twelve tribes of Israel”__ (Matthew 19:28); John saw the bench filled — __“round about the throne… four and twenty sym:sym-elders[*elders*] sitting, clothed in white raiment”__ (Revelation 4:4).

Isaiah had already set that bench in the sky:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 24:23 — rendered from the Hebrew]
____
Then the sym:sym-moon[moon] shall be confounded, and the sym:sym-sun[sun] ashamed, when the LORD of *hosts* shall reign in sym:sym-mountain[mount Zion], and in Jerusalem, and before his sym:sym-elders[*elders*], glory.
____

The moon, the sun, the LORD of hosts enthroned, and His elders in glory — one scene; and the old Greek translation renders the word _presbyteroi_, the very word of Revelation's twenty-four.footnote:isa24elders[The word is _zaqen_, the Bible's ordinary word for elders — the same word the King James renders __“elders”__ throughout the law and the histories; here it prints __“and before his ancients gloriously.”__]

The seven stars of Revelation are __“the *messengers* of the seven assemblies”__ (Revelation 1:20) — each assembly's leading light, held in the right hand.
The Hebrew Revelation makes the office explicit at both ends of the vision: it addresses the book __“to the seven *elders* in Asia,”__ and reads the seven menorahs as __“seven *elders*.”__

Even the psalm of the faithful witness holds court and witness in one sky: the __“congregation of the saints”__ meets where none __“in the heaven”__ — the _shachaq_ — __“can be compared unto the LORD,”__ who is __“to be had in reverence of all them that are *about him*”__ (Psalm 89:5-7); and thirty verses on, the moon stands __“a faithful witness”__ in that same _shachaq_ (89:37).
The elders of the assemblies stand among the host of heaven: the named and brightest of the stars.

=== The Court in Figures

What do the members of the court look like?
John describes animals, studded with eyes.

[quote.scripture, Revelation 4:6-7]
____
…and round about the throne, were four beasts *full of* sym:sym-eye[eyes] before and behind.
And the first beast was like a *lion*, and the second beast like a *calf*, and the third beast had a face as a *man*, and the fourth beast was like a flying *eagle*.
____

Ezekiel saw the same court with the same jewelling — rings __“*full of eyes* round about”__ (Ezekiel 1:18) — and Scripture says plainly what such eyes are: __“the sym:sym-eye[*eyes of the LORD*], which *run to and fro through the whole earth*”__ (Zechariah 4:10); __“seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God *sent forth* into all the earth”__ (Revelation 5:6).
Eyes that travel the whole earth; watchers that are sent — the stars' own commission, told in the stars' own numbers.

And Jesus says what an eye *is* in this language:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 6:22 — rendered from the Greek]
____
The *lamp* of the body is the sym:sym-eye[*eye*]: if therefore thine eye be *single*, thy whole body shall be *full of* sym:sym-light[light].
____

The word is _lychnos_, the sym:sym-lamp[lamp]footnote:matt622lamp[The King James prints __“the light of the body is the eye”__; the Greek _lychnos_ is the *lamp* — built, Strong's notes, on _leukos_, *white*.] — and the lamp is where the sym:sym-wise[wise] keep their sym:sym-oil[oil]: __“the wise took *oil in their vessels* with their *lamps*”__ (Matthew 25:4).
Eyes that are burning lamps, and a court of figures *full* of them: the righteous alight, each body full of light, shining as the stars shine.

And the Hebrew idiom tells what fills the lamp, or darkens it.
The __“*bountiful* eye”__ is, in the letters, a *good eye* — טוֹב־עַיִן — and its goodness is giving: __“he giveth of his *bread* to the sym:sym-poor[poor]”__ (Proverbs 22:9); the __“*evil* eye”__ hoards its bread (Proverbs 23:6); and Jesus keeps the same pair in the lamp-saying itself — __“but if thine sym:sym-eye[eye] be *evil*, thy whole body shall be full of sym:sym-darkness[darkness]”__ (Matthew 6:23); __“Is thine eye *evil*, because I am *good*?”__ (Matthew 20:15).
The eye-lamp burns on what it gives: the palm spread to the poor is the shining.

Then Revelation describes the Son three times over, and the description is the court's own:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 1:14]
____
His head and his hairs were *white like wool*, as white as snow; and his sym:sym-eye[eyes] were as a *flame of fire*.
____

The white Head of Daniel's vision — and eyes of burning flame (so again at 2:18 and 19:12, under __“many crowns”__).
Now we know who His eyes are: the burning ones, His righteous, sent __“to and fro through the whole earth.”__

Now stand outside on a clear night and say what you see: figures of animals — a lion, a bull, an eagle among them — drawn in points of light, wheeling through the whole earth in their seasons.
The beasts full of eyes are the court of heaven in figures, each figure studded with its stars — the Mazzaroth brought forth __“in his season”__ (Job 38:32).

And the pattern was copied once on earth, in the seat of the wisest king:

[quote.scripture, 1 Kings 10:18-20]
____
Moreover the king made a great throne of *ivory*, and overlaid it with the best gold… the top of the throne was *round* behind… And *twelve lions* stood there on the one side and on the other upon the six steps: there was *not the like made in any kingdom*.
____

White, round, and twelve beasts round about it — and seated on it, the man to whom God gave sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom] __“even as the sand that is on the sea shore”__ (1 Kings 4:29).
Not the like in any kingdom below, for its like hangs above every kingdom: Daniel's seated One, __“whose garment was *white as snow*, and the hair of his head like the pure *wool*”__ (Daniel 7:9) — the white circle enthroned among the twelve figures of the night.

=== The Gates of the Sky

The sky is built like a dwelling, and Scripture says so plainly: He __“stretcheth out the heavens like a curtain, and spreadeth them out as a *tent* to dwell in”__ (Isaiah 40:22); __“In them hath he set a *tabernacle* for the sym:sym-sun[sun], which is as a *bridegroom coming out of his chamber*, and rejoiceth as a strong man to *run a race*”__ (Psalm 19:4-5).
And a dwelling has doors.
Jacob woke and named what he had seen — __“this is none other but the house of God, and this is the *gate of heaven*”__ (Genesis 28:17); the psalmist says He __“opened the *doors of heaven*”__ to rain down manna (Psalm 78:23); John's vision begins, __“behold, a *door was opened in heaven*”__ (Revelation 4:1).

The lights keep their appointments through those doors.
The bridegroom-sun comes out of his chamber to run his circuit; and Ezekiel's east sym:sym-door[gate], shut the six working days, *opens* on the appointed days (Ezekiel 46:1).
And the city whose builder is God hangs the count on its wall: __“*twelve gates*… and names written thereon, which are the names of the twelve tribes”__ (Revelation 21:12).
Twelve tribes, twelve figures of the Mazzaroth, twelve gates in the sky's house: the doors of the tabernacle above, through which the sun runs his year and the moon enters at her fulness.

And Scripture ties the gates to the tribes by name, at both ends of the canon.
In the wilderness the twelve camped around the tabernacle in four triads — __“on the *east* side toward the rising of the sun… the standard of the camp of *Judah*”__ (Numbers 2:3), Reuben's standard on the south, Ephraim's on the west, Dan's on the north.
When Ezekiel surveys the city, the camp has become architecture — __“and the *gates* of the city shall be after the *names of the tribes* of Israel”__ (Ezekiel 48:31), three to a side — and John sees the same wall: __“On the east three gates; on the north three gates; on the south three gates; and on the west three gates”__ (Revelation 21:13).
The earthly camp was never the original, for the sanctuary is __“a copy and sym:sym-shadow[shadow] of what is in heaven,”__ built __“according to the *pattern*”__ shown on the mountain (Hebrews 8:5) — and the pattern stands overhead every night: twelve figures around the dwelling of God, three to a season.

=== The Wandering Stars

Not every light keeps its station in a figure: among the twelve wander the planets.
One verse seems to curse the wanderers, so set its two witnesses side by side.
The Greek text of Jude, describing the false teachers of his letter, reads:

[quote.scripture, Jude 1:13 — from the Greek]
____
Raging waves of the sym:sym-sea[sea], foaming out their own shame; *wandering stars*, to whom is reserved the blackness of sym:sym-darkness[darkness] for ever.
____

The Hebrew text of Jude describes the same men — with no star in the sentence:

[quote.scripture, Jude 1:13 — the Hebrew text]
____
And like waves of the sym:sym-sea[sea] that cast out *mire and mud*.
____

The Hebrew is Isaiah word for word — __“the *wicked* are like the troubled sea… whose waters cast up mire and dirt”__ (Isaiah 57:20) — and the Greek's added figure does not change the subject: in both witnesses the curse falls on *men*, wicked men who keep no station, not on any light of the sky.

And the Greek needs no correction either, for Jude names his source in his next breath — __“*Enoch* also, the seventh from Adam, *prophesied of these*”__ (Jude 1:14) — and Enoch's vision says exactly which stars have the darkness reserved:

[quote.scripture, 1 Enoch 18:14-16 — the book Jude quotes by name]
____
This place is the end of heaven and earth: this has become a *prison for the stars and the host of heaven*.
And the stars which roll over the fire are they which have *transgressed the commandment* of the Lord in the beginning of their rising, because they *did not come forth at their appointed times*.
And He was wroth with them, and *bound* them…
____

The star bound over to darkness is not the wanderer that keeps its circuit; it is the star that *broke its appointed times* — the lawless light, and the men who shine like it.
Read with the source Jude himself quotes, the two witnesses agree: the Hebrew condemns the wicked in Isaiah's storm-waves, the Greek condemns the same men in Enoch's time-breaking stars — and the charge inside the star-figure is precisely this book's charge, the abandoning of the appointed times.
No planet is condemned for lawful wandering — the wanderers keep their circuits and their seasons — and two of them carry the two claims this chapter has been weighing.

The king of the wanderers — the greatest of them — the Hebrews name _Tzedeq_: *Righteousness*.
And *King of Righteousness* is a title with an Owner: _Malki-Tzedek_, __“first being by interpretation *King of righteousness*”__ (Hebrews 7:2) — the priest-king whose order the Messiah holds for ever: __“Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek”__ (Psalm 110:4).
Look at it through any glass: it wears *stripes* across its whole face; it bears one great *wound* in its side; and its color is the red-orange of a sardius stone.
John saw that stone on the throne — __“he that sat was to look upon like a jasper and a *sardine stone*”__ (Revelation 4:3) — and Isaiah saw the marks on the Servant: __“he was *wounded* for our transgressions… and with his *stripes* we are healed”__ (Isaiah 53:5).
The king planet wears the King's own marks — striped, pierced, sardius-red — and its Hebrew name is Righteousness.

And one wanderer the nations served, and Scripture names the service:

[quote.scripture, Amos 5:26]
____
But ye have borne the tabernacle of your *Moloch* and *Chiun* your images, the *star of your god*, which ye made to yourselves.
____

Chiun is the old name of *Saturn* — Stephen, quoting the verse, reads it __“the star of your god *Remphan*”__ (Acts 7:43) — and Amos sets the star beside *Moloch*, the idol fed with children, exactly as the nations' own stories tell of Saturn devouring his sons.
The goat-shaped devils Israel was forbidden to serve — __“they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto *devils*”__ (Leviticus 17:7) — are the _se'irim_, the same word the King James elsewhere renders *satyrs* (Isaiah 13:21).

And the satyr's planet wears its own regalia: a six-sided crown of storm stands on its pole, and round about it hangs a great ring — a *millstone*.
__“It were better for him that a *millstone* were hanged about his neck, and he cast into the sym:sym-sea[sea], than that he should offend one of these little ones”__ (Luke 17:2) — the sentence pronounced on those who offend the little ones, worn as a collar by the star of the god who ate his own.
Two wanderers among the twelve: the King's, wearing the wounds and the stripes of Righteousness; and the counterfeit's, wearing the millstone.

In the next chapter we will discover how God documented Satan's plan to corrupt these signs — to *change the times and the laws*.
