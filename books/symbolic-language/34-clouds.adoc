Where does a cloud come from, and what is it for?
Scripture answers the first question with water: __“he causeth the *vapours to ascend* from the ends of the earth”__ (Jeremiah 10:13) — drawn up out of the seas, carried, and given back as rain: __“he maketh small the drops of water: they pour down rain according to the vapour thereof: which the *clouds* do drop and distil upon man abundantly”__ (Job 36:27-28).

And it answers the second in the psalm above: __“who maketh the clouds his *chariot*: who walketh upon the *wings of the wind*: who maketh his angels *spirits*.”__
The chariot, the wind's wings, the spirits — recall the four winds, Zechariah's chariots that are __“the four *spirits* of the heavens”__ (Zechariah 6:5).

A cloud in Scripture is scarcely ever weather.
It is a *vehicle*, and its cargo is water.

[discrete]
=== His Chariot

__“Behold, the LORD *rideth upon a swift cloud*, and shall come into sym:sym-egypt[Egypt]”__ (Isaiah 19:1) — not appears over: *rides*.
__“There is none like unto the God of Jeshurun, who *rideth upon the heaven* in thy help”__ (Deuteronomy 33:26); __“extol him that *rideth upon the heavens*”__ (Psalm 68:4); __“the *chariots of God* are twenty thousand, even thousands of angels”__ (Psalm 68:17).
David saw the ride: __“he *rode upon a cherub*, and did fly: and he was seen upon the *wings of the wind*”__ (2 Samuel 22:11).

Nahum places the clouds in the picture exactly: __“the LORD hath his way in the whirlwind and in the storm, and the *clouds are the dust of his feet*”__ (Nahum 1:3) — He is moving, and the clouds are what His moving raises.
The figure is so fixed that Jeremiah reaches for it to describe an invader: __“he shall come up *as clouds*, and his *chariots* shall be as a whirlwind”__ (Jeremiah 4:13) — cloud and chariot in one breath, the text's own parallel columns.

[discrete]
=== The Pillar That Drives

Israel walked behind the proof for forty years.
The pillar of cloud __“went before them by day… *to lead them the way*”__ (Exodus 13:21).
At the sea it maneuvered:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 14:19-20]
____
And the angel of God, which went before the camp of Israel, removed and *went behind them*; and the pillar of the cloud went from before their face, and stood behind them:
And it came *between* the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a *cloud and darkness* to them, but it gave *light by night* to these: so that the one came not near the other all the night.
____

From van to rear guard, dark on one side and lit on the other — which no weather does.
It carried its Rider — __“he spake unto them in the *cloudy pillar*”__ (Psalm 99:7); __“Lo, I come unto thee in a *thick cloud*”__ (Exodus 19:9).
And its motion set the nation's schedule:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 40:36-37]
____
And when the cloud was *taken up* from over the tabernacle, the children of Israel *went onward* in all their journeys: But if the cloud were *not taken up*, then they *journeyed not* till the day that it was taken up.
____

When the cloud moves, the camp moves.
When it settles — filling the tabernacle, filling the temple so that __“the priests could not stand to minister”__ (1 Kings 8:10-11) — the Presence has arrived, and the journey rests.

[discrete]
=== Who Rides

Daniel sees the kingdom's coronation in the same vehicle: __“one like the *Son of man* came *with the clouds of heaven*, and came to the Ancient of days”__ (Daniel 7:13) — and Jesus claims that seat by name: __“Hereafter shall ye see the Son of man… *coming in the clouds of heaven*”__ (Matthew 26:64; Luke 21:27; Revelation 1:7).
At the ascension it arrived in front of witnesses, and the angels answered the men staring after it:

[quote.scripture, Acts 1:9, 11]
____
…while they beheld, he was taken up; and a *cloud received him* out of their sight… this same Jesus… shall *so come in like manner* as ye have seen him go into heaven.
____

The vehicle of the departure is the vehicle of the return.

The two witnesses __“ascended up to heaven *in a cloud*; and their enemies beheld them”__ (Revelation 11:12).
The reaper of the last sym:sym-harvest[harvest] works from one: __“behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one *sat* like unto the Son of man… and he *thrust in his sickle* on the earth”__ (Revelation 14:14-16).
Even the disciples rode once, and trembled at the boarding: __“there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they *feared as they entered into the cloud*.
And there came a *voice out of the cloud*”__ (Luke 9:34-35).
Elijah went up behind the same horses — __“a *chariot of fire*, and horses of fire… and Elijah went up by a whirlwind”__ (2 Kings 2:11).

[discrete]
=== The Cargo Is the Word

Now open the hold, because Scripture tells us what the water is.
link:/books/symbolic-language/the-parables-of-the-kingdom/[The rain is doctrine] — Moses’ teaching dropping as the rain, Isaiah's word coming down from heaven to give sym:sym-seed[seed] to the sower and sym:sym-bread[bread] to the eater (Deuteronomy 32:2; Isaiah 55:10-11).footnote:fn30rain[Derived in the chapter link:/books/symbolic-language/the-parables-of-the-kingdom/[The Parables of the Kingdom].]
The cloud is the vehicle; the rain is the word it was sent to pour.

And the rains run on the covenant's calendar: __“I will give you the rain of your land *in his due season*, the *first rain* and the *latter rain*”__ — for obedience (Deuteronomy 11:14); __“he shall come unto us *as the rain*, as the *latter and former rain* unto the earth”__ (Hosea 6:3) — the LORD Himself arriving on the rain's own schedule; __“he hath given you the *former rain* moderately, and he will cause to come down for you… the former rain, and the *latter rain*”__ (Joel 2:23).
The age itself is framed between the two rains:

[quote.scripture, James 5:7]
____
Be patient therefore, brethren, *unto the coming of the Lord*.
Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the *early and latter rain*.
____

Ask for it in season — __“Ask ye of the LORD rain *in the time of the latter rain*; so the LORD shall make *bright clouds*, and give them showers of rain”__ (Zechariah 10:1).

And the withholding of rain is the withholding of the word.
When the vineyard is judged, God grounds the clouds first: __“I will also *command the clouds* that they *rain no rain* upon it”__ (Isaiah 5:6) — and Amos names the resulting drought exactly: __“not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but *of hearing the words of the LORD*”__ (Amos 8:11).
A land whose clouds are silenced starves for the word.

[discrete]
=== Clouds Without Water

So the test of a cloud is simple, and Scripture states it as a proverb: __“If the clouds be *full of rain*, they *empty themselves* upon the earth”__ (Ecclesiastes 11:3).
A full cloud rains.

And the counterfeit is the cloud that does not: __“clouds they are *without water*, carried about of winds”__ (Jude 12); __“wells without water, clouds that are carried with a tempest”__ (2 Peter 2:17); __“Whoso *boasteth himself of a false gift* is like clouds and wind *without rain*”__ (Proverbs 25:14).
The false teacher has everything a cloud has except the cargo — height, motion, the look of coming blessing — and the field beneath him stays dry.
Mark the direction of motion too: the true cloud is *driven* where its Rider commands; the empty one is __“carried about of winds,”__ blown by whatever blows.

[discrete]
=== Risen from the Sea

Where do clouds come from?
From the waters — and recall what the waters are.
Elijah's servant watched one form: __“Behold, there ariseth a little cloud *out of the* sym:sym-sea[sea], like a man's hand”__ (1 Kings 18:44) — and the sym:sym-sea[sea] is the mass of the nations.

A cloud, in that grammar, is what has *risen out of the peoples* — and Scripture uses exactly that figure for the faithful: __“so great a *cloud of witnesses*”__ (Hebrews 12:1); the exiles coming home, __“Who are these that *fly as a cloud*, and as the doves to their windows?”__ (Isaiah 60:8).
The counterfeit multitude wears the same figure with the sign reversed — Gog comes up __“like a sym:sym-storm[storm]… like a *cloud* to cover the land, thou, and all thy bands, and *many people* with thee”__ (Ezekiel 38:9, 16).

Then read the gathering with the vocabulary in hand:

[quote.scripture, 1 Thessalonians 4:17]
____
Then we which are alive and remain shall be *caught up* together with them *in the clouds*, to meet the Lord *in the air*: and so shall we ever be with the Lord.
____

He comes the way the angels said — in the clouds, in like manner — and His people board the way Elijah boarded, the way the two witnesses board, the way the disciples once entered the cloud trembling and heard the voice inside.
And they are themselves the vapour the sea at last gives up: the cloud of witnesses, risen from every nation, carried to meet the King.

The cloud that shadows Zion afterward is the same vehicle at rest — __“a cloud and smoke by day… a tabernacle for a sym:sym-shadow[shadow] in the daytime from the heat, and for a *place of refuge*”__ (Isaiah 4:5-6), the pillar standing over the camp as its covering, dark to the pursuer and light to the pursued (Exodus 14:20).
A cloud, then, from first to last: water out of the sea, lifted and driven by His wind, carrying His rain, pouring it where He commands — and known, like every servant in this book, by what it pours out.
The rain waters the earth, __“that it may give seed to the sower”__ — and what springs up from that watering waits among the case studies of Part Six: the trees.
As for the catching-up itself, Paul gives it a clock: it comes __“in a sym:sym-moment[moment], in the *twinkling of an eye*”__ (1 Corinthians 15:52) — the next chapter's word.
