In the last chapter we found that a mountain is a kingdom — a seat of rule. But a kingdom is never empty; it rules over people. So the next question writes itself: if the mountains are the kingdoms, what are the seas and waters those kingdoms rise from and rule over? Scripture answers as plainly as it did for the mountain — and, remarkably, in the very same chapter of Revelation.

In Revelation 17 an angel shows John a woman, Babylon, and decodes the vision piece by piece. We have already seen verse 9: the seven heads are seven mountains, seven kingdoms. Both just before and just after, the same angel decodes the waters she sits upon:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 17:1]
____
…the judgment of the great whore that sitteth upon *many waters*:
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 17:15]
____
And he saith unto me, The *waters* which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are *peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues*.
____

There it is, as direct as the definition of the mountain: the waters are peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues — humanity in the mass. One chapter of Revelation hands us both halves of the picture: Babylon is enthroned on _mountains_ (kingdoms) that rise over _waters_ (peoples). Mountains rule; waters are ruled.

And as with the mountain, this is no one-off. Isaiah states the equation as an open comparison:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 17:12-13]
____
Woe to the multitude of *many people*, which make a noise like the noise of the *seas*; and to the rushing of *nations*, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty *waters*! The *nations* shall rush like the rushing of *many waters*…
____

People parallel nations; seas parallel waters. The sound of massed humanity is the sound of the sea. The Psalms set the same two phrases side by side:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 65:7]
____
Which stilleth the noise of the *seas*, the noise of their *waves*, and the *tumult of the people*.
____

Seas, waves, and the tumult of the people — named in one breath as the same thing.

Now watch how this locks together with the mountain. There a kingdom was a mountain, a fixed seat of rule. Daniel shows us where such kingdoms come from: they rise up out of the sea.

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:2-3]
____
…the four *winds* of the heaven strove upon the great *sea*. And four great *beasts* came up from the *sea*, diverse one from another.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:17]
____
These great *beasts*, which are four, are *four kings*, which shall arise…
____

The beasts are kings — kingdoms — and they come up out of the sea. Empires do not appear from nowhere; they take shape out of the churning mass of peoples, stirred by unseen winds. Those winds are themselves a symbol we will take up in a later chapter: the four spirits of heaven that __“go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth”__ (Zechariah 6:5) — for throughout Scripture, wind is spirit. Invisible powers, sent from God’s throne, agitate the nations, and out of that agitation the kingdoms take shape. Revelation reuses the very same image:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 13:1]
____
And I stood upon the sand of the *sea*, and saw a *beast* rise up out of the *sea*, having seven heads and ten horns…
____

The beast — the great world empire that wars against God — rises out of the sea of nations. So the sea is both the source of kingdoms and the realm they rule. This is why, when a kingdom falls, Scripture pictures it as a mountain _cast into the sea_ (Revelation 8:8, from the last chapter): the seat of rule dissolved again into the ungoverned peoples it once stood over.

What is the sea’s nature? Restlessness. It cannot be still:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 57:20]
____
But the *wicked* are like the troubled *sea*, when it cannot rest, whose *waters* cast up mire and dirt.
____

Not near the sea, not merely threatened by it — the wicked _are_ the sea: turbulent, self-polluting, and unable to rest. Jude says the same of ungodly men:

[quote.scripture, Jude 1:13]
____
*Raging waves of the sea*, foaming out their own shame…
____

And when the last days bring the nations to the end of themselves, the same image returns:

[quote.scripture, Luke 21:25]
____
…upon the earth distress of *nations*, with perplexity; the *sea and the waves roaring*;
____

Distress of nations _is_ the sea and the waves roaring — the verse names the symbol and its meaning in a single line.

There is a particular form the symbol takes that is worth marking. When the waters rise and overflow, they picture not peoples at rest but a nation on the move — an invading army. Isaiah names it outright:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 8:7]
____
…the Lord bringeth up upon them the *waters of the river*, strong and many, *even the king of Assyria* and all his glory…
____

The flood _is_ the king of Assyria — the invading empire and its armies. Jeremiah uses the same picture against another land:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 47:2]
____
Behold, *waters rise up out of the north*, and shall be an *overflowing flood*, and shall *overflow the land*…
____

And in Revelation the dragon attacks the woman the same way — by sending a flood, a surge of hostile people, after her:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 12:15-16]
____
And the serpent cast out of his mouth *water as a flood* after the woman… And the earth helped the woman, and the earth opened her mouth, and *swallowed up the flood*…
____

A flood after the woman is an army or a hostile people sent to sweep her away; the earth swallowing it is her deliverance. Once you see that a flood is a nation in motion, dozens of __“overflowing”__ and __“drowning”__ passages become legible.

If the sea is the peoples, then the people in it are fish — and that is exactly how Scripture speaks:

[quote.scripture, Habakkuk 1:14-15]
____
…makest *men as the fishes of the sea*… They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in their *net*, and gather them in their drag…
____

Men are the fishes of the sea, taken in nets. This is the image behind __“I will make you fishers of men,”__ and behind the kingdom-net Jesus describes:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:47-48]
____
…the kingdom of heaven is like unto a *net*, that was cast into the *sea*, and *gathered of every kind*… gathered the good into vessels, but cast the bad away.
____

A net cast into the sea, gathering people of every kind, then sorting them — the catch is humanity, drawn out of the nations. Recall from the last chapter that Jeremiah’s fishers and hunters worked __“from every mountain, and from every hill”__ (Jeremiah 16:16); here we see the other half — the fishers also work the sea.

Return now to Babylon. She is a mountain — a destroying kingdom (Jeremiah 51:25, from the last chapter) — and she sits enthroned on the sea of peoples:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 51:13]
____
O thou that *dwellest upon many waters*, abundant in treasures, thine end is come…
____

She rules the many waters. Yet the same sea that is her throne becomes her grave:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 51:42]
____
The *sea* is come up upon *Babylon*: she is covered with the *multitude of the waves* thereof.
____

The peoples she sat upon rise and cover her. The sea both seats Babylon and outlasts her.

Through all of this, one figure stands over the sea and is not moved by it. God rules the raging of the peoples as a man rules the surf:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 89:9]
____
Thou *rulest the raging of the sea*: when the *waves* thereof arise, thou stillest them.
____

So when Jesus stands in a boat and silences a literal storm, His disciples ask the right question without knowing it:

[quote.scripture, Mark 4:39-41]
____
…he… rebuked the wind, and said unto the *sea*, Peace, be still… What manner of man is this, that *even the wind and the sea obey him*?
____

The One who commands the sea is the One with authority over the nations. The miracle on the water is a parable of His dominion over the peoples.

There is one more turn to the symbol, and Jeremiah states it twice (Jeremiah 10:13; 51:16): the waters are not only below us — God draws them up.

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 10:13]
____
…there is a *multitude of waters in the heavens*, and he causeth the *vapours to ascend from the ends of the earth*…
____

The same word that names the sea of nations below names a multitude of waters _above_. From the first chapter of Genesis there have been waters above the firmament and waters beneath it, divided one from another (Genesis 1:6-7) — and here is the traffic between them: the vapours rise from the ends of the earth, gather as a multitude in the heavens, and come down again as rain. We will follow those waters above — the clouds, and the rain that falls from them — in their own chapter; for now mark only that the water-symbol spans both realms: a restless sea of peoples below, and a multitude of waters gathered above.

And the direction of that traffic is the point. When Babylon — __“the great whore that sitteth upon many waters”__ (Revelation 17:1) — comes to her fall, a voice cries, __“Come out of her, my people”__ (Revelation 18:4); and the people who answer are drawn upward, __“caught up together… in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air”__ (1 Thessalonians 4:17). Read flatly, it is a riddle — why meet Him in the _air_? Read by the symbol, it is the water rising: the people lifted from the sea of nations like vapour, gathered into the clouds, to meet their Lord above, in the very hour the harlot’s sea is judged. It is a detail you cannot see until you know the waters are people. (The clouds, the meeting itself, and Babylon’s fall each await their own chapter; here it is enough to watch the water rise.)

And that __“above”__ is itself a sea. The firmament God called __“Heaven”__ divides __“the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above”__ (Genesis 1:6-8) — a restless sea of peoples below, and gathered waters above; for the upper waters are named in one breath with the heavenly host: __“Praise ye him, all his angels… sun and moon… all ye stars… ye heavens of heavens, and ye waters that be above the heavens”__ (Psalm 148:2-4). John sees that upper sea before the throne, no longer raging but still and clear — __“a sea of glass like unto crystal”__ (Revelation 4:6) — and standing upon it __“them that had gotten the victory over the beast… having the harps of God”__ (Revelation 15:2). (Ezekiel saw the same architecture: a firmament __“as the colour of the terrible crystal,”__ and above it a throne — Ezekiel 1:22, 26.) So there are two seas of people — the lower, the restless nations, which is judged; and the upper, the heavenly assembly of angels and the redeemed, on which the overcomers stand — and the vapour drawn up from the sea below rises to join the sea of glass above.

And so the story arrives at its end. In the last chapter, Daniel’s stone became a mountain that __“filled the whole earth”__ (Daniel 2:35) — God’s kingdom over all. Here is that same consummation seen from the other side:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:1]
____
And I saw a new heaven and a new earth… and there was *no more sea*.
____

__“No more sea”__ is not the draining of the oceans; it is the end of ungoverned, restless, rebellious humanity. When the mountain has filled the earth, there is no sea left for it to rise over. Habakkuk pictures the same outcome as a flood of another kind:

[quote.scripture, Habakkuk 2:14]
____
For the earth shall be filled with the *knowledge of the glory of the LORD*, as the *waters cover the sea*.
____

=== Sand

The sand of the sea shore is the beach — grains beyond all counting, heaped where the waters meet the land. That is why __“the sand of the sea”__ is Scripture's byword for a multitude past number. God promises Abraham a seed __“as the sand which is upon the sea shore”__ (Genesis 22:17); Israel grows into a people __“as the sand of the sea, which cannot be measured nor numbered”__ (Hosea 1:10); and the last enemy host is reckoned the same way — Gog and Magog, __“the number of whom is as the sand of the sea”__ (Revelation 20:8). Sand is the innumerable mass, the many beyond counting.

But sand is loose and forever shifting, and that makes it the one ground a man must never build on:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:26-27]
____
…a *foolish man*, which built his house upon the *sand*: And the rain descended, and the *floods came*, and the *winds blew*, and beat upon that house; and it *fell*: and great was the fall of it.
____

A rock is the covenant; the sand is the multitude. To build on the sand is to found your house on the shifting mass of people — the crowd, the consensus, the many; and when the flood comes and the winds blow — the waters of judgment, the spirits sent forth — the house on the sand is swept away, while the house on the covenant-rock __“fell not”__ (Matthew 7:25). The fool builds on the many; the wise builds on the covenant. (The two builders are weighed in the Fool & Wise chapter.)

=== Islands

If the sea is the nations, an island is what rises out of that sea — the head of a mountain, a rock breaking the surface, dry land lifted above the waters. And both of those we have already fixed: a mountain is a kingdom, and a rock is the covenant. So an island is the covenant-rock standing up out of the sea of peoples — a people set apart, founded on the rock and raised above the chaos. The word is _iy_, and it falls on the sanctified: the distinguished, the marked-off. The very first __“isles”__ are the differentiated nations of Genesis — __“by these were the isles of the Gentiles divided… every one after his tongue”__ (Genesis 10:5) — peoples marked off from one another, not a swarm. And from there the use never wavers. God summons them as those who answer to Him: __“Keep silence before me, O islands… let us come near together to judgment”__ (Isaiah 41:1); __“Listen, O isles, unto me”__ (Isaiah 49:1). They _wait for His law_ — __“the isles shall wait for his law”__ (Isaiah 42:4), __“the isles shall wait upon me”__ (Isaiah 51:5), __“surely the isles shall wait for me”__ (Isaiah 60:9) — for it is by the truth, the law, that men are sanctified (__“Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth,”__ John 17:17). They _glorify_ Him — __“glorify ye the LORD… in the isles of the sea”__ (Isaiah 24:15); __“let the multitude of isles be glad”__ (Psalm 97:1). And they are _gathered home_ — when God recovers the remnant of His people He brings them __“from the islands of the sea”__ (Isaiah 11:11), and sends His glory __“unto the isles afar off”__ (Isaiah 66:19). Summoned, waiting, glorifying, gathered: across the prophets the isles are one thing — the set-apart peoples, turned toward the God who set them apart. The meaning never bends.

So when the last judgment shakes the world, the islands and the mountains are named together — and parted:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 16:20]
____
And every *island fled away*, and the *mountains were not found*.
____

A mountain is a kingdom; an island is the set-apart people — __“the island of the innocent,”__ in Job's words, whom God will __“deliver… by the pureness of thine hands”__ (Job 22:30; the unpointed consonants, _iy_, are the very ones that name the isles). At the end the two go opposite ways: the kingdoms are __“not found”__ — toppled, gone — while the islands __“flee away,”__ carried off to safety, delivered out of the sea before it is judged (so too __“every mountain and island were moved out of their places,”__ Revelation 6:14).

And that deliverance is acted out as a story — Paul's last voyage, a lived parable of the saved brought through the storm. The ship, sailing the sea among the nations, is caught in a killing tempest; for days __“neither sun nor stars… appeared,”__ and __“all hope that we should be saved was then taken away”__ (Acts 27:20). Then the word comes: __“Fear not, Paul… God hath given thee all them that sail with thee”__ (Acts 27:24) — __“there shall be no loss of any man's life among you, but of the ship”__ (Acts 27:22). They must hold together to be saved — __“Except these abide in the ship, ye cannot be saved”__ (Acts 27:31) — and not a hair of their heads will fall (Acts 27:34). Two hundred and seventy-six souls in all (Acts 27:37). The ship strikes __“a place where two seas met,”__ and the waves break it to pieces (Acts 27:41); yet every soul, some swimming, some on the wreckage, __“escaped all safe to land”__ (Acts 27:44) — onto the island called Melita (Acts 28:1). There a serpent fastens on Paul's hand, and he __“shook off the beast into the fire, and felt no harm”__ (Acts 28:5); and on that ground the sick are healed (Acts 28:8). The ship is lost; the people are not. They are carried through the raging sea, every one, onto the set-apart island — where the serpent's bite is nothing and the sick are made whole. That is the island: the set-apart gathered out of the sea of nations onto holy ground, not one of them lost. (The timing of that gathering, and the feast it answers to, is the work of _Time Tested Tradition_; here the symbol is enough.)

And it ends where the sea ends. In the new creation there is __“no more sea”__ (Revelation 21:1): the chaotic mass is gone, and with it the need for islands — not because the set-apart vanish, but because all is holy ground, and there is nothing left to stand apart from.

One caution before we close. The same words for __“water”__ carry a second, opposite meaning. The gathered, salt, restless waters are the peoples — but the _living_ water that springs and flows from God (__“a fountain of living waters,”__ Jeremiah 2:13; __“rivers of living water,”__ John 7:38) is something else entirely: the Spirit and word of God going out to give life. The two even meet, for the prophet sees the living water flow from the temple and _heal_ the sea (Ezekiel 47:8-9). We will give that water its own chapter; for now simply note that a sea of salt and a river of life are not the same symbol, and the context tells you which is which.

Set the two chapters side by side and the anatomy of power stands bare. The sea is the peoples; the beasts — kingdoms — rise out of the sea; the mountains are those kingdoms enthroned; Babylon sits on the sea and is at last covered by it; and God stills the sea, rules it, and finally makes an end of it. Mountain and sea are not two unrelated pictures but two halves of one map: the governing and the governed, the rule and the ruled.

Along the way we have again gathered other symbols in passing — the sym:sym-beast[beasts] and the sym:sym-four[winds] of Daniel 7, the sym:sym-flood[flood] that is an invading army, the sym:sym-fish[fish] and the sym:sym-net[net], and the sym:sym-island[island] and dry land that God separates out of the sea. Each we will define the same way we have defined the mountain and the sea: by letting Scripture interpret Scripture.
