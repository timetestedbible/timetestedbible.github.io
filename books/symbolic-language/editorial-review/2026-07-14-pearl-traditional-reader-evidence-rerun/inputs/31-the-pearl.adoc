Jesus gives the pearl parable in one sentence:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:45-46]
____
Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking *goodly pearls*: who, when he had found *one pearl of great price*, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.
____

The little parable turns on 2 questions: Who is the merchant, and what did he purchase?

The parable immediately before it tells of a man who sells all to buy a sym:sym-field[field] for the treasure hidden within it (Matthew 13:44).
The link:/books/symbolic-language/the-coin/[Coin] chapter has already followed those symbols: the field is the world, and the treasure within it is God's people — His peculiar treasure, refined as silver and gold (Matthew 13:38; Exodus 19:5; Job 23:10; Zechariah 13:9).
Within the same sequence, Jesus has identified the man sowing in that field as the Son of man (Matthew 13:37).
The next sentence repeats the purchase in a new form: a merchant finds one pearl, sells all, and buys it.
The buyer has not changed.
Proverbs 31 will bind both purchases into one woman's portrait: she is priced above sym:sym-pearl[pearls], is likened to the merchant's ships, calls her merchandise good, and buys the sym:sym-field[field].
The field and the pearl are not separate clues; Solomon gathers both parables around the same woman.

The Merchant is the Son, and the pearl is the bride He came to purchase.

[discrete]
=== The Bride in the Sky

Jesus begins by naming the realm: __“The kingdom of heaven is like”__ — literally, the kingdom of the heavens.
Genesis names the firmament *Heaven* and places the sym:sym-sun[sun], sym:sym-moon[moon], and sym:sym-stars[stars] within it (Genesis 1:8, 14-18).
Before the merchant takes a step, the parable has already lifted our eyes to the sky.

The link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter established the relationships pictured there.
The sym:sym-sun[sun] represents the Father and the husband.
The sym:sym-moon[moon] is the sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness]: the Son reflecting His Father, and the sym:sym-marriage[bride] reflecting her Bridegroom.
The moon represents the submissive side of a covenant relationship — the Son under His Father, and the bride under her husband (1 Corinthians 11:3; Ephesians 5:22-24).
It does not produce an independent light; it receives the light of its head and faithfully makes that light visible.

The parable asks us to find one complete pearl in that heavenly arrangement.
The full moon already answers in 2 ways: it has the visible shape of one complete pearl, and the moon represents the bride in the covenant relationship.
The next step is to follow the words of both purchases through Scripture: *field*, *treasure*, *buy*, *merchant*, *found*, *one*, *good*, *pearl*, *great price*, and *heaven*.

[discrete]
=== Following the Words

*Pearl* and *great price* lead directly to Proverbs.
First, sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] is priced above pearls:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:15, KJV — “pearls” restored]
____
She is *more precious than* sym:sym-pearl[pearls]footnote:fn31peninim[Strong's H6443 defines _peninim_ as __“probably a pearl (as round).”__ The full lexical case—including the ancient translations and the difficult witness of Lamentations 4:7—is documented in link:https://timetested.bible/blog/peninim-pearl-or-ruby/[“Peninim: Why Pearl Fits Better Than Ruby”] at TimeTested.Bible.]: and all the things thou canst desire are not to be compared unto her.
____

Proverbs 8:11 and Job 28:18 repeat the comparison.
Then the woman of valour — the bride — is priced above the same pearls:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:10 — MEAT rendering]
____
A woman of valour who can find? for her *price is far above* sym:sym-pearl[pearls]footnote:fn31peninim[].
____

The find-verb of Proverbs 3:13 returns in 2 revealing statements: __“whoso findeth *me*”__ (Proverbs 8:35, Wisdom speaking) and __“whoso findeth *a wife*”__ (Proverbs 18:22).
Wisdom and the bride occupy the same place in Solomon's search, and both lead back to the merchant's defining action: __“when he had *found* one pearl of great price”__ (Matthew 13:46).

*Merchant* and *good* converge there too.
Wisdom's __“merchandise”__ is good (Proverbs 3:14).
The bride __“tasteth that her merchandise is good”__ (Proverbs 31:18).
The kingdom's merchant seeks __“goodly pearls”__ (Matthew 13:45).
Wisdom, bride, pearl — the good merchandise is one continuous trail.

That leaves *one*.
If the trail is sound, Scripture should not merely compare a woman to a pearl; it should gather the picture into one complete bride.

These searches lead to 2 poems in Proverbs.
The first prices Wisdom above pearls; the second prices the bride above pearls and places the merchant and good merchandise inside her portrait.
We will read each poem whole before examining its lines.
Bold type marks the words under examination.
The line-by-line reading names the familiar King James wording first, then shows whether the MEAT hearing comes from another attested sense, the unpointed consonants, or a scriptural cross-reference.
Then Scripture can answer whether the woman they describe is the *one* pearl in the heavens.

=== Proverbs 3, Line by Line

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:13 — MEAT rendering]
____
Blessed is the man that findeth wisdom, and the man that *draweth out understanding*.
____

The poem begins with the merchant's action: he *finds*.
The King James reads __“the man that *getteth* understanding.”__
The second Hebrew verb, _puq_ (H6329), can mean to issue, bring forth, or draw out; the MEAT rendering keeps that other attested sense.
Understanding is not purchased from outside; it is drawn from the Word like treasure brought out of a field.
The same search will end with the wife: __“whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing”__ (Proverbs 18:22).

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:14 — MEAT rendering]
____
For the gain of her *going-round* is better than the going-round of silver, and her *yield* than *fine gold*.
____

The King James reads __“the *merchandise* of it”__ and __“the *gain* thereof.”__
Strong's makes the movement behind *going-round* explicit: H5503, the root beneath both forms of _sachar_, means __“to travel round (specifically as a pedlar).”__
Merchandise is what that traveling trader gains; the MEAT rendering keeps the root's motion audible.
BDB lists _tevuah_ as __“product, yield”__ and treats its use here as Wisdom's figurative gain.
*Yield* is what a field, a tree, or a life produces.
The line joins the merchant's circuit to Wisdom's yield.
Its opening phrase, __“her merchandise is good,”__ returns in the bride's portrait, where she tastes that her merchandise is good (Proverbs 31:18).
The pearl parable has entered Solomon's vocabulary before a pearl is named.

Keep *fine gold* in view.
The word here is _charuts_, gold as mined; the light-bearing picture develops across Scripture rather than from an alternate definition of this noun.
Job moves from __“bright light”__ in a cleansed sky to _zahav_, the ordinary gold-word that the King James renders __“fair weather”__ (Job 37:21-22).
The Song portrays the Bridegroom's head as fine gold and His hands as gold rings, then sets bright ivory beside sapphire (Song of Solomon 5:11, 14).
Solomon's gold-covered sanctuary held golden lamps before the oracle (1 Kings 6:20-22; 7:49), and the Bride-city is __“pure gold, like unto clear glass”__ under the light of God's glory (Revelation 21:18, 23).
Gold does not yet define Wisdom's yield; it gives the poem a visible property to carry forward: luster — received light made visible.
The next line calls Wisdom bright; the line after it gives her shining.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:15 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is more *bright than pearls*: and all thy delights are not to be compared unto her.
____

The word the King James renders *precious* can also describe brightness; the King James itself uses it for __“the moon walking in *brightness*”__ (Job 31:26).
The poem therefore prices Wisdom above pearls in the moon's own vocabulary: precious, and heard again, bright.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:16 — MEAT rendering]
____
*Length and appropriateness of days* is in her right hand; and in her left hand riches and *shining*.
____

The King James reads __“*length of days*”__ and __“riches and *honour*.”__
The word translated *length*, _orek_, can also describe what reaches or fits its appointed point.
The line therefore holds both the count of days and their fitness — time measured and time appointed.
The word behind *shining*, _kavod_, is glory or honour, displayed as radiance when the LORD's glory rises and His people shine (Isaiah 60:1).

The hands also carry direction.
Strong's gives the right hand as the south and the left as the dark or northern side.
The complete witness holds the days in her bright hand and their shining even in her dark one.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:17 — MEAT rendering]
____
Her ways are ways of *splendour*, and all her paths are *wholeness*.
____

The King James reads __“ways of *pleasantness*”__ and __“paths are *peace*.”__
The word translated *splendour*, _noam_, also includes beauty, delight, and suitability.
The word translated *wholeness*, _shalom_, is more than the absence of conflict: it also includes completeness and welfare.
A full witness walks a complete path.
The poem is beginning to describe not merely a jewel but a light completing its appointed course.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:18 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her: and blessed is every one that *holdeth her fast*.
____

The King James reads __“happy is every one that *retaineth* her.”__
The Hebrew verb, _tamak_, can mean to retain, grasp, sustain, or hold fast; the MEAT line uses the last of those attested senses.
It is the action the bride's palms will perform when they hold the circuit.
It is also the verb of Moses' hands held steady — _emunah_, faithfulness — until the going down of the sun (Exodus 17:12).
The Tree of Life also keeps the moon's measure: it bears 12 fruits and yields its fruit __“every month”__ (Revelation 22:2).
Wisdom is held as the faithful witness holds its course.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:19, KJV]
____
The LORD by wisdom hath founded the earth; by understanding hath he *established* the sym:sym-heaven[heavens].
____

The poem now tells us where its picture belongs.
The Hebrew verb translated *established*, _konen_ (H3559), is echoed when David looks upon __“the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*”__ (Psalm 8:3).
The Wisdom priced above pearls is the Wisdom that built the sky: __“when he prepared the heavens, I was there”__ (Proverbs 8:27).

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:20 — MEAT rendering]
____
By his knowledge the depths were broken open, and the *skies* drop down the dew.
____

The King James reads __“the depths are *broken up*, and the *clouds* drop down the dew.”__
*Broken open* states the same verb in current English and recalls the fountains of the flood (Genesis 7:11).
The word translated *clouds*, _shachaqim_, can also name the fine skies; the MEAT rendering keeps that attested sense because it is the sky where the faithful witness is established (Psalm 89:37).
The poem ends with the heavens opened above and the measured waters below.
Moses joins the same deep and dew to __“the precious fruits brought forth by the sun”__ and __“the precious things put forth by the moon”__ (Deuteronomy 33:13-14).
Its Wisdom belongs to the ordered creation whose lights govern days, months, and appointed times.

[discrete]
=== Eight Verses, One Complete Picture

The count is striking: 8 verses, with the lunar thread unbroken in every one.
Verse 13 gives the merchant's *finding* of the heavenly pearl; verse 14, the *going-round* and gold's luster; verse 15, the *bright pearl*; verse 16, measured days, appointed time, direction, and shining; verse 17, the complete path; verse 18, the Tree of Life yielding every month and held fast; verse 19, the moon established in the heavens; and verse 20, the deep and dew joined to what the moon puts forth.
No verse drops the thread.
The poem has not merely compared Wisdom with a pearl; it has drawn one complete pearl across the whole sky.

__But wait, there's more.__

=== Proverbs 31, Line by Line

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:10 — MEAT rendering]
____
A woman of valour who can find? for her *price is far above pearls*.
____

The first poem opened with a man who finds Wisdom; this one opens by asking who can find the woman.
The Hebrew noun _chayil_ names strength or valour.
The same pearls that priced Wisdom now price the woman.
The thing the Merchant seeks has now been given a woman's form.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:11 — MEAT rendering]
____
The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, and gain shall *never diminish*.
____

The King James reads __“he shall have no need of *spoil*.”__
The noun rendered *gain*, _shalal_, can mean spoil or gain, while the verb behind *diminish*, _chaser_, can mean to lack, decrease, or fail.
The MEAT line keeps both alternate senses: her husband's gain does not diminish.
The faithful wife does not diminish her husband's gain; she sustains and reflects it.
In the heavenly portrait, her husband is the sun, and his gain is the light seen upon her.
Her whole visible face receives and faithfully returns his light.
The line points specifically to the full moon, not a waxing or waning one.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:12, KJV]
____
She doeth him good and not evil all the days of her life.
____

This is submission expressed as covenant faithfulness, not passivity.
Her life is ordered toward the good of her husband.
The moon likewise serves the office appointed by its head through every one of its days.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:13 — MEAT rendering]
____
She seeketh wool, and flax, and worketh willingly with her *palms*.
____

The King James renders the final word simply as __“*hands*.”__
The Hebrew noun is _kaph_, the palm, rather than the more general word for hand; Strong's traces it to _kaphaph_, __“to curve,”__ and uses the same noun for the hollow of a hand and the bowl of a dish.
The palm is the open, curved face of the hand — a vessel made to receive, hold, and cover.
Job places light in that same vessel: __“By two palms He hath covered the light”__ (Job 36:32, YLT).
Flax is the stalk-fiber that is spun and woven into linen.
The poem begins with wool and flax as raw material; by verse 22 she is clothed in white linen, and by verse 24 she makes fine linen.
The bride is already preparing the garments by which her deeds will be made visible.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:14 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is like the ships of *the one who travels round*; she bringeth her sym:sym-bread[bread] from afar.
____

The merchant-word returns.
The King James reads __“the *merchant's ships*.”__
The phrase *the one who travels round* renders _socher_, which can name a trader and belongs to the same Hebrew travel-and-trade family as _sachar_ in Proverbs 3:14.
Her sym:sym-bread[bread] is the Word: __“man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD”__ (Deuteronomy 8:3).
That Word is __“a lamp unto my feet, and a sym:sym-light[light] unto my path”__ (Psalm 119:105).
Psalm 19 gives speech to the heavens, sends their words to the end of the world, and then shows the sun running his circuit (Psalm 19:1-6).
The earthly woman brings bread home by merchant ship; in the heavenly hearing, the bride brings her Husband's Word from above and bears it through the night as light while she travels her appointed course.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:15 — MEAT rendering]
____
She riseth also while it is yet night, and giveth meat to her household, and *the appointed portion* to her maidens.
____

The light has now entered the night.
Of all the lights, the full moon rises as darkness falls and remains through the night.

The King James reads __“giveth *meat* to her household, and a *portion* to her maidens.”__
The meat-word itself, _teref_, is unchanged; Psalm 111:5 joins that same meat to remembrance of the covenant.
The word translated *portion*, _choq_, can also name an appointment, statute, or fixed time — the word-family of the ordinances of the moon and stars (Jeremiah 31:35).
Only *appointed* is an alternate lexical sense here; the covenant connection comes from the scriptural cross-reference, not from the noun itself.
The bride distributes the calendar's own food at the appointed time.

Jesus names that exact office:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:45, KJV]
____
Who then is a *faithful and* sym:sym-wise[wise] *servant*, whom his lord hath made ruler over his *household*, to give them sym:sym-meat[meat] *in due season*?
____

Jesus calls the servant both *faithful* and sym:sym-wise[wise].
Proverbs has attached sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] to the woman, while Psalm 89 attaches the title sym:sym-faithful-witness[Faithful Witness] to the sym:sym-moon[moon].
The woman performs the deed of that faithful and wise servant: under her lord, she gives the household meat at the appointed time.
Her food and her light are both received; she gives neither her own word nor her own calendar, but faithfully delivers what her head appointed.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:16, KJV — “palms” restored]
____
She considereth a sym:sym-field[field], and *buyeth* it: with the fruit of her *palms* she planteth a vineyard.
____

The sym:sym-field[field] is the world, and the vineyard is the house of Israel (Matthew 13:38; Isaiah 5:7).
The sym:sym-moon[moon's] meaning follows the covenant relationship: bride → Messiah → Father.
Under Messiah, the moon represents the sym:sym-marriage[bride] receiving her Bridegroom's light; under the Father, it represents Messiah receiving and bearing His Father's light.
The woman remains the bride in Proverbs' plain portrait, but the lunar figure can carry both relationships.
As the bride, she is the sym:sym-pearl[pearl] Messiah purchased; in the moon's other relational role, the field-buyer is Messiah, purchasing the field for the treasure hidden within it (Matthew 13:44).
The parable of the gold hidden in the field has returned through the moon's other office, and the vineyard planted within that field identifies the treasure as Israel.
Proverbs 31 now holds both adjacent parables: verse 10 supplies the sym:sym-pearl[pearl] and its price; verses 14 and 18 supply the merchant and good merchandise; verse 16 supplies the sym:sym-field[field] and its purchase.
Solomon has bound the pearl and the field into one portrait.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:17, KJV]
____
She girdeth her loins with strength, and strengtheneth her arms.
____

Daniel sees the same pattern in a body filled with light:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 10:5-6, KJV]
____
Then I lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and behold a certain man clothed in *linen*, whose *loins were girded with fine gold* of Uphaz:
His body also was like the beryl, and his *face as the appearance of lightning*, and his *eyes as lamps of fire*, and his *arms* and his feet like in colour to *polished brass*, and the voice of his words like the voice of a multitude.
____

The portraits align line by line.
Her flax becomes white linen; Daniel's man is clothed in linen.
She girds her loins with strength; his loins are girded with fine gold.
She strengthens her arms; his arms shine like polished brass.
In the next line her sym:sym-lamp[lamp] does not go out in the night; his face flashes like lightning and his eyes are lamps of fire.
Later she opens her mouth with sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom]; the voice of his words sounds like a multitude.

The figure then touches Daniel and strengthens him: __“Be strong, yea, be strong … thou hast strengthened me”__ (Daniel 10:18-19).
He does not merely display strength; he gives it.
The woman likewise strengthens what she plants, and her lamp bears light into the night.
The sequence reveals the moon's Messiah-role in the woman's portrait: what comes from the Head is faithfully carried and given onward.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:18 — MEAT rendering]
____
She *tasteth that it is good*, her merchandise: her sym:sym-lamp[lamp] goeth not out *in the night*.
____

The King James reads __“she *perceiveth* that her merchandise is good”__ and __“her candle goeth not out *by night*.”__
The verb translated *perceiveth* can also mean *tasteth*: she tastes that her merchandise is good.
The same line can be read *in the night* rather than *by night*.
The MEAT rendering keeps those 2 direct senses without changing the sentence's meaning.
The same __“it is good”__ was spoken over the light (Genesis 1:4), and the same merchandise was introduced in Proverbs 3:14.
Now its lamp burns in the night.
Wisdom, bride, merchant, good, and light have arrived in one sentence.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:19 — MEAT rendering]
____
She layeth her hands to the spindle, and her *palms hold fast the circuit*.
____

The King James reads __“her hands hold the *distaff*.”__
The plain housewife reading is valid: _pelekh_ names a spindle or whorl.footnote:fn31whorl[A spindle whorl is a small weighted disk fitted around a spindle. It keeps the spindle turning evenly while fiber is twisted into thread.]
The word also comes from roundness and can carry the sense of a circuit; the MEAT line gives that second hearing while preserving the spinning image.
The housewife holds the spinning tool; the heavenly bride holds the round.
The palm supplies the open, curved face; the circuit supplies the circle.
Together they form the full moon's open face, receiving light and holding its appointed course.
The verb *hold fast*, _tamak_, is the one Wisdom used in Proverbs 3:18.
The two poems touch palm to circuit and hold it in place.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:20 — MEAT rendering]
____
She spreadeth out her palm to the poor; yea, she reacheth forth her hands to the needy.
____

The open face now turns outward and gives what it received.
The King James again prints __“*hand*,”__ while the Hebrew again uses _kaph_, the curved palm.
That is the King's signature: __“He shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also”__ (Psalm 72:12).
The same psalm sets this mercy __“with the sun and *before the face of the moon*”__ through all generations (Psalm 72:5).

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:21 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is not *worried by the whiteness* for her household: for all her *lineage wraps in years*.
____

The King James follows the pointed reading: __“not afraid of the *snow*… clothed with *scarlet*.”__
That reading already contributes to the lunar portrait: snow is white and scarlet is red, the 2 colors of the ordinary full moon and the blood moon.
It also prepares the white linen and royal maroon of the next verse.
But that is 1 reading; the unpointed letters carry additional hearings.
The word rendered *worried*, _yare_, can describe anxious concern.
The word for *house*, _bayit_, can also name a lineage, and the verb rendered *wraps*, _labash_, properly means to wrap around and, by implication, to clothe.
The consonants under __“scarlet”__ can be heard as _shanim_, years, or _shnayim_, double.footnote:fn31clothing[The Septuagint, Vulgate, and Smith's Literal Translation support doubled garments; the Masoretic pointing reads scarlet. The same consonants permit _shanim_, years. The several readings are kept visible rather than collapsed.]
The years wrap around her household: successive months circle through generations, gathering them into the completed count the lights were made to measure.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:22 — MEAT rendering]
____
She maketh herself coverings; her clothing is *white linen* and *royal maroon*.
____

The King James reads __“her clothing is *silk and purple*.”__
The word translated *white linen*, _shesh_, names bleached linen rather than silk.
The word rendered *royal maroon*, _argaman_, names the costly murex-dyed cloth of royalty and the sanctuary, whose historical range includes wine, burgundy, and dark maroon rather than only modern lilac.
This is semantic drift hiding a meaning that the symbolic language recovers.
Read alone, *purple* suggests the modern violet or lilac range; read beside the preceding snow and scarlet, the surrounding witnesses confirm the white-and-red pair.
White linen and royal maroon preserve that same pair in the bride's clothing.
The full moon's ordinary face is white; under eclipse the same complete face becomes blood-dark and royal maroon.
The 2 colors belong to one garment and one witness.

=== The Sapphire Form

Scripture places sym:sym-sapphire[sapphire] inside the same wisdom-and-pearl chain.
Job names __“the precious onyx, or the sapphire”__; two verses later, the same price-list declares, __“the price of wisdom is above *pearls*”__ (Job 28:16, 18).
Lamentations returns to the pearl and adds its colors:

[quote.scripture, Lamentations 4:7-8, KJV — “pearls” restored]
____
Her *Nazarites* were purer than *snow*, they were *whiter* than *milk*, they were more *ruddy*footnote:fn31ruddy[“Ruddy” means red or reddish in color.] in body than sym:sym-pearl[pearls]footnote:fn31peninim[], their polishing was of sym:sym-sapphire[sapphire]:
Their visage is *blacker* than a *coal*; they are not known in the streets: their skin cleaveth to their bones; it is withered, it is become like a stick.
____

The subject is set apart before a color appears: her Nazarites, those consecrated, set apart, and appointed to God's service.
Then come two whites — snow and milk — a red body priced against pearls, and a sapphire form; the next verse turns the portrait black.
A blood moon is a full moon under eclipse: the same complete light that appears white can turn red beneath the shadow, with blackness as its counter-image.
The passage binds consecration, double whiteness, redness, darkness, pearl, and sapphire in one figure.

Scripture defines sapphire's office elsewhere.
Moses sees beneath God's feet __“a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in its *brightness*”__footnote:fn31clearness[The KJV reads __“in his clearness.”__ Here __clearness__ means visible brightness or luster, not ease of understanding. The Hebrew _tohar_ (H2892) ranges from purity to brightness; Psalm 89:44 renders it __“glory,”__ while the Septuagint renders this phrase __“in its purity.”__ The surrounding sapphire, sky, and glass imagery supplies the reflective surface.] (Exodus 24:10).
Ezekiel twice sees the stone as the appearance of __“the likeness of a throne”__ (Ezekiel 1:26; 10:1).
Psalm 89 sets that throne as the sun and establishes it as the moon, while the same letters, _kisse_ and _keseh_, join throne to full moon.
Scripture has given the lunar throne a mineral likeness: sapphire is the full moon in its enthroned form.

Scripture itself unfolds this *brightness*.
The Hebrew word behind it grows from *clean* and appears only 4 times: twice for the days of __“purifying”__ (Leviticus 12:4, 6), once for this bright sapphire pavement — and once more as __“glory”__ in the psalm of the throne: __“Thou hast made his *glory* to cease, and cast his throne down to the ground”__ (Psalm 89:44).
Purifying, brightness, glory — one word spans them all, and it names a shining that is *worn*.
The king's glory can be made to *cease* because a reflector's light was never its own.

Job hangs the mirror in the same sky.
__“Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a *molten looking glass*?”__ (Job 37:18) — the very heaven where the psalm's faithful witness stands (Psalm 89:37).
Three verses later Job tells us what happens to that mirror when the wind passes: __“men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and *cleanseth* them”__ (Job 37:21).
The sky is a cast mirror; to cleanse it is to clear it until the light comes through; and the pavement under the God of Israel is __“the body of heaven”__ in exactly that brightness — a pure surface returning light.
John keeps the geometry in the throne room: __“before the throne”__ stands __“a sea of glass”__ — *before* in the sense of *in the face of* (Revelation 4:6).
What faces a light returns it, and the sym:sym-sea[Sea & Waters] study in Part Six will meet that mirror-sea and the temple's copy of it.

The law's mirror and the sky's are one surface.
James sets the hearer before __“a mirror”__ that shows a man __“his natural face,”__ and names the glass: __“the perfect law of sym:sym-liberty[liberty]”__ (James 1:23-25) — the Word shows a man himself.
Paul turns the same glass toward its Owner: __“we all, with open face *beholding as in a glass* the glory of the Lord, are *changed into the same image* from glory to glory”__ (2 Corinthians 3:18).
One mirror — the Word — gives back both faces, and the looking transforms the one into the other.
The moon is that mirror hung in the sky: the faithful witness bearing light not his own, as __“the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, and we beheld his *glory*”__ (John 1:14).

Marriage completes the portrait.
The Song places sapphire within the husband-and-wife figure: the Beloved is white and ruddy, bright ivory overlaid with sapphires; the one woman is __“fair as the moon, clear as the sun”__ (Song of Solomon 5:10, 14; 6:9-10).
Isaiah calls the woman's Maker her Husband, then promises, __“I will lay thy foundations with sapphires”__ (Isaiah 54:5, 11).
Revelation calls the holy city __“the bride, the Lamb's wife,”__ sets sapphire as its second foundation, and makes every gate one pearl (Revelation 21:9, 19, 21).

The pearl names the full moon by visible form — one, white, round, complete.
Sapphire names the same witness by throne and marriage office.
Revelation seals the union in one Bride-city: sapphire in the foundations, one pearl at every gate.

=== The One Pearl of the Night Sky
Mark the pearl's properties as Scripture handles the figure.
It is *one*: the merchant found __“one pearl of great price”__ and stopped collecting, for the kingdom is not a collection but one bride, bought whole.
It is *round and complete*: the roundness sits inside Strong's definition itself.
It is *white* — a bearer of soft, borrowed light out of darkness — and it is born of the *waters*.
Then Revelation prices the city's entrances in it:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:21]
____
And the twelve sym:sym-door[gates] were twelve *pearls*; *every several gate was of one pearl*…
____

The Greek doubles the point — _each one_ of the gates, _of one_ pearl — and the Hebrew Revelation doubles it the same way: every *single* gate, of *one* pearl.
Not gates studded with pearls: each gate *is* one pearl — one, complete, whole.
So the pearl, wherever Scripture spends it, is the one complete thing of great price — and it is a *gate*.
Now ask the merchant's question: has the King hung such a thing anywhere in plain sight — one, round, white, complete, and standing at a door?

Look up.
Of all the moon's phases and all the lights of heaven, exactly one is white, round, and complete: the night sky owns precisely one pearl.
And Scripture had already assigned that light its office: the moon appointed for the _moedim_, the appointed times, from the fourth day (Psalm 104:19; Genesis 1:14).

The pairing was set at the pattern's founding: Joseph's dream read the moon as the *mother* (Genesis 37:9-10), and the sun comes out of his chamber __“as a *bridegroom*”__ (Psalm 19:5) — a bridegroom's answering light is his bride, the counterpart the link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter already seated facing him, moon toward her Bridegroom as the Son is moon toward His Father.

=== The Letters Confirm It

If the pearl has led truly, the letters should be waiting at the same spot — and they are.
Scripture keeps a __“fulness”__ overhead.
The Hebrew is _keseh_ — Strong's: __“properly, *fulness* or the *full moon*”__ — and Psalm 81 places it on the feast day:footnote:fn31keseh[The King James prints __“in the time appointed.”__ The NKJV, ESV, NASB, JPS, and Smith's Literal Translation read *full moon*. The Septuagint calls it the conspicuous or well-marked day of the feast. The Hebrew contains no __“and”__ separating new moon from full moon; translations that insert one divide what the sentence presents together.]

[quote.scripture, Psalm 81:3, NKJV]
____
Blow the trumpet at the time of the New Moon, at the *full* sym:sym-moon[moon], on our solemn feast day.
____

Of the moon's phases exactly one is full and conspicuous, while the dark conjunction is the one day the sky shows no sign at all.
The Hebrew places the new-moon trumpet, the full moon, and the festival sacrifice on one day: the full moon is the head of the month and feast-day of Numbers 10:10 and 28:11.

Solomon uses the same word for the day the traveling husband returns: __“he will come home at the *full moon*”__ (Proverbs 7:20) — _keseh_ again — and there it is spelled כסא, letter for letter the spelling of _kisse’_, __“throne.”__
The last chapter stood on that identity from the enemy's side; here is the LORD's side of it. Both words grow from one root, _kasah_, to cover, to fill — the throne is the covered seat, the full moon the filled light — and Job holds the two senses up in a single verse: __“He holdeth back the face of *his throne*, and spreadeth *his cloud* upon it”__ (Job 26:9), the veiled face of His full sym:sym-moon[moon].

If it is asked which way the covering runs — whether the throne-word is covered in darkness or covered in light — Scripture describes the throne itself.
Daniel saw the throne-room itself, and every detail belongs to this chapter:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9, NKJV]
____
I watched till *thrones* were *put in place*, and the *Ancient of Days* was seated; His garment was *white as snow*, and the hair of His head like *pure wool*. His throne was a *fiery flame*, its wheels a burning fire.
____

The thrones are *put in place* like lights in their stations.
And the title under __“Ancient of days”__ is built on a root of moving onward, going before — read once, He is the Ancient of Days; read again, He is the *Foremost of Days*: the One at the head of the days, seated where the days begin.
His garment white as snow, His hair like the pure wool, His throne flame, His wheels burning fire — and the plural on __“wheels”__ is the ending this book met on Daniel's sevens: an intensive, not a count — *the wheel complete*, the perfect circle of fire.

Whiteness enthroned on light; brightness round about it, __“as the appearance of the sym:sym-bow[bow] that is in the cloud in the day of rain”__ (Ezekiel 1:26-28); and its Occupant __“coverest thyself with *light* as with a garment”__ (Psalm 104:2).
If the throne is the _keseh_, Daniel's vision draws what the letters spell: the full moons set in their stations, and the Head of Days taking His seat — white, complete, covered in light — where the month begins.

The psalm that gave this book the sym:sym-faithful-witness[faithful witness] link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[already sets the throne] as the sym:sym-sun[sun] and as the sym:sym-moon[moon] — Psalm 89:36-37 — plain simile, not wordplay, and the throne is a throne of light.footnote:fn26thronepsalm[Derived in the link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] chapter.]
Where darkness stands near it, it stands *outside*, as a veil: __“clouds and darkness are *round about* him”__ (Psalm 97:2) — round about, not within — the cloud of Job 26:9 spread *upon* the face, holding the light back.
The _keseh_ is covered in light; only the counterfeit is covered in dark.

=== The Gate That Opens

The sky is built like a sanctuary — the tabernacle Psalm 19 pitched for the sun — and its front, like the tabernacle's, faces the sunrise, the sym:sym-east[east].
Now hear Ezekiel on the gates of the house:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 46:1]
____
Thus saith the Lord GOD; The sym:sym-door[gate] of the inner court that looketh toward the sym:sym-east[east] shall be *shut the six working days*; but on the *sabbath* it shall be opened, and in the day of the *new* sym:sym-moon[moon] it shall be opened.

Three kinds of days — working days, sabbaths, new-moon days (Amos knew the third kind too: __“When will the *new moon* be gone, that we may sell corn?”__ Amos 8:5) — and on the day that begins the month, the east gate stands open.
A gate is opened because something is meant to come through it.
And exactly one phase enters by the east gate at the fall of night: the *full* moon enters in the east at the hour the portrait already fixed — through the front door of the sky's tabernacle, the faithful witness at maximum.

The sliver, by contrast, is hunted low in the *west*, at sunset, beside the sun — and the sheepfold has a rule about entrances:

The true light enters at the front sym:sym-door[gate]; the counterfeit climbs in from the dark side — which is precisely the address his heart chose in the last chapter.

=== One Pearl, One Gate

Now set the pieces together.
The pearl is the full moon; the city's pearl-gates are His appointed times — for the _moedim_ are, as the last chapter read the word, *meetings*: the fixed days on which the King receives.
Twelve gates; twelve months of full moons; the sym:sym-stars[stars] of God gathered into the twelve constellations, the __“Mazzaroth”__ brought forth __“in his season”__ (Job 38:32), through which the full moon makes its yearly round.
And there Solomon's woman collects what her portrait promised — praised in the gates her fulness passes through, her husband at home among the elders.

The Merchant who sold all He had was not buying His own entrance into the kingdom.
He was purchasing the Bride-city whose people are His treasure and whose gates are pearls — the bride gathered to meet her King at His appointed times.
Isaiah had already welded the paired parables into one sentence: __“sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom] and knowledge shall be the stability of thy *times*… the sym:sym-fear[fear] of the LORD is his *treasure*”__ (Isaiah 33:6).
The treasure and the bride; the pearl and the gate; the King and the appointed meeting.

__“It is the glory of God to *conceal* a thing: but the honour of kings is to *search out* a matter”__ (Proverbs 25:2) — He hid the bride's portrait where the Merchant who would give everything for her was already looking.

=== Pearls Before Swine

Jesus spent the pearl-word one other time, and it is a warning:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6]
____
Give not that which is *holy* unto the sym:sym-dog[dogs], neither cast ye your *pearls* before *swine*, lest they *trample them under their feet*, and turn again and rend you.
____

The dogs and the swine are one family — unclean sym:sym-beast[beasts], the nations outside the covenant table.
And what swine do to pearls is exactly what Scripture says the nations do to holy things: they tread them underfoot — __“it is given unto the *Gentiles*: and the holy city shall they *tread under foot*”__ (Revelation 11:2); the sanctuary and the host __“*trodden under foot*”__ (Daniel 8:13); __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles, until the *times* of the Gentiles be fulfilled”__ (Luke 21:24).

Mark the last word of that sentence: what the trampling runs until is a matter of *times*.
If the pearl is the appointed time of meeting, the warning reads whole: hand the holy times to the nations and they will not keep them — they will trample them, and turn and rend the hand that gave them.
Daniel named the plan — to change the times and the laws; this verse is the Lord's advance notice of how the theft would look on the ground — the pearl underfoot.

=== The Two Moons

Read this chapter and the last as the pair they are.
The enemy raises the moon covered in *darkness*; the LORD's sign is the same seat covered in *light* — the Father's whole face borne through the dark.
One moon reflects everything; the other reflects nothing, for its source produces only dark.
One enters by the door; the other climbs up some other way.

The sky itself stages the ambition, for the two moons stand in two different places: the full moon, standing always opposite, can never come between; only the moon covered in darkness can stand *over* the sun — slide before his face and put out the day at noon — and blot the sym:sym-stars[stars] from the same sky.
That is the plan of the last chapter drawn in one picture — __“Woe unto them… that put *darkness for light*, and *light for darkness*”__ (Isaiah 5:20).

The sym:sym-stars[stars] — the righteous, who keep time — follow one moon or the other.
That is why the parable's Merchant sells *all*: the pearl is not an ornament a sinner purchases from the kingdom; it is the bride the Son redeems, pictured at the gate where she meets her King.
Only the Son could pay that price, and Scripture says He paid it gladly.

Grade this chapter the way the Parables of the Kingdom chapter graded everything, for the method matters more than any single finding.
Every link stands in the text: wisdom and the bride priced above _peninim_; the city's gates each of *one pearl*; the moon set over the _moedim_ from the fourth day; _keseh_, the full moon, sharing the letters of __“throne”__; the east gate opened on the new-moon day; and the two poems painting the same light from beginning to end.
The chain that joins the links is a reading — this book's reading, offered to be tested, not swallowed.

The reckoning that follows from it — where the month truly begins, how the sabbaths fall, what history did to the calendar and what the sky and the records of the nations still testify — is a book of its own.footnote:fn26ttt[The complete case is made in _Time Tested Tradition: The Renewed Biblical Calendar_.]
This chapter needs only the symbol.
The kingdom has a gate; the gate is one complete pearl; and the Bridegroom gave all to purchase the bride who meets Him there.

The appointed times the pearl governs are counted in sevens — and the word Scripture counts them with is the next chapter.
