## Current

```adoc
When that testimony is put on the stand, weigh what it actually brings.
Its witnesses are dated, nearly to a man, *after the fall of the temple*: the Mishnah was compiled around AD 200, the Talmuds centuries later still, and the fixed, calculated calendar in use today is attributed to Hillel II in the fourth century — three hundred years after the altar went cold.
From the years when the temple stood, the defenders of the inherited calendar bring almost nothing; and the evidence that does survive from those years — the eclipse-dated regnal records, Josephus, Philo, the Scriptures themselves — reads against them.
So the argument runs backwards.
Where the record is silent, silence is pressed into service as proof: _no one recorded a change, therefore nothing changed_ — as though a hostile majority ever preserves the records of the minority it silenced; the writings of the losing side are not archived by the winners, they are neglected and destroyed.
That same argument from silence once dismissed the house of David as legend — until the Tel Dan stone surfaced in 1993 with __“House of David”__ cut into it; and Pontius Pilate — until his stone came out of the ground at Caesarea in 1961.
Where the record speaks, it is dismissed for contradicting the conclusion.
And when the accounting is finally demanded — a date, a source, a stone — the answer given is __“tradition,”__ said twice, and louder the second time.
```
