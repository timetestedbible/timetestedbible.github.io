## Summary

Replace “furniture/architecture” language in the Rich Man and Lazarus discussion.

## Current

```adoc
The rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16) is the fourth parable in a chain, told in the furniture of the day. Its spoken point is not the architecture of the afterlife but the sufficiency of the Scriptures: __“They have *Moses and the prophets*; let them hear them… If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead”__ (Luke 16:29, 31).
A parable's furniture cannot overrule the plain testimony of the very Moses and prophets it commands us to hear — the dead know not anything, and David is not ascended.
```

## Proposed

```adoc
The rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16) is the fourth parable in a chain, told with familiar images from its day. Its spoken point is not to describe the afterlife but to declare the sufficiency of the Scriptures: __“They have *Moses and the prophets*; let them hear them… If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead”__ (Luke 16:29, 31).
A parable's imagery cannot overrule the plain testimony of the very Moses and prophets it commands us to hear — the dead know not anything, and David is not ascended.
```

## Rationale

The sentence is making a hermeneutical point, not describing objects in a room or a building plan. “Familiar images” and “imagery” keep the point clear and lower parse load.
