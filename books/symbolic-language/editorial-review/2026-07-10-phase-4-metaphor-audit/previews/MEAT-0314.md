## Summary

Replace “one prop” with “one enacted sign” in the Moses/Babylon contest.

## Current

```adoc
The contest opened, both times, with one prop — a similitude acted out before a word was argued.
```

## Proposed

```adoc
The contest opened, both times, with one enacted sign — a similitude acted out before a word was argued.
```

## Rationale

“Prop” sounds theatrical. “Enacted sign” fits the chapter’s own similitude language and is more precise.

## Context

```text
    43: === The Rod, the Serpent, and the Rod Again
    44: 
>   45: The contest opened, both times, with one prop — a similitude acted out before a word was argued.
    46: __“What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod. And he said, Cast it on the ground. And he cast it on the ground, and it became a *serpent*”__ (Exodus 4:2-3).
    47: The rod is no neutral stick: the Hebrew _matteh_ means *staff* and *tribe* in one word — when the princes bring twelve rods, they are the twelve tribes by the language itself (Numbers 17:2) — and Aaron's rod is the rod __“for the house of *Levi*”__ (Numbers 17:8): the priesthood in his hand.
```
