# Phase 2: Chapter References to Textual and Translation Notes

Phase 1 created the canonical apparatus in `49x-textual-and-translation-notes.adoc`.
Phase 2 should edit chapters only after each claim has been assigned one of the routes below.

## Routing rule

### 0. Use an existing translation first

If the NKJV or another familiar, permitted translation already preserves the word or relationship the chapter needs, cite that translation and do not create a MEAT rendering or translation note. Keep an apparatus reference only when the chapter makes an additional textual, grammatical, or symbolic claim beyond that translation.

### A. Thesis-bearing reading

Keep the chapter self-contained in this order:

1. Present the reading.
2. Unfold its implications.
3. Give the focused defense required to judge the chapter's thesis.
4. Point to the canonical note for the technical record.

The apparatus supplements the chapter; it does not replace the proof.

### B. Supporting reading

Keep the meaning the reader needs in one sentence, remove duplicated lexical or manuscript detail, and point to the canonical note.

### C. Method example

Keep enough evidence to demonstrate the book's method, then point to the note. Do not turn a method chapter into a lexicon.

### D. Secondary textual witness

Name the witness in the chapter so the wording cannot be mistaken for the Greek or Masoretic base text. Put the manuscript detail and limits in the canonical note.

## Reference behavior

Before the chapter pass, add one shared inline macro for note references. Its source form should be compact, for example `tn:genesis-1-16[Genesis 1:16]`.

- Web output: link to `/books/symbolic-language/textual-and-translation-notes/#ttn-genesis-1-16` so the URL remains shareable.
- Combined PDF output: use the internal `ttn-genesis-1-16` anchor and print the destination page.
- Audio output: omit navigation language and retain only wording necessary to understand the claim.

Do not hand-code three different link forms in every chapter.

## Completion checks for every chapter

- The reading appears before objections and technical defense.
- Every quotation identifies its English version, MEAT rendering, or named textual witness wherever the source is not otherwise clear.
- A second hearing is never described as the only grammatical translation.
- A symbolic paraphrase is never placed inside a Scripture quotation without a label.
- Repeated technical proof has one canonical home.
- Print, web, and audio twins remain synchronized where the chapter has an audio source.
- The book builds, the note link resolves, and the source verse remains in the Scripture Index.
