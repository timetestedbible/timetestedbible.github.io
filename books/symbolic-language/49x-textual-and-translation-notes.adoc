[[ttn-genesis-1-16]]
=== Genesis 1:16

[quote.scripture, Genesis 1:16, KJV]
____
The greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: *he made the stars also*.
____

[quote.scripture, Genesis 1:16, MEAT rendering]
____
The greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night *with the stars*.
____

The Hebrew contains neither a second verb meaning __“he made”__ nor a word meaning __“also.”__
Its sequence is simply, __“the lesser light for the rule of the night, and the stars.”__
Psalm 136:9 supplies the relationship in plain words: __“the moon and stars to rule by night.”__
The word *with* is supplied from that scriptural parallel; no separate Hebrew word for *with* appears in Genesis 1:16.

[[ttn-isaiah-22-3]]
=== Isaiah 22:3

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 22:3, KJV]
____
All thy rulers are fled together, they are *bound by the archers*.
____

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 22:3, literal Hebrew]
____
All your rulers fled together; they were *bound by the bow*.
____

_Qesheth_ (H7198) is the ordinary word for a bow; *archers* turns the weapon into a collective name for those carrying it.
The verb _asar_ (H631) means to bind or yoke and belongs to the same word-family as a binding obligation or vow.

The preposition can describe being bound by, because of, or away from the bow, so the verse remains difficult.
Its bow-and-binding language permits a covenant image, but this verse alone does not prove that every bow symbolizes covenant.

[[ttn-isaiah-24-23]]
=== Isaiah 24:23

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 24:23, KJV]
____
When the LORD of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and *before his ancients gloriously*.
____

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 24:23, MEAT rendering]
____
When the LORD of hosts shall reign in mount Zion, and in Jerusalem, and *before his elders, glory*.
____

_Zaqen_ (H2205) is the ordinary word for an elder, not merely an ancient person, and the Septuagint likewise uses _presbyteroi_, elders.
With that wording restored, _kavod_ names the glory displayed before them instead of becoming the adverb *gloriously*.

[[ttn-isaiah-66-17]]
=== Isaiah 66:17

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 66:17, KJV]
____
They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens *behind one tree in the midst*.
____

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 66:17, literal Hebrew]
____
They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens, *after one in the midst*.
____

*Tree* is supplied by the KJV translators and printed in italics for that reason.
The feminine *one* may point back to a feminine noun understood from context, and translations have supplied different nouns to complete the thought.

Removing *tree* reveals the ambiguity; it does not by itself prove __“among the nations, following one.”__
That symbolic paraphrase also depends on the established garden-and-tree definitions and on the gathering of nations in verses 18-19.

[[ttn-jeremiah-31-31]]
=== Jeremiah 31:31

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 31:31, KJV]
____
I will make a *new covenant* with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah.
____

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 31:31, contextual reading]
____
I will *renew the covenant* with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah.
____

The adjective _chadash_ (H2319) means new or fresh.
Its related verb, written from the same root, means to renew or repair: the kingdom is renewed (1 Samuel 11:14), the altar repaired (2 Chronicles 15:8), and a right spirit renewed (Psalm 51:10).
Jeremiah also names the same covenant Head and the same 2 houses, with the law written inwardly rather than omitted (Jeremiah 31:31-33).

Even so, the word in verse 31 is grammatically the adjective *new*, not the verb *renew*.
*Renewed covenant* explains this covenant's continuity and newness; the shared root alone does not compel that translation.

[[ttn-daniel-7-9]]
=== Daniel 7:9

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9, NKJV]
____
I watched till *thrones were put in place*, and the *Ancient of Days* was seated.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9, symbolic second hearing]
____
I watched till thrones were put in place, and the *Foremost of Days* was seated.
____

The Aramaic verb _remah_ (H7412) can describe placing or setting, and the court that follows is seated for judgment; the thrones are installed, not overthrown.

*Foremost of Days* is a second hearing within the familiar title *Ancient of Days*.
The underlying Aramaic word concerns age or advancement, allowing the symbolic title to picture the One at the head of the days.
It unfolds the established title rather than replacing it.

[[ttn-daniel-9-24]]
=== Daniel 9:24

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:24, pointed Hebrew]
____
*Seventy weeks* are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:24, consonantal second hearing]
____
*Seventy seventy* are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city.
____

The second hearing is tested as 70 complete seven-of-seven cycles—70 jubilees.

The Masoretic vowel points distinguish _shivim_, *70* (H7657), from _shavuim_, *sevens* or *weeks* (H7620), but the 2 words are written with the same consonants.
Smith's Literal Translation independently preserves __“Seventy seventy.”__
Leviticus 23:15-16 and 25:8-10 then supply matching seven-of-seven counts: 7 complete sevens lead to Pentecost at the scale of days and to the jubilee at the scale of years.

The matching consonants make the second hearing possible; they do not prove 70 jubilees by themselves.
The familiar grammar still supports __“70 sevens,”__ conventionally understood as 490 years.
The larger reading must stand on its comparison with Jeremiah, the Torah's nested counts, and the chronology—not on the shared spelling alone.

[[ttn-daniel-9-25]]
=== Daniel 9:25

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:25, KJV]
____
Unto the Messiah the Prince shall be *seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks*: the *street shall be built again, and the wall*, even in troublous times.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:25, proposed second reading]
____
Until Messiah the Prince shall be *seven seventies—490 years—then Jubilee 60, and another 490*: *they will come back and build again, with the plaza and the moat*, and in tribulation the times end.
____

==== Plaza, moat, and return

_Rechov_ (H7339) is an open or broad place, plaza, or street.
_Charuts_ (H2742) can denote a trench or moat; the ASV and JPS already use *moat*.
_Tashuv_ is a finite form of _shuv_, to return.
The traditional syntax folds it into __“shall be built again,”__ while the proposed reading lets the verb speak independently: __“they will return, and it will be built.”__

==== The number sequence

The consonantal text contains the words for 7, sevens, 60, and 2 or second.
_Shenayim_ (H8147) can carry *twofold*, *second*, or *another* as well as the number 2.
The second reading hears this sequence through the jubilee framework introduced in verse 24 and applies *another* to a second 490-year count.

The customary __“7 sevens and 62 sevens”__ remains the most straightforward grammatical reading.
This sequence depends on reassigning the number words within the jubilee framework and identifying 2 rebuilding decrees in history.
That makes it a proposed chronological reading of the consonants, not a simple lexical clarification like *moat*.

[[ttn-daniel-9-26]]
=== Daniel 9:26

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:26, KJV]
____
Messiah shall be *cut off, but not for himself*: and *the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary*.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:26, proposed second reading]
____
Messiah shall *cut a covenant and vanish*; to himself the *city—the watchful—and the holy, and he will sanctify them*; and *he shall destroy the people of the coming ruler*.
____

Several pieces of that reading are direct lexical observations; the reconstructed sentence as a whole is not the conventional Hebrew parsing.

==== Cut

_Karath_ (H3772) is the standard verb used when a covenant is *cut*.
The pointed form here is passive, __“shall be cut off.”__
Without the points, the same consonants can be heard actively as __“shall cut.”__
The object *a covenant* is drawn from verse 27; no written noun meaning *covenant* follows the verb in verse 26 itself.

==== Vanish

_Ayin_ (H369) expresses absence: nothing, not, or there is not.
The clause _ve-ein lo_ is conventionally __“and he has nothing”__ or __“but not for himself.”__
Genesis 5:24 uses the same absence idiom of Enoch—__“he was not; for God took him”__—which opens the way for *he vanishes*.

==== City, holy place, and destroyer

_Ir_, city, belongs to a word-family associated with wakefulness, allowing the symbolic gloss *the watchful*.
_Qodesh_ can name the holy place or what is holy, and the unpointed sequence can be heard alongside a verbal form concerning sanctification.
The verb for destroying is singular.

In the ordinary syntax, that singular verb agrees with the collective singular subject __“the people of the coming prince.”__
The proposed reading instead rearranges the objects and keeps Messiah as the subject through the clause.
Singular agreement permits the question, but it does not demand the reconstruction; the whole alternative syntax must be judged as a proposal.

[[ttn-daniel-9-27]]
=== Daniel 9:27

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:27, KJV]
____
For the *overspreading of abominations he shall make it desolate*.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:27, literal Hebrew]
____
Upon the *wing of abominations, one making desolate*.
____

The noun _kanaph_ (H3671) is rendered elsewhere as *wing*, *skirt*, *border*, *corner*, or *extremity*.
*Overspreading* explains what a covering wing may do, but it removes the noun that connects this clause with the Bible's wing imagery.
The Hebrew remains compact enough to support several arrangements, and translations disagree over what stands on the wing and what makes desolate.
Restoring *wing* is the lexical point; identifying the abomination and resolving the whole clause still require interpretation.

[[ttn-daniel-12-11-12]]
=== Daniel 12:11-12

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:11, KJV]
____
From the time that the daily sacrifice *shall be taken away*, and the abomination that maketh desolate *set up*.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:11, MEAT rendering]
____
From the time that the daily *was taken away*, *going toward the setting up* of the abomination that *becomes desolate*.
____

_Husar_ is a completed passive form: *was removed* or *had been removed*.
_Ve-latet_ includes the directional preposition _le_, naturally giving __“to the setting up of.”__
Together they support a count running from a completed removal toward a later installation.

The final participle, _shomem_, is normally understood causatively as __“causing desolation.”__
The second reading instead explores the result __“becoming desolate,”__ making the abomination itself destined for destruction.
The completed removal and motion toward the setting up are the stronger grammatical observations; the reversal of the participle is the more debatable step.

[[ttn-jonah-1-17]]
=== Jonah 1:17 and Matthew 12:40

[quote.scripture, Jonah 1:17; Matthew 12:40, NKJV]
____
The LORD had prepared a *great fish* to swallow Jonah … Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the *great fish*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 12:40, Greek lexical range]
____
Jonah was three days and three nights in the belly of the *great sea creature* or *sea-monster*.
____

Jonah's Hebrew is _dag gadol_, *great fish*.
Matthew's Greek _ketos_ (G2785) has the wider sense of a great sea creature or sea-monster, while the Shem Tov Hebrew Matthew repeats Jonah's fish-word.

*Sea-monster* describes the Greek word's range; it does not identify a modern zoological species.
The creature's symbolic identification as a ruler and kingdom must come from the wider pattern, not from an argument over animal classification.

[[ttn-jonah-2-5]]
=== Jonah 2:5

[quote.scripture, Jonah 2:5, KJV]
____
The *weeds* were wrapped about my head.
____

[quote.scripture, Jonah 2:5, MEAT rendering]
____
The *reeds* were wrapped about my head.
____

The Hebrew noun _suph_ (H5488) more specifically names reeds or rushes and is the same word found in _yam suph_, the sea traditionally called the Red Sea.
*Weeds* fits the underwater scene; *reeds* keeps the Exodus echo visible.

The same unpointed consonants can spell _soph_ (H5490), *end* or *conclusion*.
That is a wordplay, not the ordinary noun in Jonah's sentence.
The plain picture remains reeds wound around the prophet's head.

[[ttn-matthew-5-22]]
=== Matthew 5:22 and the words translated “hell”

[quote.scripture, Matthew 5:22, KJV]
____
Shall be in danger of *hell fire*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 5:22, MEAT rendering]
____
Shall be in danger of the *fire of Gehenna*.
____

_Geenna_ (G1067) is a name derived from the Valley of Hinnom.
It is not the same word as _hades_ (G86), the Greek realm of the dead corresponding broadly to Sheol, or _tartaro'o_ (G5020), the verb used in 2 Peter 2:4 for angels confined in Tartarus.

Older English translations can call all 3 *hell*, causing one familiar label to merge places and images Scripture kept distinct.
Retaining their names preserves those distinctions.
That separation does not define them by itself; each term must still be studied in every passage that uses it.

[[ttn-matthew-7-6]]
=== Matthew 7:6

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6, Greek and KJV]
____
Give not *that which is holy* unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they *trample* them under their feet, and *turn again* and rend you.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6, Shem Tov Hebrew Matthew]
____
Give not *holy flesh* unto the dogs, neither cast your pearls before swine, lest they *chew or waste* them under their feet, and *return* and rend you.
____

The Hebrew witness adds details to the picture.
That verb is associated with the boar wasting the vine in Psalm 80:13.
Its final verb for returning, _chazar_, also sounds against _chazir_, swine.

Those details belong to the medieval Hebrew witness; they are not hidden Hebrew words beneath the received Greek.
The familiar warning stands without them.
The witness matters because it independently gathers holy meat, swine, the wasted vine, and returning into one proverb.

[[ttn-matthew-7-22-23]]
=== Matthew 7:22-23

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:22-23, NKJV]
____
Have we not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name, and done *many wonders* in Your name? … Depart from Me, you who *practice lawlessness*!
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:22, Hebrew Gospel]
____
Have we not prophesied in Your name, cast out demons in Your name, and done many *signs* in Your name?
____

The Greek word behind *wonders* is the plural of _dynamis_ (G1411), power or mighty act.
_Anomia_ (G458) is the condition of being without law; 1 John 3:4 uses the same word when it defines sin as lawlessness.
The Hebrew Gospel says *signs* where the Greek says powers.
Both witnesses make the same contrast: the rejected claimants appeal to supernatural evidence, while Jesus identifies them by their lawless deeds.

[[ttn-matthew-8-12]]
=== Matthew 8:12; 22:13; 25:30

[quote.scripture, Matthew 8:12; 22:13; 25:30, Greek and KJV]
____
Cast out into *outer darkness*: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 8:12; 22:13; 25:30, Hebrew Matthew]
____
Cast out into *the dark places of Gehinnom* … into *lowest Sheol* … into *the dark places of the depths*.
____

The Hebrew witness gives a different phrase at each occurrence.
Its gnashing verb is the Hebrew Bible's _charaq_, used when the wicked grind their teeth at the righteous (Psalm 112:10).

These are 3 variant readings in that witness, not 3 dictionary meanings concealed inside the Greek adjective *outer*.
Their value is comparative: all 3 place the warning in the Old Testament world of the grave, the depths, and the rage of the excluded.

[[ttn-matthew-13-44]]
=== Matthew 13:44

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:44, Greek and KJV]
____
The kingdom of heaven is like unto *treasure hid in a field*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:44, Shem Tov Hebrew Matthew]
____
The kingdom of heaven is like unto *gold hidden in the field*.
____

Gold sharpens the refining picture: ore remains scattered through a field until it is mined, crushed, and purified.
The identification of the treasure with the covenant people does not depend on this variant; Exodus 19:5 and Psalm 135:4 already call the people God's peculiar treasure.
The Hebrew witness makes an established connection more concrete.

[[ttn-matthew-22-12]]
=== Matthew 22:12

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:12, KJV]
____
And he was *speechless*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:12, literal Greek]
____
And he was *muzzled*.
____

The Greek verb _phimoo_ (G5392) is stronger: he was *muzzled* or silenced.
The same verb describes muzzling an ox (1 Corinthians 9:9), Jesus silencing the storm (Mark 4:39), and His silencing an unclean spirit (Mark 1:25).
*Speechless* gives the result; *muzzled* preserves the picture of a stronger power stopping the mouth.

[[ttn-matthew-22-19]]
=== Matthew 22:19

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:19, KJV]
____
Shew me the *tribute money*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:19, literal Greek]
____
Show me the *coin of the census*.
____

The Greek phrase is _nomisma tou kensou_.
_Nomisma_ is the current coin or legal money, and _kensos_ is the Roman census or poll tax.
Translations such as *tribute money* or *tax money* give its function but leave the census vocabulary out of sight.
That vocabulary makes the comparison between Caesar's levy, the temple ransom, and the beast's counterfeit census possible.

[[ttn-matthew-23-2-3]]
=== Matthew 23:2-3

[quote.scripture, Matthew 23:2-3, Greek and KJV]
____
The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses' seat: all therefore whatsoever *they bid you observe, that observe and do*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 23:2-3, Shem Tov Hebrew Matthew]
____
On the seat of Moses sit the Pharisees and the sages. Now, all that *he says to you, keep and do*; but according to *their ordinances and their deeds, do not do*.
____

The Hebrew uses the singular *he*, naturally pointing back to Moses, and names the leaders' _taqqanot_, their enactments.
It rejects those enactments along with their deeds.
The received Greek plainly says *they*, so the Hebrew wording must not be silently substituted for it.
It is cited as a distinct witness because it resolves the tension between obeying everything the Pharisees command and Jesus' condemnation of their traditions.
The larger distinction between God's commandments and human enactments also stands independently in Matthew 15:3-9.

[[ttn-matthew-25-1]]
=== Matthew 25:1

[quote.scripture, Matthew 25:1, most Greek manuscripts]
____
Ten virgins … went forth to meet *the bridegroom*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 25:1, Hebrew and other ancient witnesses]
____
Ten virgins … went forth to meet *the bridegroom and the bride*.
____

The Shem Tov Hebrew Matthew reads _likrat chatan ve-khalah_, __“to meet a bridegroom and a bride,”__ and the expanded reading also appears in Old Latin, Syriac, and the Greek-Latin Codex Bezae tradition.
Psalm 45:14-15 independently pictures the bride entering with virgins who follow her.

Because most Greek manuscripts omit *and the bride*, the added phrase is a textual witness to the distinction between the bride and her companions, not undisputed wording.

[[ttn-matthew-28-1]]
=== Matthew 28:1 and the “first day of the week” formula

[quote.scripture, Matthew 28:1 and parallel passages, familiar English]
____
The *first day of the week*.
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 28:1 and parallel passages, literal Greek form]
____
*One of the sabbaths*.
____

Matthew 28:1, Mark 16:2, Luke 24:1, John 20:1, Acts 20:7, and 1 Corinthians 16:2 all use forms of _mia ton sabbaton_ or _mia sabbaton_: *one* followed by the plural *sabbaths*.

Greek and Jewish usage can extend *sabbath* to the seven-day week, so __“first day of the week”__ is a legitimate idiomatic translation.
The literal form reminds us that the week is named from the Sabbath.
It does not by itself prove a special count of 7 Sabbaths or settle every calendar question; the context must decide whether an ordinary weekday or an appointed count is in view.

[[ttn-john-1-5]]
=== John 1:5

[quote.scripture, John 1:5, KJV]
____
The light shineth in darkness; and the darkness *comprehended it not*.
____

[quote.scripture, John 1:5, second Greek sense]
____
The light shines in darkness, and the darkness *did not overcome it*.
____

[quote.scripture, John 1:5, Cochin Hebrew John]
____
The light shines in darkness, and the darkness *had no power over it*.
____

The Greek verb _katalambano_ (G2638) can mean to seize, take hold of, apprehend, comprehend, overtake, or overcome.

Both Greek senses remain possible, and John may invite both.
The darkness neither masters the light nor truly grasps it.
The Hebrew witness supports the power-reading without replacing the received Greek.

[[ttn-romans-9-32]]
=== Romans 9:32

[quote.scripture, Romans 9:32, Received Text and KJV]
____
Because they sought it not by faith, but as it were by the *works of the law*.
____

[quote.scripture, Romans 9:32, early Greek witnesses]
____
Because they pursued it not by faith, but as if by *works*.
____

Early and diverse Greek witnesses omit _nomou_, *of law*.

Either text condemns the way righteousness was pursued rather than condemning obedience itself.
The shorter reading places even more emphasis on works treated as an earning principle.
It does not prove that every Pauline use of __“works of the law”__ carries exactly the same referent.

[[ttn-romans-10-4]]
=== Romans 10:4

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:4, KJV]
____
Christ is the *end* of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth.
____

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:4, contextual rendering]
____
Christ is the *aim* of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.
____

_Telos_ can name an endpoint, result, outcome, purpose, or goal.
Paul uses it for the destination reached by fruit—__“the end everlasting life”__ (Romans 6:22)—and for the commandment's intended result—__“the end of the commandment is love”__ (1 Timothy 1:5).
Romans 10 is discussing righteousness, while Paul's larger argument says faith establishes the law rather than making it void (Romans 3:31).

_Telos_ can truly mean termination elsewhere.
*Aim* is therefore not a universal replacement for *end*; it is the sense supported in this context.

[[ttn-first-corinthians-10-11]]
=== 1 Corinthians 10:11

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 10:11, KJV]
____
All these things happened unto them for *ensamples*.
____

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 10:11, MEAT rendering]
____
All these things happened unto them as *types*.
____

*Ensample* is an older form of *example*, but the Greek is _typoi_, the plural of _typos_ (G5179): a mark left by a blow, a pattern, example, or type.
*Types* preserves Paul's claim that the events themselves formed patterns in advance, not merely moral illustrations added afterward.

[[ttn-galatians-3-24]]
=== Galatians 3:24

[quote.scripture, Galatians 3:24, KJV]
____
The law was our *schoolmaster to bring us unto Christ*.
____

[quote.scripture, Galatians 3:24, MEAT rendering]
____
The law was our *guardian until Christ*.
____

A _paidagogos_ (G3807) in a Greek or Roman household was not primarily a classroom teacher.
He was a custodian who supervised and escorted a child.
The household office is therefore rendered __“our guardian until Christ.”__

*Guardian* clarifies the office, but it does not settle the theological question by itself.
Paul's surrounding argument must still tell us what aspect of that guardianship reaches its appointed end.

[[ttn-colossians-2-14]]
=== Colossians 2:14

[quote.scripture, Colossians 2:14, KJV]
____
Blotting out the *handwriting of ordinances* that was against us.
____

[quote.scripture, Colossians 2:14, MEAT rendering]
____
Blotting out the *handwritten debt-record, with its decrees*, that was against us.
____

_Cheirographon_ (G5498) is something handwritten, especially a signed record of obligation or debt.

The document is singular, stands against the debtors, and is blotted out as their trespasses are forgiven in the preceding verse.
The noun _nomos_, *law*, does not occur in the sentence.
Even so, _cheirographon_ literally means *handwriting*, and the attached word _dogmasin_ still brings decrees or ordinances into the syntax.
*Debt-record* explains the legal setting; it should not be stretched into a claim that no ordinances are involved.

[[ttn-hebrews-10-20]]
=== Hebrews 10:20

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 10:20, KJV]
____
By a *new and living way*, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh.
____

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 10:20, etymological second hearing]
____
By a *freshly slain and living way*, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh.
____

_Prosphatos_ (G4372) means new or recent and has historically been associated with fresh slaughter; _sphazo_ is the New Testament verb for slaying.
Because the immediate context names Christ's flesh and blood, the sacrificial resonance has unusual force.
*Living* then completes the paradox: the way is opened through death, yet it lives.

Major lexicons and translations still render _prosphatos_ *new* or *recent*, and its exact etymology has been disputed.
*Freshly slain* is therefore an etymological and contextual second hearing, not a claim that *new* is impossible.

[[ttn-hebrews-11-19]]
=== Hebrews 11:19

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 11:19, KJV]
____
From whence also he received him *in a figure*.
____

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 11:19, literal Greek]
____
From whence also he received him *in a parable*.
____

The Greek phrase is _en parabole_.
_Parabole_ (G3850) is the New Testament's ordinary word for a parable, comparison, or figurative correspondence.
*Figure* gives the sense; *parable* preserves Scripture's own claim that the lived history of Abraham and Isaac functioned as a parable.

[[ttn-james-1-17]]
=== James 1:17

[quote.scripture, James 1:17, Greek and KJV]
____
Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the *Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning*.
____

[quote.scripture, James 1:17, Cochin Hebrew James]
____
Every good and perfect gift comes down from the *light of the Father*, beside whom there is *no exchange of light and darkness*.
____

It uses the luminary-word of Genesis 1 and adds that beside Him there is no exchange of light and darkness.

The Hebrew wording does not correct the received Greek.
It is a parallel witness that makes the luminary and light-darkness pattern already present in James more explicit.

[[ttn-second-peter-2-4]]
=== 2 Peter 2:4

[quote.scripture, 2 Peter 2:4, KJV]
____
God spared not the angels that sinned, but *cast them down to hell*.
____

[quote.scripture, 2 Peter 2:4, MEAT rendering]
____
God did not spare the angels who sinned, but *confined them in Tartarus*.
____

Peter uses _tartaro'o_ (G5020), a verb found only here in the New Testament, rather than the ordinary words for Gehenna or Hades.
Keeping the name *Tartarus* visible prevents the English word *hell* from erasing that distinction.

[[ttn-jude-13]]
=== Jude 13

[quote.scripture, Jude 13, Greek and KJV]
____
*Raging waves of the sea*, foaming out their own shame; *wandering stars*, to whom is reserved the blackness of darkness for ever.
____

[quote.scripture, Jude 13, Cochin Hebrew Jude]
____
Like *waves of the sea that cast out mire and mud*.
____
That wording follows Isaiah 57:20, where the wicked are a troubled sea casting up mire and dirt.

The 2 witnesses use different but compatible scriptural images for the same teachers.
The Hebrew does not prove that *wandering stars* was added to the Greek, and the Greek does not condemn literal planets merely for moving.

[[ttn-revelation-1-4-20]]
=== Revelation 1:4, 20

[quote.scripture, Revelation 1:4, 20, Greek]
____
John to the *7 churches* which are in Asia … the 7 candlesticks which thou sawest are the *7 churches*.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 1:4, 20, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
John to the *7 elders* which are in Asia … the 7 menorahs which thou sawest are the *7 elders*.

[[ttn-revelation-2-11]]
=== Revelation 2:11; 20:6, 14; 21:8

[quote.scripture, Revelation 2:11; 20:6, 14; 21:8, Greek]
____
The *second death*.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 2:11; 20:6; 21:8, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
*Another death*; a *death of another kind*.
____

The Cochin wording appears in 3 of the 4 occurrences.
The Hebrew draws attention to a difference in kind, while the Greek ordinal marks sequence.
It helps prevent *second* from being reduced to a mere repetition of the first death without replacing the Greek count.

[[ttn-revelation-2-17]]
=== Revelation 2:17

[quote.scripture, Revelation 2:17, Greek and KJV]
____
I will give him a *white stone*, and in the stone a new name written.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 2:17, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
I will give him a *good testimony*, and in that testimony a new name written.
____
The Hebrew witness makes the favorable verdict explicit and supports the stone's judicial use.
The Greek _psephos_ already names a pebble used in counting or voting, so that connection does not depend on the Hebrew reading alone.

[[ttn-revelation-12-1]]
=== Revelation 12:1

[quote.scripture, Revelation 12:1, NKJV]
____
Now a great *sign* appeared in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a *garland of 12 stars*.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 12:1, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
There was seen a great *sign* in the heavens: a certain woman clothed in the sun, and the moon under her feet; and *from her head begins the cycle, the first of the 12 stars*.
____

The Greek noun is _semeion_ (G4592), the ordinary New Testament word for a sign.
The Hebrew vocabulary permits *sign* or raised standard, *crown* or royal circuit, *head* or beginning, and *one* or first.
Those senses expose the annual circuit seen in the picture.

That more elaborate wording belongs to the Cochin Hebrew witness, not to the received Greek.
Its __“cycle … first of the 12”__ depends on lexical and syntactical choices that must be judged as a rendering of that witness rather than attributed to every manuscript of Revelation.

[[ttn-revelation-13-18]]
=== Revelation 13:18

[quote.scripture, Revelation 13:18, Greek and KJV]
____
It is the number of *a man*.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 13:18, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
It is the number of *one man*.
____

The Hebrew uses _adam echad_ and a verb from ledger reckoning.

The Greek counting verb _psephizo_ (G5585) means to calculate or tally with pebbles and is related to the _psephos_, the voting or counting stone of Revelation 2:17.
The Hebrew's *one man* and accounting language sharpen the contrast between a single covenant head and the number 666.
The Greek itself does not contain a separate numeral *one*; that added emphasis belongs to the Hebrew witness.

[[ttn-revelation-14-6]]
=== Revelation 14:6

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:6, Greek and KJV]
____
Having the *everlasting gospel* to preach unto them that dwell on the earth.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:6, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
Holding a certain *stone tablet*, to make known to those dwelling on the earth.
____

The Hebrew word is _gilyon_.
That reading gives the everlasting announcement a written covenant form and supports the connection between gospel and kingdom law.
It is wording found in the Hebrew witness, not a new definition imposed on the Greek word _euangelion_, gospel.

[[ttn-revelation-14-7]]
=== Revelation 14:7

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:7, Greek and KJV]
____
*Fear God, and give glory to him* … and *worship him* that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters.
____

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:7, Cochin Hebrew Revelation]
____
*Fear YHWH and make your prayer to him* … and *make prayer to the one* who created the heavens and the earth and the sea and the wells.
____

The Hebrew replaces the Greek act of homage with prayer directed to the Creator.
Both readings describe an act addressed to a person; neither defines worship as music.
The Hebrew is comparative evidence, not a hidden Hebrew definition of the Greek verb.
