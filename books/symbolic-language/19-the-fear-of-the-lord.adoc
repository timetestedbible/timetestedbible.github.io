Solomon spent a whole book trying every road under the sun, and ended it in one sentence:

[quote.scripture, Ecclesiastes 12:13]
____
Let us hear the *conclusion of the whole matter*: *Fear God, and keep his commandments*: for this is the *whole duty of man*.
____

This is the last symbol of the book because it is the root of all the rest — and, like the rest, it has been hollowed out. The fear of the LORD is taken either as raw terror or as a soft “reverence” that asks nothing of anyone. Scripture means neither. It defines the fear of God by what it does.

[discrete]
=== Not Terror, but Obedience

Moses asks the question outright, and answers it:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 10:12-13]
____
…what doth the LORD thy God *require* of thee, but to *fear* the LORD thy God… to *keep the commandments* of the LORD…
____

To fear God is to keep His commandments — the two are set down as one requirement; and again, __“the LORD commanded us to do all these statutes, to fear the LORD our God”__ (Deuteronomy 6:24). It shows itself as a turning from evil: __“the fear of the LORD is to hate evil”__ (Proverbs 8:13); __“by the fear of the LORD men depart from evil”__ (Proverbs 16:6); __“the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding”__ (Job 28:28). Fear is not a feeling about God; it is obedience to God — the same as love, the same as faith.

[discrete]
=== The Beginning of Wisdom

It is also where wisdom starts:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 9:10]
____
The *fear of the LORD* is the *beginning of wisdom*: and the knowledge of the holy is understanding.
____

— and the wise, we saw, are those who __“do his commandments”__ (Psalms 111:10). So the fool, who has said __“There is no God,”__ has no fear, and therefore no wisdom, however clever he may be; he is back on the sand. Every wise man in Scripture begins here.

[discrete]
=== Fear Him

And the fear is owed to One only — not to men, not to the powers that can reach the body:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 10:28]
____
…fear not them which kill the body… but rather *fear him* which is able to *destroy both soul and body in hell*.
____

__“Sanctify the LORD of hosts himself; and let him be your fear”__ (Isaiah 8:13). The world fears a thousand things and not God; those who fear God need fear nothing else. And this fear is no burden — it is __“clean, enduring for ever”__ (Psalms 19:9), a __“treasure”__ (Isaiah 33:6), __“a fountain of life”__ (Proverbs 14:27).

[discrete]
=== The Whole Duty

It is fitting that the last word of the Bible’s symbolic language should be its first demand. When the everlasting gospel is carried to every nation at the end, it is this:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:7]
____
…*Fear God, and give glory to him*; for the hour of his judgment is come…
____

— and four verses on, the ones who belong to God are __“they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith of Jesus”__ (Revelation 14:12). Fear and obedience, first to last.

So the symbols come home. The kingdom is a mountain and the peoples a sea; the gospel is the renewed covenant, the law going forth from Zion; the way, the truth, and the life are that law walked; the name is the covenant owned, the garment the covenant worn, the light the covenant made visible; the wise are those who do it, and the fools those who do not. Strip every figure back to the thing it guards, and one command stands under them all: __“Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man.”__
