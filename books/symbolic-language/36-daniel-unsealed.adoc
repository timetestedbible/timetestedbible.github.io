Daniel is the one prophet ordered to lock his own book.
__“Shut up the words, and sym:sym-seal[seal] the book, even to the time of the end”__ — and when he asks what his own visions mean, he is refused:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:9-10]
____
…Go thy way, Daniel: for the words are *closed up and sealed till the time of the end*… none of the wicked shall understand; but the sym:sym-wise[wise] shall sym:sym-understanding[understand].
____

A sealed book expects an unsealing.

A book sealed in Hebrew was never going to be unsealed by a translation of a translation: the vowel points are themselves the first translation, and our English Bibles translate the pointing, not the bare letters — so part of the seal sits in readings that first translation chose away.
This chapter takes the covering off with tools already forged — the chapter link:/books/symbolic-language/what-is-the-point/[_What Is the Point?_] made the case for revisiting these books with fresh eyes, the vowel points set aside — and unseals the way a seal demands: by translating again, this time from the letters themselves.

[discrete]
=== Seventy of What?

Start where Daniel himself started: with a promise of Jeremiah's that did not add up.

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 25:11-12]
____
And this whole land shall be a desolation, and an astonishment; and these nations shall serve the king of sym:sym-babel[Babylon] seventy sym:sym-year[years].
And it shall come to pass, when seventy years are accomplished, that I will punish the king of Babylon, and that nation, saith the LORD, for their iniquity, and the land of the Chaldeans, and will make it *perpetual desolations*.
____

Many people, Daniel among them at first, understood this as Israel serving Babylon for seventy literal years.
But read carefully what the seventy must contain: all of Jeremiah's words brought upon that land — __“even all that is written in this book”__ (Jeremiah 25:13) — and Babylon made a desolation for ever.

History did something else.
Babylon fell to Cyrus in 539 BC without a fight, during the Feast of Tabernacles, and was not made a perpetual desolation; the captives went home in 538 BC — some sixty years after Daniel was carried off, not seventy.
Either Jeremiah is a false prophet, or __“seventy years”__ counts seventy of some larger unit — for the word translated __“year”__ is a flexible measure of time, the way __“day”__ is.
Daniel had the same question:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:2]
____
In the first year of his reign I Daniel understood by books the number of the years, whereof the word of the LORD came to Jeremiah the prophet, that he would accomplish seventy sym:sym-year[years] in the desolations of Jerusalem.
____

He prays, and the angel Gabriel brings the answer:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:24 — the letters read again]
____
Seventy sym:sym-week[weeks] are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to *make an end of* sym:sym-sin[sins], and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], and to *complete the vision and prophecy*, and to anoint the most Holy.
____

Now set the verse side by side, word by word — the received points beside this book's, and beside every claimed sense its proof: the verse where the King James prints the same sense, or the repointed word itself.

Sins have not been ended, and the vision of Jeremiah has not been completed — so seventy __“weeks,”__ four hundred ninety years, cannot be the whole of it.
Look at the letters.
The Hebrew under __“weeks”__ is the seven-word in a plural form — and with the points removed, the verse opens with the same word written twice:

[.text-center.breath]
שבעים שבעים — *seventy seventy*

[.breath]
The plural ending makes the seven at least double — and a complete cycle of sevens, seven sevens, is the jubilee.
Jeremiah's unit was already compared to a year, and a year is itself a complete cycle of time; so the letters give us seventy sevens-of-sevens: *seventy jubilees* — seventy times forty-nine years, 3,430 in all.footnote:jubilee[The count runs from Israel crossing the Jordan — the year the jubilee clock started — with the date and the counting conventions (no year zero; a year is the space between the marks, not the marks) established in my chronology study, _Time Tested Tradition_.]
The jubilee clock started at the Jordan crossing in 1406 BC, and the seventieth jubilee year closed in the spring of 2026.

That places 2026-2027 in the *third year* of Hezekiah's sign — two fallow years, __“and in the third year *sow ye, and reap*”__ (Isaiah 37:30) — and the third is the year of going up: __“on the *third day* thou shalt *go up unto the house of the LORD*”__ (2 Kings 20:5).
The letters also permit a second reading, and both can stand: seventy seventy as seventy times seventy — 4,900 years __“determined upon *thy people*,”__ the seed of Abraham.
That count runs from the covenant itself, sealed in 1876 BC, four hundred thirty years to the selfsame day before the Exodus (Exodus 12:40-41) — and if the reign of a thousand years is its final millennium, the reign opens 3,900 years in: the very year the seventieth jubilee closed.
Two counts, two starting lines four centuries apart, and one door.

=== The Count at Two Scales

The Torah has already run this very count at two scales.
The word under Daniel's __“weeks”__ is the name of a feast — _Shavuot_, the Feast of sym:sym-week[Weeks], the Feast of Sevens — and that feast is counted exactly the way the jubilee is counted: __“seven sabbaths shall be complete: even unto the morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number *fifty days*”__ (Leviticus 23:15-16), as the jubilee numbers __“seven sabbaths of sym:sym-year[years]”__ and hallows __“the *fiftieth year*”__ (Leviticus 25:8, 10).
Seven sevens of days to a fiftieth day; seven sevens of years to a fiftieth year.

The Feast of Weeks is the jubilee run in days — the Torah's own day-for-a-year model, rehearsed every spring on the way to Pentecost.
So when Gabriel answers a question about years in *sevens*, the scale is already licensed: the count that walks in days walks in years.

Daniel marks his own scales.
One chapter later, mourning three ordinary weeks, he writes __“three sevens *of days*”__ (Daniel 10:2-3) — the King James prints __“three *full* weeks”__ — the numeral leading and the scale named, exactly as the feast counts: __“*seven* weeks shalt thou number”__ (Deuteronomy 16:9).
This prophecy inverts that order — *Sevens seven… Sevens sixty-and-two* — the noun leading its own count, and no scale named anywhere: the bare Sevens are not day-weeks and not year-weeks; they are the feast's unit, the jubilee's shape.

The letters go one step further.
Written without points, the plural here is letter for letter the number *seventy* — the spelling Scripture uses some ninety times — and the pointing that reads “weeks” appears only in this prophecy; the vowels decide, and context chooses the vowels.
Scripture's own idiom crowns the choice: a doubled word is the thing perfected — _shalom shalom_ the King James prints __“*perfect* peace”__ (Isaiah 26:3); _tzedek tzedek_, __“*altogether* just”__ (Deuteronomy 16:20) — and this verse doubles at both ends: the Sevens doubled at its head, and __“the *most Holy*”__ — the holy of holies — doubled at its close.
The doubling also multiplies: when Jesus set the measure of forgiveness He reached for this figure — __“until *seventy times seven*”__ (Matthew 18:22) — the Greek hearing the pair multiplied, 490, the count of the very prophecy given to make an end of sins; the Hebrew Matthew hears __“seventy and seven,”__ Lamech's vengeance-count (Genesis 4:24) answered in forgiveness.
One skeleton, every arithmetic the letters allow — and the fiftieth that crowns each seven-of-sevens carries the same cargo at every scale: __“proclaim *liberty* throughout all the land”__ (Leviticus 25:10) in the fiftieth year; the Spirit poured out on the fiftieth day, and __“where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is *liberty*”__ (2 Corinthians 3:17).

=== The Two Decrees

Gabriel then divides the count from a landmark anyone can check:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:25]
____
Know therefore and understand, that from the going forth of the commandment to *restore and to build Jerusalem* unto the *Messiah the Prince* shall be seven sym:sym-week[weeks], and threescore and two weeks: the street shall be built again, and the wall, even in troublous times.
____

The __“weeks”__ here carry the same plural form as verse 24 — and the word the King James renders __“two”__ is also the Hebrew for *second, another*; the word for __“seventy”__ can stand for the jubilee-cycle it completes.
Read the letters again:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:25 — the letters read again]
____
Know therefore and understand, that from the going forth of the commandment to restore and build Jerusalem until Messiah the Prince shall be *seven seventies* — four hundred ninety years — then *Jubilee sixty, and another* four hundred ninety: they will come back and build again, with the *plaza and the moat*, and in tribulation the times end.
____

History proves the reading out, twice over.
The first decree went forth in 458 BC; count the four hundred ninety, and the last of them runs from spring 32 to spring 33 — the year of the cross, where Messiah *cut the covenant* and rose.footnote:ad32[The dating of the crucifixion and resurrection to AD 32 — with the calendar evidence behind it — is established in my _Time Tested Tradition_.]

In 1535 — on the sixtieth jubilee — Sultan Suleiman gave the *second* decree to rebuild Jerusalem, and that decree names the plaza and the moat; the words were carved in stone at the moat and on the Temple Mount, where they stand today.
The two rebuildings could not be confused with each other.
In the first, the Jews refused foreign hands and built with a weapon in one hand; in the second, a foreign empire raised the walls — exactly as written: __“the sons of sym:sym-stranger[strangers] shall *build up thy walls*”__ (Isaiah 60:10) — a verse that sits, for whoever is counting, at chapter sixty, verse ten.
And four hundred ninety years from that second decree is where the reading says the times end, __“in tribulation.”__

=== The Cutting and the Vanishing

The verse between the decrees carries its own buried readings, and the letters deserve one more pass.

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:26]
____
And after threescore and two weeks shall *Messiah be cut off, but not for himself*: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a sym:sym-flood[flood], and unto the end of the war desolations are determined.
____

First, the opening: verse 25 wrote its sevens twice with no article, but verse 26 writes *the* sevens — and a definite article points at a system already named: *the* jubilee count of verse 24, the sixtieth, and the second decree.
The consonants under __“cut off”__ are _karath_ — the standard covenant verb of the whole Old Testament: covenants are not signed in Hebrew; they are *cut*.
Messiah does not merely suffer the verb; He performs it: Messiah shall *cut a covenant*.

The phrase __“but not for himself”__ — _v'ain lo_ — reads __“and he vanishes”__: the same idiom as Enoch, __“he *was not*; for God took him”__ (Genesis 5:24) — and when the Greek translators met Enoch's _ain_, they rendered it __“was not *found*,”__ the very phrase the New Testament writes at the empty tomb: __“and *found not* the body of the Lord Jesus”__ (Luke 24:3).
The pair that follows is Daniel's own: __“behold, a *watcher* and an *holy one* came down from heaven”__ (Daniel 4:13) — the watchful and the holy, three times in his book — and the same letters carry the waking as a verb, _he'ir_, __“the LORD *stirred up* the spirit of Cyrus”__ (Ezra 1:1), and the holy as its act, _haqdesh_, __“I had *wholly dedicated*”__ (Judges 17:3).
The destroying verb stays singular — *he* shall destroy, not *they* — and the received reading must front its objects to say otherwise, reading the city and the sanctuary backward across the verb; this reading walks the verse in its own order.

Now read the whole verse the second time through:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:26 — the letters read again]
____
And after *the* Sevens — sixty, and a second — Messiah shall *cut a covenant*, and he *vanishes* — to himself — and *the watchful*, and he *sanctifies*: and *he* shall destroy the people of a ruler who is coming.
And his end is in the sym:sym-flood[flood], and until the end of war it is decreed: desolations.
____

Under the traditional reading, the __“he”__ of these verses has divided Christendom for centuries: is it Messiah, or antichrist?
Read from the letters, the ambiguity dissolves: Messiah is the subject throughout — He cuts, He vanishes, He sanctifies, He destroys — and the antichrist appears only as the *coming ruler*, whose people He will destroy.

=== The Marriage Covenant

The covenant He cut has a second act.

[quote.scripture, Daniel 9:27]
____
And he shall *confirm the covenant* with many for one sym:sym-week[week]: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease.
____

The verb is the hinge.
Verse 26 *cut* the covenant — _karath_; verse 27 does not cut a new one — it *strengthens* the one already cut: _gabar_, to make strong.
Two verbs, one covenant, two stages — and the two stages are the Hebrew sym:sym-marriage[marriage], exactly as the link:/books/symbolic-language/marriage-and-divorce/[Marriage and Divorce] chapter walked them.

The groom offers the cup, and the covenant is cut: __“This cup is the *new testament in my blood*”__ (Luke 22:20).
He abstains until the wedding: __“I will not drink of the fruit of the vine, *until the kingdom of God shall come*”__ (Luke 22:18).
He departs to prepare the bridal chamber, and vanishes: __“I go to *prepare a place* for you… I will come again, and *receive* you unto myself”__ (John 14:2-3) — and His __“receive”__ is the very Greek word of __“one shall be *taken*, and the other left”__ (Matthew 24:40).
The cutting is the cross; the vanishing is the ascension; the strengthening is the return — the covenant confirmed with many at the marriage supper.

=== The Abomination's Desolation

Daniel's twelfth chapter seals the timeline — and the King James points the tenses forward that the letters point back:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:11-12]
____
And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be *taken away*, and the abomination that maketh desolate *set up*, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety sym:sym-day[days].
Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days.
____

The verb under __“shall be taken away”__ is a completed past — *was taken away* — and the setting up is a direction of travel, not a date behind us; the __“abomination that maketh desolate”__ can as readily be read the other way around: the abomination *that becomes desolate* — the thing destined for destruction.
Read the letters again:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:11-12 — the letters read again]
____
From the time that the daily *was taken away*, going toward the setting up of the abomination *that becomes desolate*, a thousand two hundred and ninety days.
Blessed is he that waiteth, and cometh to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days.
____

Now count, a sym:sym-day[day] for a sym:sym-year[year].
The daily was taken away in 598 BC, the year Daniel's city fell to Nebuchadnezzar and the temple service failed.
One thousand two hundred ninety years arrive at AD 692 — and inside the Dome of the Rock, on the Temple Mount, a dedicatory inscription dates the building to the Islamic year 72, which runs from July 691 to July 692.
And the Lord had made that very sight the signal to flee:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:15-16]
____
When ye therefore shall see the *abomination of desolation*, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, *stand in the holy place*, (whoso readeth, let him sym:sym-understanding[understand]:) then let them which be in Judaea flee into the sym:sym-mountain[mountains].
____

The abomination was *set up* on schedule, in the holy place, where He said to watch for it.

The blessing has a second number: add the thousand three hundred thirty-five, and the count runs to the spring of 2028 — the abomination made desolate, and the waiting ones arrived.
Set that end against the seventy jubilees and the second decree's four hundred ninety, and the three counts close on the same few years — the tribulation of the times’ end that the ninth chapter named.

__“Whoso readeth, let him understand.”__
The understanding was never withheld from the wise; it was sealed — and the seal was never wax.
It is the reading tradition laid over the letters, and it comes off with the tools you now hold.

The counts, once unsealed, lean forward onto a single scene: the Son of man coming *with clouds*.
