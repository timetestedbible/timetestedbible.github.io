Bread and wine sit together on the table of every covenant.
We have already fixed the first: __“man doth not live by bread only, but by every word… of the LORD”__ (Deuteronomy 8:3) — the sym:sym-bread[bread] is the word, and the word is the sym:sym-truth[truth].
But bread is only half the table.
What is the wine?

Jesus gives the pair Himself, and makes the two of them the whole of true worship:

[quote.scripture, John 4:23-24]
____
The true worshippers shall worship the Father in *spirit and in truth*: for the Father *seeketh* such to worship him.
God is a Spirit: and they that worship him must worship him in *spirit and in truth*.
____

Truth is the bread.
The other half is the wine — and wine is spirit.
The Father will have both, and never one without the other.

[discrete]
=== Wine Is Spirit

Scripture does not leave this to guesswork; it says the same thing of wine and of spirit, in the same words.
Both are __poured out__: the LORD __“poured out”__ the __“spirit of deep sleep”__ (Isaiah 29:10) and the __“spirit of grace”__ (Zechariah 12:10), exactly as wine is poured.
Both are __drunk__: we are __“all made to drink into one Spirit”__ (1 Corinthians 12:13), as the nations are made to __“drink”__ the cup of fury (Jeremiah 25:15).
Both are __mingled__: the LORD __“mingled a perverse spirit”__ (Isaiah 19:14) as wine is mixed and mingled (Psalm 75:8).

And once the prophet says it flat out — the drunkard reeling in the street is not drunk on drink at all:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 29:9-10]
____
They are *drunken, but not with wine*; they stagger, but not with strong drink.
For the LORD hath *poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep*…
____

Drunkenness without wine — because the wine was never the point; the spirit was.
Paul sets the two side by side as rival fillings: __“be not drunk with wine, wherein is excess; but be filled with the Spirit”__ (Ephesians 5:18).
A man will be filled and carried by some spirit; the only question is which.
So when the Spirit was first poured out at Pentecost, the mockers reached for the nearest word for a man overcome and possessed — __“these men are full of new wine”__ (Acts 2:13).
They had the picture right; they only mistook the vintage.

[discrete]
=== Pressed from the Fruit

Where does a man's wine come from?
From his own sym:sym-fruit[fruit].
The sym:sym-olive[vine] bears grapes, and a people's grapes are its works; trodden in the press, those grapes become the wine.
The spirit a man carries is the pressed-out juice of his deeds.

So the fruit decides the wine.
Where the fruit is corrupt, the wine is poison:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 32:32-33]
____
Their *vine is of the vine of Sodom*… their *grapes are grapes of gall*, their clusters are bitter: their *wine is the poison of dragons*, and the cruel venom of asps.
____

Grapes of gall press out to venom.
Isaiah walks the same vineyard: God fenced Israel, __“planted it with the choicest vine,”__ and __“made a winepress therein”__ — then __“looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes”__ (Isaiah 5:2-4).
A vineyard with a press built into it, waiting for fruit: the works were always meant to become the wine.
Wild grapes yield nothing worth drinking, only a cry for judgment (Isaiah 5:7).
It is the law of sowing and reaping, stated in the press: __“he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life”__ (Galatians 6:8).

This is the twin of the sym:sym-oil[oil].
The same fruit, pressed, gives two things: the oil that is burned for sym:sym-light[light] — proven works made visible — and the wine that is drunk for the spirit within.
Deeds tested under pressure become both the light a man shows and the spirit he carries.

[discrete]
=== The Two Wines

Because the wine is pressed from the fruit, there are two wines, and only two.
From righteous fruit comes the good wine — the __“wine that maketh glad the heart of man”__ (Psalm 104:15), the cup of the covenant, __“my blood of the new testament”__ (Matthew 26:28), the __“spirit of grace”__ poured on the house of David (Zechariah 12:10).
This is the Holy Spirit: gladness, grace, and life, drunk in and filling the man.

From corrupt fruit comes the other wine, an unholy spirit — and Scripture names its kinds.
There is the __“spirit of whoredoms”__ that makes a people __“err”__ and __“go a whoring”__ (Hosea 4:11-12) — the same cup sym:sym-harlot[Babylon] holds out, __“the wine of her fornication,”__ that makes __“all the earth drunken”__ until __“the nations are mad”__ (Jeremiah 51:7; Revelation 17:2).
There is the stupor, the __“spirit of deep sleep”__ (Isaiah 29:10).
There is the __“strong delusion”__ God sends on __“them that received not the love of the truth”__ (2 Thessalonians 2:10-11) — refuse the bread and you are handed the poisoned wine.
And there is the wine of wrath itself, __“the wine of the fierceness of his wrath”__ poured __“into the cup of his indignation”__ (Revelation 14:10; 16:19), pressed out when the wicked are trodden in the great winepress (Isaiah 63:3; Revelation 19:15).

One wine gladdens, the other maddens.
Both are spirit; the fruit decides which.

[discrete]
=== A Feast of New Wine

Nowhere are the two wines set closer than at one feast.
Pentecost — the feast of weeks, __“the firstfruits of wheat harvest”__ (Exodus 34:22) — has been kept twice, and both times the crowd was full of wine.

At the first, Moses was on the mountain receiving the law when a noise rose from the camp.
Joshua took it for the sound of war, but Moses knew it; and the Septuagint records the name he gave it — not the shout of battle nor the cry of defeat, but __“the voice of them that begin the banquet of wine”__ (Exodus 32:18).
Around the golden calf the people had made their own feast — they __“sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play”__ (Exodus 32:6) — a wine-feast of the wrong spirit; and three thousand fell (Exodus 32:28).

At the second Pentecost the charge of wine was raised again — __“these men are full of new wine”__ (Acts 2:13) — but this time the wine was the true one, the __“firstfruits of the Spirit”__ (Romans 8:23) poured out on men who __“gladly received his word”__; and three thousand were added (Acts 2:41).
One feast, both crowds full: one of the counterfeit wine that felled them, one of the new wine that gave them life.

[discrete]
=== Spirit and Truth

This is why the Father __“seeketh”__ worshippers __“in spirit and in truth”__ — in wine and in bread, and in both at once. sym:sym-truth[Truth] without spirit is the dead letter; and when the truth itself goes sour it is the sym:sym-leaven[leavened] loaf of false doctrine.
Spirit without truth is the wine of fornication, the reveller's cup — a people __“drunken, but not with wine.”__
Neither alone is worship.
But where a man keeps the truth and bears the fruit of it, his deeds press out into the right wine, and he is filled with the Spirit of grace — and that, the bread and the wine together, is the worship the Father is seeking.

[discrete]
=== New Wine, New Wineskins

Yet the wine we are given now is only the firstfruits — a first pouring, not the full measure; for the new wine cannot yet be fully held.
Jesus said it is never put __“into old wineskins”__; the new wine, still working, will __“burst the skins”__ and be spilled, and __“the skins will be destroyed”__ (Luke 5:37; the word is _askos_, a wineskin).
The body is the wineskin, and this mortal, corruptible body cannot contain the fulness of the Spirit — so we have the firstfruits only, and __“groan within ourselves, waiting for… the redemption of our body”__ (Romans 8:23).

The full measure waits for new wineskins.
At the resurrection __“this corruptible must put on incorruption”__ (1 Corinthians 15:53) — a new body, a new skin — and into it the new wine is poured in full: __“a new heart also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you”__ (Ezekiel 36:26).
The firstfruits are drunk now; the whole vintage is gathered at the last feast, the __“feast of ingathering at the year's end”__ (Exodus 34:22) — new wineskins for new wine, the incorruptible body at last made to hold the Spirit in full.
