DESCRIPTION (~300 words):

Hell is one English word laid over two different realities — and the
doctrine changes with the word beneath. The mark of the beast is the
counterfeit of a census Moses took at Sinai: its name where God's Name
goes, its number where God's count goes, its money where the atonement
money went. The harlot of Revelation is named by her own crime scene —
"where also our Lord was crucified." The sabbath names far more than a
day. And a coin — dug from a field, refined in fire, struck with its
owner's image — is Scripture's own picture of you.

These are not speculations. The Bible defines its own symbolic language:
a mountain is a kingdom, the sea is the peoples, bread is teaching —
each meaning defined openly in one passage, used consistently in the
rest, contradicted by none. MEAT teaches that method of discovery and
applies it symbol by symbol until you can run it without help: more than
140 symbolic words in a working glossary, each with its defining verse,
carried by over 2,500 citations from 64 of the Bible's 66 books and a
complete Scripture Index.

Then every definition was stress-tested. A blind experiment measured the
whole glossary against mainstream teaching. Where the church has read
carefully, the method lands on the same ground — proof it is no private
invention. But thirty-five entries say what you have almost certainly
never heard taught — twenty-two overturning the common reading outright
— and every one is badged in the glossary with the common view printed
beside it. The contested ground is marked, and you are invited to fight
over every inch of it.

Heaven and hell, the seal of God and its counterfeit, marriage and
divorce, the remnant and how narrow the way is, Babylon and its fall,
the path to salvation — read plainly, from the vocabulary Scripture
itself supplies. Take no man's word — verify.
