"I have fed you with milk, and not with meat: for hitherto ye were not able to bear it." Paul's rebuke names this book. Milk is the word pre-digested — teaching handed over ready to swallow. MEAT is Scripture's solid food: the Bible's own symbolic language, and the skill of chewing it yourself. Say the title aloud and it is also an invitation: meet the Bible's symbolic language.

What that food tastes like: The mark of the beast, decoded — the counterfeit of a census Moses took at Sinai, its name where God's Name goes, its number where God's count goes, its money where the atonement money went. The sign of Jonah — the only sign Jesus gave — opened scene by scene as a prophecy of His betrayal, arrest, death, resurrection, and the gospel's turn to the nations. Daniel's seal, which was never wax: it is the reading tradition laid over the prophet's letters, and when it comes off, the text counts seventy jubilees. Lucifer's plan to corrupt the signs of heaven, read from the record God Himself published. The Name — not a sound to pronounce, but the covenant itself, owned as a bride takes her husband's.

You have not read these in seminary notes, because they are not there. They come from a teachable method: let Scripture define its own symbols — each meaning stated in one passage, used consistently, contradicted by none — and read the letters the prophets wrote rather than the vowel-tradition added centuries later. The result is a working glossary of more than 140 terms, over 2,500 citations from 64 books of the Bible, and a complete Scripture Index.

Every definition was stress-tested blind against mainstream teaching. Where the church has read carefully, the method agrees. Thirty-five entries say what has never been taught — each badged in the glossary with the common reading printed beside it.

By now, you ought to be eating meat. Take no man's word — verify.
