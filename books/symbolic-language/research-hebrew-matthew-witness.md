# Research: Hebrew Matthew (Shem Tov) — Matt 23:2-3, 24:29, 24:35-36, and the authenticity question

Letter-verified from George Howard, *The Gospel of Matthew according to
a Primitive Hebrew Text* (1987), read visually from the archive.org
scan (Hebrew pp. 112, 122; English facing). 2026-07-06. Companion to
research-ten-virgins.md (which carries 25:1-13) and to the full
transcription being produced into data/hg-chapters/Matthew-*.json.

## Matt 23:2-3 — the seat of Moses (Howard p. 112)

v2: לאמר על כסא משה ישבו הפירושים והחכמים
— "saying: Upon the seat of Moses sit the Pharisees and the sages."

v3: ועתה כל אשר יאמר לכם שמרו ועשו ובתקנותיהם ומעשיהם אל תעשו שהם אומרים והם אינם עושים
— "And now, all that **he says** (יאמר, SINGULAR — Moses / the seat's
Torah) to you, keep and do; but by their **takkanot** (תקנותיהם — the
technical rabbinic term for enacted ordinances, i.e. the oral-law
legislative layer) and their deeds do NOT do, for they say and do not."

Greek reads plural εἴπωσιν ("whatever THEY say… do") and has only ἔργα
("works") where the Hebrew has the pair תקנותיהם ומעשיהם. The whole
doctrinal delta hangs on ONE VAV: יאמר (he says) vs יאמרו (they say) —
the same jot-class variance as ka'aru/ka'ari in Ps 22:16 (ch 20's case).

Context checks (same chapter): v4 heavy burdens bound on shoulders =
the takkanot; vv16-22 Jesus overturns their oath-rulings (worked
examples of takkanot); v5 names ציציות explicitly ("large tzitziot,"
with a Romance gloss in the ms — glossary sym-tzitzit tie). The Hebrew
reading agrees with Matt 15:3-9 (their tradition voids God's command)
and 16:12 (beware their doctrine); the Greek reading contradicts both
and its own chapter thirteen verses later.

## Matt 24:29 (Howard p. 122)

ובאותה שעה אחרי הימים ההם יחשך השמש והירח לא יגיה אורו והכוכבים יפלו
מהשמים וחיל השמים יתנדדו
— "And **in that hour, after those days**, the sun shall darken…" NO
"immediately" (εὐθέως), NO "tribulation of" — Howard's English brackets
"(the tribulation of)" as absent from the Hebrew. Apparatus at page
bottom not resolvable at scan resolution — whether any ms adds צרת
remains to be checked (transcription agent tasked with it).

## Matt 24:35-36 (Howard p. 122)

v35: והשמים והארץ יעברו — "and the heavens and the earth shall pass
away." (No "but my words shall not pass away.")

v36: ומהיום ההוא ומהעת ההיא אין מי שיודע ולא מלאכי השמים אלא האב בלבד
— "And **FROM that day and FROM that time** (מ prefix on both nouns),
there is none who knows — not even the messengers of the heavens — but
the Father alone." Supports the author's Matt 24:36 doctrine in the
letters themselves: from THAT day (the heaven-and-earth passing of v35,
the immediately preceding clause) forward, no one knows. Also: NO
"neither the Son." Shem Tov's own section break (פרק) falls AFTER v36 —
the structure binds v36 backward to v35, not forward to the Noah unit
(v37, which reads "the DAYS of the Son of Man," not "coming").

Nearby: v31 gathers the chosen "with a SHOFAR (בשופר) and a great
voice… from the four winds of the heavens"; v33 "near to the GATES"
(לשערים).

## Back-translation / doctrinal-crafting assessment

Layered answer; evidence read directly from Howard's own pages:

1. **Not Shem Tov's own composition.** Howard pp. 178-179: Shem Tov's
   marginal comments ATTACK readings in his own Matthew (e.g. after
   21:9 he faults Matthew/John against Zech 9:9 aton/chamor). A
   polemicist does not forge a text and then refute it; he transmits
   what he received. Howard: "this section of Matthew was not
   translated into Hebrew by Shem-Tob."
2. **Ancient textual DNA.** Howard pp. 204-205: agreements with the
   Gospel of Thomas (22), Codex Bezae (15), Marcion (17), Old Latin
   (48), Old Syriac, Pseudo-Clementines — witnesses lost or obscure
   until modern recovery, unavailable to a 14th-c. Spanish rabbi.
3. **The drift runs the other way.** Howard p. 204: du Tillet (1555) is
   "an extensive revision of the Shem-Tob type text… designed to bring
   the Hebrew into agreement with the canonical Greek and Latin texts."
   Documented direction of correction = TOWARD the Greek. Later Shem
   Tov mss assimilate likewise. So where a reading deviates from the
   Greek, it is the EARLIER stratum, not the later hand.
4. **Criterion of embarrassment at 23:3.** The custodians of this text
   were rabbinic Jews who lived by the takkanot. "Do what Moses says,
   not their takkanot" put in Jesus' mouth is AGAINST the transmitters'
   own interest — the reading a rabbinic hand would soften (toward the
   Greek "do what they say," which validates rabbinic authority), never
   invent. A Christian back-translator had no exemplar for it either —
   his Greek/Vulgate reads plural. Nobody with motive had the source;
   nobody with the source had motive.
5. **Mechanics favor Hebrew→Greek.** יאמר→εἴπωσιν needs only a
   translator normalizing the verb to the nearer plural antecedent (or
   a vav's difference in his copy); תקנותיהם ומעשיהם collapsing to ἔργα
   is ordinary translational flattening of a technical term. The
   reverse direction requires deliberate invention.
6. **Honesty column.** The text is NOT simply "the original Matthew":
   it is a medieval copy with abbreviations (יש"ו), Romance glosses
   (the tzitziot gloss at 23:5), harmonistic touches, and scholarly
   dispute (Petersen argues medieval harmony-tradition dependence
   against Howard). Use it as this book uses the LXX and DSS: an elder
   WITNESS to be weighed reading-by-reading — every occurrence
   gathered, counter-texts weighed — not a new autograph. For 23:3,
   24:29, 24:36, and 25:1 the directional tests all point to old
   readings the Greek flattened, and each also passes the book's own
   canon-coherence test.
