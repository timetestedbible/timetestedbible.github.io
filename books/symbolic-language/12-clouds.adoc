The sea chapter made a promise: the clouds would get a chapter of their own.
Here it is, and it can open with the verse that gives the whole finding away — the psalm that hangs the last chapter and this one on a single line:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 104:3-4]
____
Who layeth the beams of his chambers in the waters: who maketh the *clouds his chariot*: who walketh upon the sym:sym-wings[*wings of the wind*]: Who maketh his angels *spirits*; his ministers a flaming fire.
____

The chariot, the wind's wings, the spirits — the four winds chapter just read the chariots of Zechariah as __“the four *spirits* of the heavens”__ (Zechariah 6:5), and here the psalm parks the whole fleet in one verse.
A cloud in Scripture is scarcely ever weather.
It is a *vehicle* — the chariot of God — and once you see the chariot, you will find the passenger list runs through the whole Bible.

[discrete]
=== His Chariot

Start with the plain statements.
__“Behold, the LORD *rideth upon a swift cloud*, and shall come into sym:sym-egypt[Egypt]”__ (Isaiah 19:1) — not appears over, not hovers: *rides*, the verb of a mounted king.
__“There is none like unto the God of Jeshurun, who *rideth upon the heaven* in thy help”__ (Deuteronomy 33:26); __“extol him that *rideth upon the heavens*”__ (Psalm 68:4) — and the same psalm counts the motor pool: __“the *chariots of God* are twenty thousand, even thousands of angels”__ (Psalm 68:17).
David saw the ride itself: __“he *rode upon a cherub*, and did fly: and he was seen upon the *wings of the wind*”__ (2 Samuel 22:11) — the living chariot of Ezekiel's visions, the wind for wings.
Nahum says even the clouds' place in the picture: __“the LORD hath his way in the whirlwind and in the storm, and the *clouds are the dust of his feet*”__ (Nahum 1:3) — He is not sitting still in the sky; He is *traveling*, and the clouds are the dust His travel kicks up.
The figure is so fixed that Jeremiah reaches for it even to describe an invader: __“he shall come up *as clouds*, and his *chariots* shall be as a whirlwind”__ (Jeremiah 4:13) — cloud and chariot, one breath, the text's own parallel columns.

[discrete]
=== The Pillar That Drives

Israel spent forty years following the proof.
The pillar of cloud __“went before them by day… *to lead them the way*”__ (Exodus 13:21) — a vehicle with a heading.
At the sea it *maneuvered*: the cloud __“removed and went behind them… and it came *between* the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel; and it was a *cloud and darkness* to them, but it gave *light by night* to these”__ (Exodus 14:19-20) — it repositioned from van to rear guard, and ran dark on one side and lit on the other, which no weather does.
It carried an occupant — the Angel of God rode in it, and __“he spake unto them in the *cloudy pillar*”__ (Psalm 99:7); __“Lo, I come unto thee in a *thick cloud*”__ (Exodus 19:9).
And its motion was the nation's marching order:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 40:36-37]
____
And when the cloud was *taken up* from over the tabernacle, the children of Israel *went onward* in all their journeys: But if the cloud were *not taken up*, then they *journeyed not* till the day that it was taken up.
____

When the vehicle moves, the camp moves.
When it parks — filling the tabernacle, filling the temple so that __“the priests could not stand to minister”__ (1 Kings 8:10-11) — the Presence has arrived and the journey rests.

[discrete]
=== The Passenger List

Now watch who boards.
Daniel sees the destination of all history in transit terms: __“one like the *Son of man* came *with the clouds of heaven*, and came to the Ancient of days”__ (Daniel 7:13) — and Jesus claims the seat by name: __“Hereafter shall ye see the Son of man… *coming in the clouds of heaven*”__ (Matthew 26:64; Luke 21:27; Revelation 1:7).
At the ascension the vehicle pulled up in front of witnesses: __“while they beheld, he was taken up; and a *cloud received him* out of their sight”__ — and the angels file the return schedule on the spot: he __“shall *so come in like manner* as ye have seen him go”__ (Acts 1:9, 11).
The two witnesses ride the same line: __“they *ascended up to heaven in a cloud*; and their enemies beheld them”__ (Revelation 11:12).
The reaper of the last sym:sym-harvest[harvest] works *from* one: __“behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one *sat* like unto the Son of man… and he *thrust in his sickle* on the earth”__ (Revelation 14:14-16) — the cloud as working platform, the combine of the end of the age.
Even the disciples were passengers once, and did not enjoy the boarding: at the transfiguration __“there came a cloud, and overshadowed them: and they *feared as they entered into the cloud*.
And there came a *voice out of the cloud*”__ (Luke 9:34-35).
Elijah left in the same fleet's colors — __“a *chariot of fire*, and horses of fire… and Elijah went up by a whirlwind”__ (2 Kings 2:11) — the chariot, the wind: the last chapter's vehicles and this one's, dispatched together.

[discrete]
=== Clouds Made of People

But the sea chapter left one thread hanging on purpose, and it is time to pull it.
Where do clouds come from?
They rise from the waters: __“he causeth the *vapours to ascend* from the ends of the earth”__ (Jeremiah 10:13) — and Elijah's servant watched it happen: __“Behold, there ariseth a little cloud *out of the sea*, like a man's hand”__ (1 Kings 18:44).
The sym:sym-sea[sea], this book has read from the beginning, is the mass of the nations — so what is a cloud, in that grammar, but *what has risen out of the sea*: a multitude drawn up out of the peoples, airborne?

Scripture uses exactly that figure.
The writer of Hebrews looks at the assembled faithful of every age and calls them __“so great a *cloud of witnesses*”__ (Hebrews 12:1) — a cloud made of people.
Isaiah watches the exiles come home flying: __“Who are these that *fly as a cloud*, and as the doves to their windows?”__ (Isaiah 60:8).
And the counterfeit multitude gets the same figure with the sign reversed: Gog comes up __“like a *storm*… like a *cloud* to cover the land, thou, and all thy bands, and *many people* with thee”__ (Ezekiel 38:9, 16) — an army is a cloud in motion; and the false teacher is __“a cloud *without water*, carried about of winds”__ (Jude 12; Proverbs 25:14) — the vehicle that carries nothing, blown instead of driven.

Now the sea chapter's boldest promise can be paid in full.
__“Then we which are alive and remain shall be *caught up* together with them *in the clouds*, to meet the Lord *in the air*”__ (1 Thessalonians 4:17).
Read it with the vocabulary in hand.
The Lord comes the way the angels filed it — in the clouds, in like manner — and His people are *caught up in the clouds*: the gathering boards the transport, the way Elijah boarded, the way the two witnesses board, the way the disciples once entered the cloud trembling and heard the voice inside.
And they themselves, drawn up out of every nation, are the vapour the sea finally yields: the *cloud of witnesses* airborne at last, riding to meet the King on His own chariot-road.
The verse is not about escaping creation; it is about the King's convoy forming in the sky.

[discrete]
=== The Covering on the Road

One more office, and it hands the baton forward.
Over Zion restored, __“the LORD will create upon every dwelling place… a *cloud* and smoke by day… and there shall be a tabernacle for a *shadow* in the daytime from the heat, and for a *place of refuge*”__ (Isaiah 4:5-6) — the chariot parked over the camp becomes its covering, as the pillar stood between Israel and Egypt, dark to the pursuer and light to the pursued (Exodus 14:20).
The cloud that carries also covers; the shadow it casts is a defence — and the sym:sym-shadow[shadow] has a chapter of its own ahead.
And when the cloud has watered the earth on its circuit — for the rain comes down __“and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth, that it may give *seed to the sower*”__ (Isaiah 55:10) — what springs up under it is the next chapter's symbol: the trees.
