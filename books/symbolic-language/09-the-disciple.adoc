The gospel's last command is to *disciple* the nations, and everyone thinks he knows what the word means: a follower, a learner, a student of Jesus.
The dictionary is not wrong; it is empty.
A learner of *what*?
Scripture does not leave the answer open — it defines the word exactly once, and it puts the definition in the mouth of the law itself:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 8:16]
____
Bind up the testimony, *seal the law* among my *disciples*.
____

The Old Testament's word for disciples — _limmudim_, the taught ones — appears here in the law's own custody clause.
A disciple is where the law is kept when the testimony is bound: the scroll sealed in a living person.
Isaiah uses the word once more, as a promise: __“all thy children shall be *taught of the LORD*”__ (Isaiah 54:13) — _limmudei YHWH_, the LORD's own disciples — and Jesus quotes that very line for His hearers (John 6:45).

[discrete]
=== The Gates

Jesus did not describe discipleship as a mood of admiration.
He stated its terms, and three times He said *cannot*:

* __“If any man come to me, and *hate not* his father, and mother… he *cannot* be my disciple”__ (Luke 14:26)
* __“whosoever doth not *bear his cross*, and come after me, *cannot* be my disciple”__ (Luke 14:27)
* __“whosoever he be of you that *forsaketh not all* that he hath, he *cannot* be my disciple”__ (Luke 14:33)

These are gates, not growth curves — and inside the gates He named the marks: __“If ye *continue in my word*, then are ye my disciples *indeed*”__ (John 8:31); __“By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have sym:sym-love[love] one to another”__ (John 13:35) — and love is the keeping of the commandments (1 John 5:3); __“Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much sym:sym-fruit[fruit]; so shall ye be my disciples”__ (John 15:8) — and fruit is works.
_Indeed_ is the word to weigh: it is spoken to men who had just *believed* (John 8:30-31) — believed as a profession believes: His word accounted factual and worth trusting, an assent even __“the devils”__ manage, who __“believe, and tremble”__ (James 2:19).
Scripture's full *belief* walks: __“He that *believeth* on the Son hath eternal life; but he that *obeyeth not* the Son shall not see life”__ (John 3:36, ASV) — one verse, and the opposite of believing is disobeying.
So He hands the professors the walking itself as the test: continue in the word, and __“then are ye my disciples indeed.”__

[discrete]
=== Baptized into the Name

The commission binds the entry rite to the definition: __“baptizing them *in the* sym:sym-name[name] of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost”__ (Matthew 28:19).
The sym:sym-name[NAME] is the covenant — and baptism places the disciple *into* it: __“buried with him by baptism… even so we also should *walk* in newness of life”__ (Romans 6:4); __“as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have *put on* Christ”__ (Galatians 3:27) — the covenant worn as a sym:sym-garment[garment].
And the covenant one is baptized into has stated content:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 31:33]
____
this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel… I will put my *law* in their inward parts, and *write it in their hearts*.
____

The new covenant is not the law's retirement; it is the law's change of address — from tables of stone to the tables of the heart.
Jeremiah's promise and Isaiah's seal are one act described twice: the law, written within.
To be baptized into the Name is to enroll in that covenant — the heart handed in as the next table to be written.

[discrete]
=== As His Master

The church has a word for the goal of discipleship — *Christlikeness* — and the word is correct; it has simply been left undefined.
Jesus defined it:

[quote.scripture, Luke 6:40]
____
The disciple is not above his master: but every one that is *perfect* shall be *as his master*.
____

The disciple's finish line is likeness.
Then everything hangs on one question: what is the Master like?
He is the One of whom the Father said, __“obey his voice… for *my name is in him*”__ (Exodus 23:21); who kept His own in that name (John 17:11); and who set the pattern in a single sentence:

[quote.scripture, John 15:10]
____
If ye *keep my commandments*, ye shall abide in my love; even as *I have kept my Father's commandments*, and abide in his love.
____

He kept the law — perfectly: __“Which of you convinceth me of sin?”__ (John 8:46); not to destroy but to fulfil (Matthew 5:17).
To become like Him is therefore not a sentiment; it has exact content: __“He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also *so to walk, even as he walked*”__ (1 John 2:6).
He walked in the commandments.
Christlikeness *is* the law sealed within — the consensus's own word, given the Master's own content.

[discrete]
=== The Name on the Forehead

The road that begins in baptism ends where the next chapter, link:/books/symbolic-language/the-seal/[The Seal], ends: __“a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having *his Father's name written in their foreheads*”__ (Revelation 14:1); __“I will *write upon him the name* of my God”__ (Revelation 3:12).
Baptized into the Name; perfected as the Master in whom the Name is; sealed with the Name at the last — the disciple's whole arc is one Name carried from water to forehead, and the Name carries the law.
Seal the law among my disciples: the commission, Isaiah's clause, and the 144,000 are one sentence spoken three times.
