__“Judgment”__ lands on the modern ear as a threat.
It is the gavel, the sentence, the Day — a word for condemnation, so that __“do not judge”__ has become, for many, the only commandment left standing.
__“Justice”__ fares no better: an abstraction, argued about, marched for, never quite located.
But Scripture does something with these words that neither sense allows.
It commands them — as deeds: __“to *do* justice and judgment”__ (Genesis 18:19), __“*execute* judgment”__ (Jeremiah 22:3), __“*keep* ye judgment”__ (Isaiah 56:1).
You cannot do a condemnation or keep an abstraction.
The word __“judgment”__ falls in over four hundred verses; gather them, and both words come back from Scripture defined — and joined.

Start with __“justice,”__ and with a wobble in the English of the kind the sym:sym-shadow[shadow] chapter taught us to treat as a signpost.
Set these two commands side by side:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 18:19]
____
For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the sym:sym-way[way] of the LORD, to do *justice* and judgment…
____

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 22:3]
____
Thus saith the LORD; Execute ye judgment and sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], and deliver the spoiled out of the hand of the oppressor: and do no wrong, do no violence to the sym:sym-stranger[stranger], the sym:sym-fatherless[fatherless], nor the sym:sym-widow[widow]…
____

__“Justice and judgment”__; __“judgment and righteousness.”__
In the Hebrew these are the same two words — the word rendered __“justice”__ in Abraham’s verse is the word rendered __“righteousness”__ in Jeremiah’s.
The Psalter says the same sentence twice, once under each rendering: __“*Justice* and judgment are the habitation of thy throne”__ (Psalm 89:14) and __“sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] and judgment are the habitation of his throne”__ (Psalm 97:2).
And Isaiah puts both renderings in a single verse: __“Keep ye judgment, and do *justice*: for my salvation is near to come, and my sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] to be revealed”__ (Isaiah 56:1).
So the first word resolves at once, because Scripture has already defined sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness] out of its own mouth: __“it shall be our sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], if we observe to *do all these commandments*”__ (Deuteronomy 6:25); __“all thy commandments are sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness]”__ (Psalm 119:172).
Justice is righteousness under a second English name — not an abstraction to be argued about but the commandments done, toward God and toward one’s neighbor.
That is why justice can be commanded, learned, and performed.

Now __“judgment”__ — Hebrew _mishpat_ — and watch what company it keeps.
Moses uses it for the law itself:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 4:8]
____
And what nation is there so great, that hath *statutes and judgments* so righteous as *all this law*, which I set before you this day?
____

The statutes and judgments are taught __“for to *do* them”__ (Deuteronomy 4:1).
The longest psalm prays the word over and over as a name for God’s own word: __“the *judgments of thy mouth*”__ (Psalm 119:13); __“every one of thy righteous judgments endureth *for ever*”__ (Psalm 119:160) — sworn to and kept like any commandment (Psalm 119:106).
A judgment, in Scripture, is not the gavel falling; it is the *ruling* — what the law declares to be due.
That is why judgment can be __“kept”__: to keep judgment is to render what God’s rulings say is owed.
And it is why the loss of judgment is described as a failure of law, not a failure of courts: __“the *law* is slacked, and *judgment* doth never go forth”__ (Habakkuk 1:4).
Judgment goes forth from the law the way a verdict goes forth from a statute.

Owed to whom?
Scripture answers with a list we have met before:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 7:9-10]
____
Thus speaketh the LORD of hosts, saying, *Execute true judgment*, and shew mercy and compassions every man to his brother:
And oppress not the sym:sym-widow[widow], nor the sym:sym-fatherless[fatherless], the sym:sym-stranger[stranger], nor the sym:sym-poor[poor]…
____

Execute true judgment — and immediately, the four names of the gleaning corner.
This is judgment’s home address in Scripture.
God __“doth execute the *judgment* of the fatherless and widow”__ (Deuteronomy 10:18); Israel is told to __“seek judgment, relieve the oppressed, *judge the* sym:sym-fatherless[fatherless], *plead for the* sym:sym-widow[widow]”__ (Isaiah 1:17); and the indictment of the princes is exactly its absence — __“they judge *not* the fatherless, neither doth the cause of the widow come unto them”__ (Isaiah 1:23), __“the *right* of the needy do they not judge”__ (Jeremiah 5:28) — where __“right”__ is _mishpat_ again: the needy man’s judgment is his *due*, the portion God’s law rules to be his.
Job wore it: __“I put on sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], and it clothed me: my *judgment* was as a robe and a diadem”__ — and then the inventory of what the sym:sym-garment[garment] was made of: __“I delivered the poor that cried, and the fatherless… I was eyes to the blind, and feet was I to the lame… a father to the poor: and the cause which I knew not I *searched out*”__ (Job 29:12-16).
And God sets the crown on this meaning with a definition this book could not improve upon:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 22:15-16]
____
…did not thy father eat and drink, and *do judgment and justice*, and then it was well with him?
He *judged the cause of the poor and needy*; then it was well with him: was not this to sym:sym-knowing[know] me? saith the LORD.
____

To do judgment for the poor _is to know God_ — and sym:sym-knowing[knowing], in the Bible’s symbolic language, is covenant relationship proven by obedience.
The man who renders the weak their due is not merely imitating God’s character; he is inside it.

Ezekiel writes the whole equation out as a case study, and adds a third English rendering to our pair:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 18:5-9]
____
But if a man be just, and do that which is *lawful and right*,
…and hath not oppressed any, but hath *restored to the debtor his pledge*… hath *given his bread to the hungry*, and hath *covered the naked with a garment*;
He that hath not given forth upon *usury*… hath *executed true judgment* between man and man,
Hath *walked in my statutes*, and hath *kept my judgments*, to deal truly; he is *just*, he shall surely *live*, saith the Lord GOD.
____

__“Lawful and right”__ is — once more — the same Hebrew pair: _mishpat_ and _tsedaqah_, judgment and justice, rendered now a third way.
Three English costumes, one Hebrew command; and Ezekiel’s inventory shows what the command looks like on a weekday: the pledge restored, the hungry fed, the naked covered, no usury taken, honest rulings between man and man — the statutes walked in, the judgments kept.
__“He is just, he shall surely live.”__

Jesus reads the word exactly this way.
His sharpest woe against the Pharisees hangs on it:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 23:23]
____
Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the *weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and* sym:sym-faith[faith]: these ought ye to have *done*, and not to leave the other undone.
____

Judgment is a matter _of the law_ — one of its weightiest — and it is a thing left _undone_, not a doctrine left unbelieved.
Isaiah had said the Messiah would come for precisely this, in parallel columns that seal the word’s meaning: __“he shall bring forth *judgment* to the Gentiles… till he have set *judgment* in the earth: and the sym:sym-island[isles] shall wait for his *law*”__ (Isaiah 42:1-4) — judgment answered by law, line upon line — and Matthew quotes the passage of Jesus (Matthew 12:18-20).
The Branch of David __“shall execute *judgment and justice* in the earth”__ (Jeremiah 23:5); His throne is established __“with judgment and with justice from henceforth even for ever”__ (Isaiah 9:7); thrones in Scripture stand on nothing else, for __“justice and judgment are the *habitation* of thy throne”__ (Psalm 89:14).
Solomon was given his kingdom for this stated purpose — __“therefore made he thee king, *to do judgment and justice*”__ (1 Kings 10:9) — and David held it the same way: he __“executed judgment and justice unto *all* his people”__ (2 Samuel 8:15).
A king who will not render the law’s due is not a flawed ruler; he is a throne with the habitation missing.

English has drifted on this word in one more direction, and testing it recovers a whole office.
To the modern ear a judge only sentences; in Scripture, to judge is to _rule_.
The book of Judges is not a book of magistrates: __“the LORD raised up judges, which *delivered* them out of the hand of those that spoiled them”__ (Judges 2:16) — Othniel __“judged Israel, and *went out to war*”__ (Judges 3:10), and Nehemiah calls the whole line __“*saviours*, who saved them”__ (Nehemiah 9:27).
When Israel demanded a monarchy, they defined the word for us: __“make us a king to *judge* us… that our king may judge us, and *go out before us, and fight our battles*”__ (1 Samuel 8:5, 20).
Isaiah stacks the offices into a single confession: __“the LORD is our *judge*, the LORD is our *lawgiver*, the LORD is our *king*; he will *save* us”__ (Isaiah 33:22).
To judge is to govern by the law — to guide, defend, and set right — which is why Moses could describe a day of judging as a day of teaching: __“I judge between one and another, and I do *make them know the statutes* of God, and his laws”__ (Exodus 18:16).
And it is why God describes covenant-making itself in judgment-language.
Hear Him promise a second Exodus:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 20:35-37]
____
And I will bring you into the wilderness of the people, and there will I *plead with you face to face*.
Like as I *pleaded with your fathers* in the wilderness of the land of Egypt, so will I plead with you, saith the Lord GOD.
And I will cause you to *pass under the rod*, and I will bring you into the *bond of the covenant*:
____

__“Plead”__ is, in the Hebrew, the judge-verb itself — more literally, _I will enter into judgment with you face to face_ — and the sentence before names what that judging is: __“with a mighty hand… will I *rule over* you”__ (Ezekiel 20:33).
When God entered into judgment with Israel in the wilderness, He was not condemning Israel; He was becoming her King — disputes judged rightly by His law, the flock passed under the shepherd’s rod, the people bound into the covenant.
Condemnation falls only where the rule is refused: __“I will purge out from among you the rebels”__ (Ezekiel 20:38).
Judgment is the rule; condemnation is what the rule does to lawbreakers — one verdict the office renders, not the office itself.

Read with that office in view, Scripture does with this word what our English no longer permits.
The righteous _pray for_ judgment: __“*Judge me*, O God, and plead my cause… O *deliver* me”__ (Psalm 43:1; so also 26:1; 35:24) — words no defendant says to a hanging judge, and exactly what an oppressed subject says to a good king.
Creation _rejoices_ at its approach: __“let the heavens rejoice… then shall all the sym:sym-tree[trees] of the wood rejoice before the LORD: for he cometh, for he cometh to *judge the earth*”__ (Psalm 96:11-13) — the field is joyful because the Judge is the rightful King arriving to put the world in order.
And judgment is a thing _given to_ the saints as an inheritance: __“*judgment was given* to the saints of the most High; and the time came that the saints *possessed the kingdom*”__ (Daniel 7:22) — thrones, in Revelation’s replay of that scene: __“I saw thrones, and they sat upon them, and *judgment was given* unto them… and they lived and *reigned* with Christ”__ (Revelation 20:4); __“ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, *judging* the twelve tribes of Israel”__ (Matthew 19:28).
To receive judgment is to be seated — given a shepherd’s share in the King’s own office.

And now the Day itself comes back into focus, because the God of Scripture judges with the same word.
Abraham’s question at Sodom — the first courtroom scene in the Bible — asks it plainly: __“Shall not the *Judge* of all the earth *do right*?”__
(Genesis 18:25), where __“do right”__ is, in the Hebrew, _do mishpat_: the Judge of all the earth does judgment, renders what is due, and can be argued with on exactly those terms — as Abraham then does.
Solomon ends his book on the two clauses this book keeps finding joined: __“sym:sym-fear[Fear] God, and *keep his commandments*: for this is the whole duty of man.
For God shall bring every work into *judgment*”__ (Ecclesiastes 12:13-14).
The judgment men fear as an alien terror is nothing but the rulings they were handed at Sinai, applied at last by the Judge who wrote them — __“So speak ye, and so do, as they that shall be judged by the *law of* sym:sym-liberty[liberty]”__ (James 2:12).
Judgment Day holds no surprises for the man who kept the judgments.

What God feels about all this, He states in the first person.
__“To do justice and judgment is *more acceptable* to the LORD *than sacrifice*”__ (Proverbs 21:3) — the altar itself ranks below it, as obedience ranks above sacrifice.
__“What doth the LORD require of thee, but to *do justly*, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?”__
(Micah 6:8).
And when God writes His own résumé, this is it:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 9:24]
____
But let him that glorieth glory in this, that he understandeth and sym:sym-knowing[knoweth] me, that I am the LORD which exercise lovingkindness, *judgment*, and sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], in the earth: for in these things I *delight*, saith the LORD.
____

Against that delight stands the collapse, and Isaiah paints it in the Bible’s own symbolic vocabulary: __“the sym:sym-way[way] of sym:sym-peace[peace] they know not; and there is no *judgment* in their goings: they have made them crooked paths”__ — __“judgment is turned away backward, and *justice* standeth afar off: for sym:sym-truth[truth] is fallen in the street”__ (Isaiah 59:8, 14).
Way, judgment, justice, truth — the symbols fall together, because they were never separate things: the way is walked, the truth is the law, and judgment and justice are the law rendered to one’s neighbor.
A generation that calls evil good hears no answer but Malachi’s echo of its own voice: __“Where is the God of judgment?”__
(Malachi 2:17).

So the two words stand defined, and the definition was hiding in the book’s first use of them.
When God says why He chose Abraham, He names the whole walk in one clause: __“they shall keep the sym:sym-way[way] of the LORD, *to do justice and judgment*”__ (Genesis 18:19) — the first time Scripture speaks of the way of the LORD, it defines it, and the definition is these two words.
Judgment is the law’s ruling — what God declares due, kept like a commandment and owed first to the widow, the fatherless, the stranger, and the poor.
Justice is righteousness wearing its public name — the commandments done where a neighbor can be wronged or righted.
To do them is to know God; to abandon them is to slacken the law; and to be judged, at the last, is only to meet again the rulings we were given to keep — the perfect law of liberty, which is where we turn next.
