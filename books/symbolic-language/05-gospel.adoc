Ask an AI for the gospel in a sentence, and it will hand you the average of what most of Christianity teaches:

[quote, Grok, asked for the gospel in one sentence]
____
The Gospel is the good news that Jesus Christ, the Son of God, died for our sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, and rose again on the third day, so that through repentance and faith in Him alone we can be forgiven, reconciled to God, and given eternal life.
____

People will tell you this is the core message, summarized by Paul in 1 Corinthians 15 and proclaimed throughout the New Testament.


[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 15:1-4, NKJV]
____
Moreover, brethren, I declare to you the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] which I preached to you… by which also you are saved, if you hold fast that word which I preached to you — unless you sym:sym-belief[believed] in vain.
For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins *according to the Scriptures*, and that He was buried, and that He rose again the third day *according to the Scriptures*.
____
   
Stop here and you might think the gospel is the death and resurrection themselves; but Paul anchors it twice — *according to the Scriptures* — and the Scriptures he preached from were the Old Testament.

The word __“gospel”__ falls in ninety-eight verses of the King James, and its verb — __“preach the gospel,”__ __“bring good tidings”__ — in dozens more; behind them stand one Greek word-family (_euangelion_, the good news; _euangelizō_, to announce it) and one Hebrew family (_basar_, to bear tidings; _besorah_, the tidings themselves).

Here are the titles the gospel wears, counted across the King James:

.The titles the gospel wears — and no title of death, cross, or forgiveness among them
[.prevalence-table%unbreakable, frame=topbot, grid=none, cols="6,^2", options="header"]
|===
| Title | Uses
| the gospel of Christ | 11
| the gospel of God | 7
| the gospel *of the kingdom* | 4
| the gospel of sym:sym-peace[peace] | 2
| the gospel of the grace of God | 1
| the gospel of your salvation | 1
| the *everlasting* gospel | 1
|===

The Hebrew Revelation gives that last title a body: where the Greek reads __“everlasting gospel,”__ the Hebrew text hands the messenger a certain *stone tablet* (_gilyon_) __“to make known to those dwelling upon the earth”__ (Revelation 14:6) — the good news, written in stone.

Of these, John the Baptist, Jesus, and His disciples preached the gospel *of the kingdom* — and they preached it *before* Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 4:23; 9:35]
____
And Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *of the kingdom*, and healing all manner of sickness and all manner of disease among the people.
____

Mark gives us the first clue what Jesus was saying as he preached the sym:sym-gospel[gospel]:

[quote.scripture, Mark 1:14-15]
____
Jesus came into Galilee, preaching the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *of the kingdom of God*, And saying, The time is fulfilled, and the *kingdom of God is at hand*: *repent ye, and* sym:sym-belief[believe] *the* sym:sym-gospel[gospel].
____

Hear what the gospel asks for.
Not __“accept this fact,”__ but __“repent ye, and believe”__ — turn, trust, and obey; for to sym:sym-belief[believe], as the link:/books/symbolic-language/knowing-faith-love-and-belief/[Knowing, Faith, Love, and Belief] chapter will prove, is to obey.

The word for preaching it is the herald’s word — the Greek _kēryssō_, what a royal crier does — he stands in the square and announces the decree of the one who sent him.
It is a king’s proclamation, and the fitting response to a king’s proclamation is allegiance.

Isaiah gives the publication its single sentence:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 52:7]
____
How beautiful upon the sym:sym-mountain[mountains] are the feet of him that sym:sym-gospel[bringeth good tidings], that publisheth sym:sym-peace[peace]; that sym:sym-gospel[bringeth good tidings] of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, *Thy God reigneth*!
____

There is the whole of it compressed to three words: __“Thy God reigneth.”__
The Hebrew verb here is _basar_; the Greek Old Testament renders it with _euangelizō_, the origin of the English _evangelize_; and Paul quotes this very verse as gospel-preaching: __“How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of sym:sym-peace[peace]”__ (Romans 10:15).

Look once more at the herald’s third clause: __“that publisheth salvation”__ — the Hebrew is _yeshuah_ (Jesus).
Read it as Israel heard it, and the herald publishes salvation; read it as the name it became, and the Old Testament’s gospel-verse publishes Jesus. 

Mark where the herald stands — upon the sym:sym-mountain[mountains]; what a mountain is, the link:/books/symbolic-language/mountain/[Mountain] chapter will prove.

Nahum carries Isaiah's verse almost word for word — and adds the hearer's marching orders:

[quote.scripture, Nahum 1:15]
____
Behold upon the sym:sym-mountain[mountains] the feet of him that bringeth sym:sym-gospel[good tidings], that publisheth sym:sym-peace[peace]! *O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows*: for the wicked shall no more pass through thee; he is utterly cut off.
____

If we are to hearken to the herald's cry, the stakes of understanding the symbolic language of the link:/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/[Sun, Moon, and Stars] run much higher.

When Jesus stood up in His hometown synagogue to announce His own commission, listen to which scripture He chose:

[quote.scripture, Luke 4:18]
____
The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to *preach the* sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *to the* sym:sym-poor[poor]; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach *deliverance to the captives*, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at sym:sym-liberty[liberty] them that are bruised,
____

The passage is Isaiah 61:1-2, and its closing proclamation — __“the *acceptable* sym:sym-year[year] *of the LORD*”__ — is the *year of release*, link:/books/symbolic-language/liberty/[the seventh year of Deuteronomy 15], when debts are cancelled and captives go free.footnote:fn05release[The full derivation is in the link:/books/symbolic-language/liberty/[Liberty] chapter.]
The gospel, in Jesus’ own chosen words, is a royal proclamation of release — and He sat down and told them, __“This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears”__ (Luke 4:21).
(That year of release is dated to the Day of Atonement, AD 29, in _Time Tested Tradition_; what matters here is which text Jesus reached for when He defined His gospel.)

Now the hinge that the common definition cannot survive.
Jesus preached this gospel — the whole of it — _before_ He died:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 4:17]
____
From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the *kingdom of heaven is at hand*.
____

From the opening of His ministry, years before the cross, He was already preaching the gospel of the kingdom (Matthew 4:23), and commanding hearers to __“sym:sym-belief[believe] the gospel”__ (Mark 1:15).
If the gospel were the message _about_ His death and resurrection, He could not have preached it for three years while both were still ahead of Him — nor commanded men to believe it before it existed.
Even when He told the twelve plainly what was ahead, __“they understood none of these things”__ (Luke 18:34).

What He preached was the arrival of God’s reign and the way of life under it: the kingdom and its terms.
The gospel is the message Jesus _taught_, not merely the message _about_ Jesus.

Nor was it new even to Him.
The book of Hebrews drops a line most readers pass over: the gospel was preached long before, to the generation in the wilderness —

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 4:2]
____
For unto us was the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *preached*, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with sym:sym-faith[faith] in them that heard it.
____

__“Unto them”__ is Israel at Sinai.
The same gospel-word describes what was proclaimed there — and what was proclaimed there was a kingdom:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 19:6]
____
And ye shall be unto me a *kingdom of priests*, and an *holy nation*.
____

God, having broken the siege of Egypt and set the captives at liberty, declares His reign over them and calls them to enter it as His kingdom.
That is the gospel of the kingdom — preached at Sinai.
Earlier still, to Abraham:

[quote.scripture, Galatians 3:8]
____
And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through sym:sym-faith[faith], *preached before the* sym:sym-gospel[gospel] unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall *all nations be blessed*.
____

__“Preached before the gospel”__ is a single Greek word, _proeuangelizomai_ — _pre-gospeled_.
Abraham received the gospel, and its reach — all nations — is the same reach it has from first to last.
One gospel runs the length of Scripture: Abraham, Sinai, Jesus, the apostles.
Revelation even gives it its proper adjective, and states its content one final time:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:6-7]
____
And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the *everlasting* sym:sym-gospel[gospel] to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people, Saying with a loud voice, sym:sym-fear[Fear] *God, and give glory to him*; for the hour of his sym:sym-judgment[judgment] is come: and sym:sym-worship[worship] *him that made* heaven, and earth, and the sym:sym-sea[sea], and the fountains of waters.
____

_Everlasting_ — a thing cannot be everlasting and also have begun at the cross.
And read what the everlasting gospel says when it opens its mouth: fear God, give Him glory, worship the Maker, the judgment is come.
The last gospel-preaching in the Bible is the herald’s cry of the reigning Creator — the same three words Isaiah gave it.

If the gospel announces a King’s reign, it necessarily carries terms, for a king reigns by covenant.
Here the threads draw together: the gospel of the kingdom is the _covenant_ of the kingdom.
And the covenant, Scripture is careful to say, was never scrapped and replaced — it was _renewed_.
The promise everyone calls the __“new covenant”__ rests on a Hebrew word, _chadash_, whose root means renew, repair, rebuild:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 51:10]
____
Create in me a clean heart, O God; and *renew* a right spirit within me.
____

[quote.scripture, 2 Chronicles 24:4]
____
And it came to pass after this, that Joash was minded to *repair* the house of the LORD.
____

David does not ask for a spirit he never had; Joash does not build a different temple.
_Chadash_ restores what already stands.
So the __“new”__ covenant is the _same_ covenant, renewed — and Jeremiah, who gave the promise, says precisely what changes and what does not:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 31:31-33]
____
Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a *new* covenant with the house of Israel… Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers… *which my covenant they brake*… I will put my *law* in their inward parts, and *write it in their hearts*; and will be their God, and they shall be my people.
____

The fault lay not with the covenant but with the people — __“my covenant they brake.”__
The law is the same law; the terms are the same terms.
What changes is the location — from tablets of stone to the inward parts, from a code outside us to a writing within.
Same covenant, new surface.
This is why Jesus could insist, in the very sermon where He pressed the law to its depths:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 5:17]
____
Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to *fulfil*.
____

Not destroy — _fulfil_.
And __“fulfil”__ is the Greek _plēroō_: to fill full, to fill to the brim — the exact opposite of __“destroy”__ (_kataluō_, to tear down).
Jesus did not come to abolish the law, nor to complete it and retire it, but to fill it full — to preach it in its fullness, packing back the weight the surface reading had drained away.

Filling full is precisely what He does next: __“Ye have heard that it was said… but I say unto you,”__ pressing past the act to the heart behind it (Matthew 5:21-22) — filling each commandment full, writing it where Jeremiah said it would be written.
The Sermon on the Mount is the renewed covenant in motion: the same Torah, filled full and taught from the inside.
_This_ is the message Jesus taught — not a new religion about Himself, but the ancient covenant of the kingdom, renewed and written on the heart.

The gospel, then, is no abolition of the law of Moses but its establishment — __“we establish the law,”__ as Paul says (Romans 3:31).
The gospel of the kingdom _is_ the law of that kingdom: the Torah preached in the wilderness, the constitution of God’s reign, the very law the prophets foresaw going forth from Zion when the King at last reigns from His mountain:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 2:3]
____
…Come ye, and let us go up to the sym:sym-mountain[mountain] of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his sym:sym-way[ways], and we will walk in his paths: for *out of Zion shall go forth the law*, and the word of the LORD from Jerusalem.
____

One law, from first to last: preached in the wilderness, written now upon the heart, and one day issuing from Zion over all the earth (Micah 4:2 says the same).

This is why Scripture speaks of the gospel in a way that makes no sense for a mere announcement of facts: it speaks of _obeying_ it.
__“They have not all *obeyed* the gospel”__ (Romans 10:16); the Lord returns __“in flaming fire taking vengeance on them… that *obey not* the gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ”__ (2 Thessalonians 1:8); and Peter asks, __“what shall the end be of them that *obey not* the gospel of God?”__
(1 Peter 4:17).
Information is believed or doubted; nobody obeys a news bulletin.
But a king’s proclamation with terms is obeyed or defied — and Scripture says the gospel is the kind of thing men will be judged for not obeying.

Mark how Paul glosses his own words inside that first verse:

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:16]
____
But they have not all *obeyed* the sym:sym-gospel[gospel].
For Esaias saith, Lord, who hath sym:sym-belief[believed] our report?
____

Obeyed in one sentence, believed in the next — one act under two names.
That is what sym:sym-belief[believe] means in Scripture’s mouth: not thinking a thing true, but trusting enough to obey.
The equation runs both directions — the Greek under __“sym:sym-belief[believeth] not”__ in John 3:36 link:/books/symbolic-language/knowing-faith-love-and-belief/[locks it from the other side].footnote:fn05belief[The word is opened in the link:/books/symbolic-language/knowing-faith-love-and-belief/[Knowing, Faith, Love, and Belief] chapter.]
To believe the gospel is to obey the King.

Where, then, is the cross in all this?
Exactly where Scripture sets it — as the act that secures the kingdom, not as the whole content of the announcement:

[quote.scripture, John 19:30]
____
When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, *It is finished*: and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.
____

__“It is finished”__ completes the work that ratifies the covenant in blood.
The cross is indispensable — it is how the kingdom is purchased and the renewed covenant sealed.
But the purchase is not the proclamation.
The proof stands where we left it: Jesus preached the whole gospel of the kingdom for years before Calvary.

Here the fair objection rises: does not Paul himself define the gospel as the death and resurrection?
He does say it plainly:

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 15:3-4]
____
For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that *Christ died for our* sym:sym-sin[sins] *according to the scriptures*; And that he was buried, and that he *rose again the third day according to the scriptures*.
____

Read closely, this confirms rather than overturns what we have seen.
Twice Paul anchors it __“according to the scriptures”__ — the death and resurrection fulfill what the prophets had already announced; they are how the long-promised kingdom is secured, not a new message that cancels it.
And he is expressly defending the resurrection here — the burden of the whole of 1 Corinthians 15 — summarizing the hinge of the gospel, not redrawing its borders.
Where Paul opens a letter by stating his gospel at length, the kingdom is unmistakable:

[quote.scripture, Romans 1:1-4]
____
Paul… separated unto the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *of God*, (Which he had *promised afore by his prophets in the holy scriptures*,) Concerning his Son Jesus Christ our Lord, which was made of the *seed of David* according to the flesh; And *declared to be the Son of God with power*… by the *resurrection from the dead*.
____

Every clause is kingdom language.
The gospel was __“promised afore by his prophets”__ — the same ancient announcement, not a novelty.
Its subject is the __“seed of David”__ — the rightful heir to the throne.
And the resurrection __“declared”__ Him __“the Son of God with power”__ — the enthronement of the King.

Paul’s gospel is that the Son of David has been raised and installed as Lord; the cross and the empty tomb are how He took the throne.
That is the gospel of the kingdom, told from the side of the King’s accession.

It is why Paul can stake everything on there being only one gospel — __“though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you… let him be accursed”__ (Galatians 1:8) — the very gospel, he reminds the same readers, that was __“preached before… unto Abraham”__ (Galatians 3:8).
And it is why, at the end of the age, the message has not changed:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:14]
____
And this sym:sym-gospel[gospel] *of the kingdom* shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall *the end come*.
____

The last gospel preached before the end still wears the only content-title it has ever worn.

Now return to the sentence the AI gave us, and grade it.
Every clause in it is true, and every clause can be found in Scripture — the death for sins, the burial, the third day, the call to repentance and faith.
But as a definition it fails the corpus, and the failure has a shape.

It is a report of events, where Scripture’s gospel is the declaration of a kingdom — a kingdom those events secured, ruled by the King who announced it, defined by the eternal law that is its constitution.
It is the gospel _about_ Jesus standing in for the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] Jesus _preached_ — the difference between telling a man that salvation exists and teaching him how to be saved.
It asks for belief and omits the obedience the gospel will judge; and it starts at the cross a message that Scripture calls everlasting, preached to Abraham, thundered at Sinai, and announced for three years by the King Himself.
The common sentence is a true part wearing the name of the whole — and __“believe,”__ its load-bearing word, we have already watched trade places with __“obey”__ in Paul’s own mouth: the trusting obedience the wilderness generation lacked (Hebrews 4:2), a symbol with a full study of its own ahead.

The word we thought we owned opens up like all the rest.
The sym:sym-mountain[mountain] is the kingdom; the gospel is the herald’s cry that the kingdom has come — __“Thy God reigneth”__ — published upon the mountains and carried to every nation: the siege broken, the captives freed, the covenant renewed onto hearts, the King enthroned.
Read it as a private transaction and it shrinks to a fraction of itself.
Read it as Scripture defines it, and the turning of the nations that closed the last chapter stands revealed for what it is: not the spread of information about a man, but the kingdom of God going forth to fill the earth.
