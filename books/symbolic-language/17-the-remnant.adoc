Ask almost anyone whether they expect to be saved, and they answer yes — quietly assuming they stand with the majority, on the safe side of whatever line God draws.
Scripture never lets that assumption stand.
Someone once asked Jesus the question outright:

[quote.scripture, Luke 13:23-24]
____
Lord, are there *few* that be saved?
And he said unto them, *Strive to enter in at the strait gate*: for many, I say unto you, will *seek to enter in, and shall not be able*.
____

He does not answer __“few”__ with a number and move on; He answers it with a warning, because the question is not academic — it is the one question every reader of this book has been silently asking since the first chapter.
This chapter gathers the sym:sym-remnant[remnant], the word this book has used since the sym:sym-widow[widow] gleaned the sym:sym-wings[edge] of the field, and asks what Scripture actually says about its size.

[discrete]
=== The Edge of the Field, by the Numbers

Return to the gleaning law that has run through this book from the household chapter onward:

[quote.scripture, Leviticus 19:9-10]
____
…thou shalt not wholly reap the *corners* of thy field… thou shalt leave them for the *poor and stranger*…
____

__“Corners”__ is _pe’ah_ — the edge — and a field’s edge is not a figure of speech but a shape, fixed by plain geometry.
Wheat and barley are sown in dense rows; leave the outer rows of a field unreaped, as the law commands, and the edge shrinks as a share of the whole the larger the field grows — roughly one row in ten on a single acre, and under one row in a thousand across a hundred.
The gleaning law does not merely say a remnant will be left; the ratio is built into the size of the field itself.
The larger the sym:sym-harvest[harvest], the smaller its edge — and __“the field is the world”__ (Matthew 13:38).

Isaiah paints the same ratio as an orchard stripped bare:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 17:5-6, ASV]
____
…as when the harvestman gathereth the *standing grain*, and his arm reapeth the *ears*…
Yet gleaning grapes shall be left in it, as the *shaking of an olive tree*, *two or three berries* in the top of the uppermost bough, *four or five* in the outmost fruitful branches…
____

This is not a metaphor but an orchardman’s fact: beat an olive tree for its harvest, and only the handful of berries too high and too outermost to reach are left clinging.
Two or three on the tallest bough — that is what an eyewitness generation would have pictured the moment they heard the word __“remnant.”__

[discrete]
=== A Fixed Ratio Kept Through Every Generation

The pattern is not a single bad century; Scripture keeps producing the same fraction wherever it counts.
Elijah, alone and despairing, cries __“I, even I only, am left”__ (1 Kings 19:10), and God corrects the arithmetic, not the despair:

[quote.scripture, 1 Kings 19:18]
____
Yet I have left me *seven thousand* in Israel, all the knees which have not bowed unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not kissed him.
____

Seven thousand sounds like a multitude until it is set against the nation it was drawn from — a northern kingdom of a million or more souls in Ahab’s day, by the most conservative reckonings of the record.
Seven thousand out of over a million is not a fraction anyone would guess from the word __“remnant”__ alone; and Paul, centuries later, refuses to treat it as a one-time low point:

[quote.scripture, Romans 11:5]
____
Even so then at this present time also there is a *remnant according to the election of grace*.
____

__“At this present time also”__ — the ratio of Elijah’s day is declared the standing pattern, not the exception; and Paul had already staked the same claim from Isaiah: __“Though the number of the children of Israel be as the *sand of the sea*, a *remnant* shall be saved”__ (Romans 9:27) — the sym:sym-sea[sea]-sand multitude and the remnant, side by side, in one sentence.
The city that goes out by a thousand comes home with a hundred; the one that goes out by a hundred comes home with ten (Amos 5:3) — Scripture’s arithmetic never inflates the remnant to comfort the reader.

[discrete]
=== Rarer Than Gold

Isaiah names the ratio outright, and reaches for the rarest thing he knows:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 13:12]
____
I will make a man more precious than *fine gold*; even a man than the *golden wedge of Ophir*.
____

Gold is the standing figure of scarcity because it is scarce: barely a few parts in a billion of the earth’s crust by mass, and even a rich vein yields only a small fraction of a percent of ore as pure metal.
The refined __“wedge of Ophir”__ — smelted, purified gold — is rarer still, for most of what is dug is not gold at all but the rock and waste around it.
Ezekiel names that waste directly, and names it Israel:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 22:18]
____
Son of man, the house of Israel is to me become *dross*: all they are brass, and tin, and iron, and lead, in the midst of the *furnace*; they are even the dross of silver.
____

__“Dross”__ is the slag skimmed off in smelting — the discarded majority of the mass — and ancient metallurgy typically drew only a small fraction of pure silver out of raw ore.
God is not describing a nation gone rotten at the edges; He is describing a smelting operation, and naming which part of the output is which.
Zechariah completes the picture with the operation’s own numbers:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 13:8-9]
____
…in all the land, saith the LORD, *two parts* therein shall be cut off and die; but the *third* shall be left therein.
And I will bring the *third part* through the fire, and will *refine them as silver is refined*, and will *try them as gold* is tried…
____

Two parts cut off; one part refined — and even that surviving third is not simply spared but put through the fire, exactly as ore is smelted before the metal is called pure.
Read this beside the treasure hidden in the sym:sym-field[field] of the sym:sym-gospel[gospel]’s own parables: the field is bought whole, and what is dug from it is not coin but ore, refined at cost.
The remnant is the gold; the furnace is not a threat added to the promise but the very method by which the promise is kept.

[discrete]
=== Ruth, the Remnant Enacted

Before the numbers, Scripture gave the remnant a face.
Ruth arrives in Bethlehem carrying every name of the gleaning household at once: poor, __“I went out full, and the LORD hath brought me home again empty”__ (Ruth 1:21); a sym:sym-stranger[stranger], __“seeing I am a stranger”__ (Ruth 2:10); a sym:sym-widow[widow], her husband dead (Ruth 1:5); and sym:sym-fatherless[fatherless] in every practical sense, having __“left thy father and thy mother, and the land of thy nativity”__ (Ruth 2:11).
And where does she glean?
In the field of Boaz — the _goel_, the kinsman-redeemer, the one who will restore her inheritance, marry her, and raise up seed.
The gleaner finds the Redeemer in the field.
She is the remnant in miniature: poor, widowed, fatherless, a stranger, gleaning the edge of the covenant land — and she does not merely survive the harvest; she enters the line of the Messiah (Matthew 1:5).
The gleaning law does not only fix how many survive the sym:sym-harvest[harvest]; Ruth shows who they are.

[discrete]
=== The Ratio Holds at Every Scale

Run the same test on the New Testament’s own crowds, and the ratio does not loosen.
Five thousand men, besides women and children, are fed on a hillside — a company that by any reasonable count ran into the tens of thousands with families included.
By the next morning, __“many of his disciples went back, and walked no more with him”__ (John 6:66), and Jesus turns to the twelve — not the thousands — and asks, __“Will ye also go away?”__
(John 6:67).
From a multitude to twelve within a day; and even among the twelve, __“one of you is a devil”__ (John 6:70) — eleven left, out of tens of thousands who had eaten their fill of bread the day before.
The same collapse repeats at the feeding of the four thousand.
It is not a single dramatic exception; it is the field’s edge, measured again on a different hillside.

[discrete]
=== A Remnant by Design, Not by Failure

None of this is God falling short of a larger plan.
The remnant was the plan from the beginning:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 7:7]
____
The LORD did not set his love upon you, nor choose you, because ye were *more in number* than any people; for ye were the *fewest* of all people…
____

He did not choose the many and lose most of them; He chose the few on purpose, and told them so before the choosing.
Noah’s household was eight, out of a world (1 Peter 3:20).
Gideon’s army was cut from thirty-two thousand to three hundred before the LORD would use it (Judges 7:2-7) — not because a smaller army fights better, but because __“Israel vaunt themselves against me, saying, Mine own hand hath saved me.”__
The strait gate and the narrow way, __“and few there be that find it”__ (Matthew 7:14), is not a tragic outcome God is powerless to widen; it is __“your Father’s good pleasure to give *you* the kingdom”__ (Luke 12:32) — a little flock, given a kingdom, by design.

[discrete]
=== Fifteen Lines Converging, and What They Mean

Count them together and they do not merely agree; they converge from every direction Scripture and the world can supply.
The gleaning law’s geometry, the shaken olive tree, Elijah’s seven thousand against a nation, Isaiah’s gold and Ezekiel’s dross, Zechariah’s two-thirds cut off, the crowds that dissolve to twelve and then to eleven, the census math of Menahem’s Israel — prophetic, agricultural, metallurgical, narrative, and historical evidence, all landing in the same narrow band.
That is not the shape of a coincidence; it is the shape of a signature, the same signature this book found in the plagues of Egypt and the sign of Jonah: a truth so consistently overdetermined across independent lines that no human editor could have coordinated it.

And here the remnant answers its own hardest question.
If the remnant is by design, then the furnace that makes it is not cruelty but the ordinary cost of the thing being real: gold in the ground is never found refined, and a people called out of __“the sand of the sea”__ (Romans 9:27) is never called out unscathed.
__“A very small remnant”__ was still enough to keep Israel from becoming __“as Sodom”__ (Isaiah 1:9) — smallness was never God’s defeat; the very same book that says few are chosen also says the chosen are given the kingdom outright.
The gold was always going to be rare.
It was never going to be worthless.
