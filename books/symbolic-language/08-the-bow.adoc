Everyone knows the rainbow as God’s sign after the flood. But the Hebrew names it plainly: it is a _qesheth_, a *bow* — the same word used everywhere else for the warrior’s weapon. And Scripture states outright what this bow is:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 9:13]
____
I do set my *bow* in the cloud, and it shall be for a *token of a covenant* between me and the earth.
____

__“A token of a covenant.”__ This is not an analogy we are reading into the text; the text sets the war-bow in the sky and names it the sign of God’s covenant. The bow is the covenant.

And see its reach. God makes this first covenant __“between me and you and every living creature of all flesh”__ (Genesis 9:15) — and in the language of these symbols a living creature is a nation: the beasts of Daniel’s sea are kingdoms, the living creatures of Revelation peoples. So the very first bow-covenant is struck not with one tribe but with _every nation on earth_ — a universal covenant of peace, set over all the families of the ground. That reach is the hinge of everything that follows.

[discrete]
=== The Bow Is the Covenant

Once you see it here, the symbol runs everywhere, because _qesheth_ is never merely hardware. It is a kingdom’s _might_: __“the bow of Elam, the chief of their might”__ (Jeremiah 49:35). And it is a _binding bond_: Isaiah pictures fleeing rulers __“bound”__ — the Hebrew is _asar_, to yoke, the root of the word for a *vow* — __“by the bow”__ (Isaiah 22:3, where the KJV hides the word as “archers”). Weapon, might, covenant, vow: one word carries them all, because they are one thing. A kingdom’s armed power is the outward face of the covenant that holds it together. The bow is what a people is _strung together_ by.

And the New Testament keeps the symbol whole. Its single bow — _toxon_, the only one in all the Greek Scriptures — belongs to the conqueror on the white horse:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 6:2]
____
…he that sat on him had a *bow*; and a *crown* was given unto him: and he went forth *conquering, and to conquer*.
____

He carries a bow, yet no arrow is ever loosed. He conquers not by the shot but by the bow itself — he wins the nations by _covenant_. (Whether this rider is the true King binding the nations to Himself or a counterfeit binding them to a lie is its own study; either way, the instrument of conquest is a covenant.)

[discrete]
=== Make Us a Name

If the bow that holds a kingdom together is finally a covenant, then the bond can be made of nothing but a word. So it was with the first kingdom built against heaven: it was founded not on a weapon but on a name. At Babel men gather to build, and they name their purpose:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 11:4]
____
…let us make us a *name*, lest we be *scattered abroad* upon the face of the whole earth.
____

Mark the reason they give: a _name_ — __“lest we be scattered.”__ The name is the bond that holds them as one people; cut it, and they fly apart. And a **name**footnote:name[_Name_ — the covenant that binds a people; established in the link:/books/symbolic-language/the-name/[The Name] chapter.], as the last chapter showed, _is_ a covenant — the binding constitution of a people. Babel’s project is a self-made covenant, a bow of their own stringing, forged to bind a kingdom together in defiance of the God who set the nations apart. And God answers in kind: He does not topple the tower, He cuts the bond.

[quote.scripture, Genesis 11:9]
____
…the LORD did there *confound the language* of all the earth: and from thence did the LORD *scatter them abroad* upon the face of all the earth.
____

Their one speech was the string of their bow; God cuts it, and the kingdom springs apart. The very scattering they built a name to prevent is the scattering He brings. Break the bond, and the people disperse.

[discrete]
=== Elam and the Mortar of Babel

This is the system Scripture calls _Babylon_, and it has a partner that shares its judgment: _Elam_. The two stand together in the first war in the Bible — four kings against five, the coalition led by __“Chedorlaomer king of Elam”__ alongside __“Amraphel king of Shinar”__ (Genesis 14:1, 9), and Shinar _is_ Babel (Genesis 10:10). And where do they fight? In a valley __“full of slimepits”__ (Genesis 14:10) — where the word for that slime, _chemar_, is the very word for the mortar of Babel’s tower: __“slime had they for morter”__ (Genesis 11:3). The Elam-Babylon war is fought on ground paved with Babel’s own building-material. Against them stands Abram with his household, __“confederate”__ — covenant-bound — with his allies (Genesis 14:13): a true covenant set against the self-covenant. So when the prophets take up Elam, they take up Babylon under another name — Jeremiah’s oracle against Elam standing as the prologue to his great prophecy of Babylon’s fall.

[discrete]
=== Break the Bow

How, then, does God bring down a kingdom bound by such a bow? He breaks the bow. Hear it where it cannot be missed — bow-breaking set side by side with covenant-making:

[quote.scripture, Hosea 2:18]
____
And in that day will I *make a covenant* for them… and I will *break the bow* and the sword and the battle out of the earth, and will make them to *lie down safely*.
____

To break the bow is covenant work: the violent self-bond shattered, God’s own covenant of peace put in its place. So the sentence on the Babylon-system reads exactly so:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 49:35]
____
Behold, I will *break the bow of Elam*, the chief of their might.
____

And against Babylon herself, __“every one of their bows is broken”__ (Jeremiah 51:56). The might, the constitution, the binding covenant of the kingdom are all that one bow, and God snaps it — __“he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder”__ (Psalm 46:9). The kingdom that strung itself together against Him comes unstrung, and scatters.

[discrete]
=== Two Names, Two Bows

Set the two covenants side by side and the whole symbol stands clear. At Babel men say, __“let us _make us_ a name”__ — a covenant seized, self-strung, to bind a kingdom against heaven; and God scatters it. In the very next breath of Scripture He turns to one man and says the opposite:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 12:2]
____
…I will *make of thee a great nation*, and I will bless thee, and *make thy name great*; and thou shalt be a blessing.
____

Not _make us_ a name but _I will make thy_ name — a covenant given, not grasped; and where Babel’s name was built to keep one people from scattering, Abraham’s name is given to bless _all the families of the earth_. And the bow God first hung in the cloud is hanging there still — __“the everlasting covenant”__ (Genesis 9:16), His war-bow bent in peace over every nation on earth. That universal reach is the very thing Babel grasped after to bind one people, and that Abraham was given to carry as blessing to all. Two names, two covenants, two bows: one strung by men and broken, one set in the heavens by God and kept; every bow drawn against Him comes, in the end, unstrung. The covenant is the bow; and the only bow that abides is the one He strings.
