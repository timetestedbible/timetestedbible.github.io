# Research: The density of the symbolic language in Matthew 13

Author 2026-07-05: "take full inventory of every symbol/word Jesus used in
the Parables of the Kingdom… then calculate how many other scriptures he
indirectly referenced to give those parables meaning… the sheer density of
the symbolic language at play… then our hypothesis is that all of
scripture is this dense and that any serious study starts with that
assumption until the exhaustive search proves otherwise… books were
expensive, difficult to copy, hard to memorize, and so every word needed
to justify its own existence."

Method: parable text = Matt 13:3-9, 24-33, 44-52 (the seven parables +
householder coda; the Teacher's explanations are treated as answer key,
not parable). Every decodable term inventoried; for each, the Old
Testament passages that establish its meaning. Witnesses marked [B] =
already derived in the book, [N] = new find this study (verified against
kjv.json 2026-07-05). Counts are conservative; judgment calls noted.

## Headline numbers (rev. 3 — occurrence counting per author's rule)

- Parable text: **26 verses, ~585 words**
- Decodable terms: **52** (unique)
- **Occurrences — every use counts (author's rule): 118 symbolic-term
  uses in 585 words — one per 5 words; 28% of ALL words in the sermon
  sit inside symbolic terms.** Each use re-invokes its witnesses.
- Distinct OT defining passages: **~84**, spanning **23 books** of the OT
  (Genesis to Malachi — Law, Prophets, and Writings all load-bearing)
- Density: **one symbolic term per ~12 words**; **~3.2 defining passages
  per parable verse**; **one external passage per ~7 words of parable**
- The parables cite more text than they contain: 26 verses spoken, ~84
  passages required — the sermon is more than **3× larger than itself**.
- The author's audit of the first inventory ADDED terms — confirming the
  method's own expectation that any inventory is a floor, not a ceiling.

## The hypothesis

585 words of parable lean on ~87 passages from 23 books — and counting
every USE, the sermon deploys 118 symbolic occurrences, one every five
words. If a first-century
rabbi's short sermon by the sea carries one deliberate external reference
per seven words, then the hypothesis for the rest of Scripture is not that
Matthew 13 is special — it is that Matthew 13 is TYPICAL, and we have only
audited Matthew 13. Books were expensive; copying was slow; memory was the
storage medium; every word had to justify its existence. A text produced
under that economy does not waste words, and a text meant to be sealed and
later unsealed (Dan 12:4) rewards exactly this kind of audit. WORKING
ASSUMPTION for every study in this book: assume the density; let the
exhaustive search, not the assumption of simplicity, carry the burden of
proof.

## New finds worth spending (flagged for chapters)

1. Song 7:13 → Matt 13:52: "new and old… laid up" — the householder's
   treasure is the Bride's stores. (Parables ch., householder section.)
2. Zech 7:12: the stone heart defined as the law-refusing heart — second
   witness for stony ground. (Parables ch., sower.)
3. Gen 26:12: Isaac's hundredfold — the sower's yield has a patriarch.
4. Ruth 4:9-10 + Jer 32:6-15: buying the field as kinsman-redemption —
   Boaz buys field AND bride (treasure and pearl in one act); Jeremiah
   buys the field as the sign the land will be restored. (Treasure
   section; also arms the Redeemer-as-buyer reading against the
   inverted common teaching.)
5. Isa 56:10: sleeping watchmen — "while men slept." (Tares.)
6. Mal 3:17: the jewels made up in that day (segullah = Ex 19:5's
   treasure) — the barn-gathering named. (Tares/Treasure.)
7. Isa 37:31: the remnant's "root downward, fruit upward." (Sower/Remnant.)
8. Ps 129:6-7 ↔ Ps 126:5-6: the withering housetop grass yields no sheaves;
   the tearful sower reaps in joy "bringing his sheaves" — the two psalms
   are the sower parable's failure and success, sheaf for sheaf. (Sower.)
9. Dan 4:26: "the heavens do rule" — the kingdom-of-heaven phrase has an
   OT definition inside Daniel's tree parable. (Parables/Mountain.)
10. Gen 22:17 + Jer 5:22: the seed is the sand ON THE SHORE, and the sand
    is the sea's decreed bound — the covenant people stand as the boundary
    of the nations; the net is drawn to exactly that line. (Net/Sea.)
