# Every "swine" verse in Scripture — the other white meat (2026-07-07)

Working reference for the chapter "The Other White Meat." Coverage: every KJV verse
containing the swine-family words, gathered by Strong's number and verified against
kjv_strongs.txt and wlc.txt — Hebrew *chazir* H2386 (swine, boar; 7 verses), Greek
*choiros* G5519 (swine; 13 verses), *hus* G5300 (sow; 1 verse). Also swept: the boar's
verb *karsam* H3765 (Ps 80:13, its only OT occurrence), the priestly name *Hezir* H2387
(the same consonants חזיר), and the trampling verbs *katapateō* G2662 / *pateō* G3961
that define what swine do. Full occurrence lists in the appendix — nothing dropped
silently. The English "sow" as a verb (sowing seed) is a different family entirely and
is excluded (listed at the end). No entries for these verses in
data/translation-patches.json.

**✦ = verse already quoted in a published chapter** (noted which).

Vocabulary note: the symbol matches by structure, not spelling. "Swine," "boar," and
"sow" are one Hebrew word (*chazir*) and, in Greek, one animal under two nouns
(*choiros* the herd-word, *hus* the classical word — the LXX renders Asaph's boar with
the same noun Peter uses for the sow). The dog travels with the swine in three
independent texts (Isa 66:3; Matt 7:6; 2 Pet 2:22); they are a fixed pair, but distinct
symbols.

---

## The finding

The law defines the swine by a mismatch, and the mismatch is the symbol.

Lev 11:4-7 and Deut 14:7-8 sort the beasts by two tests: the parted hoof (the outward
test — the walk) and the chewed cud (the inward test — the food brought back up and
worked again). The camel, the coney, and the hare all fail the same way: cud without
hoof. **The swine is the only beast named in either list that fails the other way —
hoof without cud.** It wears the outward mark of the clean and lacks the inward work.
It walks the walk and never chews the word.

That structure has a name in the New Testament: hypocrisy — the whited sepulchre that
"appears beautiful outward, but is within full of dead men's bones and of all
uncleanness" (Matt 23:27, said of the Pharisees, ✦-adjacent to the leaven definition
the glossary already carries). The cud is the meat/milk mechanic in animal form: milk
is the word received; meat is the will done; the clean beast receives and then
*ruminates* — brings the word back up and chews it again ("in his law doth he meditate
day and night," Ps 1:2; Josh 1:8). The swine swallows once, anything, and never brings
it back.

So the standing glossary entry (Swine: the unclean beast par excellence — the nation
outside the covenant that tramples what is holy) holds across every occurrence, and the
law's definition explains *why* that nation tramples: what has no second chewing has no
sense of what it swallows. Pearls and slop are the same mouthful to it.

The range, verse by verse: the law states the structure (Lev 11; Deut 14); Asaph shows
the beast at work against the vine (Ps 80); Solomon shows the mismatch worn as
ornament (Prov 11:22); Isaiah shows Israelites *becoming* the swine — self-sanctified,
"holier than thou," eating the unclean among the graves (Isa 65:3-5; 66:3, 17); Jesus
names the swine as the trampler of holy things (Matt 7:6), evicts Legion into the herd
and the herd into the sea (Matt 8; Mark 5; Luke 8), and sets the lost son feeding them
in the far country (Luke 15); Peter closes the case on the washed sow that returns to
the mire (2 Pet 2:22). One structure at every depth: **the appearance of clean without
the inward work — the counterfeit meat.**

The chapter title works against the book's: MEAT is doing the will of the Father
(John 4:34 — the symbol the book is named for). The swine is the other white meat —
the pork board's slogan sold pig as if it were poultry, clean-looking flesh; the law's
swine is exactly that, the meat that *presents* clean (hoof parted, plain to see) and
is not (the cud is chewed in secret, or not at all).

## Tier 1 — the law states the structure

- **Lev 11:7-8** — "And the swine, though he divide the hoof, and be clovenfooted, yet
  he cheweth not the cud; he is unclean to you. Of their flesh shall ye not eat, and
  their carcase shall ye not touch." The *though* is the definition: the one beast
  disqualified despite the visible sign.
- **Deut 14:8** — "And the swine, because it divideth the hoof, yet cheweth not the
  cud" — Moses' restatement sharpens it to *because*: the parted hoof is not merely
  insufficient, it is the ground of the indictment. The sign worn without the substance
  makes the beast unclean, not almost-clean.
- The inverse failures (camel, coney, hare — cud without hoof, Lev 11:4-6) complete the
  frame: inward talk without the outward walk is equally unclean, but Scripture never
  builds a symbol on them. Every prophetic and gospel use singles out the hoof-without-
  cud beast. The Spirit's target is the counterfeit, not the incomplete.

## Tier 2 — the symbol at work

### The boar and the vine (Ps 80:8-13)

"Thou hast brought a vine out of Egypt" (80:8 ✦ Trees ch. 37) — Israel planted, hedged,
then the hedges broken: "The boar out of the wood doth waste it, and the wild beast of
the field doth devour it" (80:13). The boar is *chazir*; the wood is the *ya'ar*, the
forest — and the Trees chapter has already established the forest: the gentile empires
are the towering forest trees. The boar out of the wood is the unclean nation out of
the gentile forest, gnawing the covenant vine. Its verb, *karsam* H3765 ("doth waste"),
occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible — remember it; the Hebrew Matthew reaches for
it below. The LXX renders the boar σῦς/ὗς — the same noun Peter uses for the sow
(2 Pet 2:22), one word joining the psalm's beast to the epistle's.

[Historical note, verify before print: Legio X Fretensis — the legion that besieged
Jerusalem and then garrisoned it — carried the boar among its standard emblems; its
boar-stamped tiles have been excavated in Jerusalem. The boar out of the forest wasting
the vine, on the ensigns of the army that did it.]

### Pearls before swine (Matt 7:6)

"Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine,
lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you." ✦ The Pearl
ch. 27 quotes it in full and runs the trampling chain: what swine do to pearls is what
Scripture says the nations do to holy things — the holy city given to the Gentiles to
"tread under foot" (Rev 11:2 ✦), the sanctuary and host "trodden under foot" (Dan 8:13
✦), Jerusalem "trodden down of the Gentiles" (Luke 21:24 ✦).

The Hebrew Matthew (bibles/hg.txt) tightens both halves:

- "Do not give **holy flesh** to dogs" — *basar qodesh*, the sacrificial meat, the very
  phrase of Haggai 2:12 ("If one bear holy flesh in the skirt of his garment" ✦ Wings
  ch. 10). What is withheld from the dogs is MEAT — holy meat. The chapter title sits
  inside the verse.
- "lest they **chew** them before your eyes" — the verb is *karsam* H3765, the boar's
  one-occurrence verb from Ps 80:13. The Hebrew witness deliberately equates the swine
  at the pearls with the boar at the vine: same beast, same mouth, vine or pearls.

The full trampling family, for the chapter's use: *katapateō* G2662 — pearls (Matt
7:6), the savourless salt "trodden under foot of men" (Matt 5:13), the wayside seed
(Luke 8:5), the crowd treading one another in the very scene where Jesus defines the
leaven of the Pharisees as hypocrisy (Luke 12:1), and the apostate who "hath trodden
under foot the Son of God" (Heb 10:29 ✦ Path to Salvation ch. 34). That last verse
closes a loop: the trampler "hath counted the blood of the covenant... an unholy
thing" — Greek *koinon* G2839, "common," the exact word of Acts 10:14. Peter was
forbidden to call any *man* common; the apostate calls the *blood* common. The swine's
error in one word.

### The ring in the snout (Prov 11:22, with its twin 25:12)

"As a jewel of gold in a swine's snout, so is a fair woman which is without
discretion [*ta'am*, taste]." The jewel is not a generic gem: it is the *nezem*
H5141, the nose-ring — the bridal ornament. The servant puts the *nezem* on Rebekah
when the bride is chosen (Gen 24:22, 47), and God Himself lists it first when He
adorns Jerusalem as His bride: "I put a *nezem* upon thy nose" (Ezek 16:12 — the
Hebrew is *nezem al-appech*, nose, the KJV's "forehead" flattens it; quote a literal
translation here). The proverb's picture is word-for-word the bridal scene relocated:
*nezem* + *af* (nose) — **the bride's ring in the swine's nose.**

And Proverbs supplies its own control case: 25:12 opens with the identical two words,
*nezem zahav* — "As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise
reprover upon an **obedient ear**." Twin proverbs, one ring, two mountings: on the
*ear that hears* reproof, or in the *nose without taste*. Job 12:11 names the two
organs — "Doth not the *ear* try words? and the *mouth* taste his meat?" — the ring
belongs on the organ of reception; hung on the swine, it decorates a palate that
cannot tell gold from garbage.

This is the same image Matt 7:6 builds with pearls. The *peninim* are Scripture's
price-standard for wisdom and the bride — "the price of wisdom is above *peninim*"
(Job 28:18; so Prov 3:15; 8:11), "who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is
far above *peninim*" (Prov 31:10) — and Prov 20:15 sets the whole cluster in one
verse: "There is gold, and a multitude of *peninim*: but the lips of knowledge are a
precious jewel." Gold, pearls, and knowledge-speech, weighed together. Matt 7:6
assembles exactly these pieces: the bride's-price pearls set before the swine — the
beast already wearing the bride's gold in the organ that roots in mire. The fair
woman without *ta'am* is the anti-31:10 woman; under the woman-as-assembly symbol,
she is the adorned assembly without discernment — beautiful, ringed, and unable to
taste.

The *nezem*'s own history carries the warning: the golden calf was made of *nezem*
rings (Ex 32:2-3); Gideon's ephod-snare of the *nezem* rings of Midian (Judg
8:24-26); Jacob buried them with the strange gods under the oak (Gen 35:4); the
harlot "decked herself with her *nezem*... and went after her lovers" (Hos 2:13).
The bride's gold either marks the covenant bride or funds the idol — there is no
third use in the record.

### The Gadarene herd (Matt 8:28-32; Mark 5:1-16; Luke 8:26-34)

Read the scene against Isaiah 65:3-5 and it is the prophecy walking:

| Isaiah 65:3-5 | The Gadarene scene |
|---|---|
| "which remain among the graves, and lodge in the monuments" (65:4) | "who had his dwelling among the tombs" (Mark 5:3) |
| a people provoking to the face, in the gardens and mountains (65:3, 7) | "always, night and day, he was in the mountains, and in the tombs" (Mark 5:5) |
| "which eat swine's flesh" (65:4) | "there was there nigh unto the mountains a great herd of swine feeding" (Mark 5:11) |
| "a smoke in my nose, a fire that burneth all the day" (65:5) | the torment the demons expect (Mark 5:7) |

The herd feeds "nigh unto the mountains" (Mark 5:11) — on the kingdoms — and ends in
the sea: the demons plead their way out of the man and into the swine, and "the herd
ran violently down a steep place into the sea... and were choked in the sea" (Mark
5:13 ✦ Sea & Waters ch. 36, which reads it as Legion — a Roman army word — drowned
like Pharaoh's host). The unclean spirits' own choice of house is the unclean nation;
granted it, the nation and its garrison go the way of every army the sea has taken.
Two thousand (Mark 5:13); the keepers fled to the city, and the city "began to pray
him to depart out of their coasts" (Mark 5:17) — the economy of the unclean preferred
its herd to the healed man. And the healed man, forbidden to follow, is sent to
publish in Decapolis what the LORD had done (Mark 5:19-20) — the first herald planted
in gentile ground.

### The prodigal among the swine (Luke 15:15-16)

The younger son "joined himself to a citizen of that country; and he sent him into
his fields to feed swine. And he would fain have filled his belly with the husks that
the swine did eat: and no man gave unto him." The lost house in the far country,
bonded to the unclean nation, tending its appetites, envying its slop — and finding
that the far country feeds its swine and not its servants. The husks are *keration*
G2769, carob pods, the word's one NT occurrence. The restoration is already ✦ in
Garments ch. 39: "Bring forth the best robe" (Luke 15:22) — the covenant covering
back on the returned son. Feeding swine is what exile *is*; the robe is what return
looks like.

### The sow that was washed (2 Pet 2:22, with Prov 26:11)

"It is happened unto them according to the true proverb, The dog is turned to his own
vomit again; and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire." The dog half
is Prov 26:11 ("As a dog returneth to his vomit, so a fool returneth to his folly");
the sow half has no OT source-verse — Peter calls both "the true proverb" and supplies
the swine's own. The context (2 Pet 2:20-21) is the apostate: "escaped the pollutions
of the world... again entangled therein," for whom "it had been better not to have
known the way." The washing was real; the nature was not changed by it; the sow's
appetite survived her bath. This is the law's definition run forward: the outward
cleansing without the inward work ends where it began, in the mire — and the mire is
the trodden street-clay (see research-clay.md, "The mire of the streets": the trodden
mass). The washed sow goes back under the feet that trample.

Wordplay (second dimension): *chazir* חזיר sits on the consonants of *chazar* חזר,
"to return" (the ordinary word for "return" in later Hebrew and Aramaic). The swine is
the *returner* — and both halves of Peter's proverb turn on the word "returned"
(*epistrepsas*). The pun is the proverb.

### Isaiah's indictment — the swine inside Israel (Isa 65:3-5; 66:3, 17)

The OT's last three swine verses all aim inward, at covenant men behaving as the
unclean nation:

- **Isa 65:3-5** — sacrificing in gardens, incense on "altars of brick" (man-made,
  man-fired — see research-clay.md on brick), remaining among the graves, eating
  swine's flesh with "broth of abominable things in their vessels" — and the punch
  line: "which say, Stand by thyself, come not near to me; for I am holier than thou."
  The swine-eater claims *extra* holiness. Hoof without cud, worn as a boast.
- **Isa 66:3** — four lawful offerings equated to four abominations: ox to man-slaying,
  lamb to a dog's neck, oblation to swine's blood, incense to blessing an idol —
  "they have chosen their own ways." Worship from the self-willed *is* the unclean
  offering; the dog and the swine stand in the middle of the equation, paired again.
- **Isa 66:17** — "They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens
  behind one tree in the midst, eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the
  mouse, shall be consumed together." ✦ Trees ch. 37 quotes it in full (the one tree
  in the midst). Self-sanctification plus the unclean diet, judged at the LORD's
  coming — the mouse is also on the Lev 11 list (11:29), so the menu is a deliberate
  law-breaking set, not a random one. The diet is real and the symbol rides on it:
  the command stands, and the man who eats the counterfeit meat has made himself the
  emblem of it.

### Peter's sheet (Acts 10) — the boundary of the symbol

✦ Trees ch. 37 already argues it: the sheet of "all manner of fourfooted beasts" with
"Rise, Peter; kill, and eat" is covenant language (the sacrifice slain and the meal
shared), and Peter gives the vision's own interpretation twice — "God hath shewed me
that I should not call any *man* common or unclean" (Acts 10:28 ✦). Beasts are men;
the unclean beast is the nation outside the covenant; the vision is God taking
nations *into* it. The vision changes what the nations are, not what the dinner is —
Peter, years later, still describes the apostate as a washed sow returning to mire;
the beast that stays a beast stays unclean. For the chapter: this is where the symbol
guards the command instead of dissolving it. The swine's uncleanness is the *picture*;
a man may stop being pictured by it (the Gadarene, the prodigal); the picture itself
never becomes food.

## Tier 3 — literal and name uses

- **1 Chr 24:15; Neh 10:20** — *Hezir* H2387, the same consonants חזיר: the
  seventeenth priestly course bears the name, and a Hezir seals the covenant with
  Nehemiah. A priestly house named Swine — the consonantal record keeps the reminder
  inside the priesthood itself. (The Bene Hezir tomb in the Kidron valley is the
  oldest of the monumental priestly tombs — a swine-named house lodged among the
  monuments, Isa 65:4's own vocabulary. Historical note; verify before any use in
  print.)
- **Matt 8:30** — "a good way off from them" (the herd's distance in Matthew's
  telling); **Mark 5:14, 16** — the feeders flee and report "concerning the swine";
  narrative furniture of the Gadarene scene, carried with it.

## Wordplay and consonantal notes (the second dimension)

- **בשר — flesh and tidings are one written word.** Strong's splits the consonants
  into H1320 *basar* "flesh" and H1319 *basar* "to bear tidings" (the *mevasser* of
  Isa 52:7; 61:1; *besorah* H1309, the tidings — the gospel family the Gospel chapter
  ch. 5 establishes), but the split is a pointing-era overlay; even the sin/shin dot
  is a later mark. Unpointed, בשר says both, everywhere it stands. The readings this
  opens, each at full strength alongside the plain one:
  - **Hag 2:12 / Hebrew Matt 7:6** — בשר קדש: "holy flesh" and "the holy tidings."
    Carried in the *kanaph* of the garment (Hag 2:12) — the hem that carries the
    commandments (Num 15:38-39); withheld from dogs (Matt 7:6), where the second
    reading makes the parallelism synonymous: the holy tidings // your pearls — two
    figures for teaching set before what cannot value it.
  - **John 1:14** — the Word became *basar*: the Word became the word whose own name
    is tidings; the announcement embodied.
  - **John 6:51-63** — "my *basar* is meat indeed," decoded in-text as "the words
    that I speak unto you" (6:63): the consonants were carrying the decoding all
    along.
  - **Isa 65:4; 66:17** — eating swine's *basar*: receiving the swine-nation's
    tidings — the other *besorah*, "another gospel: which is not another"
    (Gal 1:6-7). The chapter title's third floor: the other white MEAT is the
    counterfeit gospel.
- *chazir* חזיר / *chazar* חזר "return" — the swine is the returner-to-mire; Peter's
  proverb turns on the same verb in Greek (*epistrepsas*). And the Hebrew Matthew
  writes the pun into the swine verse itself: Matt 7:6 ends ויחזרו — "and they
  *turn* (chazar) to tear you." The chazir chazars; the beast does what its name
  says. See above.
- *Hezir* H2387 — the priestly course and covenant-sealer on the same consonants.
- *karsam* H3765 — the boar's gnawing, once in the OT (Ps 80:13), reappearing in the
  Hebrew Matthew's "lest they chew them" (Matt 7:6): one verb, vine and pearls.
- **עץ הדעת טוב ורע (Gen 2:9, 17)** — the first eating, in its own consonants.
  (a) דעת unpointed is both the noun "knowledge" and the infinitive "to know" — and
  the grammar leans to the verb: it takes "good and evil" as an object. "The tree of
  the *knowing* good and evil" — an act, not a content; the pointing froze a verb
  into a noun. (b) עץ/עצה, tree/counsel: Ps 1 opposes them deliberately — the man
  who refuses עצת רשעים (the counsel of the wicked, v. 1) becomes כעץ שתול (the
  tree planted by water, v. 3). To eat of a tree is to take in its counsel; the
  thing actually internalized in Eden was the serpent's word, the fruit its vehicle.
  (c) The first command is a food law — לא תאכל, "thou shalt not eat": the boundary
  of the mouth is Eden's grammar, and Lev 11 continues it. And Gen 3:6 shows Eve
  performing the tree's own verb before the bite — "the woman *saw that* the tree
  *was good* (טוב)": deciding good by her own eyes first, the eating only completing
  it; Isa 66:3's swine-blood offerers who "have chosen their own ways" are her heirs.
  The tree's advertised payoff, נחמד להשכיל ("desired to make wise"), is wisdom for
  the price of a snack — against the wisdom priced above *peninim*.
- *gerah* (the cud, Lev 11) — from dragging back up; the rumination the swine lacks is
  the same motion as meditation, the word brought back and worked again.
- LXX: the boar of Ps 80:13 (LXX 79:14) and the sow of 2 Pet 2:22 are one Greek noun
  (σῦς/ὗς, one word in two spellings).

## The Hebrew-NT second witness (bibles/hg.txt)

*Chazir* H2386 stands in the Hebrew NT at Matt 7:6; 8:30, 31, 32; Mark 5:11, 12, 13,
16 (Luke's swine verses are rendered without Strong's tags but read "pigs" at Luke
8:32-34; 15:15-16). The two decisive readings:

- **Matt 7:6** — "Do not give *holy flesh* to dogs, and do not set your pearls before
  *swine*, lest they *chew* [karsam] them before your eyes and turn to tear you."
  Holy flesh (Hag 2:12) + the boar's verb (Ps 80:13) in one verse. The consonants
  verified against the manuscript data (data/hg-chapters/Matthew-7.json): בשר קדש
  unpointed (flesh/tidings — see the consonantal notes), יכרסמנו (karsam), and
  ויחזרו — "and they turn," *chazar* on the swine's own consonants. One manuscript
  oddity worth a footnote: "your pearls" stands as פניכם (the transcriber marks it
  [sic]) — the expected פניניכם (*peninim*, pearls) shortened to what also reads
  "your faces" (*panim*); pearls and faces share their consonants, and the verse
  already turns on לפני, "before the face of" the swine.
- **Mark 5:11** — "many swine were grazing" around the mountains, as in the Greek.

## The substitution test — FLESH as teaching, BODY as the communion

Substitute *flesh = teaching (bread)* and *body = the many made one* (the communion
parallel to Jesus' own body) and the swine texts lock together. The substitution is
not imported; Scripture states both equations and then decodes them in-text.

### Flesh = teaching: the stated equation

- **Job 12:11 and 34:3** — stated twice: "Doth not the ear try words? and the mouth
  taste his meat?" / "the ear trieth words, as the mouth tasteth meat." Ear is to
  words what mouth is to meat — *eating is Scripture's own figure for testing and
  receiving teaching.* Both "taste" = H2938 *ta'am*, the verb of Ps 34:8, "O taste
  and see that the LORD is good."
- **Prov 11:22** — the find that closes the loop: "a fair woman which is without
  *discretion*" — the word is H2940 ***ta'am*, TASTE**, the noun of Job's equation
  and Ps 34:8's imperative. The verse pairs the gold jewel in the swine's snout with
  the woman *turned aside from taste*: one picture, stated in its own vocabulary —
  **the swine's defect is the palate.** Consumption without discernment; a snout
  that roots gold and mire alike. (Heb 5:14 gives the clean inverse, on the book's
  own meat ground: strong meat belongs to them who "by reason of use have their
  senses exercised to discern.")
- **John 6, decoded in-text** — "I am the bread of life" (6:35) → "the bread that I
  will give is my flesh" (6:51) → "my flesh is meat indeed" (6:55) → and then the
  decoding: "the flesh profiteth nothing: **the words that I speak unto you, they
  are spirit, and they are life**" (6:63). Eating His flesh is receiving His words —
  He says so. And the scene ends with the returners: "From that time many of his
  disciples **went back**, and walked no more with him" (6:66) — at the meat, the
  chazir-motion, going back (structural echo; the Greek is "went away back," not
  the LXX return-verb).
- **Matt 16:12** — leaven of bread = "the doctrine of the Pharisees" (the glossary's
  Leaven entry already owns this); eaten words elsewhere: Jer 15:16 ("thy words were
  found, and I did eat them"); Ezek 3:1-3; Rev 10:9-10.

Run the substitution through the swine verses:

- **Isa 65:4** — "which eat swine's flesh, and broth of abominable things is in their
  vessels": ingesting the unclean nation's *teaching* — and the broth is flesh boiled
  down and thinned, **unclean doctrine reduced to extract and carried in their own
  man-made vessels** (see research-clay.md on vessels and on the altars of brick two
  verses up: man-made altar, man-made vessel, borrowed broth).
- **Isa 66:17** — swine's flesh, the abomination, and the mouse: a deliberate
  curriculum of the forbidden, eaten by the self-sanctified.
- **Isa 66:3 with Heb 10:29** — the blood completes it: offering "swine's blood" as
  oblation is offering the unclean nation's *life* as worship; and the apostate
  "hath counted the blood of the covenant... an unholy thing (*koinon*)" — the two
  verses mirror: swine's blood presented as covenant blood; covenant blood counted
  as swine's. Flesh and blood travel together here as in John 6:55 — the teaching
  and the life it carries.
- **Hag 2:12-13** — the asymmetry that explains Matt 7:6: holy flesh carried in the
  skirt does *not* sanctify what the skirt touches, but the dead-body touch *does*
  defile. Holiness does not transfer by contact; uncleanness does. So holy flesh
  handed to dogs cleanses no dog — it only profanes the flesh and endangers the
  hand. The Hebrew Matthew's "give not holy flesh to dogs" is Haggai's priestly
  ruling turned into a command.

### Body = the many made one: the communion and its counterfeit

- **Luke 22:19** — "This is my body which is **given** for you"; **John 2:21** — the
  temple of his body; **1 Cor 10:16-17** — "The bread which we break, is it not the
  communion of the body of Christ? For we being **many** are **one** bread, and one
  body."
- **The inversion, in the swine's own scene** — "My name is **Legion: for we are
  many**" (Mark 5:9): many made one *herd*. Against the many-made-one-body stands
  the many-made-one-herd — and Paul names the two tables: "ye cannot be partakers of
  the Lord's table, and of the **table of devils**" (1 Cor 10:21). The Gadarene herd
  is the table of devils incorporated — the counterfeit communion — and it runs down
  into the sea, drowned like the last army that pursued the redeemed.
- **The washing without the baptism** — "by one Spirit are we all baptized **into one
  body**" (1 Cor 12:13). The sow "that was washed" (2 Pet 2:22) was washed and never
  incorporated; nature unchanged, she returns to the mire-communion. And the frame of
  2 Peter 2 is doctrinal end to end: it opens with "false **teachers**... damnable
  heresies" (2:1) and describes them as "**wells without water**" (2:17) — empty
  vessels — before closing on the sow. The washed-sow proverb is a verdict on
  teaching, not hygiene.
- **The carcase** — "their **carcase** shall ye not touch" (Lev 11:8): the swine's
  dead body defiles by contact (the Hag 2:13 rule). The counterfeit body is a dead
  one — the whited sepulchre "full of dead men's bones and of all uncleanness"
  (Matt 23:27); Isa 65:4's swine-eaters "remain among the graves"; the Gadarene had
  "his dwelling among the tombs." The members of the counterfeit body lodge with its
  carcase.
- **The anti-communion of the prodigal — Luke against Luke** — at the swine trough:
  "he would fain have filled his belly with the husks... and **no man gave unto
  him**" (15:16); at the table: "this is my body which is **given** for you"
  (22:19). One evangelist, one verb: the far country's table has no giver. The son's
  own realization is a bread-count — "how many hired servants of my father's have
  **bread enough and to spare**, and I perish with hunger!" (15:17) — the father's
  house has teaching to spare for the least servant; the far country feeds pods to
  pigs. And the return ends in the given meal: "bring hither the fatted calf...
  and let us eat" (15:23).
- **Acts 10, completed** — "kill, and eat" as covenant meal (Trees ch. 37 ✦) is
  incorporation language: at Cornelius' house the Spirit falls and the Gentiles are
  baptized — washed *and* baptized into the one body (1 Cor 12:13, "whether we be
  Jews or Gentiles"). The sow is the control case: washed only.

### Eating — the symbol under the whole study, defined by the command to eat the Lamb

Eating is itself the symbol the swine texts ride on, and Scripture defines it in its
own words. The manna was fed *as a lesson with a stated moral*: "that he might make
thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by **every word** that
proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD" (Deut 8:3). Isaiah decodes eating as
hearkening in a single breath: "Wherefore do ye spend money for that which is not
bread?... **hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good**" (Isa 55:2).
The prophets eat the words outright (Jer 15:16; Ezek 3:1-3; Rev 10:9-10); a man's
belly is filled with "the fruit of his mouth," and lovers of the tongue "shall eat
the fruit thereof" (Prov 18:20-21). And eating does more than receive: the priests
eat the sin offering "**to bear the iniquity** of the congregation" (Lev 10:17) —
what is eaten is taken into the eater and borne by him; "we being many are one
bread, and one body: for we are all partakers of that one bread" (1 Cor 10:17).

So the meaning: **eating is incorporation — taking a life, a word, into yourself
until it becomes your own flesh and your own walk.** The ear hears; the mouth makes
it *you*. Hence both commandments of the mouth: eat the Lamb; eat no swine. You
become one body with what you eat, and you share its fate — "they that... eat
swine's flesh... shall be consumed **together**" (Isa 66:17); "he that eateth me,
even he shall **live by me**" (John 6:57). The first sin in Eden was a meal for the
same reason: eating from that tree took the serpent's word in (Gen 3:6) — the first
counterfeit communion.

Read the Passover ordinance clause by clause under this and every clause speaks
(Ex 12:8-11, 43-48):

- **The blood outside, the flesh inside** — the blood on the doorposts covers; the
  eaten flesh constitutes. Covering and incorporation are both commanded; the
  covered house must also be a fed house.
- **"Roast with fire... not raw, nor sodden at all with water"** (12:9) — the flesh
  must pass through the fire (the consuming presence — Heaven and Hell ch. 13)
  before it is eaten: the word fire-tested; not raw (untested), not boiled to broth
  (diluted). The counterfeit is prepared the forbidden way in the forbidden animal:
  "**broth** of abominable things is in their vessels" (Isa 65:4) — swine, watered
  down, in man-made vessels.
- **"His head with his legs, and with the purtenance thereof... let nothing of it
  remain"** (12:9-10) — the whole Lamb or none: the head (His mind), the legs (His
  walk — the hoof), the inward parts (His heart). No selective eating; the teaching
  is not a menu.
- **"Loins girded, your shoes on your feet, and your staff in your hand... eat it
  in haste"** (12:11) — the meal that makes walkers. Cud and hoof united at the
  table: the word taken in issues at once in the walk — the clean-beast structure,
  enacted.
- **"No stranger... no uncircumcised person shall eat thereof"** (12:43, 48) —
  incorporation is bounded by covenant. The mirror of Lev 11:8 ("their carcase
  shall ye not touch"): the Lamb's table is fenced from the outside, the swine's
  carcase fenced from contact — two boundaries of one mouth.

Paul closes the decoding at the same feast: "Christ our passover is sacrificed for
us: therefore let us keep the feast... with the unleavened bread of **sincerity and
truth**" (1 Cor 5:7-8) — what the feast feeds you is named, and it is doctrine and
character, purged of the puffing leaven.

The chapter's spine, tightened: the swine eats without tasting (Prov 11:22, *ta'am*),
so it cannot receive the flesh that is teaching (Job 12:11; John 6:63); having no cud,
it never brings the word back up to work it (Lev 11:7; Ps 1:2); its communion is a
herd, not a body (Mark 5:9; 1 Cor 10:17-21); its washing is not a baptism into
anything (2 Pet 2:22; 1 Cor 12:13); and its table has no giver (Luke 15:16; 22:19).
The other white meat is the counterfeit of every course: flesh without words, bread
without taste, body without members, washing without the Spirit, a table without a
gift.

## The counter-texts — "he declared all foods clean"

The definition must survive the sweep (four texts carry the whole claim), and each
one, read in its own vocabulary, is about *commonness* — man's category of
defilement by association — or about *men*. None touches the written food law.
The load-bearing distinction runs through all four: Scripture keeps two separate
words, **κοινός (common — defiled by association, a category the law never wrote)**
and **ἀκάθαρτος (unclean — designated by God in Lev 11)**. Peter holds them apart
in a single breath — "I have never eaten any thing **common or unclean**" (Acts
10:14) — so any reading that collapses them must first explain Peter.

### Mark 7:19 — "purging all meats"

Four independent controls, any one sufficient:

1. **The question on the table** (7:2, 5): disciples eating *bread* with "defiled,
   that is to say, with unwashen, hands" — Greek κοιναῖς χερσίν, **common hands** —
   a "tradition of the elders." No unclean species is anywhere in the scene; no
   first-century Judean table had swine on it. A ruling on Lev 11 would answer a
   question nobody asked.
2. **The vocabulary**: the dispute runs entirely on κοινόω, "make common" (7:2, 15,
   18, 20, 23) — never ἀκάθαρτος. Whatever 7:19 cleanses, it cleanses out of
   *commonness* — the hand-washing category — not out of Leviticus. And Peter,
   whose preaching stands behind Mark's Gospel, still keeps both categories distinct
   years later (Acts 10:14): if Mark 7 had abolished them, Mark's own source never
   heard it.
3. **The grammar and the witnesses**: the modern parenthesis "(Thus he declared all
   foods clean)" translates three Greek words — καθαρίζων πάντα τὰ βρώματα. No
   "thus," no "he," no "declared" stands in any manuscript; the sentence's subject
   must be imported from two verses back (7:18, "he saith") across the belly and
   the draught. The KJV construes the participle with the process — "goeth out into
   the draught, **purging all meats**" — the digestion purging the food, which is
   what the sentence is about [the Received Text behind the KJV reads the neuter
   καθαρίζον, agreeing with the process; the critical text's masculine καθαρίζων is
   what carries the "he declared" reading — verify the collation before print]. And
   the Hebrew Mark (bibles/hg.txt) reads the clause with no object at all: "goes
   out through nature, **cleansing**" — the second witness has no "all foods."
4. **The chapter's own thesis** (7:9-13): Jesus is prosecuting the Pharisees for
   "making the **word of God** of none effect through your **tradition**" — Corban
   against the fifth commandment. The scene's whole argument is that tradition must
   yield to the written command; a reading in which Jesus voids a written command
   mid-scene inverts the chapter. Matthew records the verdict actually reached:
   "to eat with **unwashen hands** defileth not a man" (Matt 15:20) — hands, not
   species.

### Acts 10 — the sheet (see the Tier 2 section)

The vision is interpreted three times, by Peter, in the text: "God hath shewed me
that I should not call any **man** common or unclean" (10:28; 11:18). Peter's "never
eaten" (10:14) stands years after the resurrection; the text never shows him eating
from the sheet; and the voice's ruling — "What God hath **cleansed**, that call not
thou **common**" (10:15) — answers the *koinos* category: God cleansing the nations
He is about to take in at Cornelius's house, not re-designating species. The symbol
(beast = nation) does the work the menu-reading cannot: it is the reason the vision
*means* what Peter says it means.

### Romans 14 — "nothing unclean of itself"

The dispute is defined in the second verse: "one believeth that he may **eat all
things**: another, who is weak, **eateth herbs**" — meat against vegetables, the
idol-market conscience question (1 Cor 8 is the same case argued at length), plus
private fast-days (14:5-6). The operative word at 14:14 is κοινός again — "nothing
is **common** of itself: but to him that esteemeth any thing to be common, to him it
is common" — which is *true by definition of the category*: commonness is
association, a verdict of esteem, so nothing carries it "of itself." Lev 11
uncleanness is designation, never esteem, and neither swine, nor Moses, nor the law
appears anywhere in Romans 14. The chapter opens by limiting itself to "doubtful
disputations" (14:1); the written law is not a doubtful disputation.

### 1 Tim 4:3-5 — "every creature of God is good"

The verse carries its own boundary, twice: the meats God created "to be received...
of them which believe and **know the truth**," and — the instrument — "it is
sanctified by the **word of God** and prayer." *Sanctified* means set apart, and the
word of God is precisely what sets creatures apart as food (Lev 11; Deut 14): the
verse's own test admits the clean and excludes what the word never sanctified. The
heresy in view forbids marriage and commands abstinence from what God *gave* —
ascetic prohibition of the permitted (the Essene/proto-gnostic direction), which no
first-century writer could confuse with Moses; "doctrines of devils" is not a name
any apostle gives the Torah.

### Col 2:16-17 — "a shadow of things to come"

The judging party at Colossae is named in 2:8 — "philosophy and vain deceit, after
the **tradition of men**" — and the verse's grammar keeps the feasts' cargo ahead:
"which **are** a shadow of things **to come**." A shadow of a coming thing is not
discharged before the thing comes; and the Shadow chapter (ch. 14) has already done
the deeper work: a shadow is not a lesser copy to discard but the covering attached
to its body. "Let no man judge you" shields the keeper from the critic — it does not
deputize the critic.

### The texts that hold the other end

Isa 66:17 hangs judgment *at the LORD's coming* on the swine-eaters — the food law
is in force in Scripture's final vision, inside the new-heavens frame of Isa 65-66.
And Matt 5:17-19 fixes the term: not one jot passes from the law till heaven and
earth pass, and "whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments,
and shall teach men so..." The chapter's argument runs the same direction as the
whole book: because eating is incorporation, the food law was always *also* a
picture — and the picture stands because the command stands. The symbol deepens the
commandment; it never dissolves it.

## Meaning (glossary confirmation)

The standing entry survives the full sweep and needs no correction:

> **Swine** — the unclean beast par excellence — the nation outside the covenant that
> *tramples* what is holy: pearls under their feet, the holy city under the Gentiles,
> the washed sow back in the mire. (Lev 11:7; Matt 7:6; Rev 11:2; Isa 66:17;
> 2 Pet 2:22)

The research adds the mechanism the chapter can teach: the law's own definition —
**the parted hoof without the chewed cud, the outward walk without the word worked
inwardly** — which is why the beast cannot tell pearls from slop, why Isaiah's
"holier than thou" swine-eaters wear it as a boast, why the washed sow returns, and
why the NT's one-word name for the structure is hypocrisy (Luke 12:1, in the
trampling crowd).

Glossary candidate arising from the study: **Eat** — incorporation; taking a word or
a life into oneself until it becomes one's own flesh and walk (Deut 8:3; Isa 55:2;
Jer 15:16; Lev 10:17; John 6:57, 63; 1 Cor 10:17). It passes the symbol test (the
scriptural meaning is not the dictionary meaning), it is the verb under Meat, Milk,
Bread, Leaven, and Swine alike, and the command "eat the Lamb" (Ex 12) is its
Tier-1 definition scene.
