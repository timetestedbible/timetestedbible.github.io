# Research: Marriage and Divorce — the corpus behind the proposed ch. 9

Done 2026-07-02 at the author's direction. Seed: Grok's average view —
"Yes, Jesus permits divorce on the grounds of sexual immorality (Greek
porneia)… Matthew 5:32 and 19:9"; porneia defined by Grok as "the broad
New Testament term for illicit sexual activity." Tested below against
the law, the Greek, and the Bible's symbolic language.

## 1. The collision the common view never faces

Under the law, literal adultery is a CAPITAL crime, not a divorce
ground:
- Lev 20:10 — "the adulterer and the adulteress shall surely be PUT TO
  DEATH"; Deut 22:22-24 (married and betrothed cases; stoning).
- John 8:5 — "Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be
  STONED."
A dead spouse needs no bill of divorcement. If the exception clause
means "divorce is permitted for adultery," Jesus quietly commuted a
death penalty fifteen verses after "one jot or one tittle shall in no
wise pass" (Matt 5:18) — in the same sermon. The common reading makes
Jesus amend the law He said He came to fill full (plēroō, ch. 6).

## 2. The vocabulary: porneia ≠ moicheia (tagged)

- Matt 5:32 = Matt 19:9: "saving for the cause of FORNICATION{G4202
  porneia}, causeth her to commit ADULTERY{G3429 moichaō}" — BOTH words
  in one sentence. Jesus had the adultery-word in His mouth and did not
  use it for the exception.
- Matt 15:19 lists them as separate sins: "ADULTERIES{G3430},
  FORNICATIONS{G4202}."
- Counts: porneia G4202 26×; moicheuō G3431 22×; moichaō G3429 6×.
- Mark 10:11-12 and Luke 16:18 carry NO exception at all — the absolute
  form. Only Matthew (writing to Israel, with the Joseph narrative)
  carries the clause.
- Second witness: the Hebrew NT (Ele Ha-Sodot/Cochin, bibles/hg.txt +
  data/hg-chapters/) gives Luke 16:18 and Mark 10:11-12 absolute, with
  *na'aph* H5003 — the very verb of Lev 20:10.

## 3. The divorce statute and its ervah-bridge

Deut 24:1-4: the ground is "some{H1697} UNCLEANNESS{H6172}" — Hebrew
*ervat davar*, "the NAKEDNESS of a thing." H6172 *ervah* is the exact
word of Lev 18's forbidden-union catalog ("uncover the NAKEDNESS of…,"
54× corpus-wide, concentrated in Lev 18/20) — the statute's own word
points at the unions that were never lawful, not at adultery (which
ch. 28 of Deuteronomy has already sentenced to death two chapters
earlier). The statute regulates: bill in her hand, sent out, free to
remarry (24:2), and the NO-RETURN rule — the first husband "may not
take her again… after that she is defiled; for that is abomination"
(24:4). Note also: two cases where divorce is forbidden forever
(Deut 22:19, 29); the freed wife's rights (Ex 21:10-11 — ch. 8 uses
this); "put away" ≠ "give a bill" (Matt 5:31 assumes the bill; the
hard-hearted put away without one).

## 4. Fornication in the Bible's symbolic language (proves sym-fornication)

The union itself can BE the fornication:
- Ex 34:15-16 — intermarriage with the nations IS "go a WHORING after
  their gods… and make thy sons go a whoring after their gods."
- Prov 2:16-17 — the strange woman "forsaketh the guide of her youth,
  and forgetteth the COVENANT OF HER GOD" — marriage = covenant of God.
- Jer 3:9 — "committed ADULTERY with STONES and with STOCKS" — idols;
  2 Chr 21:11-13 Jehoram "caused Jerusalem to commit FORNICATION" =
  high places; Rev 17:2 the kings' fornication with the harlot.
- Hos 1-2 — the marriage commanded as a sign (ch. 4 already carries
  it); "she is not my wife… let her put away her whoredoms" (Hos 2:2).

## 5. The COMMANDED divorce (the author's seed, confirmed)

- Ezra 9:1-2; 10:2-3, 11 — the holy seed mingled; "let us make a
  covenant with our God to PUT AWAY all the wives… according to the
  counsel… of those that TREMBLE AT THE COMMANDMENT of our God; and let
  it be done ACCORDING TO THE LAW… separate yourselves from the people
  of the land, and from the strange wives." Divorce not permitted but
  commanded — for out-of-covenant unions.
- Neh 13:23-27 — same, with Solomon as the proof case ("even him did
  outlandish women cause to sin").
- Ground: Deut 7:3-4 ("they will turn away thy son from following me").
- Mal 2 holds BOTH edges in one oracle: v. 11 Judah "hath MARRIED THE
  DAUGHTER OF A STRANGE GOD" (condemned, cut off) — vv. 14-16 the "WIFE
  OF THY COVENANT" betrayed: "the LORD… hateth PUTTING AWAY." Putting
  away the covenant wife = treachery; keeping the strange god's
  daughter = treachery. Divorce is evil or obedience depending entirely
  on WHICH union it severs.
- Harmonize 1 Cor 7:12-15: the man CALLED while already married to an
  unbeliever "pleased to dwell" does NOT put away (sanctified spouse,
  called to peace) — the opposite case from Ezra's covenant community
  knowingly marrying into the abominations. New marriages: "only in the
  Lord" (7:39). If the unbeliever departs: "not under bondage."

## 6. God's own case file — He keeps His own law

- He did not stone His adulterous wife; He divorced her: "backsliding
  Israel committed adultery; I had PUT HER AWAY, and given her a BILL
  OF DIVORCE" (Jer 3:8); "Where is the bill of your mother's
  divorcement?" (Isa 50:1 — already quoted in ch. 16). The bill =
  exile/scattering.
- The return-riddle: Jer 3:1 quotes Deut 24:4 — "shall he return unto
  her again? shall not that land be greatly polluted? …YET RETURN AGAIN
  TO ME, saith the LORD." God poses His own legal impossibility.
- The solution is the gospel: Rom 7:1-4 — the wife is bound "so long as
  he liveth… but if the husband be DEAD, she is loosed from the law of
  her husband… ye also are become DEAD… by the BODY OF CHRIST; that ye
  should be MARRIED TO ANOTHER, even to him that is RAISED from the
  dead." The death that dissolves the bond falls on the HUSBAND — the
  death penalty the wife earned, He takes — and the risen Husband weds
  her new. Jer 31:31-32 frames the renewed covenant as exactly this
  remarriage: "which my covenant they brake, ALTHOUGH I WAS AN HUSBAND
  UNTO THEM."
- Hosea enacts it: "Go yet, love… an adulteress… So I BOUGHT her to me"
  (Hos 3:1-2); "thou shalt call me ISHI [my husband]" (2:16); "I will
  BETROTH thee unto me for ever… in righteousness, and in judgment"
  (2:19-20 — betrothal in the vocabulary of ch. 28).
- Isa 54:4-7 — "the reproach of thy WIDOWHOOD" forgotten; "thy MAKER is
  thine HUSBAND… with great mercies will I GATHER thee."
- John 8:3-11 — the death-penalty case laid before the Husband-to-die;
  the only One qualified to cast the stone declines: He will take the
  stroke Himself. "Neither do I condemn thee: GO, AND SIN NO MORE."

## 7. The two women at the end (the sequence IS the law)

Rev 17-19: the harlot's fornication judged — "he hath JUDGED the great
WHORE" (19:2), "utterly BURNED with fire" (18:8; cf. Lev 21:9, the
priest's daughter who plays the whore is burned) — and THEN, in the
same breath, "the MARRIAGE of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made
herself ready… arrayed in fine linen… the RIGHTEOUSNESS of saints"
(19:7-8; sym-garment). Execution of the false wife; wedding of the
true. Eph 5:25-32 the mystery ("Christ and the church"); 2 Cor 11:2
the chaste virgin espoused.

## 7b. Vashti and Esther — the deposed queen (author's addition, confirmed)

Divorce/deposition for DISOBEDIENCE, with no sexual sin in sight — and
the estate-transfer formula of the whole Bible:
- Esther 1:12 — "the queen Vashti REFUSED TO COME at the king's
  COMMANDMENT" — the ground is refusal of the king's summons, nothing
  else. Processed as a legal question: "What shall we do unto the queen
  Vashti ACCORDING TO LAW?" (1:15), settled by an unalterable decree
  (1:19).
- The stated stake is realm-wide obedience: "this deed of the queen
  shall come abroad unto ALL WOMEN, so that they shall DESPISE THEIR
  HUSBANDS" (1:17); the decree's purpose: "all the wives shall give to
  their husbands HONOUR" (1:20; 1:22). The King's wife must obey, or
  the whole land learns rebellion — the public dimension of the
  covenant marriage (cf. Ezek 36:20-23, the name profaned before the
  nations).
- The verdict formula: "let the king give her royal estate UNTO ANOTHER
  THAT IS BETTER THAN SHE" (1:19) = 1 Sam 15:28 "the LORD hath rent the
  kingdom of Israel from thee… and hath given it to a neighbour of
  thine, THAT IS BETTER THAN THOU" (Saul) = Matt 21:43 "the kingdom of
  God shall be TAKEN FROM YOU, and GIVEN to a nation bringing forth the
  fruits thereof." One formula, three depositions.
- Solomon completes the chain from the strange-wives side: 1 Kgs 11:1-11
  — strange wives turn his heart, and the same verdict falls: "I will
  surely REND THE KINGDOM from thee, and will GIVE IT to thy servant"
  (Neh 13:26 cites him as the proof case).
- The replacement bride: Esther, taken from the scattered exiles, made
  "queen INSTEAD OF Vashti" (2:17) — and she inverts Vashti exactly:
  Vashti refused to come when summoned; Esther comes UNSUMMONED, at
  peril of death, to intercede for her people — "I will go in unto the
  king, which is not according to the law: and IF I PERISH, I PERISH"
  (4:16). The deposed queen and the interceding bride are the two wives
  of the whole symbolic story.

## 8. Common-teaching survey (VERIFY quoted sources before any table)

The scholarly debate itself (Grok's summary, supplied by the author)
maps the camps — and shows the blind spot:
- "Many see it as any serious sexual immorality (including ongoing
  adultery)." — never asks what the LAW does with adultery (death, not
  divorce).
- "Some (the 'betrothal view')… sexual sin discovered during the Jewish
  betrothal period… similar to Joseph's situation." — half-sees it
  (Matt 1:18-19 is real data) but stays literal.
- "A minority link it to incestuous or forbidden-degree marriages
  (drawing from Acts 15)." — half-sees the ervah/Lev 18 bridge but
  stays literal.
- "The majority lexical and contextual evidence supports a broad
  understanding…" — consensus reached without once consulting the law's
  own penalties or the Bible's symbolic usage (Ex 34:15-16; Ezra), i.e.
  every camp reads the exception as a PERMISSION about literal sex;
  none follows porneia to the commanded divorce of the out-of-covenant
  union. That blind spot is the chapter's opening argument.

- Grok (supplied by author, usable as the AI-average like ch. 6):
  divorce permitted for porneia = broad sexual immorality.
- Majority evangelical: adultery + desertion grounds (Westminster
  Confession 24.5-6 — note WCF 24.5 itself half-preserves the betrothal
  case: "adultery or fornication committed AFTER A CONTRACT, being
  detected BEFORE MARRIAGE"). Verify wording before quoting.
- Catholic: marriage indissoluble; porneia read as unlawful union →
  annulment, not divorce (CCC ~1650, 2382-84) — ironically close to the
  unlawful-union reading. Verify.
- Historical setting of Matt 19:3 "for EVERY CAUSE": the Hillel
  "any-cause" divorce vs Shammai — the Pharisees' question quotes the
  rabbinic debate. Verify a citable source (m. Gittin 9:10).
- Betrothal view (Joseph, Matt 1:18-19: "her HUSBAND… minded to PUT HER
  AWAY privily" — espoused, called husband, resolved by putting away,
  called JUST while doing it — inside Matthew's own first chapter).

## 9. Meaning (synthesis)

Marriage = the covenant in miniature — the one-flesh union God joins
(Gen 2:24; Matt 19:6), the wife called by the husband's name under his
wing (ch. 8). Literal adultery inside it is answered by death, not
divorce — and at the cross the Husband took that death Himself.
Divorce exists in the law for the union that is itself fornication —
the out-of-covenant joining — where it is not permitted but commanded
(Ezra), because "what God hath joined" was never joined by God. God
followed His own statute: He gave adulterous Israel the bill (exile),
faced His own no-return rule, and answered it by dying — freeing the
wife to be married to Him raised, in the renewed covenant. The Bible
closes the case file with a burning and a wedding.
