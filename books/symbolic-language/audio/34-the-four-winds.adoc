MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Thirty-Four: The Four Winds.

[long pause]
Prophecy is written in wind: in Hebrew, wind, breath, and spirit are one word — _ruach_.
Here we take up that symbol and fix what the four winds _are_; the Sea study in Part Six will meet these same winds stirring the nations.

The plainest text is Zechariah’s.
He sees four chariots drawn by colored horses come out from between two mountains of brass, and asks what they are.
The angel answers directly:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 6:5]
____
…These are the *four spirits of the heavens*, which *go forth from standing before the Lord* of all the earth.
____

[pause]

The word for __“spirits”__ is _ruach_ — the same word rendered __“wind”__ and __“breath”__ everywhere else in the Old Testament.
So the four winds are four spirits, and they hold an office: they __“go forth from standing before the Lord.”__
They are dispatched from the throne.

[long pause]

Now consider Not Directions, Not Weather.

[long pause]

The dictionary flattens __“the four winds”__ two ways: into the four points of the compass — north, south, east, west, a figure for __“everywhere”__ — or into wind itself, the weather.
Scripture allows neither.
The angel did not call them four directions; he called them four _spirits_.
The number four gives them their reach — they cover the four quarters of the whole earth — but the winds are the _agents_ sent across those quarters, not the quarters themselves.

Nor are they weather.
The same word names what stands in the divine council:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 104:4]
____
Who maketh his *angels spirits*; his ministers a flaming fire.
____

[pause]

Angels are spirits; spirits are winds — one word in Hebrew.
The four winds are commissioned beings: they stand before the Lord and go forth on assignment, then return to report.
And all wind answers to its Maker — He rebukes even a literal storm, __“Peace, be still,”__ and it obeys; the more so these spirit-winds He sends and recalls.
When Scripture speaks of the four winds, it speaks of God’s executing power under His direct authority.

[long pause]

Now consider The Four Horsemen.

[long pause]

The New Testament shows these same four spirits under another image.
In Zechariah they are chariots and horses; in Revelation they are the four horsemen, commissioned one by one as the Lamb opens the seals.
The colors mark their assignments: the white horse goes out conquering with a bow — a case study of its own in Part Six; the red takes __“peace from the earth”__ with __“a great sword”__; the black carries scales for famine; and the fourth is Death, who carries what all four deal:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 6:8]
____
…behold a *pale* horse: and his name that sat on him was *Death*… to kill with sword, and with *hunger*, and with *death*, and with the *beasts of the earth*.
____

[pause]

Those four are the judgments Ezekiel had already named:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 14:21]
____
…my *four sore judgments*… the sword, and the *famine*, and the *noisome beast*, and the *pestilence*…
____

[pause]

They are the covenant curses of Leviticus and Deuteronomy, executed by the four spirits.
Jesus lists the same set — __“wars… famines, and pestilences”__ — and calls them __“the beginning of sorrows.”__
One sequence, recorded three times: Ezekiel’s judgments, Zechariah’s spirits, John’s horsemen.

[long pause]

Now consider Scatter and Gather.

[long pause]

The four winds are not assigned to judgment only.
The same spirits carry opposite errands, according to what the throne sends.
They _scatter_ a people in judgment:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 49:36]
____
…upon Elam will I bring the *four winds*… and will *scatter them* toward all those winds…
____

[pause]

They _gather_ a people in restoration:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:31]
____
…he shall send his angels… and they shall *gather together his elect* from the *four winds*, from one end of heaven to the other.
____

[pause]

They _stir_ the sea so that the beast-kingdoms rise from it — __“the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea.
And four great beasts came up”__.
They carry _breath_ to the dead — __“Come from the four winds, O breath… that they may live”__.
Scatter and gather, stir and raise: the wind’s direction is set by its Sender, not by itself.

The gathering has an address: the elect are collected __“from the *four winds*, from one end of heaven to the other”__ — from the four _corners_ of the earth where the dispersed wait, corners the Wings chapter searched; the winds sweep the quarters, and the quarters give up their gleanings.

The judgment-wind even has a compass habit.
It was __“a strong east *wind*”__ that split the sea all night; an east wind carried in the locusts; a __“vehement east wind”__ broke Jonah; and Hosea names whose wind it is: __“an *east wind* shall come, *the wind of the LORD*… and his spring shall become dry”__.

When the promised Spirit came, it came in this symbol’s own dress:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Acts 2:2]
____
And suddenly there came a sound from heaven as of a *rushing mighty wind*, and it filled all the house where they were sitting.
____

[pause]

Ezekiel’s breath arrived on the appointed day.
Jesus had already tied the knot for Nicodemus: __“The *wind* bloweth where it listeth… so is every one that is *born of the Spirit*”__ — one word for wind and Spirit, in Hebrew and in Greek alike.
Even the wind wears the covenant edge: God __“did fly upon the *wings* of the wind”__ — _kanphei-ruach_, the hem-word on the wind itself — and the creation psalm sets chariot, wings, and ministers side by side: __“who maketh the clouds his chariot: who walketh upon the *wings of the wind*: who maketh his angels *spirits*”__.
A wind can carry a people in that hem; the first deliverance said so — __“I bare you on *eagles' wings*, and brought you unto *myself*”__.

The four-going-out from the presence is older than the chariots.
The Spirit moved on the face of the waters at creation, and the water tells the wind's story: __“a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into *four heads*”__ — one source at the presence, parted four ways to reach the whole earth, as the four spirits go forth from standing before the Lord.
Wind above, water below — two carriers of one _ruach_, sent out fourfold at the beginning and gathering home from the same four corners at the end.
And mark who waits at the corners: on the garment, the corner is where the law hangs its reminder — __“that ye may look upon it, and *remember all the commandments* of the LORD”__ — and at the earth's four wings wait __“the remnant… which *keep the commandments* of God”__.
The winds gather from the hem those who kept the hem.

[long pause]

Now consider Held Until the Sealing.

[long pause]

One more fact completes the picture: the four winds can be held.
Before they are loosed, John sees them restrained:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 7:1-3]
____
…four angels… holding the *four winds* of the earth, that the wind should not blow… *Hurt not* the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, *till we have* sealed *the servants of our God* in their foreheads.
____

[pause]

The winds are kept back until God’s servants are marked.
And mark where the scene stands: after the sixth seal is opened — the riders commissioned at the first four seals are still reined in at the four corners when the sixth is broken.
The seals commission; they do not release.
Not a wind blows until the last servant is sealed.

The four winds, then, are God’s executing spirits — four because they cover the whole earth, the four quarters of heaven.
They are dispatched from the throne and recalled to it; they stir the nations, raise and topple empires, scatter and gather peoples, deal death and breathe life, and are held or loosed at a word.
They are not blind forces of nature, and not powers in revolt.
They are the ministers of the One who __“maketh his angels spirits,”__ and they do only what He sends them to do.
