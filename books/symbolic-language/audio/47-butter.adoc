MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Forty-Seven: Butter.

[long pause]
Butter begins at the churn: milk — the word received — worked by labor until it sets solid.
Very few would connect butter to sound doctrine and meat.
Scripture does, and in more places than the churn.

[long pause]

Now consider The Discernment Diet.

[long pause]

Isaiah serves butter to the survivors:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 7:22]
____
…for the abundance of milk that they shall give he shall eat butter: for butter and honey shall every one eat *that is left in the land*.
____

[pause]

The remnant eats the worked word.
The outcome of that diet was named seven verses earlier — Immanuel eats butter and honey to __“know to refuse the evil, and choose the good”__, the discernment Hebrews assigns the strong-meat eater.

[long pause]

Now consider Washed Steps.

[long pause]

Job, remembering his best days, packs four symbols into one sentence:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Job 29:6]
____
When I washed my steps with butter, and the rock poured me out rivers of oil;
____

[pause]

The steps are the walk — the way a man's feet keep; and Job washed his in butter: a walk cleansed by the worked word.
Beside it, the rock — the covenant — pours out rivers of oil, the pressed and proven works.
No literal dairy explains the sentence; the symbols read it in one breath.

[long pause]

Now consider Butter in the Wilderness.

[long pause]

When David fled Absalom, provision met him in the wilderness:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, 2 Samuel 17:28-29]
____
…wheat, and barley, and flour… and honey, and *butter*, and sheep, and cheese of kine, for David, and for the people that were with him, to eat: for they said, The people is *hungry, and weary, and thirsty*, in the wilderness.
____

[pause]

The anointed king, hungering and thirsting in the wilderness, is fed butter by faithful hands.
The counterfeit belongs to the very same story.
Psalm 55 is David's psalm of the betraying counselor in that crisis, and it names his speech:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 55:21]
____
The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but *war was in his heart*: his words were softer than oil, yet were they drawn swords.
____

[pause]

Counterfeit butter and counterfeit oil in a single verse — speech worked smooth to imitate sound doctrine, with drawn swords beneath.
And mark the third symbol: the hidden thing is *swords*, and the sword is the word — __“the sword of the Spirit, which is the *word of God*”__.
All three terms of the psalm's ledger are word-symbols — butter, oil, sword.

A symbol's sense can turn with its bearer, and here every term is the deceiver's version; but the structure of the analogy holds, and its holding is itself a witness: even the counterfeit prices itself in the true currency.
In one crisis Scripture sets both dishes on the table: true butter carried to the fugitive king, and butter-smooth counsel hunting him.

[long pause]

Now consider A Lordly Dish.

[long pause]

Jael's hospitality escalates through the whole dairy:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Judges 5:25]
____
He asked water, and she gave him milk; she brought forth *butter* in a lordly dish.
____

[pause]

Asked for water, she serves milk and sets out butter — and the enemy of Israel, fed the richest the tent held, lies down and is finished.
Whether the asked-for water carries the living-water register is a question this book leaves open; the escalation itself — water, milk, butter — runs the symbol's whole ladder in one verse.

The churn stands, then, as more than a figure of growth.
Butter is the word worked solid: the discernment diet, the remnant's food, the wash of the walking feet — and the LORD himself accepted it at Abraham's table and listed it in His own provision.
Taste what is set before you, and mark whether it was churned — or merely smoothed.
And the meat on the same table has its counterfeit too — the flesh that presents clean and is not — and it is the next chapter.
