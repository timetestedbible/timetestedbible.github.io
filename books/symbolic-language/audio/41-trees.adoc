MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Forty-One: Trees.

[long pause]
The four winds are held back __“till we have sealed the servants of our God,”__ under the command __“Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees”__.
The _sea_ is the restless mass of peoples; the _tree_ stands beside it in that verse, and Scripture defines it just as plainly.
When Nebuchadnezzar dreams of a great tree filling the earth, Daniel does not guess; he decodes it:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 4:22]
____
*It is thou, O king*, that art grown and become strong: for thy greatness is grown, and reacheth unto heaven, and thy *dominion to the end of the earth*.
____

[pause]

The tree is the king and his kingdom — Babylon as a national body.
This is the symbol’s fixed sense: a tree is a nation, a people group in its living, rooted form.
The same equation recurs across the prophets — Israel is a __“vine”__, Assyria a __“cedar”__, the covenant people an __“olive tree”__.
Whatever the species, a tree is a nation — and the species itself marks which.

[long pause]

Now consider By Their Kind.

[long pause]

The kind of tree is no accident; it names the nation.
Israel appears as the trees of the covenant land — the _vine_, the _fig_, and the _olive_: __“The LORD called thy name, A green olive tree”__; often all at once — __“I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness; I saw your fathers as the firstripe in the fig tree”__, and __“he hath laid my vine waste, and barked my fig tree”__.
The gentile empires are the towering forest trees — Assyria a _cedar_, and Pharaoh likened to the same __“among the trees of Eden”__.

When Jesus curses a _fig tree_, or tells of a barren one in a vineyard, He is not teaching horticulture; He is pronouncing on Israel.
The kind of tree names the nation — and often its character: the cedar’s imperial height, the vine’s call to fruit, the fig’s covenant standing.

[long pause]

Now consider The Anatomy of a Nation.

[long pause]

The tree is a precise symbol because its parts carry meaning.
Paul lays out the anatomy with the olive tree:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Romans 11:17]
____
And if some of the *branches be broken off*, and thou, being a *wild olive tree*, wert *graffed in* among them, and with them partakest of the *root* and fatness of the olive tree…
____

[pause]

The _root_ is the nation’s foundation — the patriarchs, the covenant that makes the whole holy.
The _branches_ are its members.
Branches can be broken off (cut from the nation) and others grafted in, and the tree persists through the exchange.
A nation is a living structure: rooted in its origin, composed of members, able to lose some and gain others without ceasing to be itself.

[long pause]

Now consider Fruit and the Fire.

[long pause]

A tree is judged by what it produces.
Jesus states it as a rule:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:17]
____
…every *good tree* bringeth forth *good* fruit; but a *corrupt tree* bringeth forth *evil fruit*.
____

[pause]

If the tree is a nation and its fruit is its works, this is how nations are weighed — by what they do.
The barren tree is cut down:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:19]
____
Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is *hewn down*, and cast into the fire.
____

[pause]

This is the standard image for the judgment of a nation.
The axe __“is laid unto the root of the trees”__; Babylon’s great tree is felled — __“Hew the tree down”__.
And a nation can be fruitful in form yet condemned: Israel was __“an empty vine”__ that __“bringeth forth fruit unto himself”__ — full of works, empty of covenant purpose.

[long pause]

Now consider A Forest of Nations.

[long pause]

Because every nation is a tree, the world’s nations together make a forest, and Scripture treats them so.
The tree-texts that decoded the mustard seed — Ezekiel’s riddle of the low tree exalted and Daniel’s great tree that was a king — are the forest’s charter, and the riddle’s closing word surveys the whole stand:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 17:24]
____
And all the *trees of the* field shall know that I the LORD have *brought down the high tree*, have *exalted the low tree*, have dried up the green tree, and have made the dry tree to flourish…
____

[pause]

__“All the trees of the field”__ are all the nations; God deals with them as a gardener deals with trees — felling the proud, raising the low.

The same parable fixes what the cedar is — and is not: the __“high cedar”__ of that chapter is the royal house of David itself, and God promises to crop __“of the highest branch”__ a tender twig and plant it __“in the mountain of the height of Israel,”__ where it becomes __“a goodly cedar”__ sheltering __“all fowl of every wing”__.
Assyria a cedar, David's house a cedar, the righteous growing __“like a cedar in Lebanon”__: the cedar is height and majesty, whoever bears it — the symbol is the stature, not a badge of the heathen.
And the great trees shelter others: under Nebuchadnezzar’s tree __“the beasts of the field had shadow… and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs”__ — the peoples and tribes that live within an empire’s reach.

This fixes the tree among the symbols already defined.
A _mountain_ is a kingdom — the seat of ruling authority.
A _tree_ is the nation, the living body of a people, which grows _on_ the mountain, under its jurisdiction.
The _sea_ is those same peoples in their unrooted, turbulent state — beasts _rise from_ the sea, but shelter _under_ the tree.
And beneath the tree is the _grass_ — the individual mortal, which is its own study.
Kingdom, nation, person: mountain, tree, grass.

Two more branches of the symbol remain before the beasts.
The pair of olive trees Zechariah saw return in Revelation as the two witnesses — covenant trees standing before God, taken up in the Jacob, Israel, and Ephraim chapter.
And the symbol scales down, as the sea did: the righteous _man_ is a tree in miniature — __“he shall be like a *tree planted* by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season”__; the redeemed are __“trees of righteousness, the *planting of the LORD*”__.
Grass is the mortal in his frailty; the tree is the same man rooted in covenant, watered, and bearing.

[long pause]

Now consider Beasts.

[long pause]

Look again at the beasts that sheltered under Nebuchadnezzar's tree — all the animals under its branches.
For the beast is the other figure of a nation.
Where the tree is a people rooted and fruitful, the beast is a people wild and ravening; and Daniel decodes it as plainly as he did the tree:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:17]
____
These *great beasts*, which are four, are *four kings*, which shall arise out of the earth.
____

[pause]

A beast is a kingdom, and the kind of beast marks which — as the species of tree marked which nation.
The lion, the bear, the leopard, and the nameless fourth are four empires; __“the ram… are the kings of Media and Persia… the rough goat is the king of Grecia”__.
And these come up not from the garden but __“from the sea”__ — the same peoples, seen now as predators rising from the restless deep.

The two figures meet in one man.
The king who was the great tree is sentenced to become a beast — __“Let his heart be changed from man's, and let a beast's heart be given unto him”__ — until he __“did eat grass as oxen”__.
The ruler who forgets God is carried down through the whole order, from the tree that filled the earth to a beast on all fours feeding on grass; for __“man that is in honour, and understandeth not, is like the beasts that perish”__.

This is why clean and unclean divide the animals as the covenant divides mankind.
God's own are the flock — the sheep who hear His voice; the nations are the wild beasts.
So when God opens the door to the Gentiles He shows Peter a sheet of __“all manner of fourfooted beasts… and wild beasts, and creeping things”__, with the command __“Rise, Peter; kill, and eat… What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common”__.
To _kill_ and to _eat_ is the language of covenant-making — the sacrifice slain and the meal shared that seal it — so the vision is God taking the nations into covenant.
Peter gives its meaning himself, twice over:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Acts 10:28]
____
Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another *nation*; but God hath shewed me that I should not call *any man* common or unclean.
____

[pause]

The unclean animals are the nations, cleansed by covenant.

It is why the Gentile is called a dog — __“It is not meet to take the children's bread, and to cast it to dogs,”__ and the woman answers, __“yet the dogs eat of the crumbs”__: outside the house, an unclean animal, until she is brought in.
Mark keeps the table in her answer — __“the dogs *under the table* eat of the children's crumbs”__ — and the table is the dog's defining line: the unclean beast outside the covenant meal.
And the crucifixion psalm shows that beast at its work: __“Dogs have compassed me… they *pierced* my hands and my feet”__ — the psalm rings the cross with beasts, the bulls of Bashan, the roaring lion, the dogs — and it was the dog, the Gentile power of Rome, whose hands drove the nails.

Tree or beast, forest or field, clean or unclean, the figure is one — the nations of the earth, which God plants and fells, tames and cleanses.

[long pause]

Now consider Gardens.

[long pause]

If a tree is a nation, a garden — a planted stand of trees — is a grouping of nations.
Ezekiel says as much: he sets Egypt among __“the trees of Eden, that were in the garden of God”__, and names them — __“This is Pharaoh and all his multitude”__.
The garden of God is a field of nation-trees.

Read so, a recurring charge in Isaiah changes register.
Isaiah indicts Judah for worship __“in gardens”__ — __“that sacrificeth in gardens, and burneth incense upon altars of brick”__, and shames her __“for the gardens that ye have chosen”__.
The usual reading makes these literal pagan groves; by the symbol they are the nations Judah chose to run to — sacrificing among them, trusting in them — and the sentence fits the crime: __“ye shall be as an oak whose leaf fadeth, and as a garden that hath no water”__.
She chose the gardens of the nations; she becomes a dead garden herself.

Which unlocks the hardest of them:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 66:17]
____
They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the *gardens* behind *one tree* in the midst, eating *swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse*, shall be *consumed together*…
____

[pause]

By the symbol it is plain: they sanctify themselves _in the gardens_ — among the nations; _behind one_ — following after one of them; _eating_ the swine, the abomination, and the mouse — taking up the nations’ unclean ways.
They enter the nations, follow one, eat its abominations, and share its end — __“consumed together.”__

The traditional reading takes this as a literal garden-cult, but it rests on a word that is not in the text.
The Hebrew says only __“after one in the midst”__; __“tree”__ is supplied by the translators — and with it gone, the literal reading has nothing left to name, so it guesses: a sacred tree, a priest in the midst, a deity called __“One.”__
The symbol supplies nothing — __“in the gardens, after one”__ is among the nations, following one — and it reads with the context, for the next verses are the LORD gathering __“all nations and tongues”__ and sending the escaped __“unto the nations”__.
The whole passage is about the nations; so is the garden.

The end-times setting proves the diet itself eternal.
This is the last judgment — the LORD coming __“with fire”__ to __“plead with all flesh”__, with all nations gathered to it.
At that consummation God still consumes those who eat __“swine’s flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse.”__
The line between clean and unclean is not abolished; it holds to the end of the age.

We have already seen that the law was not abolished but filled full, and that the covenant-name is everlasting — and the food law is part of that law; here it stands as the very ground of the verdict.
Nor does the symbol soften the letter; the two are one act.
To eat the nations’ unclean food is to sit at the nations’ table and take their defilement into yourself — so those who go in among the nations, follow one, and eat as it eats are consumed with it.
The name is eternal, the law is eternal, and the diet is eternal with them.

Return, then, to the sealing.
Before the four winds are loosed, the order is __“Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees”__ — and a tree is a nation, as the sea beside it is a people.
The God who plants and fells the trees of the field is the One the winds obey.
