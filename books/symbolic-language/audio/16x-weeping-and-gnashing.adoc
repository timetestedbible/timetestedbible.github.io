MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Bonus Study: The Weeping and the Gnashing.

[long pause]

Psalm 112 ends with this: __“The wicked shall *see* it, and be *grieved*; he shall *gnash with his teeth*, and *melt away*: the desire of the wicked shall perish.”__

[long pause]

Seven times Jesus ends a judgment scene with the same words — and the English hides a detail the Greek insists on.
He does not say there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, as though describing a noise.
He says there shall be *the* weeping, and *the* gnashing of teeth.
[beat]
Definite articles. A known scene — fixed, one His hearers were expected to recognize.
Luke shows us the scene whole:

[quote.scripture, Luke 13:28]
____
There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, *when ye shall see* Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and *you yourselves thrust out*.
____

[pause]
The weeping and the gnashing are not the soundtrack of a torture chamber.
They happen *at a sight* — the kingdom opened, the patriarchs seated, and the claimants outside, looking in.

[long pause]

The Psalm That Wrote the Scene.

[long pause]

Scripture composed this scene long before the Gospels quoted it, and it runs element for element:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 112:10]
____
The wicked shall *see* it, and be *grieved*; he shall *gnash with his teeth*, and *melt away*: the desire of the wicked shall perish.
____

[pause]
He sees. He grieves — there is the weeping. He gnashes. And he melts away.
[beat]
The psalm spends its first nine verses on what the wicked man is looking at — the man who fears the LORD and delights in His commandments, blessed, established, exalted with honor.
The gnashing is the wicked man's answer to *that* — and its end is not an endless scream but a dissolution: __“the desire of the wicked shall perish.”__

[long pause]

What Gnashing Is.

[long pause]

Run every occurrence of the Hebrew word, and one definition stands in all of them — gnashing is what the wicked do *at the righteous*.
The psalmist watches it happen:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 37:12]
____
The wicked plotteth against the just, and gnasheth upon him with his teeth.
____

[pause]
Job's enemy __“gnasheth upon me with his teeth.”__
[beat]
The __“hypocritical mockers”__ of the feasts, says the psalmist, __“gnashed upon me with their teeth.”__
[beat]
Jerusalem's enemies, in Lamentations, __“hiss and gnash the teeth”__ over her fall.
[pause]
Never once is it the sound of pain.
It is the face of hatred — teeth ground at someone else's standing.

[pause]

The New Testament stages it in its own narrative, at the trial of Stephen:

[quote.scripture, Acts 7:54-56]
____
When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they *gnashed on him with their teeth*… But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and *saw the glory of God*, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God.
____

[pause]
Hold the two halves of that scene together. [beat] The council, gnashing. The righteous man, seeing the kingdom opened.
It is what Luke showed us, rehearsed in history with the roles' endings not yet dealt — and at the end of the age the same scene stands final, with the sight given to the ones outside.

[long pause]

Who Receives the Sentence.

[long pause]

Now walk the seven texts and mark who is cast out — for it is never the heathen.
[beat]
__“The *children of the kingdom* shall be cast out.”__
[beat]
The tares — __“all things that offend, and them which do *lawlessness*”__ — gathered *out of His kingdom*.
[beat]
The bad fish, severed __“from among the *just*.”__
[beat]
The wedding *guest*, without the garment.
[beat]
The *evil servant*, appointed __“his portion with the *hypocrites*.”__
[beat]
The *unprofitable servant*.
[beat]
The ones who __“have eaten and drunk in thy presence.”__
[pause]
Every one is an insider — a claimant of the house, a servant of the master, a guest at the wedding.
One of the seven closes a loop the psalms opened. The evil servant's portion is __“with the hypocrites”__ — and the gnashers at the feasts were the __“*hypocritical* mockers.”__
The company he joins is the company that gnashes.

[long pause]

The Hebrew Witness.

[long pause]

The Hebrew Matthew carries the formula in the Old Testament's own vocabulary — the gnashing is _charaq_, the very root of the psalm that wrote the scene — and it names the destination without the English fog.
[beat]
Where the English says *outer darkness*, over the cast-out children of the kingdom, the Hebrew reads *the dark places of Gehinnom*.
[beat]
Over the wedding guest, it reads *lowest Sheol* — the _tachtit_ of Moses' song in Deuteronomy, where the fire of His anger burns down to the grave.
[beat]
Over the unprofitable servant — *the dark places of the depths*.
[pause]
Outer darkness, the furnace of fire, the portion with the hypocrites, *Gehinnom*, lowest *Sheol* — five names, one scene, and every Hebrew name is the vocabulary of the grave.
The witness agrees with the psalm's ending — the gnasher *melts away*.

[long pause]

The Reading.

[long pause]

The weeping is grief over the loss; the gnashing is rage at the blessed — sorrow for self and hatred of the righteous in one face.
The formula does not describe torments inflicted on the excluded.
It describes the excluded *unchanged* — confronted with the kingdom they claimed, they do not fall down and repent; they grind their teeth at the ones inside, as the council ground theirs at Stephen.
[pause]
The gnashing is the evidence read into the verdict — the exclusion is just — and the sentence of the psalm completes it.
They melt away, and the desire of the wicked perishes.

[long pause]
