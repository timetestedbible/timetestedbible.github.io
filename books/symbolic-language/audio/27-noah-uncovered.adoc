MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Twenty-Seven: Noah Uncovered.

[long pause]
On the first day of the first month — the day Israel would later call New Year's day — a man who has been shut in a ship for almost a year opens the roof.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 8:13]
____
And it came to pass in the six hundredth and first year, in the first month, the first day of the month, the waters were dried up from off the earth: and Noah *removed the covering* of the ark, and *looked, and, behold*, the face of the ground was dry.
____

[pause]

A date, a dried earth, a covering removed, a look.
It reads like a ship's log, and it is one.

Mark three things before moving on.
This is the only event in Genesis dated to the first day of the first month — and every Nisan 1 that Scripture dates after it carries the same freight: a tabernacle raised, a temple sanctified, a going up from Babylon begun.
The date phrase itself is written backwards: __“in the *one* and six hundredth year”__ — where Genesis 7:6, one chapter earlier, gives the standard order, __“six hundred years.”__
Hebrew fronts a word to point at it; the text is waving.
And Jesus took this narrative, of all narratives, as the type of the end: __“as it was in the days of Noe, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man”__.

Do what this part of the book has learned to do.
Remove the vowel points, and ask what else the letters say.

_Be'achat_ — __“in the one”__ — is the letters of _echad_, the Shema's word: __“the LORD our God, the LORD is *one*”__ — one as in unified, whole, nothing missing.
At the completeness.
_Me'ot_ — __“hundreds”__ — divides into _m_ + _ot_: *of signs* — and _ot_ is the covenant-sign word, the blood on the doorposts, circumcision, the Sabbath.
Paul rules the equation this book needs: __“the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness”__ — a sign is a seal.
_Shanah_ — __“year”__ — is written on the root that means *to turn*: a year is a turning, and a turning is an age.

Set the three together and the backwards date reads: *at the completeness of six seals of the age* — the very three words the disciples would one day hand to Jesus as a question: __“what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the *age*?”__.

_Charvu hamayim_ — __“the waters were dried up”__ — but חרב is two words in one skin: _charav_, to lay waste, and _cherev_, the sword.
And the waters have been defined since this book's fourth chapter: __“the waters… are *peoples*, and multitudes, and nations”__.
The peoples were desolated — _me'al ha'aretz_, __“from off the earth,”__ or read the preposition's other face, *from above* the earth, the direction every judgment-by-fire arrives from: __“the same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven… Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed”__.

_Vayyasar Noach et-mikhseh hatevah_ — __“and Noah removed the covering of the ark.”__
Every word in this clause is double.

Noah's name is _nuach_ — *rest*, the Sabbath's own root — and Scripture defines the man the first time it introduces him: __“just and *perfect* in his *generations*”__, where __“perfect”__ is _tamim_, complete, and __“generations”__ is _dorot_, the ages: the righteous, complete through the ages of the Rest.
The verb __“removed”__ is singular; make Noah the righteous — a company — and the singular verb goes looking for a singular subject: *God took up* the righteous, __“caught up together… to meet the Lord”__.
The little word _et_ — untranslatable object-marker — is also, in the same two letters, the word *with*: took them up *with* the covering.

The __“covering”__ is _mikhseh_, from _kasah_, to cover — the same letters that, pointed otherwise, read as _keseh_, the full moon, and _kisse_, the throne, a pair of readings the Pearls of Wisdom chapter of Part Four will spend in full.
And the __“ark”__ is _tevah_ — a word Scripture uses for exactly two vessels in all its pages: Noah's ship and the basket that carried the infant Moses, both bearing a life through the waters of a death-decree — and Noah's is sealed with _kaphar_ — the King James prints __“pitch,”__ and everywhere else the word is *atonement*.
The covering of the covenant.

_Vayyar v'hinneh_ — __“and looked, and, behold.”__
The letters of __“looked”__ read three ways: he *saw*; they *appeared*; he *revered*.
__“When Christ, who is our life, shall appear, then shall ye also *appear* with him in glory”__.
The closing clause returns the opening verb: _charvu penei ha'adamah_ — the *face* of the ground was desolated — and _adamah_, the ground, wears _adam_, mankind, in its letters.

Now read the whole verse the second time through:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 8:13 — the letters read again]
____
At the completeness of six seals of the age — in the first month, the first day of the month — the *peoples* were desolated and laid waste *from above* the earth.
God *took up the righteous* of the Sabbath's ages *with the covering* of the covenant, and they *appeared* — and behold, the face of the earth was desolated.
____

[pause]

This second reading is not a new oracle; it is one John saw.
Open the sixth seal: the sun black, the moon as blood, the heavens departing — and the kings of the earth crying to the mountains:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 6:16]
____
And said to the mountains and rocks, Fall on us, and hide us from the *face* of him that sitteth on the *throne*, and from the wrath of the Lamb…
____

[pause]

The face, and the throne — _kisse_, the covering-word's other pointing.
And the very next scene is the taken-up company: __“a great multitude… clothed with white robes… These are they which came out of great tribulation”__ — the covered ones.
The flood account and the sixth seal are the same picture, and the second was written inside the first, letter for letter, two thousand years before John opened his eyes on Patmos.

Hold this reading to the same law as every reading in this book.
Each word must be attested in the lexicon — no invented senses; the assembled sentence must agree with Scripture written elsewhere — and here it is Revelation itself, seal by seal, that vouches; and the second reading must never unseat the first, and never touch a command.
The log of the flood still stands; the ship still dried on a real mountain; nothing here loosens a verse from its plain sense.
The warrant for hearing twice is no scholar's technique — it is the practice these chapters opened from Scripture's own hand: God decoding His own pun for Jeremiah, David's *spoken once, twice have I heard*, Isaiah rhyming his verdict into the letters.

Now weigh what this verse would cost to write.
A poet can rhyme a line; a careful author can plant a pun and land it.
But sit down and try to compose twelve consecutive words that read as a dated entry in a ship's log — grammar sound, narrative plain — while the same letters, pointed differently, read as the sixth seal of a book that would not be written for two and a half millennia: every alternate sense attested, every symbol matching the definitions Scripture gives elsewhere, the two readings never fouling each other from the first word to the last.

One pun is wit.
A sustained double reading the length of a sentence, hidden under the points and waiting while empires rose and translation after translation printed only the log — that is not wit.
This is the fingerprint the Introduction found in the web — the last chapter called the puns the same hand pressing deeper; here the hand writes an entire verse twice in a single stroke.
And the key that unlocks it was hung on the door by the Lord Himself: as it was in the days of Noah — the days of Noah were *written* as the days of the Son of man.

Genesis 8:13 is one example, and here the printed reading keeps the first seat: the log is primary, and the prophecy rides within it.
It is not always so.
The chapter on Lucifer's declared plan — the reading this book's own cover is painted from — waits in Part Four to show the other case: a verse whose primary reading is, by the letters’ own weight, the one lying hidden under the points — hidden by the very one the verse is warning about.

There is one dimension deeper still.
Before Israel wrote in the square letters of Babylon, the letters themselves were pictures — and the pictures preach.
What the oldest pictures spell in the Name of God is the next chapter.
