MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Twenty: The Fool and the Wise.

[long pause]
A single word can put a man __“in danger of hell fire”__:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 5:22]
____
…whosoever shall say, Thou *fool*, shall be in danger of *hell fire*.
____

[pause]

If calling someone a fool is that grave, the word is worth knowing — and it has drifted far from its meaning in Scripture.
To us a fool is someone slow or silly.
In the Bible a fool is something else entirely, and it has nothing to do with intelligence.

[long pause]

Now consider Hearing and Doing.

[long pause]

The fool is marked first by what he says in his heart:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 14:1]
____
The *fool* hath said in his heart, *There is no God*.
They are corrupt, they have done abominable works…
____

[pause]

Not “there is no God” as a theory, but as a way of living — as though no one were watching and nothing were owed.
From there the rest follows: __“fools despise wisdom and instruction”__; __“the way of a fool is right in his own eyes”__ — his own eyes again, his own measure of right.
And the Lord Himself draws the line — not between those who hear and those who don’t, but between two men who hear the very same words:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:24-26]
____
Whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and *doeth them*, I will liken him unto a *wise* man, which built his house upon a rock… And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and *doeth them not*, shall be likened unto a *foolish* man, which built his house upon the sand.
____

[pause]

Both hear.
One does, one does not.
That is the whole difference between the wise and the fool.
Wisdom, then, is not cleverness but obedience: __“a good understanding have all they that do his commandments”__; __“keep therefore and do them; for this is your wisdom”__.
The fool is not the dull man; he is the disobedient one, however clever.

[long pause]

Now consider The Ten Virgins.

[long pause]

With that definition in place, the parable of the ten virgins opens, for it is built of symbols already defined.
Begin with who the virgins are — and who they are not.
The Hebrew Matthew reads the parable's first line: the kingdom of heaven is like ten virgins who took their lamps and went out to meet *the bridegroom and the bride*.
The virgins are not the bride; they go out to meet her.
Scripture painted the scene long before, in the royal wedding psalm:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 45:14-15]
____
She shall be brought unto the king in raiment of needlework: the *virgins her companions* that follow her shall be brought unto thee.
With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought: they shall *enter into the king's palace*.
____

[pause]

The virgins are the bride's espoused company, following her in — each one __“espoused… as a chaste virgin”__.
And in the law, a virgin can already belong to a husband.
__“Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the *husband of her youth*”__ — espoused, the wedding still ahead, and yet the law already counts her his *wife*.
Virginity in this language is covenant standing — espoused to one and gone after no other — not a grade earned.

The number is not decoration.
When the LORD tore the kingdom from Solomon's son, the prophet Ahijah rent a new garment and counted the pieces: __“Take thee *ten* pieces… I will give *ten* tribes to thee”__ — and it is that ten-tribe house, put away for her adultery and promised home again, whom the prophets insist on calling __“the *virgin* of Israel”__.
__“Turn again, O virgin of Israel”__ stands in the very chapter that announces the renewed covenant: the wife lawfully freed by her Husband's death is espoused afresh — as though a maiden.
And the kingdom of heaven is likened to *ten* virgins.

All ten therefore stand in one covenant standing.
None is a harlot; all are espoused, all carry lamps, all wait for one Bridegroom — and still five are shut out.
What divides them is not their purity as a literal virgin in the modern sense, but their store:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 25:3-4]
____
They that were *foolish* took their lamps, and *took no oil* with them: But the *wise* took *oil* in their vessels with their lamps.
____

[pause]

The Hebrew Matthew names the two natures outright: five were _lazy fools_, and five _diligent and wise_ — laziness is precisely the fool's disease, hearing without rising to do.
They are not careless about a detail; they are foolish _women_ — the nature comes first, the empty lamp only reveals it.
When the cry comes at midnight, their own mouths pronounce the verdict — __“our lamps are *put out*”__: in the Hebrew, _nid'akhu_, the verb Scripture keeps for one lamp only — __“the lamp of the *wicked* shall be *put out*”__.
They are virgins by standing, not by deed — just as the house of Israel kept the name “virgin” through all her wanderings — and their own confession files them with the wicked: the fool was never the defiled woman; he is the hearer who does not do.
They beg the wise for oil, are refused, and are sent off to buy it — and while they are gone, the door is shut.

One verse decodes the scene: __“the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light”__.
The lamp is the commandment — and all ten carry it, just as both builders heard the very same sayings.
The light is the law _lived_, as the next chapter will show.
Between the lamp and the light stands the oil: burn it, and the commandment in hand becomes a life that shines; lack it, and the lamp holds the commandment still, but gives no light.

Read the whole verse, for it does not stop at the lamp and the light: __“the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and *reproofs of instruction are the way of life*”__.
The Psalms name that reproof *oil*: __“let the righteous smite me; it shall be a kindness: and let him *reprove me*; it shall be an excellent *oil*”__.
Lamp, light, and oil are not three metaphors gathered from three unrelated places.
Proverbs sets the commandment, the law, and corrective instruction in one sentence; the Psalm supplies the oil-word for the correction that turns the hearer toward the way of life.

[long pause]

Now consider What Is the Oil?.

[long pause]

Everything turns on the oil, so everything turns on knowing what it is.
I asked Claude how the oil is commonly explained, and the answers ranked like this:

The table is titled, How the “oil” is usually read (ten virgins).
Rank 1. Common interpretation: The Holy Spirit. Estimated share: 65 percent.
Rank 2. Common interpretation: Saving faith. Estimated share: 12 percent.
Rank 3. Common interpretation: Grace. Estimated share: 10 percent.
Rank 4. Common interpretation: Good works, or readiness. Estimated share: 8 percent.
Rank 5. Common interpretation: The word, or a relationship with Christ. Estimated share: 5 percent.
[pause]

Nearly all of it gathers to one answer — the Holy Spirit — and the parable refuses it.
The first reading brings the wrong oil to the lamp.
Exodus lists __“oil for the light”__ and __“spices for anointing oil”__ as separate provisions: the first is pure beaten olive oil for burning, while the second is compounded with 4 spices and restricted to the sanctuary, its vessels, and its priests.
The virgins carry lamp oil; no one in the parable is anointed.
Verses joining royal or priestly anointing to the Spirit therefore do not define the symbol in their vessels: they concern the oil of appointment, not oil for light.
The short study The Anointing defines that difference in full.

Proverbs has already staged the parable's inventory:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 21:20]
____
There is treasure to be desired and *oil* in the dwelling of the *wise*; but a *foolish* man spendeth it up.
____

[pause]

Wise and foolish, oil stored and oil exhausted — Matthew carries the same 3 terms into the virgins' vessels.

The mechanics refuse the Spirit reading too.
The Spirit can be received through the laying on of hands, but the same scene condemns Simon for treating that gift as something purchasable: Peter and John are ministers of God's gift, not sellers drawing down a private reserve.
The wise refuse because dividing their oil may leave too little for either company; the Spirit is not a finite, zero-sum quantity that leaves one believer short when another receives Him, and God gave Him to Christ __“not by measure”__.
The Spirit fell on Cornelius's house __“while Peter yet spake”__: the gift can arrive in a moment.
What cannot be produced in a moment is a life already lived, its obedience stored and proven before the midnight cry.

The wise send the foolish to __“buy”__ oil; but the Spirit is a gift that cannot be bought — __“Thy money perish with thee, because thou hast thought that the gift of God may be purchased with money”__.
Nor can it be grace or faith, gifts freely given, for this oil must be gathered beforehand, kept in one's own vessel, and cannot be lent to another.

The parable's word is exact, for Scripture commands just such a purchase:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 23:23]
____
*Buy* the truth, and *sell it not*; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.
____

[pause]

These are not four loose virtues; each is a symbol Scripture fixes to one thing — the law kept.
The truth is the law itself — __“thy law is the truth”__; _wisdom_ and _understanding_ are the doing of it — __“the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding”__; and _instruction_ is its discipline, which only fools despise.
To buy the truth, then, is to buy and keep the law.

Scripture also tells us what the commandment carries: __“all thy commandments are *truth*”__ and __“all thy commandments are *righteousness*”__; __“his commandment is *life everlasting*”__.
Yet carrying the commandment outwardly is not the same as carrying its truth within.
John names the foolish condition exactly: __“He that saith, I know him, and *keepeth not his commandments*, is a liar, and the *truth is not in him*”__.
The commandment can be heard and professed while its truth is not in the man.
The wise do the opposite: __“lay up my commandments with thee… write them upon the table of thine heart”__; __“Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee”__.
The renewed covenant makes the same inward store its first promise: __“I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts”__.

Now the vessels are visible.
All ten hold the lamp of the commandment; only the wise have received its truth and correction inwardly, where it can govern the life and burn as obedience.

The price is never silver — __“buy… without money and without price”__ — but a listening, obeying ear: __“hearken diligently unto me, and eat ye that which is good… Incline your ear, and come unto me: hear, and your soul shall live; and I will make an everlasting covenant with you”__.
What it costs is a whole life of hearing and doing; what it buys is the covenant itself, and life; and what is so bought is never _sold_, never passed to another hand.

Proverbs confirms the same substance without a parable, moving from open rebuke and the faithful wounds of a friend to oil, perfume, and __“hearty counsel”__.
The instruction that must be bought is the oil the wise receive and keep: truth and correction worked inward until they govern the life.

[long pause]

Now consider The Spirit Behind the Oil.

[long pause]

Lamp oil is Spirit-taught truth and correction received, kept, and worked into proven obedience.
It is the tested, pressed fruit of the Spirit stored inwardly; light is that obedience made visible as an example to others.
God names the source of that fruit:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 36:27]
____
I will put my *spirit* within you, and *cause you to walk in my statutes*, and ye shall keep my judgments, and *do them*.
____

[pause]

The Spirit is the cause; the walk and the doing are His work made manifest.
Jesus says the Spirit __“will guide you into all truth”__, and Scripture has already fixed the truth as the law — __“thy law is the truth”__.
Paul keeps the same order: __“it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure”__.
Galatians calls the result __“the fruit of the Spirit”__: not the Spirit Himself, but what His life brings forth in a person.

Zechariah's oil-fed lampstand does not reverse the order.
The word to Zerubbabel is __“Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit”__ because the work in view is the temple his hands will finish.
When Zechariah asks what the olive branches are, the angel calls them __“the two anointed ones”__; he never says, “the oil is the Spirit.”
The Spirit is the power by which God's appointed servants complete the work, while the flowing oil is the supply by which the lamp gives light.

This is the oil: Spirit-taught truth bought and kept, correction received, and the tried doing of both stored in one's own vessel.
The oil is obedience formed inwardly; the light is that obedience burning where others can see it and glorify the Father.
The foolish, sent to buy it at the last hour, find the door already shut.

The wise virgin has sat for her portrait before: __“She perceiveth that her *merchandise* is good: her *candle goeth not out by night*”__ — the buying done in time, the lamp still burning through the night watch: the very two marks the parable joins.
The Pearls of Wisdom chapter will name her.

Scripture says what oil is.
It is the pressed fruit of the olive — __“pure oil olive beaten for the light, to cause the lamp to burn always”__ — and the olive tree is Israel, the covenant people: __“The LORD called thy name, *A green* olive tree”__.
Oil is fruit crushed and tested until it yields; it is the Spirit's work ripened into obedience, proven under pressure and stored up over a life.
Burned, it is what turns the lamp to light — the commandment held becomes the law lived, obedience made visible, the symbol of the next chapter.

The foolish had the lamp — the profession, the commandment in hand — but no oil, no store of works; so when the test came, there was no light.
And no one can lend it: your neighbor’s obedience will not burn for you.

The place where the Lord Himself was pressed even bears the name: Gethsemane, the _oil press_.
And it sits on the Mount of Olives — by these symbols the mount of the olive nation: Israel itself.
On the olive-nation's own hill stood the press, and there the Lord was crushed under the weight of death — His sweat __“as it were great drops of blood”__ — and gave up the oil of a perfect obedience: __“not my will, but thine, be done”__.

The foolish are turned away with the worst words in Scripture: __“I know you not”__ — the same verdict as __“I never knew you”__ spoken over those who worked lawlessness.

The foolish virgins were not undone by a forgotten errand; they were foolish from the first — hearers who never became doers, lamps that never held oil.
And the wise were wise the way the wise man built on rock: they heard, and they did, and when the Bridegroom came their light was burning.
Between the fool and the wise there is finally one question — the question this whole book keeps asking: not what you heard, but whether you did it.
