MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Thirty-One: The Pearls of Wisdom.

[long pause]
Jesus gives the pearl parable in one sentence:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:45-46]
____
Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking *goodly pearls*: who, when he had found *one pearl of great price*, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.
____

[pause]

The little parable turns on 2 questions: Who is the merchant, and what did he purchase?

The parable immediately before it tells of a man who sells all to buy a field for the treasure hidden within it.
The Coin chapter has already followed those symbols: the field is the world, and the treasure within it is God's people — His peculiar treasure, refined as silver and gold.
Within the same sequence, Jesus has identified the man sowing in that field as the Son of man.
The next sentence repeats the purchase in a new form: a merchant finds one pearl, sells all, and buys it.
The buyer has not changed.

The Merchant is the Son, and the pearl is the bride He came to purchase.

[long pause]

Now consider The Bride in the Sky.

[long pause]

Jesus begins by naming the realm: __“The kingdom of heaven is like”__ — literally, the kingdom of the heavens.
Genesis names the firmament *Heaven* and places the sun, moon, and stars within it.
Before the merchant takes a step, the parable has already lifted our eyes to the sky.

The Sun, Moon, and Stars chapter established the relationships pictured there.
The sun represents the Father and the husband.
The moon is the faithful witness: the Son reflecting His Father, and the bride reflecting her Bridegroom.
The moon represents the submissive side of a covenant relationship — the Son under His Father, and the bride under her husband.
It does not produce an independent light; it receives the light of its head and faithfully makes that light visible.

The parable asks us to find one complete pearl in that heavenly arrangement.
The full moon already answers in 2 ways: it has the visible shape of one complete pearl, and the moon represents the bride in the covenant relationship.
The next step is to follow the parable's own words through Scripture: *one*, *good*, *pearl*, *merchant*, *great price*, and *heaven*.

[long pause]

Now consider Following the Words.

[long pause]

*Pearl* and *great price* lead directly to Proverbs.
First, Wisdom is priced above pearls:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:15, KJV — “pearls” restored]
____
She is *more precious than* pearls: and all the things thou canst desire are not to be compared unto her.
____

[pause]

Proverbs 8:11 and Job 28:18 repeat the comparison.
Then the woman of valour — the bride — is priced above the same pearls:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:10 — MEAT rendering]
____
A woman of valour who can find? for her *price is far above* pearls.
____

[pause]

The find-verb of Proverbs 3:13 returns in 2 revealing statements: __“whoso findeth *me*”__ and __“whoso findeth *a wife*”__.
Wisdom and the bride occupy the same place in Solomon's search, and both lead back to the merchant's defining action: __“when he had *found* one pearl of great price”__.

*Merchant* and *good* converge there too.
Wisdom's __“merchandise”__ is good.
The bride __“tasteth that her merchandise is good”__.
The kingdom's merchant seeks __“goodly pearls”__.
Wisdom, bride, pearl — the good merchandise is one continuous trail.

That leaves *one*.
If the trail is sound, Scripture should not merely compare a woman to a pearl; it should gather the picture into one complete bride.

These searches lead to 2 poems in Proverbs.
The first prices Wisdom above pearls; the second prices the bride above pearls and places the merchant and good merchandise inside her portrait.
Read each poem whole before examining its lines.
Bold type marks the words under examination.
The line-by-line reading names the familiar King James wording first, then shows whether the MEAT hearing comes from another attested sense, the unpointed consonants, or a scriptural cross-reference.
Then Scripture can answer whether the woman they describe is the *one* pearl in the heavens.

Now consider Proverbs 3, Line by Line.

[long pause]

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:13 — MEAT rendering]
____
Blessed is the man that findeth wisdom, and the man that *draweth out understanding*.
____

[pause]

The poem begins with the merchant's action: he *finds*.
The King James reads __“the man that *getteth* understanding.”__
The second Hebrew verb, _puq_ (H6329), can mean to issue, bring forth, or draw out; the MEAT rendering keeps that other attested sense.
Understanding is not purchased from outside; it is drawn from the Word like treasure brought out of a field.
The same search will end with the wife: __“whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing”__.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:14 — MEAT rendering]
____
For the gain of her *going-round* is better than the going-round of silver, and her *harvest* than fine gold.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“the *merchandise* of it”__ and __“the *gain* thereof.”__
The word behind *going-round*, _sachar_, belongs to trade and to the merchant who travels from place to place; the word behind *harvest*, _tevuah_, means produce or yield.
The MEAT rendering keeps those other senses visible.
The line joins the merchant's circuit to the field's fruit.
Its words return in the bride's portrait, where her merchandise is called good.
The pearl parable has entered Solomon's vocabulary before a pearl is named.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:15 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is more *bright than pearls*: and all thy delights are not to be compared unto her.
____

[pause]

The familiar line is __“more *precious* than *rubies*.”__
The pearl sense has already been established above; the word usually rendered *precious* can also describe brightness, and the King James itself uses it for __“the moon walking in *brightness*”__.
The poem therefore prices Wisdom above pearls in the moon's own vocabulary: precious, and heard again, bright.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:16 — MEAT rendering]
____
*Length and appropriateness of days* is in her right hand; and in her left hand riches and *shining*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“*length of days*”__ and __“riches and *honour*.”__
The word translated *length*, _orek_, can also describe what reaches or fits its appointed point.
The line therefore holds both the count of days and their fitness — time measured and time appointed.
The word behind *shining*, _kavod_, is glory or honour, displayed as radiance when the LORD's glory rises and His people shine.

The hands also carry direction.
Strong's gives the right hand as the south and the left as the dark or northern side.
The complete witness holds the days in her bright hand and their shining even in her dark one.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:17 — MEAT rendering]
____
Her ways are ways of *splendour*, and all her paths are *wholeness*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“ways of *pleasantness*”__ and __“paths are *peace*.”__
The word translated *splendour*, _noam_, also includes beauty, delight, and suitability.
The word translated *wholeness*, _shalom_, is more than the absence of conflict: it also includes completeness and welfare.
A full witness walks a complete path.
The poem is beginning to describe not merely a jewel but a light completing its appointed course.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:18 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her: and blessed is every one that *holdeth her fast*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“happy is every one that *retaineth* her.”__
The Hebrew verb, _tamak_, can mean to retain, grasp, sustain, or hold fast; the MEAT line uses the last of those attested senses.
It is the action the bride's palms will perform when they hold the circuit.
It is also the verb of Moses' hands held steady — _emunah_, faithfulness — until the going down of the sun.
Wisdom is held as the faithful witness holds its course.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:19 — MEAT rendering]
____
The LORD by wisdom hath founded the earth; by understanding hath he *established* the heavens.
____

[pause]

The poem now tells us where its picture belongs.
No different wording is needed here: the King James itself reads *established* and *heavens*.
The Hebrew verb translated *established*, _konen_ (H3559), is echoed when David looks upon __“the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*”__.
The Wisdom priced above pearls is the Wisdom that built the sky: __“when he prepared the heavens, I was there”__.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:20 — MEAT rendering]
____
By his knowledge the depths were broken open, and the *skies* drop down the dew.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“the depths are *broken up*, and the *clouds* drop down the dew.”__
*Broken open* states the same verb in current English and recalls the fountains of the flood.
The word translated *clouds*, _shachaqim_, can also name the fine skies; the MEAT rendering keeps that attested sense because it is the sky where the faithful witness is established.
The poem ends with the heavens opened above and the measured waters below.
Its Wisdom belongs to the ordered creation whose lights govern days and appointed times.

Now consider Proverbs 31, Line by Line.

[long pause]

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:10 — MEAT rendering]
____
A woman of valour who can find? for her *price is far above pearls*.
____

[pause]

The first poem opened with a man who finds Wisdom; this one opens by asking who can find the woman.
The King James reads __“a *virtuous woman*”__ whose price is above __“*rubies*.”__
The word translated *valour*, _chayil_, names strength or valour; *pearls* is the sense established above.
The same pearls that priced Wisdom now price the woman.
The thing the Merchant seeks has now been given a woman's form.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:11 — MEAT rendering]
____
The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, and gain shall *never diminish*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“he shall have no need of *spoil*.”__
The noun rendered *gain*, _shalal_, can mean spoil or gain, while the verb behind *diminish*, _chaser_, can mean to lack, decrease, or fail.
The MEAT line keeps both alternate senses: her husband's gain does not diminish.
The faithful wife does not consume her husband's house; she increases it.
The lunar image will make the same point in light: what she receives from her head is kept and faithfully returned.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:12 — MEAT rendering]
____
She doeth him good and not evil all the days of her life.
____

[pause]

This is submission expressed as covenant faithfulness, not passivity.
Her life is ordered toward the good of her husband.
The moon likewise serves the office appointed by its head through every one of its days.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:13 — MEAT rendering]
____
She seeketh wool, and flax, and worketh willingly with her *palms*.
____

[pause]

The King James renders the final word simply as __“*hands*.”__
The Hebrew noun is _kaph_, the curved palm, rather than the more general word for hand; the MEAT rendering preserves that physical distinction.
A palm is a hollow vessel made to receive, hold, and cover.
Wool and flax will become the garments by which the bride's works are made visible.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:14 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is like the ships of *the one who travels round*; she bringeth her bread from afar.
____

[pause]

The merchant-word returns.
The King James reads __“the *merchant's ships*.”__
The phrase *the one who travels round* renders _socher_, which can name a trader and belongs to the same Hebrew travel-and-trade family as _sachar_ in Proverbs 3:14.
Her bread comes from afar — from above, in the heavenly hearing — while her course goes round like the circuit of the merchant and the lights.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:15 — MEAT rendering]
____
She riseth also while it is yet night, and giveth meat to her household, and *the appointed portion* to her maidens.
____

[pause]

The light has now entered the night.
Of all the lights, the full moon rises as darkness falls and remains through the night.

The King James reads __“giveth *meat* to her household, and a *portion* to her maidens.”__
The meat-word itself, _teref_, is unchanged; Psalm 111:5 joins that same meat to remembrance of the covenant.
The word translated *portion*, _choq_, can also name an appointment, statute, or fixed time — the word-family of the ordinances of the moon and stars.
Only *appointed* is an alternate lexical sense here; the covenant connection comes from the scriptural cross-reference, not from the noun itself.
The bride distributes the calendar's own food at the appointed time.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:16 — MEAT rendering]
____
She considereth a field, and *taketh* it: with the fruit of her palms she planteth a vineyard.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“she considereth a field, and *buyeth* it.”__
The written verb, _laqach_, means to take or receive; the MEAT rendering uses *taketh* without making the action more specific than the Hebrew does.
The field is the world, and the vineyard is the house of Israel.
The Son buys the field for His treasure; the bride, joined to Him, receives that field and plants within it.
The purchased woman shares the work of the Purchaser.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:17 — MEAT rendering]
____
She girdeth her loins with strength, and strengtheneth her arms.
____

[pause]

She strengthens what she plants.
The contrast with the counterfeit is exact: Lucifer __“didst weaken the nations”__, and the beast wears out the saints.
The faithful bride builds what the enemy spends himself destroying.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:18 — MEAT rendering]
____
She *tasteth that it is good*, her merchandise: her lamp goeth not out *in the night*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“she *perceiveth* that her merchandise is good”__ and __“her candle goeth not out *by night*.”__
The verb _ta'amah_ can mean to taste, and the Hebrew order is direct: _ta'amah ki-tov sachrah_ — she tastes that her merchandise is good.
The written form gives *in the night*; both changes keep the direct lexical and written senses.
The same __“it is good”__ was spoken over the light, and the same merchandise was introduced in Proverbs 3:14.
Now its lamp burns in the night.
Wisdom, bride, merchant, good, and light have arrived in one sentence.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:19 — MEAT rendering]
____
She layeth her hands to the spindle, and her *palms hold fast the circuit*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“her hands hold the *distaff*.”__
The plain housewife reading is valid: _pelekh_ names a spindle or whorl.
The word also comes from roundness and can carry the sense of a circuit; the MEAT line gives that second hearing while preserving the spinning image.
The housewife holds the spinning tool; the heavenly bride holds the round.
The verb *hold fast*, _tamak_, is the one Wisdom used in Proverbs 3:18.
The two poems touch palm to circuit and hold it in place.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:20 — MEAT rendering]
____
She spreadeth out her palm to the poor; yea, she reacheth forth her hands to the needy.
____

[pause]

The curved vessel opens and gives what it received.
The King James again prints __“*hand*,”__ while the Hebrew again uses _kaph_, the curved palm.
That is the King's signature: __“He shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the poor also”__.
The same psalm sets this mercy __“with the sun and before the face of the moon”__ through all generations.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:21 — MEAT rendering]
____
She is not *worried by the whiteness* for her household: for all her *lineage wraps in years*.
____

[pause]

The King James follows the pointed reading: __“not afraid of the *snow*… clothed with *scarlet*.”__
The unpointed letters carry additional hearings.
The word rendered *worried*, _yare_, can describe anxious concern, while snow supplies Scripture's standard of whiteness.
The word for *house*, _bayit_, can also name a lineage, and the verb rendered *wraps*, _labash_, means to wrap or clothe.
The consonants under __“scarlet”__ can be heard as _shanim_, years, or _shnayim_, double.
The household is wrapped across years: successive months and generations gathered into the completed count the lights were made to measure.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:22 — MEAT rendering]
____
She maketh herself coverings; her clothing is *white linen* and *royal maroon*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“her clothing is *silk and purple*.”__
The word translated *white linen*, _shesh_, names bleached linen rather than silk.
The word rendered *royal maroon*, _argaman_, names the costly murex-dyed cloth of royalty and the sanctuary, whose historical range includes wine, burgundy, and dark maroon rather than only modern lilac.
The full moon's ordinary face is white; under eclipse the same complete face becomes blood-dark and royal maroon.
The 2 colors belong to one garment and one witness.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:23 — MEAT rendering]
____
Her husband is known in the gates, when he sitteth among the elders of the land.
____

[pause]

The husband is the sun of this portrait.
He is known in the gates of his circuit and seated among the elders of the heavenly host.
The wife is not an independent light; her position and glory identify the head whose light she bears.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:24 — MEAT rendering]
____
She maketh linen wraps, and selleth them; and delivereth girdles unto the merchant.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“she maketh *fine linen*.”__
The word translated *linen wraps*, _sadin_, more specifically names a linen wrapper or garment.
The Merchant who sought the pearl now appears inside her portrait and receives the work of her hands.
The bride does not purchase Him; her faithful deeds return to the One who purchased her.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:25 — MEAT rendering]
____
Strength and splendour are her clothing; and she *laugheth at the last day*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“honour are her clothing; and she shall *rejoice in time to come*.”__
The word behind *splendour*, _kavod_, can display honour as radiance.
The verb _sachaq_ means to laugh, and the phrase translated *the last day*, _le-yom acharon_, is preserved by Young's, Douay-Rheims, and Smith's.
She can laugh because the day is known: __“he seeth that his day is coming”__.
Beneath the laugh-verb, the same consonants can also be heard as _shachaq_, the sky where the faithful witness is established.
The ordinary sentence and the second hearing point to the same appointed end.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:26 — MEAT rendering]
____
She openeth her mouth with wisdom; and in her tongue is the law of kindness.
____

[pause]

The bride speaks with the Wisdom of the first poem.
Her nightly testimony is the speech of Psalm 19: the heavens declare, and their line goes through all the earth.
What she declares is law, delivered kindly by a faithful witness rather than imposed by an independent ruler.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:27 — MEAT rendering]
____
She *keepeth the watch* over the *walkings* of her household, and eateth not the bread of idleness.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“she *looketh well* to the *ways* of her household.”__
The verb translated *keepeth the watch*, _tsaphah_, is the watchman's word; the word translated *walkings*, _halikhah_, can mean a procession or course.
She watches the ordered walkings of her house, as Job sees __“the moon walking in brightness”__.
The witness keeps watch because the household's course marks time.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:28 — MEAT rendering]
____
Her sons arise, and call her blessed; her husband also, and he *maketh her shine*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“her husband also, and he *praiseth* her.”__
The same verb, _halal_, can mean both to praise and to shine; the MEAT line keeps its other attested sense.
The senses meet here: the husband praises her by making her shine.
This is the sun-moon relationship stated without naming either light — her brightness comes from her husband, and the household sees it.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:29 — MEAT rendering]
____
Many daughters have done valiantly, but thou hast *gone up above them all*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“thou *excellest* them all.”__
The verb, _alah_, can mean to go up, ascend, or surpass; the MEAT line preserves the upward motion.
She rises above the daughters as the full witness rises above the lesser lights.
The next biblical portrait will call her the one woman above the daughters and identify her openly with the moon.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:30 — MEAT rendering]
____
Charm is a lie, and beauty a vapour: a woman that feareth the LORD, *she it is that shall shine*.
____

[pause]

The King James reads __“favour is *deceitful*, and beauty is *vain*… she shall be *praised*.”__
The underlying words can also be heard as *charm is a lie*, *beauty is a vapour*, and *she shall shine*: _hevel_ is breath or vapour, and _halal_ again carries both praise and shining.
Surface beauty passes.
The light that remains is the fear of the LORD worked into faithful obedience.
Again _halal_ joins praise and shining.
The bride shines because she reflects the judgment and character of her Head.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 31:31 — MEAT rendering]
____
Give her of the fruit of her hands; and let her own works *make her shine in the gates*.
____

[pause]

The portrait ends with fruit, deeds, light, and gates.
The King James reads __“let her own works *praise her* in the gates.”__
The verb is again _halal_; *make her shine* keeps the same alternate sense used in verses 28 and 30.
Revelation clothes the bride in the righteous deeds of the saints and makes each gate of her city one pearl.
Her works do not buy her husband; they make visible the light of the Husband who bought her.

Through all 22 lines the poem never uses the word *moon*.
It paints a woman who rises in the dark, shines through the night with light her husband gives her, distributes appointed portions, holds the circuit, walks in brightness, and shines in the gates.
Then the Song of Songs supplies the withheld name:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Song of Solomon 6:9-10]
____
My dove, my undefiled is but *one*; she is the *only one* of her mother, she is the choice one of her that bare her.
The daughters saw her, and *blessed* her; yea, the queens and the concubines, and they *praised* her.
Who is she that looketh forth as the morning, *fair as the* moon, clear as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners?
____

[pause]

The poem paints the portrait; the Song signs it.
The one woman above the daughters is the moon toward the sun — the bride under her husband, bearing his light.

[long pause]

Now consider The Sapphire Form.

[long pause]

Scripture places sapphire inside the same wisdom-and-pearl chain.
Job names __“the precious onyx, or the sapphire”__; two verses later, the same price-list declares, __“the price of wisdom is above *pearls*”__.
Lamentations spends _peninim_ once more and adds the colors:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Lamentations 4:7-8 — peninim restored]
____
Her Nazarites were purer than snow, they were whiter than milk, they were more ruddy in body than *pearls*, their polishing was of sapphire:
Their visage is blacker than a coal; they are not known in the streets: their skin cleaveth to their bones; it is withered, it is become like a stick.
____

[pause]

The subject is set apart before a color appears: her Nazarites, the consecrated ones.
Then come two whites — snow and milk — a ruddy body priced against pearls, and a sapphire form; the next verse turns the portrait black.
A blood moon is a full moon under eclipse: the same complete light that appears white can turn ruddy beneath the shadow, with blackness as its counter-image.
The passage binds consecration, double whiteness, redness, darkness, pearl, and sapphire in one figure.

Scripture defines sapphire's office elsewhere.
Moses sees beneath God's feet __“a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness”__.
Ezekiel twice sees the stone as the appearance of __“the likeness of a throne”__.
Psalm 89 sets that throne as the sun and establishes it as the moon, while the same letters, _kisse_ and _keseh_, join throne to full moon.
Scripture has given the lunar throne a mineral likeness: sapphire is the full moon in its enthroned form.

Scripture itself unfolds what __“clearness”__ holds.
The word is _tohar_, from the root of *clean*, and Scripture uses it only 4 times: twice for the days of __“purifying”__, once for this sapphire pavement — and once more for __“glory”__ in the psalm of the throne itself: __“Thou hast made his *glory* to cease, and cast his throne down to the ground”__.
Purity, clearness, glory — one word, and it names a shining that is *worn*: the throne's _tohar_ can be made to *cease*, because a reflector's light was never its own.

Job hangs the mirror in the same sky.
__“Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a *molten looking glass*?”__ — the _shachaq_, the very heaven where the psalm's faithful witness stands — and 3 verses on, the root of _tohar_ does its proper work there: __“men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and *cleanseth* them”__.
The sky is a cast mirror; to cleanse it is to clear it until the light comes through; and the pavement under the God of Israel is __“the body of heaven”__ in exactly that clearness — purity that *shows*.
John keeps the geometry in the throne room: __“before the throne”__ stands __“a sea of glass”__ — and his __“before”__ is _enōpion_, *in the face of*; what faces a light returns it, and the Sea & Waters study in Part Six will meet that mirror-sea, and the temple's copy of it.

The law's mirror and the sky's are one surface.
James sets the hearer before __“a mirror”__ that shows a man __“his natural face,”__ and names the glass: __“the perfect law of liberty”__ — the Word shows a man himself.
Paul turns the same glass toward its Owner: __“we all, with open face *beholding as in a glass* the glory of the Lord, are *changed into the same image* from glory to glory”__.
One mirror — the Word — gives back both faces, and the looking transforms the one into the other.
The moon is that mirror hung in the sky: the faithful witness bearing light not his own, as __“the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, and we beheld his *glory*”__.

Marriage completes the portrait.
The Song places sapphire within the husband-and-wife figure: the Beloved is white and ruddy, bright ivory overlaid with sapphires; the one woman is __“fair as the moon, clear as the sun”__.
Isaiah calls the woman's Maker her Husband, then promises, __“I will lay thy foundations with sapphires”__.
Revelation calls the holy city __“the bride, the Lamb's wife,”__ sets sapphire as its second foundation, and makes every gate one pearl.

The pearl names the full moon by visible form — one, white, round, complete.
Sapphire names the same witness by throne and marriage office.
Revelation seals the union in one Bride-city: sapphire in the foundations, one pearl at every gate.

[long pause]

Now consider The One Pearl of the Night Sky.

[long pause]
Mark the pearl's properties as Scripture handles the figure.
It is *one*: the merchant found __“one pearl of great price”__ and stopped collecting, for the kingdom is not a collection but one bride, bought whole.
It is *round and complete*: the roundness sits inside Strong's definition itself.
It is *white* — a bearer of soft, borrowed light out of darkness — and it is born of the *waters*.
Then Revelation prices the city's entrances in it:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:21]
____
And the twelve gates were twelve *pearls*; *every several gate was of one pearl*…
____

[pause]

The Greek doubles the point — _each one_ of the gates, _of one_ pearl — and the Hebrew Revelation doubles it the same way: every *single* gate, of *one* pearl.
Not gates studded with pearls: each gate *is* one pearl — one, complete, whole.
So the pearl, wherever Scripture spends it, is the one complete thing of great price — and it is a *gate*.
Now ask the merchant's question: has the King hung such a thing anywhere in plain sight — one, round, white, complete, and standing at a door?

Look up.
Of all the moon's phases and all the lights of heaven, exactly one is white, round, and complete: the night sky owns precisely one pearl.
And Scripture had already assigned that light its office: the moon appointed for the _moedim_, the appointed times, from the fourth day.

The pairing was set at the pattern's founding: Joseph's dream read the moon as the *mother*, and the sun comes out of his chamber __“as a *bridegroom*”__ — a bridegroom's answering light is his bride, the counterpart the Sun, Moon, and Stars chapter already seated facing him, moon toward her Bridegroom as the Son is moon toward His Father.

[long pause]

Now consider The Letters Confirm It.

[long pause]

If the pearl has led truly, the letters should be waiting at the same spot — and they are.
Scripture keeps a __“fulness”__ overhead.
The Hebrew is _keseh_ — Strong's: __“properly, *fulness* or the *full moon*”__ — and Psalm 81 places it on the feast day:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 81:3, NKJV]
____
Blow the trumpet at the time of the New Moon, at the *full* moon, on our solemn feast day.
____

[pause]

Of the moon's phases exactly one is full and conspicuous, while the dark conjunction is the one day the sky shows no sign at all.
The Hebrew places the new-moon trumpet, the full moon, and the festival sacrifice on one day: the full moon is the head of the month and feast-day of Numbers 10:10 and 28:11.

Solomon uses the same word for the day the traveling husband returns: __“he will come home at the *full moon*”__ — _keseh_ again — and there it is spelled כסא, letter for letter the spelling of _kisse’_, __“throne.”__
The last chapter stood on that identity from the enemy's side; here is the LORD's side of it. Both words grow from one root, _kasah_, to cover, to fill — the throne is the covered seat, the full moon the filled light — and Job holds the two senses up in a single verse: __“He holdeth back the face of *his throne*, and spreadeth *his cloud* upon it”__, the veiled face of His full moon.

If it is asked which way the covering runs — whether the throne-word is covered in darkness or covered in light — Scripture describes the throne itself.
Daniel saw the throne-room itself, and every detail belongs to this chapter:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9, NKJV]
____
I watched till *thrones* were *put in place*, and the *Ancient of Days* was seated; His garment was *white as snow*, and the hair of His head like *pure wool*. His throne was a *fiery flame*, its wheels a burning fire.
____

[pause]

The thrones are *put in place* like lights in their stations.
And the title under __“Ancient of days”__ is built on a root of moving onward, going before — read once, He is the Ancient of Days; read again, He is the *Foremost of Days*: the One at the head of the days, seated where the days begin.
His garment white as snow, His hair like the pure wool, His throne flame, His wheels burning fire — and the plural on __“wheels”__ is the ending this book met on Daniel's sevens: an intensive, not a count — *the wheel complete*, the perfect circle of fire.

Whiteness enthroned on light; brightness round about it, __“as the appearance of the bow that is in the cloud in the day of rain”__; and its Occupant __“coverest thyself with *light* as with a garment”__.
If the throne is the _keseh_, Daniel's vision draws what the letters spell: the full moons set in their stations, and the Head of Days taking His seat — white, complete, covered in light — where the month begins.

The psalm that gave this book the faithful witness already sets the throne as the sun and as the moon — Psalm 89:36-37 — plain simile, not wordplay, and the throne is a throne of light.
Where darkness stands near it, it stands *outside*, as a veil: __“clouds and darkness are *round about* him”__ — round about, not within — the cloud of Job 26:9 spread *upon* the face, holding the light back.
The _keseh_ is covered in light; only the counterfeit is covered in dark.

[long pause]

Now consider The Gate That Opens.

[long pause]

The sky is built like a sanctuary — the tabernacle Psalm 19 pitched for the sun — and its front, like the tabernacle's, faces the sunrise, the east.
Now hear Ezekiel on the gates of the house:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 46:1]
____
Thus saith the Lord GOD; The gate of the inner court that looketh toward the east shall be *shut the six working days*; but on the *sabbath* it shall be opened, and in the day of the *new* moon it shall be opened.
____

[pause]

Three kinds of days — working days, sabbaths, new-moon days — and on the day that begins the month, the east gate stands open.
A gate is opened because something is meant to come through it.
And exactly one phase enters by the east gate at the fall of night: the *full* moon enters in the east at the hour the portrait already fixed — through the front door of the sky's tabernacle, the faithful witness at maximum.

The sliver, by contrast, is hunted low in the *west*, at sunset, beside the sun — and the sheepfold has a rule about entrances:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, John 10:1-2]
____
Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that entereth not by the door into the sheepfold, but *climbeth up some other way*, the same is a *thief* and a robber.
But he that entereth in by the door is the *shepherd* of the sheep.
____

[pause]

The prince of Ezekiel's house comes and goes __“by the way of the gate that looketh toward the *east*”__.
The true light enters at the front gate; the counterfeit climbs in from the dark side — which is precisely the address his heart chose in the last chapter.

[long pause]

Now consider One Pearl, One Gate.

[long pause]

Now set the pieces together.
The pearl is the full moon; the city's pearl-gates are His appointed times — for the _moedim_ are, as the last chapter read the word, *meetings*: the fixed days on which the King receives.
Twelve gates; twelve months of full moons; the stars of God gathered into the twelve constellations, the __“Mazzaroth”__ brought forth __“in his season”__, through which the full moon makes its yearly round.
And there Solomon's woman collects what her portrait promised — praised in the gates her fulness passes through, her husband at home among the elders.

The Merchant who sold all He had was not buying His own entrance into the kingdom.
He was purchasing the Bride-city whose people are His treasure and whose gates are pearls — the bride gathered to meet her King at His appointed times.
Isaiah had already welded the paired parables into one sentence: __“wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy *times*… the fear of the LORD is his *treasure*”__.
The treasure and the bride; the pearl and the gate; the King and the appointed meeting.

__“It is the glory of God to *conceal* a thing: but the honour of kings is to *search out* a matter”__ — He hid the bride's portrait where the Merchant who would give everything for her was already looking.

[long pause]

Now consider Pearls Before Swine.

[long pause]

Jesus spent the pearl-word one other time, and it is a warning:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6]
____
Give not that which is *holy* unto the dogs, neither cast ye your *pearls* before *swine*, lest they *trample them under their feet*, and turn again and rend you.
____

[pause]

The dogs and the swine are one family — unclean beasts, the nations outside the covenant table.
And what swine do to pearls is exactly what Scripture says the nations do to holy things: they tread them underfoot — __“it is given unto the *Gentiles*: and the holy city shall they *tread under foot*”__; the sanctuary and the host __“*trodden under foot*”__; __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles, until the *times* of the Gentiles be fulfilled”__.

Mark the last word of that sentence: what the trampling runs until is a matter of *times*.
If the pearl is the appointed time of meeting, the warning reads whole: hand the holy times to the nations and they will not keep them — they will trample them, and turn and rend the hand that gave them.
Daniel named the plan — to change the times and the laws; this verse is the Lord's advance notice of how the theft would look on the ground — the pearl underfoot.

[long pause]

Now consider The Two Moons.

[long pause]

Read this chapter and the last as the pair they are.
The enemy raises the moon covered in *darkness*; the LORD's sign is the same seat covered in *light* — the Father's whole face borne through the dark.
One moon reflects everything; the other reflects nothing, for its source produces only dark.
One enters by the door; the other climbs up some other way.

The sky itself stages the ambition, for the two moons stand in two different places: the full moon, standing always opposite, can never come between; only the moon covered in darkness can stand *over* the sun — slide before his face and put out the day at noon — and blot the stars from the same sky.
That is the plan of the last chapter drawn in one picture — __“Woe unto them… that put *darkness for light*, and *light for darkness*”__.

The stars — the righteous, who keep time — follow one moon or the other.
That is why the parable's Merchant sells *all*: the pearl is not an ornament a sinner purchases from the kingdom; it is the bride the Son redeems, pictured at the gate where she meets her King.
Only the Son could pay that price, and Scripture says He paid it gladly.

Grade this chapter the way the Parables of the Kingdom chapter graded everything, for the method matters more than any single finding.
Every link stands in the text: wisdom and the bride priced above _peninim_; the city's gates each of *one pearl*; the moon set over the _moedim_ from the fourth day; _keseh_, the full moon, sharing the letters of __“throne”__; the east gate opened on the new-moon day; and the two poems painting the same light from beginning to end.
The chain that joins the links is a reading — this book's reading, offered to be tested, not swallowed.

The reckoning that follows from it — where the month truly begins, how the sabbaths fall, what history did to the calendar and what the sky and the records of the nations still testify — is a book of its own.
This chapter needs only the symbol.
The kingdom has a gate; the gate is one complete pearl; and the Bridegroom gave all to purchase the bride who meets Him there.

The appointed times the pearl governs are counted in sevens — and the word Scripture counts them with is the next chapter.
