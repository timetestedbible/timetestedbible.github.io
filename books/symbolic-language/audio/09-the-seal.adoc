Chapter Nine. [beat] The Seal.

[long pause]

Revelation ends with this promise: __“They shall see his face; and his *name* shall be in their *foreheads*.”__

[long pause]

Ask what the seal of God is, and the answers run vague. [beat] A feeling of assurance. An invisible mark. A mystery reserved for the last days.
Scripture is not vague about seals.
A seal is a *signet*, and a signet carries one thing: its owner's *name*.
Judah left his signet as the pledge of his identity.
Pharaoh moved his ring onto Joseph's hand — and with it, his authority.
What the king's ring stamps, says the book of Esther, *no man may reverse*.
[pause]
Press the ring into the wax, and the wax carries the name.
Ownership marked. Authority ratified. The thing sealed kept safe against tampering, until its appointed opening.
Every use of the symbol in Scripture is one of those four pressings — and all four meet in the sealing this chapter is after: the servants of God, marked before judgment.

[pause]

The chapter on the Name carried the weight already: the name is the covenant, *owned*.
The seal is that name *impressed* — the covenant owned, now written on its owner.

[long pause]

The Inscription, Quoted.

[long pause]

Scripture does not leave the engraving to imagination.
Paul holds the seal up and reads it out:

[quote.scripture]
____
Nevertheless the foundation of God standeth sure, having this *seal*: The Lord *knoweth them that are his*. And, let every one that nameth the *name* of Christ *depart from iniquity*.
____

[pause]
Two engravings on one seal.
The first is ownership — and it is a quotation.
When Korah challenged Moses over who bears the holy name — the original dispute — Moses answered: __“the LORD will shew *who are his*, and who is holy.”__
The second engraving is obedience: whoever names the name departs from iniquity.
The Greek word there is _adikia_ — *unrighteousness* — and righteousness has a written definition.
Moses wrote it: __“it shall be our *righteousness*, if we observe to do *all these commandments*.”__
John seals it from the other side: __“all *unrighteousness* is sin.”__
To depart from unrighteousness is to come back under the law of the Name just engraved.
[beat]
Whose you are, and how his own behave. [beat] The seal says nothing else — because a signet has nothing else to say.

[long pause]

The Plate on the Forehead.

[long pause]

The forehead seal is not a novelty of Revelation.
It is priestly equipment, prescribed in the law:

[quote.scripture]
____
And thou shalt make a plate of pure gold, and grave upon it, like the engravings of a *signet*, HOLINESS TO THE LORD. And it shall be upon Aaron's *forehead*… it shall be *always* upon his forehead, that they may be accepted before the LORD.
____

[pause]
A golden plate. Engraved signet-style with the holiness of the Name. Worn *always*, on the forehead of the man who stands before God.
Now read Revelation with the plate in view.
The 144,000 stand with the Lamb, __“having his Father's *name* written in their *foreheads*”__ — a kingdom of priests, every one wearing the plate.
The book's last page makes it the estate of all His servants: they shall see his face, and his name shall be in their foreheads.

[pause]

What the plate says in gold, the law says in commandments.
Moses fixed the two places where the sign of the covenant sits — and decoded them in the same verse:

[quote.scripture]
____
And it shall be for a *sign* unto thee upon thine *hand*, and for a memorial *between thine eyes*, that *the LORD'S law may be in thy mouth*…
____

[pause]
The sign on the hand, and the memorial on the forehead, are defined out loud: the law, *in you*.
Deuteronomy says it as a command — bind them for a sign upon thine hand, let them be as frontlets between thine eyes.
Isaiah names the act with the symbol's own word: __“Bind up the testimony — *seal the law* among my disciples.”__
The seal of God, spelled three ways, is one thing: the Name and its law, impressed where the man *works* and where he *thinks*.
[pause]
One commandment carries the full signet.
A royal seal bears its owner's name, his office, and his domain — and the sabbath is where all three stand written in the law.
The LORD: the name. Who *made*: the office. Heaven and earth: the domain.
__“A sign between me and you throughout your generations — that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you.”__

[long pause]

The Man with the Inkhorn.

[long pause]

Revelation seven is not the first time God marks foreheads before judgment.
Ezekiel watched the whole scene run once.
Six men with slaughter weapons — and among them, one man clothed with linen, with a writer's inkhorn by his side.
The order is given to the scribe *first*:

[quote.scripture]
____
…set a *mark* upon the *foreheads* of the men that *sigh and that cry for all the abominations* that be done in the midst thereof… but *come not near any man upon whom is the mark*; and begin at my sanctuary.
____

[pause]
Every element of the sixth seal is here.
The forehead mark, set before the destroyers move.
The destroyers, forbidden to touch its bearers.
The criterion, written into the commission: the marked are the ones *grieving over lawbreaking*.
[beat]
The mark even has a name.
Ezekiel's word is the letter _tav_ — and in the old Hebrew script the prophets wrote, a tav is a simple crossed stroke — the last letter of the alphabet, set on the men judged worth keeping.
So when John sees four winds held back __“till we have *sealed* the servants of our God in their *foreheads*,”__ the scene is Ezekiel's, enlarged to the four corners of the earth: the scribe goes through first, and no wind blows until he is done.
The seal holds through what follows — the tormentors of the fifth trumpet are commanded to hurt *only* those men which have *not* the seal of God in their foreheads.

[long pause]

The Spirit Is the Impression.

[long pause]

Who presses the ring?
Paul answers three times.
Ye were *sealed* with that holy *Spirit* of promise.
The earnest of the Spirit, in our hearts.
Sealed, unto the day of redemption.
[pause]
The signet is the Name.
The man is the wax — Job says it: __“it is turned as *clay* to the *seal*.”__
The hand that presses is the Spirit — writing the law where the frontlets pointed all along: __“I will put my *spirit* within you, and *cause you to walk in my statutes*.”__
The Son bore the impression first: him hath God the Father *sealed*.

[pause]

This clarifies the sixth-seal scene, for the winds and the sealing are one element.
Wind is _ruach_ — spirit — and the four winds stand before the Lord as His executing spirits, as the next chapters will lay out in full.
Revelation seven is one Spirit stamping while four are held: the _ruach_ presses the Name into the servants, and not a _ruach_ of judgment blows until the last impression is made.

[long pause]

The Counterfeit Takes the Same Two Places.

[long pause]

The beast also seals:

[quote.scripture]
____
And he causeth all… to receive a *mark* in their right *hand*, or in their *foreheads*:
____

[pause]
Hand and forehead.
Precisely the two places the law binds the commandments.
The mark of the beast is the anti-frontlet: another name, another law, filling the hand that works and the head that thinks.
Nobody wears both.
The two marks are one question, asked of every man: *whose law fills your hand and your eyes?*

[pause]

Note which way the true seal faces.
God's servants do not merely carry His mark — *He wears them*.
To faithless Coniah: though you were the *signet upon my right hand*, I would pluck you thence.
To faithful Zerubbabel: I will make thee as a *signet*, for I have *chosen* thee.
The chosen man is the ring on God's own hand; the beast's men carry its mark on theirs.
That is the whole difference between the seals: one names an owner who *keeps* you — the other brands what it will *spend*.

[pause]

The bride knew the symbol's last depth, and asked for it:

[quote.scripture]
____
Set me as a *seal* upon thine *heart*, as a seal upon thine *arm*: for love is strong as death…
____

[pause]
Heart and arm — the same two places again, asked for by love.
The sealed are not inventory.
They are the espoused — kept as a fountain *sealed*, for the Husband who set His name on them.
[beat]
The seal has a common form every man has carried in his pocket: refined metal, struck with an image and a superscription — an owner's name, made money.
What Scripture does with the coin… is the next chapter.

[long pause]
