MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Bonus Study: The Parable of the Vineyard.

[long pause]
The week of the cross, in the temple, the chief priests and elders demanded of Jesus by what authority He did these things.
Part of His answer was a vineyard parable Isaiah had already keyed:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 21:33]
____
Hear another parable: There was a certain householder, which planted a *vineyard*, and *hedged* it round about, and digged a *winepress* in it, and built a *tower*, and let it out to *husbandmen*, and went into a far country:
____

[pause]

Every man in that courtyard knew the property.
Isaiah had sung it seven centuries earlier — a vineyard planted with the choicest vine, fenced, a tower built in the midst of it, a winepress made therein — and Isaiah closed his song by handing over the deed:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 5:7]
____
For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts *is the house of Israel*, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.
____

[pause]

The vineyard is decoded by the prophet who planted it.
The remaining symbols are decoded too — the wall, the tower, the winepress, the tenants, and the owner's strange, repeated verb — each one defined elsewhere in Scripture, waiting to be carried in.
Walk the parable with its own vocabulary and it stops being a fable about ingratitude; it becomes an indictment with every party named, a sentence with a date on it, and the blueprint of the building that rose from the rubble.

[long pause]

Now consider The Wall Called Salvation.

[long pause]

Isaiah names the first symbol in the act of removing it.
The song of the vineyard ends with the owner's sentence: __“I will take away the *hedge* thereof, and it shall be eaten up; and break down the *wall* thereof, and it shall be *trodden down*”__.
What a wall is, Scripture states outright — the same prophet, promising Zion restored:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 60:18]
____
Violence shall no more be heard in thy land, wasting nor destruction within thy borders; but thou shalt call thy walls *Salvation*, and thy gates Praise.
____

[pause]

Isaiah says it twice — __“*salvation* will God appoint for walls and bulwarks”__ — and Zechariah shows the substance behind the masonry: Jerusalem shall be inhabited as towns without walls, __“for I, saith the LORD, will be unto her a *wall of fire* round about”__.
A wall is salvation — the covenant's enclosure: God Himself around His people, the boundary that divides inside from outside.
Read the vineyard's sentence with the definition installed and it turns exact: to break down the wall is to withdraw salvation, and what stood inside is trodden down.

[long pause]

Now consider The Tower in the Midst.

[long pause]

Isaiah set the tower __“in the midst”__ of the vineyard, and the Hebrew Matthew keeps his word — _migdal_, the same word that stands over Scripture's two defining towers.
Solomon built the first into a proverb: __“The name of the LORD is a *strong tower*: the righteous *runneth into it*, and is safe”__ — the Name carrying the Head's Word, covenant law, and fruitful priesthood, sheltering the house that bears it.
Babel raised the second: __“let us build us a city and a tower… and let us *make us a name*”__ — the counterfeit, masonry stacked to house a self-made name.
A tower is what a name builds.

The question then answers itself: what stood in the midst of the house of Israel bearing a Name?
God's word to David about his son settles it: __“He shall build an house *for my name*”__.
The vineyard's tower is the sanctuary — the Name's stronghold planted among the vines.
Redraw the parable's real estate with the labels on: the house of Israel, enclosed in salvation, the temple standing in the middle — let out to tenant farmers, with the owner away and the fruit coming due.

[long pause]

Now consider The Winepress.

[long pause]

Isaiah's inventory ends on the piece the estate was built to serve.
The owner fenced the vineyard, cleared its stones, planted the choicest vine, raised the tower — __“and also *hewed out* a winepress therein: and he *looked* that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth *wild grapes*”__.
A press is hewn into rock and cannot be moved; the whole property is built around the day its produce is rendered.
Isaiah repeats his verb five verses later — he *looked* that it should bring forth grapes, and he *looked* for judgment and righteousness — and the repetition names the crop: the grapes this press was hewed to catch are the fruit by which a nation is judged, its works.

The crop takes one step more: when God comes looking for grapes, what He finds — or misses — is men.
__“I found Israel like *grapes* in the wilderness”__; and when Micah walks the stripped vineyard — __“there is no *cluster* to eat”__ — he names the loss in the next breath: __“the *good man* is perished out of the earth”__ — and the men who remain __“all lie in wait for *blood*”__.
The blessing side holds the same identification: __“As the new wine is found in the *cluster*, and one saith, Destroy it not; for a *blessing* is in it: so will I do for my servants' sakes”__ — the remnant spared as good grapes left on the vine.
The works ripen into the people who bear them; the vineyard's yield is persons — which is why what runs from this press, when the vintage is wild, is *blood*.

Scripture keeps the press's ledger on both sides, and Solomon writes the credit column:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 3:9-10]
____
Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the *firstfruits* of all thine increase:
So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy *presses* shall *burst out* with new wine.
____

[pause]

The bursting press is the blessing itself — Joel promises the same overflow, __“the floors shall be full of wheat, and the *fats* shall *overflow* with wine and oil”__ — and the tithe is __“*reckoned*… as the fulness of the *winepress*”__, for the press is where a year's increase is counted and rendered.
Judah's own blessing wears it: __“he washed his *garments* in *wine*, and his clothes in the *blood of grapes*”__.
Treading even had its own cry, the _heydad_, the vintage-shout — grapes trodden and merriment made — and when God sentences Moab, the sentence is a silenced press: __“the treaders shall tread out no wine in their presses; I have made their *vintage shouting* to cease”__.

Joel wrote the overflow clause twice, and the second time the vat holds something else.

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Joel 3:13]
____
Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is *ripe*: come, get you down; for the press is *full*, the fats *overflow*; for their *wickedness* is great.
____

[pause]

Same vat, same rare verb — overflowing with wine and oil in one chapter, overflowing __“for their wickedness is great”__ in the next.
One press, two vintages: what the vineyard bears decides what runs out of it.
The vintage-shout changes sides the same way — __“he shall give a *shout*, as they that *tread the grapes*, against all the inhabitants of the earth”__ — and the same chapter had already poured the result: __“Take the *wine cup* of this fury at my hand, and cause all the nations… to drink it”__.
What the press treads out, the cup serves.

Isaiah's sentence had already routed the vineyard into its own press — the wall broken down, __“and it shall be *trodden down*”__ — and Lamentations records the execution: __“the Lord hath trodden the virgin, the daughter of Judah, as in a *winepress*”__.
The growers went in as the vintage.
Isaiah saw the Treader coming back from that work, and staged the scene as a watchman's challenge and its answer:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 63:2-3]
____
Wherefore art thou *red* in thine apparel, and thy garments like him that treadeth in the *winefat*?
I have trodden the winepress *alone*; and of the people there was none with me: for I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury; and their *blood* shall be sprinkled upon my garments, and I will stain all my raiment.
____

[pause]

Genesis dressed Judah in garments washed in the blood of grapes; the Treader wears the same wardrobe with the other vintage in it, and the stain reads the verdict.
John saw the last run of the press, at full scale:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:18-20]
____
…Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the *vine of the earth*; for her grapes are fully *ripe*.
And the angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, and gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the *wrath* of God.
And the winepress was trodden *without the city*, and *blood* came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles…
____

[pause]

Isaiah's vineyard was one nation; the last vintage gathers the vine of the whole earth, held until its grapes are __“fully ripe”__ — Joel's press, full because the wickedness is great.
The Rider of Revelation 19 arrives already dressed for it, __“clothed with a vesture *dipped in blood*,”__ and __“he *treadeth the winepress* of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God”__ — Bozrah's Treader, riding out to finish the treading.
The press completes the estate the way it completes the song: the Owner will have His vineyard's yield either way — wine from good grapes, blood from wild.
Everything that happens next in the parable happens because the fruit fell due.

[long pause]

Now consider The Sent.

[long pause]

The plot moves on one drumbeat verb.
__“He *sent* his servants… again, he *sent* other servants… last of all he *sent* unto them his son”__ — _apostellō_ every time, the sending that makes an apostle.
Scripture turns that verb into a place in Jerusalem, and interprets the name on the spot:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, John 9:7]
____
Go, wash in the pool of *Siloam*, (which is by interpretation, *Sent*.) He went his way therefore, and washed, and came *seeing*.
____

[pause]

Siloam — Isaiah's Shiloah — is the city's gentle spring-channel, and its name is the parable's verb.
Isaiah had already ruled on the cost of refusing it: __“Forasmuch as this people *refuseth* the waters of Shiloah that go softly… now therefore, behold, the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the *king of Assyria*”__.
A flood is an army; refuse the soft Sent water, and the violent water comes.

Jesus filed the same case, with a tower in it.
Told of Galileans slaughtered by Pilate, He answered with Jerusalem's own dead:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Luke 13:4-5]
____
Or those eighteen, upon whom the *tower in Siloam* fell, and slew them, think ye that they were sinners above all men that dwelt in Jerusalem?
I tell you, Nay: but, except ye *repent*, ye shall all *likewise* perish.
____

[pause]

The tower of *Sent* fell on men of Jerusalem, and He said __“except ye repent”__ twice.
The same chapter holds the name's two poles side by side: eighteen men crushed under the tower, and a daughter of Abraham whom Satan had bound __“lo, these eighteen years,”__ loosed.
Wash in Sent and come seeing; refuse Sent and die under its stones.

Last of all the owner sent his Son — and the tenants __“caught him, and cast him *out of the vineyard*, and slew him”__, which is where history put the cross: He __“suffered *without the gate*”__, outside the enclosure His own parable had drawn.
One Greek word carries the location through both texts and a third — _exō_, outside: cast out of the vineyard, suffering without the gate, and the winepress __“trodden *without the city*”__ — the blood runs outside the wall, every time.
Within the generation the __“likewise”__ was paid in full: __“thine enemies shall cast a trench about thee… and shall not leave in thee *one stone upon another*; because thou knewest not the time of thy *visitation*”__.
AD 70 executed Isaiah's sentence clause by clause — the wall broken down, the tower in the midst brought down on the city's men, and the vineyard's own participle pronounced over the ruin: __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles”__ — Luke's verb is _pateō_, the treading of the winepress of wrath.

[long pause]

Now consider The Builders.

[long pause]

The hearers pronounced the verdict themselves — __“He will miserably destroy those wicked men”__ — and Jesus turned from the story to a psalm, and the turn names the tenants' trade:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 21:42]
____
Did ye never read in the scriptures, The stone which the *builders* rejected, the same is become the *head of the corner*: this is the Lord's doing, and it is marvellous in our eyes?
____

[pause]

The husbandmen of the story stand as the builders of the psalm — He swapped the words in one breath, and the men in front of Him accepted delivery: __“when the chief priests and Pharisees had heard his parables, they perceived that *he spake of them*”__.
The identification is ratified by the accused.

Builders of what?
Scripture keeps a file on the structures Israel's leaders raised while refusing the tried stone.
Solomon set the first beside the Name-tower itself, one verse later: __“The rich man's wealth is his strong city, and as an *high wall* in his *own conceit*”__ — and the two verses share their Hebrew verb, for the righteous man is set safely on high (_sagab_) in the Name, while the rich man is high (_sagab_) only in his own imagination.
Ezekiel watched the trade at work: prophets __“saying, Peace; and there was no peace,”__ building a wall and daubing it __“with untempered morter”__ — and God announcing its inspection: __“there shall be an *overflowing shower*; and ye, O great hailstones, shall fall; and a *stormy wind* shall rend it”__.

Isaiah heard the builders state their charter outright, and recorded the Owner's answer — a foundation and a surveyor's line:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 28:15-17]
____
Because ye have said, We have made a *covenant with death*… for we have made *lies our refuge*, and under falsehood have we hid ourselves:
Therefore thus saith the Lord GOD, Behold, I lay in Zion for a foundation a stone, a *tried* stone, a precious *corner* stone, a sure foundation…
Judgment also will I lay to the line, and righteousness to the plummet: and the hail shall sweep away the *refuge of lies*, and the waters shall *overflow* the hiding place.
____

[pause]

Amos saw the instrument in the Owner's hand: __“the Lord stood upon a wall made by a plumbline, with a plumbline in his hand… Behold, I will set a *plumbline* in the midst of my people Israel”__.
One Hebrew verb of overflowing — _shataph_ — carries the same flood through every counterfeit in the file: the river over its banks when Shiloah is refused, the waters over the refuge of lies, the shower that breaks the whitewashed wall.
Every structure the builders raised falls to one water.
They rejected the tried stone because it did not fit the building they had underway — a high wall of conceit, whitewashed, chartered by a covenant with death — and the plumbline hung against their wall read the verdict the storm executed.

[long pause]

Now consider The Hedge, Removed Twice.

[long pause]

Matthew's word for the vineyard's hedge is _phragmos_ — and the Hebrew Matthew fences the vineyard with _gadar_, the very root of the wall Isaiah's sentence breaks down.
Paul reaches for the identical Greek word to name one more wall:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Ephesians 2:14]
____
For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the *middle wall of partition* between us;
____

[pause]

The partition — the _phragmos_ — stood between the citizen and the stranger, __“aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers from *the covenants of promise*”__.
The vineyard's enclosure came down twice, and the two demolitions face opposite directions.
In judgment the wall was removed so that the vineyard could be trodden down; in mercy the partition was dissolved so that the stranger could come in: __“Now therefore ye are no more strangers and foreigners, but *fellowcitizens* with the saints, and of the household of God”__.
Salvation's boundary was not abolished; it was opened, and the opening runs one way — inward.

[long pause]

Now consider The New Building.

[long pause]

The rejected stone went straight to work.
Peter, writing to the scattered, lays the courses:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, 1 Peter 2:4-5]
____
To whom coming, as unto a *living* stone, disallowed indeed of men, but chosen of God, and precious,
Ye also, as *lively stones*, are built up a *spiritual house*, an holy priesthood…
____

[pause]

Two verses later he sets this chapter's two quarry-texts side by side: __“Behold, I lay in Sion a chief corner stone, elect, precious”__, and __“the stone which the builders disallowed, the same is made the *head of the corner*”__.
Paul draws the same elevation in the very paragraph where the partition falls: __“built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief *corner* stone; in whom all the building fitly framed together groweth unto an holy *temple*”__.
The tower in the midst of the vineyard was a house for the Name built of quarried rock; the house now rising is built of people — every stone alive, set on the stone the builders threw away.

The blueprint's last page still shows a wall.
John measures the city that comes down from God:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:12, 14]
____
And had a wall great and high, and had twelve gates… and *names* written thereon, which are the names of the *twelve tribes* of the children of Israel…
And the wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the *twelve apostles* of the Lamb.
____

[pause]

The wall, like the tower, is built of names — the tribes on the gates, the apostles, the *sent ones*, in the foundations — and Isaiah's definition was written for this city all along: walls called Salvation, gates called Praise.
One symbol of the parable is still in motion.
__“Whosoever shall fall on this stone shall be broken: but on whomsoever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder”__ — Daniel watched that stone strike, grind the empires to chaff, and then grow, until it __“became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth”__: the kingdom.
The kingdom is exactly what the Owner deeded away in the sentence: __“The kingdom of God shall be taken from you, and given to a *nation* bringing forth the fruits thereof”__.
Which nation bears the fruit, and by what name Scripture calls it, Jacob, Israel, and Ephraim will prove.
