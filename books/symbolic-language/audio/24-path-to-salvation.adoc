Chapter Twenty-Four. [beat] Grace, Mercy, and the Gift of God.

[long pause]

When God passed before Moses in the cleft of the rock, He proclaimed His own Name: __“The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth, keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin — and that will by no means clear the guilty.”__

[long pause]

Ask any Christian what grace is, and the answer comes back in a formula: *unmerited favor* — getting what you don't deserve.
Ask about mercy, and you get its twin: *not getting the punishment you do deserve*.
Ask about the gift of God: *salvation — free. A gift can only be received, never earned.*

[pause]

None of these is wrong.
Every one of them is half.

[pause]

The formulas tell you what grace and mercy cost you — nothing — and stop before telling you what they *do*.
Scripture goes further, and it starts higher: both words are written into God's own *Name*, the proclamation you just heard.
Merciful and gracious… keeping mercy… forgiving iniquity… and that will *by no means clear the guilty*.
[beat]
Forgiveness, and the unforgiven, stand in the same sentence of the same Name.
The formulas are missing a clause — something decides which side of that sentence a man stands on — and this chapter recovers the three words to find it.

[long pause]

Grace: Favor That Moves First.

[long pause]

Begin where the formula is right.
Grace moves before any deserving.
Paul writes:

[quote.scripture, Romans 5:8]
____
But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, *while we were yet sinners*, Christ died for us.
____

[pause]
The word's first appearance in Scripture is the same shape: __“Noah found *grace* in the eyes of the LORD”__ — favor toward one man, in a world already condemned.
Unmerited is exactly right.

[pause]

Now watch what the formula leaves out.
When John says __“*grace and truth* came by Jesus Christ,”__ he is quoting the Name-proclamation — *abundant in goodness and truth* — the pair God announced at Sinai, now walking in person.
Grace never travels alone; its companion is the truth.
Remember what the truth is: *thy law is the truth*.
Paul gives grace its job description:

[quote.scripture, Titus 2:11-12]
____
For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men,
*Teaching us* that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live *soberly, righteously, and godly*, in this present world;
____

[pause]
Grace arrives free — and then it goes to work.
A tutor. Not an exemption.
[beat]
There is one verse always quoted against this: *ye are not under the law, but under grace*.
Paul guards it himself, in the very next breath: __“What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? *God forbid*.”__
To yield to sin is to be sin's servant — or, he says, of *obedience*, unto *righteousness*.
Not under the law's *condemnation*. Never out from under its instruction.
Grace is the teacher — not the end of school.
[pause]
Scripture even names the counterfeit.
Jude warns of ungodly men __“turning the grace of our God into *lasciviousness*”__ — grace, re-sold as license.
The test between them is simple: real grace produces sober, righteous, godly living.
The counterfeit produces permission.

[long pause]

Mercy: The Covenant's Loyalty.

[long pause]

The Hebrew beneath most of the Bible's word __“mercy”__ is _chesed_ — covenant loyalty. Steadfast love.
Moses sets it in its frame:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 7:9]
____
Know therefore that the LORD thy God, he is God, the *faithful* God, which keepeth *covenant and mercy* with them that *love him and keep his commandments* to a thousand generations;
____

[pause]
Covenant and mercy travel as one phrase.
Mercy lives *inside* the covenant, kept toward its keepers — the psalmist says the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that *fear him*… to such as *keep his covenant*.
[beat]
Hebrew's other mercy-word says how deep it runs.
_Racham_ — compassion — shares its letters with _rechem_: the *womb*.
__“Can a woman forget her sucking child?… yea, they may forget, yet will I *not* forget thee.”__
Mercy is womb-love: loyal because of what you *are* to Him, not because of what you did.

[pause]

How is it obtained?
Scripture answers in mechanics, not mood:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 28:13]
____
He that *covereth* his sins shall not prosper: but whoso *confesseth and forsaketh* them shall have *mercy*.
____

[pause]
Confess — *and forsake*.
Isaiah adds the turning: let the wicked *forsake his way*… and *return* unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him — and to our God, for he will *abundantly pardon*.
Ezekiel writes the clause that should astonish more than it does:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 33:15-16]
____
If the wicked *restore the pledge*, give again that he had robbed, walk in the statutes of life, without committing iniquity; he shall surely *live*, he shall not die.
*None of his sins* that he hath committed shall be *mentioned* unto him: he hath done that which is lawful and right; he shall surely live.
____

[pause]
Turn. Do right. Restore what can be restored.
And the ledger is wiped — none of it so much as *mentioned*.
[beat]
Repentance in Scripture has four parts, not three.
Confess the sin.
Plead guilty — David's words: against thee, thee only, have I sinned… *that thou mightest be justified when thou speakest*.
Change the heart, to desire what is good.
And make restitution where restitution can be made — as Zacchaeus did, fourfold.

[long pause]

The Canceled Debt.

[long pause]

Jesus told mercy as accounting.
A king takes account of his servants, and one is brought to him owing ten thousand talents — a sum no servant sees in a lifetime.
He cannot pay. He and his wife and children are to be sold.
The servant falls down: Lord, have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 18:23-27, 32-35]
____
Then the lord of that servant was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and *forgave him the debt*.
____

[pause]
Here is mercy with its full anatomy.
The debt is real, and unpayable.
The cancellation is total — and it is granted *on asking*; the king says so later: __“I forgave thee all that debt, *because thou desiredst me*.”__
Paul names the instrument: __“blotting out the *handwriting* that was against us… *nailing it to his cross*”__ — the debt-note itself, canceled, and nailed up where every debtor can read it.

[pause]

The canceled note has terms on the back.
The same parable ends with the pardoned man refusing to pardon — and his debt *reinstated*: delivered to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due.
__“So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.”__
[beat]
This is no isolated hard saying; it is the standing rule of the kingdom.
Blessed are the *merciful* — for *they* shall obtain mercy.
If ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive yours.
James says it coldest: he shall have judgment *without mercy*, that hath shewed *no mercy*.
[pause]
Mercy in Scripture is not a one-way transaction.
It is a circuit — received, then required.
Jesus files it where the formulas never look: among the *weightier matters of the law* — judgment, *mercy*, and faith.
Mercy is law-content. A thing commanded, and *done*: do justly, and *love mercy*, and walk humbly with thy God.
[pause]
Now the Name-proclamation's two poles close.
He keeps mercy for thousands, and forgives — the confessing, the forsaking, the forgiving.
He will by no means clear the guilty — the covering, the defiant, the unmerciful.
The clause the formulas were missing is *repentance* — and mercy, practiced.

[long pause]

The Gift Against the Wages.

[long pause]

One verse holds the whole economy:

[quote.scripture, Romans 6:23]
____
For the *wages* of sin is *death*; but the *gift* of God is *eternal life* through Jesus Christ our Lord.
____

[pause]
The contrast is the verse's own structure.
The Greek for wages is _opsōnia_ — a soldier's pay: earned, owed, paid in full.
Against it stands _charisma_ — the grace-gift.
Death is the one thing every sinner has fully earned.
Eternal life is the one thing no man ever will.
[pause]
Jesus closes the earning door in a sentence.
Does the master thank the servant for doing what he was commanded?
__“So likewise you, when you have done *all* those things which you are commanded, say: We are *unprofitable* servants. We have done what was our *duty* to do.”__
[pause]
Perfect obedience earns nothing — it was already owed.
As servants, we have nothing our Creator did not give us first; we owe Him our very life, and every sin grows the debt.
The first rule of getting out of a hole is to stop digging.
[beat]
Obedience cannot pay the old debt. Only the blood cancels that.
What obedience does is stop the new one.

[pause]

Paul holds both hands of it in one passage:

[quote.scripture, Ephesians 2:8-10]
____
For by grace you have been saved through faith.
And this is not your own doing; it is the *gift* of God, not a result of *works*, so that no one may boast.
For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus *for good works*, which God prepared beforehand, that we should *walk in them*.
____

[pause]
Not *of* works — no man earns it.
*For* good works — no man receives it and stands idle.
The gift, like the wedding garment, is granted — then worn.

[long pause]

The High Priest, and the Two Kinds of Sin.

[long pause]

Grace and mercy meet at a specific address:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 4:14-16]
____
Seeing then that we have a great *high priest*… Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession…
Let us therefore come boldly unto the *throne of grace*, that we may *obtain mercy*, and find grace to help in time of need.
____

[pause]
The throne of grace is where mercy is obtained.
The two words the formulas separate work one office — and the office is the *law's* own.
It is the law that establishes a High Priest, the sacrifice he presents, and the sins his atonement covers.
And the law divides sin in two.
Numbers says it plainly: the priest shall make atonement for the person who sins *unintentionally*, and he shall be forgiven.
But the person who does wrong *willfully and defiantly* — that one is blaspheming the LORD, and shall be *cut off*.
[pause]
Unintentional sin has an altar.
Defiant sin refuses the altar's terms.
Hebrews carries the same division into the new covenant:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 10:26-29]
____
For if we sin *willfully* after we have received the knowledge of the *truth*, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a certain fearful expectation of judgment…
Of how much worse punishment, do you suppose, will he be thought worthy who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, counted the *blood of the covenant* by which he was sanctified a common thing, and *insulted the Spirit of grace*?
____

[pause]
The truth, remember, is the law.
So willful sin after receiving it is lawbreaking with the eyes open — and the most open-eyed form of all is the claim that there is *no law to break*: that it was done away, that Jesus has paid the price for you not following it.
That claim does not plead the blood.
It counts the blood a common thing.
[pause]
The risen Jesus prescribes the escape to a fallen assembly, and it is measured in works resumed: __“Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and *repent*, and *do the first works*.”__
Repentance is not a feeling. It is what is *resumed*.
The healed are dismissed with the same charge — go, and sin *no more*, lest a worse thing come upon you.
And since sin is lawlessness, *sin no more* means: go, and keep the law.

[long pause]

The War in the Members.

[long pause]

If willful sin after knowledge is worse than never knowing — who then can be saved?
Paul answers with his own struggle:

[quote.scripture, Romans 7:19, 22-25]
____
For the good that I will to do, I do not do; but the evil I will not to do, that I practice…
For I *delight in the law* of God according to the inward man.
But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members.
O wretched man that I am!
Who will deliver me from this body of death?
I thank God — through Jesus Christ our Lord!
____

[pause]
Mark where Paul stands.
He calls the law good, and delights in it in the inward man — while the flesh he drags behind him keeps breaking it.
That is not the defiant sinner of Numbers.
That is the unintentional one: at war, confessing, covered by the High Priest.
It is the publican's whole prayer: *God be merciful to me, a sinner*.
[beat]
The man who agrees with the law and loses battles against his flesh has an altar.
The man who denies the law has refused it.

[long pause]

How Then Shall We Be Saved?

[long pause]

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:9]
____
That if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.
____

[pause]
Confessing Jesus as *Lord* means He is your *Master* — and He does nothing but what the Father commanded: __“I have come down from heaven, *not to do My own will*, but to do the will of Him who sent Me.”__
Call Him Master, and His Father's law becomes your desire.
Deny the law, and you have denied the Lordship inside the confession — the word emptied, while the mouth still moves.

[pause]

The familiar three-step path holds — but each step stands on the law it forgot.
Admit you are a sinner? [beat] You cannot, without the law that defines sin.
Believe He paid for your sin on the cross? [beat] You cannot, without the law that demands the sacrifice, establishes the High Priest, and prophesies the Messiah who meets both.
Confess Him Lord? [beat] You cannot, without submitting to obey Him.

[pause]

Is perfect obedience possible?
Not in the flesh.
Is perfect obedience required for salvation?
No. [beat] But a mind and heart of obedience is.
[pause]
Many say *Lord, Lord* — while their hearts say, there are no laws, because I am forgiven.
A reckless driver, claiming there are no rules of the road because someone has paid his tickets.
The paid ticket is mercy.
The gratitude that slows down is grace, doing its teaching.
The license kept — is the gift.
He who *does* the will of the Father enters: not earning the kingdom, but refusing to insult the Spirit of the grace that brought him to its gate.

[pause]

The servant who means to keep His commands exactly will want them exactly as they were written — which raises a question this book has been leaning on since the Introduction read one word two ways.
What, precisely, did the prophets write?
[beat]
That question opens the next part of this book.

[long pause]
