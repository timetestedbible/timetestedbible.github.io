Chapter Three. [beat] Signs and Similitudes.

[long pause]

Ecclesiastes 1: __“The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun.”__

[long pause]

How did Paul read the Old Testament?
Not by a revelation the rest of us lack — he tells us his method outright, in the middle of a passage about Israel's wilderness history:

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 10:11]
____
Now all these things *happened* unto them for *ensamples*: and they are *written* for our admonition, upon whom the ends of the world are come.
____

[pause]
The King James' *ensamples* is the Greek _typos_ — types, patterns struck in advance — and mark the two verbs: the things *happened* as types before they were *written* as types.
Paul is not decorating history with lessons.
He is telling us what the history *is* — composed.
[beat]
Watch him work the method on text after text.
The rock that followed Israel — *that Rock was Christ*.
Adam — *the figure of him that was to come*.
Abraham's two households — *which things are an allegory, for these are the two covenants*: real women, real sons, and at the same time a composed likeness.
Paul is the proof that this is a method of reading and not a private gift: he points at scrolls everyone owned, and demonstrates what was always in them.

[pause]

So far this book has let Scripture define its symbols one word at a time — the seed is the word, the ground is the heart — but Paul read whole *lives* that way, because God writes with more than words: with wonders, with scenes, and with the lives of real men.
Scripture claims nothing less for the whole canvas.

[pause]

All of creation testifies: __“The heavens declare the glory of God… There is no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard.”__
All of history testifies too.
The psalm that recounts Israel's whole story opens, *I will open my mouth in a parable, I will utter dark sayings of old* — the very line Matthew lays over the parables of Jesus, the psalm the last chapter took its exam under.
A history that can be called a parable is a history that was composed.

[pause]

Scripture has two words of its own for that larger writing — *sign* and *similitude* — and this chapter defines them the same way the last chapter derived the seed and the ground: by letting Scripture use them in front of us.

[long pause]

What Scripture Calls a Sign.

[long pause]

Men who demand signs mean one thing by the word; the prophets mean another.
The demand is for a wonder performed on the spot.
Watch what Scripture calls a sign, though, and notice that there is no wonder in it anywhere.

[pause]

When Samuel anointed Saul, he sent him home with signs.
Two men would meet him by Rachel's tomb, and tell him the lost donkeys were found.
Three men would meet him at the plain of Tabor, carrying three kids, three loaves, and a bottle of wine — and would hand him two loaves.
Then a company of prophets would come down from the high place.
[beat]
Travelers on a road. A greeting. Bread changing hands.
Scenes told in advance — down to the count of the loaves.

[quote.scripture, 1 Samuel 10:9]
____
And it was so, that when he had turned his back to go from Samuel, God gave him another heart: and *all those signs came to pass that day*.
____

[pause]
Nothing was conjured.
Everything foretold simply happened.
A sign is a word given ahead of events, and its proof is that it comes true.
[beat]
A sign can be more than a spoken word.
It can be a prophet:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 24:24]
____
Thus *Ezekiel is unto you a sign*: according to *all that he hath done* shall ye do: and *when this cometh, ye shall know* that I am the Lord GOD.
____

[pause]
Not Ezekiel's sermon — Ezekiel, the man. His acted story. *All that he hath done.*
Isaiah and his message-named children were *for signs and for wonders in Israel* — and Isaiah himself walked out one prophecy barefoot, for three years, *for a sign*.

[pause]

The pattern holds into the gospels.
The shepherds are given *a sign* that is simply a scene to find: *ye shall find the babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger.*
Simeon calls the child himself *a sign which shall be spoken against*.
It holds to the last book of the Bible, whose visions are titled signs: the travailing woman is *a great wonder in heaven* — the Greek is _sēmeion_, a great *sign* — and so is the dragon that waits on her.

[pause]

Gather the uses, and the definition writes itself.
A sign is a message given ahead of time — an oracle, a scene, or a prophet's own lived story — so that its fulfillment testifies.
A wonder on demand is what men ask for.
A sign is what God gives.

[pause]

Now test the definition on the most famous sign in the Bible:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 7:14]
____
Therefore the Lord himself shall *give you a sign*; Behold, a virgin *shall conceive*, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.
____

[pause]
Is this sign not a miracle?
Its fulfillment is.
Mark what happened on the day the sign was given, though — [beat] nothing.
No wonder was performed in Ahaz's court.
The word itself was the sign, and it stood as the sign for seven centuries before its fulfillment arrived to testify.
The miracle belongs to the fulfillment.
The sign is the message that ran ahead of it.

[pause]

There is a second family of signs, and it does not point forward at all.
It points to a standing covenant.
The bow in the cloud is *the token of the covenant* — a chapter of its own ahead.
Circumcision is *the sign — a seal of the righteousness of the faith*.
The Passover blood on the houses was *a token*.
The word of God itself is bound *for a sign upon thine hand*.
The sabbath is *a sign between me and you throughout your generations… that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you — a sign for ever*.
[beat]
A sign, then, is God's mark, set on a message or on a covenant — and the covenant-signs are declared perpetual.

[long pause]

The Two Tests of a Valid Sign.

[long pause]

Moses himself gives the tests of a valid sign, and there are two.
The first is fulfillment: *if the thing follow not, nor come to pass, that is the thing which the LORD hath not spoken.*
A sign that fails is no sign, and the prophet who gave it spoke presumptuously.
[beat]
Fulfillment alone is not enough, though, and the second test overrides the first.
If a prophet gives a sign, *and the sign or the wonder come to pass*, yet he teaches you to go after other gods — you shall not hearken: *for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God.*
To go after other gods is no separate offense standing beside lawbreaking.
It is the *first commandment* broken — the head of the law — and Deuteronomy draws that conclusion itself in the next breath: *keep his commandments, and obey his voice.*
[pause]
A valid sign must both come true, and come from one who does not teach against the law.
A fulfilled sign never licenses lawbreaking.
God Himself may send one, as the proving.

[long pause]

A Likeness Made of Lives.

[long pause]

The second word comes from God's own mouth:

[quote.scripture, Hosea 12:10]
____
I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and *used similitudes*, by the ministry of the prophets.
____

[pause]
A similitude is a likeness — but not a likeness drawn in words, like a parable.
God says He *used* similitudes, *by the ministry of the prophets*: He composed likenesses out of lives.
The prophet who wrote that line was himself the proof — his whole life a sign.

[pause]

Hosea's book opens with the command:

[quote.scripture, Hosea 1:2]
____
Go, take unto thee a wife of whoredoms and children of whoredoms: for the land hath committed great whoredom, *departing from the LORD*.
____

[pause]
The interpretation is handed down in the same breath as the command.
His children were message-named, like Isaiah's: _Lo-ruhamah_ — Not-pitied; _Lo-ammi_ — Not-my-people.
When the wife was lost to her adulteries, the command came again: __“Go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel”__ — and the prophet bought his own bride back, for fifteen pieces of silver.
[beat]
A marriage, a betrayal, a redemption — lived out over years, so that Israel could watch the love of the LORD walk through town.
This is the writing Paul learned to read — the _typos_ that *happened unto them* — and it runs beyond lives, into the very furniture of the covenant: the law is *a shadow of good things to come*; the tabernacle, built *according to the pattern* of heavenly things.

[pause]

Jesus reads history the same way, time after time.
As the days of Noah were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be.
As it was in the days of Lot, even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed.
As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up.
[beat]
He does not treat the histories as illustrations that happen to fit.
He treats them as prophecies that must be fulfilled.

[pause]

The New Testament ratifies the method instance after instance, in so many words.
The manna was a similitude of *the true bread from heaven* — and the feeding of the five thousand that opens that discourse is itself a lived sign.
John calls it one — his word for the *miracle* is the Greek _sēmeion_, a sign — the sign the last chapter read in full: the Law, carried by witnesses, feeding the famished.
A chapter ahead, on the sea and its waters, will deepen the fish's half of its key.

[pause]

The temple was a similitude of *the temple of his body*.
The Passover lamb, of *Christ our passover, sacrificed for us* — John even cites the lamb-rule, *a bone of him shall not be broken*, as fulfilled prophecy.
A past man prophesied a future man: *this is Elias, which was for to come.*
Peter calls baptism the *like figure* of the flood — his Greek word is _antitypon_.
Type and antitype are Scripture's own vocabulary, not a scholar's invention.

[pause]

How far may this be carried?
The risen Lord's own claim outruns any cautious answer:

[quote.scripture, Luke 24:27]
____
And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in *all the scriptures* the things concerning himself.
____

[pause]
He said as much elsewhere: *Moses wrote of me.*
Not a proof-text here and there.
*All* the scriptures.

[long pause]

God Requireth That Which Is Past.

[long pause]

How can real history be prophecy?
Solomon gives the warrant — it is the line this chapter opened with: __“The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be… and there is no new thing under the sun.”__
[beat]
Read flat, it sounds like world-weariness.
The sun, though, is a symbol — a chapter of its own ahead — for *the LORD God is a sun*.
Under *that* Sun, the saying turns from a sigh into a signature: under the Father's hand there is no new thing, because history is composed, and the Composer repeats His themes on purpose.
Solomon says it again, with the veil pulled back:

[quote.scripture, Ecclesiastes 3:15]
____
That which hath been is now; and that which is to be hath already been; and *God requireth that which is past*.
____

[pause]
God *requires* the past — summons it back, calls for the pattern again.
What has been done will be done again, because the same Author is writing.
That is why a life can be a prophecy: the first telling is the similitude, and God will require it.

[long pause]

The Method Proved on Moriah.

[long pause]

God says to Abraham:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 22:2]
____
Take now thy son, *thine only son* Isaac, whom thou *lovest*, and get thee into the land of *Moriah*; and offer him there for a burnt offering upon one of the mountains which I will tell thee of.
____

[pause]
Mark three things in that one sentence.
It is the first time the word *love* appears in all of Scripture — and the Bible's first love is a father's love for the son he must offer.
The son is *thine only son* — the very phrase Hebrews keeps: Abraham *offered up his only begotten son*.
The place is chosen by God — the land of Moriah, the mountain where the temple would stand: *the house of the LORD at Jerusalem, in mount Moriah.*

[pause]

Now the scenes run.
*On the third day*, Abraham lifts up his eyes and sees the place.
The wood of the offering is laid on the son, and the son carries it up the hill — as another Son, on that same ridge, *bearing his cross went forth*.
The son asks where the offering is, and the father answers with the gospel, a full two millennia early — __“My son, God will provide himself a lamb”__ — the sentence John the Baptist completes: __“Behold the Lamb of God.”__
The substitute dies with his head caught in thorns: *a ram, caught in a thicket by his horns*.
Abraham names the place with a promise in the future tense: __“In the mount of the LORD it shall be seen.”__

[pause]

Hebrews then hands down the verdict on what kind of event this was:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 11:19]
____
Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him *in a figure*.
____

[pause]
*In a figure* — the Greek is _en parabolē_: *in a parable*.
Scripture itself declares the event a lived parable of death and resurrection — a father receiving his only son back from the dead — staged on the very mountain where it would be fulfilled, two thousand years in advance.
That is a similitude: not a story about the truth, but the truth, rehearsed.

[pause]

Or take Joseph, and count the frames as they go by.
Betrayed by his brothers. [beat] Stripped of his garment. [beat] Sold for pieces of silver. [beat] Condemned between two prisoners, of whom one was lifted up to the king's presence and the other hanged — as two would hang beside another condemned man. [beat] Raised out of the pit to the throne. [beat] Made the savior of the nations, with bread. [beat] Unrecognized by his own brothers.
[pause]
Then the line Stephen preached to the Sanhedrin: __“at the *second time* Joseph was made known to his brethren.”__
At the second coming, his brothers knew him.
Stephen told that story to the very council that had condemned his Lord, and they heard what he was saying — it is the sermon that got him killed.

[pause]

Or take Rahab, whose story the generation after the apostles still read this way.
A woman in a city devoted to destruction asks the spies for a sign — *give me a true token* — and the token is *this line of scarlet thread in the window*.
When the city fell, every soul in the scarlet-marked house lived: the Passover, kept this time by a gentile household — the marked house spared in the judged city.
She did not merely survive.
She entered the line of the Messiah.

[pause]

Clement of Rome, writing while men who knew the apostles still lived, says the spies gave her __“a sign… a scarlet thread, thereby showing beforehand that through the blood of the Lord there shall be redemption unto all them that believe”__ — and draws the conclusion for us: __“not only faith, but prophecy, is found in the woman.”__
Her life was a prophecy.
The verdict is Clement's, not mine.

[pause]

Now widen the lens all the way, because the master similitude is Israel herself.
Hosea wrote of the nation's past: __“When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt”__ — and Matthew quotes that line as prophecy, *fulfilled* when the child Jesus comes up out of Egypt.
A sentence about the nation's history is cited as the Son's future.
The history itself was the similitude.

[pause]

The Son thus relives the nation's story, and where Israel fell, He stands.
Through the water — as all Israel was *baptized in the cloud and in the sea*, a crossing a later chapter will read.
Then forty days fasting in the wilderness, answering Israel's forty years — turning the tempter away, each time, with words drawn from the very chapters Moses wrote about those years.
All of Israel's history is written this way: lived first, fulfilled after.

[pause]

Treat the histories as composed.
When Scripture tells a life in unusual detail — a fare paid, a garment stripped, a third day, a thicket of thorns — the details are not color.
They are lines of a similitude, and God requireth that which is past: what has been done will be done again.
[pause]
Jesus, asked to prove who He was, reached for one prophet's whole life and called it *the* sign.
The next chapter reads it scene by scene.

[long pause]
