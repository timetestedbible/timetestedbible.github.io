MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Twenty-Two: Worship.

[long pause]
Say the word __“worship”__ today and a music set answers: songs before the sermon, a genre on the radio, a team on a stage.
The word has been compressed to one of its accompaniments — and a reader who carries __“sing songs in church”__ into Scripture will misread every command that hangs on the word, up to the last angel’s cry: __“worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea”__.

The word falls in nearly two hundred verses.
Gather them, and the first thing Scripture does is separate worship from singing — inside single scenes, in parallel columns, the tools this book has used from the beginning.

Watch Hezekiah’s temple service hold three acts apart in one verse:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, 2 Chronicles 29:28]
____
And all the congregation *worshipped*, and the singers *sang*, and the trumpeters *sounded*: and all this continued until the burnt offering was finished.
____

[pause]

The congregation worshipped _and_ the singers sang: two different acts, performed by different people at the same moment.
Two verses later the acts meet in one body, and the wording shows which is which: __“they sang praises with gladness, and they *bowed their heads* and worshipped”__.
Jehoshaphat’s assembly divides the same way:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, 2 Chronicles 20:18-19]
____
And Jehoshaphat *bowed his head* with his face to the ground: and all Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem *fell before the LORD, worshipping* the LORD.
And the Levites… *stood up to praise* the LORD God of Israel with a loud voice on high.
____

[pause]

Worship falls down; praise stands up.
Singing is a fine companion to worship — the psalms command it — but Scripture never confuses the two, because the worship-word has a body in it.

The Hebrew word is _shachah_: to bow oneself down, to prostrate.
The King James renders the very same word __“worship”__ toward God, __“bow down”__ at Sinai, and __“did obeisance”__ toward men: Abraham bows himself to the children of Heth, Absalom bows to the ground before the king, and Mordecai refuses it to Haman — __“Mordecai *bowed not*, nor did him reverence”__.
The word itself is simply homage: the body bent before a superior, the subject before his king.
What makes it the worship of God is not a change of word; it is the One it bends to.

The Greek says the same thing with a different gesture.
_Proskuneō_, the New Testament’s worship-word, is built from __“toward”__ and __“kiss”__: the subject’s kiss toward his king.
And the homage-kiss is no Greek invention — Scripture’s Hebrew knows it well:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 2:12]
____
*Kiss the Son*, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little.
Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.
____

[pause]

The seven thousand faithful in Elijah’s day are counted by the same gesture withheld: __“all the knees which have not *bowed* unto Baal, and every mouth which hath not *kissed* him”__.
Apostate Israel says of its idols, __“Let the men that sacrifice *kiss* the calves”__.
And Job, clearing himself of the oldest idolatry, describes sun-worship as exactly this: __“If I beheld the sun when it shined… or my mouth hath *kissed my hand*: this also were an iniquity… for I should have *denied the God that is above*”__.

Knee and mouth, bow and kiss: worship is the body’s declaration of allegiance.
That is why it can be demanded, refused, and died for — none of which makes sense of a song.

Now consider the first time the word appears in the Bible, because Scripture’s first use of a word is a teacher.
It is Abraham speaking, on the third day, with the wood on his son’s shoulders:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 22:5]
____
And Abraham said unto his young men, Abide ye here with the ass; and I and the lad will go yonder and *worship*, and come again to you.
____

[pause]

The first worship in Scripture is a man walking up a mountain to obey the costliest command he was ever given.
No instrument, no assembly, no song — obedience with everything on the altar is what Abraham calls worship, and Scripture agrees with him: __“to *obey* is better than sacrifice”__.

From the bow, Scripture builds worship’s fixed pair.
Hear the second commandment — for the commandment about false worship uses the worship-word itself:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 20:5]
____
Thou shalt not *bow down* thyself to them, *nor serve* them: for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God…
____

[pause]

__“Bow down”__ is _shachah_ — the word rendered __“worship”__ everywhere else — and it never travels alone: bow down, *and serve*.
The pair holds wherever worship is tested.
The three Hebrews in Babylon: __“we will not *serve* thy gods, nor *worship* the golden image”__.
Paul on the pagan world: they __“*worshipped and served* the creature more than the Creator”__.

__“Creature”__ is the Greek _ktisis_ — the *made* thing, anything at all that is not the Maker — and Paul’s inventory two verses earlier is Moses’ own idolatry catalog, item for item: the image __“like to corruptible man, and to birds, and fourfooted beasts, and creeping things”__, a list Moses runs on to __“the sun, and the moon, and the stars”__.
The made thing men serve takes a fixed political shape in the Bible’s symbolic language: the beast, a kingdom of men — a different Greek word, but the same structure, for a kingdom is as much a made thing as a carved one — so that creature-worship comes to its head exactly where this chapter is going: __“all the world… worshipped the beast”__, and the everlasting gospel answers by naming God by the one act no creature shares: __“worship him that *made*.”__

When Satan asked Jesus for a single prostration — __“All these things will I give thee, if thou wilt *fall down and worship* me”__ — the answer welds the pair together forever:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 4:10]
____
…for it is written, Thou shalt *worship* the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou *serve*.
____

[pause]

The bow is the posture; the service is the life that proves the posture told the truth.
This is why Paul can define the whole worship of a Christian without mentioning an assembly at all:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Romans 12:1]
____
I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that ye present your *bodies* a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your reasonable *service*.
____

[pause]

One prostration confesses a master; the rest of the week serves him.
Satan understood the arithmetic — he asked for the bow because the service follows it.

Now look closely at what Jesus quoted, because there is a discovery in it.
__“It is written,”__ He said — in Deuteronomy, where the verse reads:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 6:13]
____
Thou shalt fear the LORD thy God, and serve him, and shalt swear by his name.
____

[pause]

Moses wrote __“fear”__; Jesus rendered it __“worship.”__
The Lord Himself treats the two words as one thing — and the rest of Scripture backs His translation in parallel columns: __“him shall ye fear, and him shall ye *worship*, and to him shall ye do sacrifice”__; __“O *worship* the LORD in the beauty of holiness: *fear* before him, all the earth”__; __“Who shall not *fear* thee, O Lord?… all nations shall come and *worship* before thee”__.
Worship is the fear of the LORD made visible — and what the fear of the LORD is, the next chapter will establish as the root under every symbol in this book.
Hold the equation; we will need it in a moment at the end of the age.

The equation also exposes the counterfeit, for Jesus named a worship that counts for nothing:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 15:9]
____
But *in vain* they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the *commandments of men*.
____

[pause]

He is quoting Isaiah — and in Isaiah, the word behind __“worship”__ is __“fear”__: __“their fear toward me is *taught by the precept of men*”__.
There is the definition of vain worship, by the same fear-worship equation: homage redefined by human precept — worship of the true God, on terms He never gave.

It is worth sitting with the irony: a generation that compressed __“worship”__ into a form of its own devising is described by the very verse.
The lips draw near, the heart is far, and the terms are ours.
God does not weigh worship by its sincerity but by whose commandments shape it.

His terms include His calendar.
Throughout Scripture, worship is a thing men __“go up”__ to do at appointed times: Elkanah __“went up out of his city *yearly* to worship and to sacrifice unto the LORD of hosts in Shiloh”__; the firstfruits are set down before the altar, __“and worship”__; Greeks __“came up to *worship at the feast*”__; and the Ethiopian eunuch __“had come to Jerusalem for to worship”__. Paul then says he __“went up to Jerusalem for to worship”__, and defines the act for the governor: __“so *worship* I the God of my fathers, believing all things which are written in the *law* and in the prophets”__.
Ask Paul what his worship is, and he answers with the law believed — which, in the Bible’s symbolic language, means the law obeyed.

The prophets set worship on the appointed days to the end of days:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 66:23]
____
And it shall come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one *sabbath* to another, shall *all flesh* come to *worship* before me, saith the LORD.
____

[pause]

The surviving nations go up __“to *worship the King*, the LORD of hosts, and to *keep the feast* of tabernacles”__ — and the family that will not go up gets no rain.
Worship, in Scripture’s grammar, is kept — like a feast, like a commandment — at His times, in His presence, on His terms.

Now the last angel’s cry can be read with every word weighed.
In the middle of heaven flies the messenger with the everlasting gospel — the scene the Gospel chapter left with us — and this is the whole proclamation:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 14:7]
____
Fear *God*, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and *worship him that made* heaven, and earth, and the *sea*, and the fountains of waters.
____

[pause]

Fear and worship, the pair Jesus welded, side by side one last time.
And listen to how the One to be worshipped is identified — __“him that *made heaven, and earth, and the sea*”__ — for Scripture has exactly one commandment that grounds itself in that formula:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 20:11]
____
For in six days the LORD *made heaven and earth, the sea*, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day: wherefore the LORD blessed the *sabbath* day, and hallowed it.
____

[pause]

The everlasting gospel’s call to worship echoes the sabbath commandment’s own ground clause.
The angel is not inventing a new religion at the end of the age; he is pointing every nation back to the Maker’s memorial — the appointed day that marks who made whom.

The Hebrew text of Revelation reads the verse the same direction: __“Fear YHWH and *make your prayer* to him… and make prayer to *the one who created* the heavens and the earth and the sea and the wells.”__
The manuscript and its one-way translation markers are presented at TimeTested.Bible.]
Where the Greek says worship, the Hebrew witness says prayer made _to_ Him — homage addressed to a person; in neither language is the angel calling the nations to a song service.

The reason the angel must cry it is that a rival is collecting bows.
The chapter before is the Bible’s last worship crisis: __“they *worshipped the* dragon… and they *worshipped the* beast”__, and the image of the beast causes __“that as many as would *not worship* the image of the beast should be *killed*”__ — Babylon’s furnace again, the bow-or-burn test of Daniel 3, staged for the whole earth.
The final division of mankind is drawn at exactly the word this age thinks means singing: everyone bows and serves — the only question, as with the shadow, is _whose_.

Scripture guards the direction of the bow so jealously that when John — John, at the end of the visions — falls at the feet of the angel who showed them, he is refused on the spot:

Let's read:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 22:9]
____
See thou do it not: for I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren the prophets, and of them which *keep the sayings* of this book: *worship God*.
____

[pause]

The compressed word unfolds, and every sense of it lines up behind one meaning.
Worship is homage in the body and allegiance in the life: to bow to God and to serve Him only — the fear of the LORD acted out, at His appointed times, on His terms and not ours.
Singing can carry it; it cannot replace it.
Its counterfeit is worship shaped by the commandments of men; its rival is the beast who wants the bow; and its final proclamation calls every nation to the Maker of heaven, and earth, and the sea.

One thread remains, the one Jesus tied worship to when He answered Satan from Deuteronomy — the fear of the LORD.
That is the last symbol these doctrine studies must define.
