Chapter Eight. [beat] The Name.

[long pause]

Proverbs 18: __“The name of the LORD is a *strong tower*: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.”__

[long pause]

Two things almost everyone believes about the name turn out to be wrong — and they are the two doors most people walk in by.
The first is that to take the LORD's name in vain is to curse — *oh my God* as an idle breath — or to mangle the sacred syllables. Yahweh. Yahuah.
The second is quieter.
We end our prayers *in Jesus' name*, as though the phrase were a key that turns the lock — and when nothing turns, we quietly wonder what went wrong.

[pause]

Both mistakes treat the name as a *sound* — a word to pronounce correctly, or a formula to attach.
But Jesus made a promise about His name that no magic word could keep:

[quote.scripture, John 14:13]
____
And *whatsoever ye shall ask in my name*, that will I do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son.
____

[pause]
*Whatsoever*. [beat] Yet how many prayers, sealed with the name, go unanswered?
Either the promise fails, or *in my name* means something deeper than a phrase we append.
To find it we must stop asking how the name *sounds*, and ask what it *is* — and Scripture builds the answer a piece at a time.

[long pause]

In My Name.

[long pause]

Begin with that promise.
To ask *in my name* is not to invoke a syllable, but to ask *as one who belongs to Him* — within His will, on His terms, inside the relationship.
Paul says the same of the whole of life: __“whatsoever ye do in word or deed, do all in the name of the Lord Jesus.”__
You cannot do *everything* by pronouncing a word over it; you can only do everything *within a relationship*.
To act *in the name* is to act inside the bond.

[pause]

The bond is something a person *carries*.
At Sinai, God named the guide who went before His people — one with the name itself *in him*:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 23:20-21]
____
Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to *keep thee in the way*, and to bring thee into *the place which I have prepared*… Beware of him, and obey his *voice*, provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for *my name is in him*.
____

[pause]
This is no created messenger.
He bears God's own *name* — His presence and authority — and Isaiah, looking back, tells us plainly who he was: __“the angel of his presence saved them… and he bare them, and carried them all the days of old.”__
The *Rock* that followed Israel through the wilderness, Paul says, *was Christ*.
So Christ — the One in whom the name dwelt — led them by the pillar of cloud and fire; and the same One came in the flesh.
[beat]
One clause does reach ahead.
This Angel *will not pardon your transgressions* — and transgress they did, at the very foot of the mountain.
The name-bearer who would at last *pardon* comes only when He takes flesh.

[pause]

When Jesus says, __“I have declared unto them thy name,”__ He is not reciting a sound He learned.
He is that name-bearer, now walking among His people — the name *borne* in a person, made visible in a faithful life, and kept the way it always was: He keeps them in the *way*, covenant-conduct, and they must obey his *voice*.
[pause]
The promise looked beyond that generation's journey.
The Angel was sent __“to bring thee into the place which I have prepared”__ — the very words the name-bearer would take up as His own: __“I go to prepare a place for you… I will come again, and receive you unto myself.”__
The One in whom the name dwells led Israel through the wilderness, came in the flesh, and will come again to gather His people into the place He has prepared — one name-bearer, from the wilderness to the end.

[long pause]

Lord, Lord.

[long pause]

Which sets up the sharpest test Jesus ever gave.
Picture the people most certain of their standing:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:22-23]
____
Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy *name*? and in thy *name* have cast out devils? and in thy *name* done many *powers*?
And then will I profess unto them, *I never knew you*: depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*.
____

[pause]
Every sentence has the name in it — prophesying, casting out devils, doing powers, *in thy name*.
No one ever spoke it more.
He turns them away as workers of *lawlessness* — the Greek word is _anomia_.
They had the sound, and not the substance.
[pause]
The name cannot be a word on the lips — a man may have it there constantly, and remain a stranger to the One who bears it.
What, then, *is* it?

[long pause]

The Name Is the Covenant.

[long pause]

Here at last Scripture says it outright.
David, settled in his palace, wants to build a house for the ark of the covenant:

[quote.scripture, 2 Samuel 7:2]
____
…I dwell in an house of cedar, but the *ark of God* dwelleth within curtains.
____

[pause]
God answers, and quietly trades one word for another:

[quote.scripture, 2 Samuel 7:13]
____
He shall build an house for my *name*, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.
____

[pause]
David said, the ark of the *covenant*.
God said, my *name*.
They are the same thing.
The name *is* the covenant — the binding relationship between God and His people; the renewed covenant the Gospel chapter recovered.

[pause]

Notice that God, answering, plays on David's own word — for a *house* in Scripture is a building *and* a family line, and a family line is exactly what carries a name.
David offered to build God a house of stone.
God replies: __“the LORD telleth thee that *he will make thee an house*”__ — not cedar, but sons. The house *of David*.
[pause]
The promise then stands established three ways at once, and every level holds.
In the flesh, the seed and throne endure — the angel tells Mary: __“the Lord God shall give unto him the *throne of his father David*… and of his kingdom there shall be *no end*.”__
In stone, Solomon raises the temple *for my name*.
And in covenant, the Son of David builds the house the first two only sketched.
__“Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up”__ — He spoke of the temple of his *body*.
Ye also, Peter writes, as lively *stones*, are built up a *spiritual house*.
Christ as a son over his own house, says Hebrews — *whose house are we*.
[pause]
Zechariah compresses the whole promise into one oracle:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 6:12-13]
____
…Behold the man whose *name* is The BRANCH; and he shall grow up out of his place, and he shall *build the temple* of the LORD:
Even he shall build the temple of the LORD; and he shall bear the glory, and shall sit and *rule upon his throne*; and he shall be a priest upon his throne: and the counsel of peace shall be between them both.
____

[pause]
Name, house, and throne, in one Man.
The wordplay is the doctrine — a house exists to carry a name, and God's answer to David binds His name and David's house into a single building.

[pause]

Now everything before falls into place.
This is why asking *in his name* is asking within the covenant.
Why the name is *borne* by the obedient.
Why the man who only *speaks* it is unknown.
It is why __“there is none other name under heaven… whereby we must be saved”__ — no other name, because no other *covenant*.
To *believe on his name* is not to credit a title, but to enter that covenant in trusting obedience.

[pause]

It is also why the name can be a refuge:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 18:10]
____
The name of the LORD is a *strong tower*: the righteous *runneth into it*, and is safe.
____

[pause]
No one takes shelter inside a sound.
A covenant can be run into — walls, a roof, and a door that shuts behind the one who keeps it.
[beat]
It even untangles an old puzzle.
God tells Moses He was *not known* to the patriarchs __“by my name JEHOVAH”__ — though Abraham plainly *heard* the word: *I am the LORD that brought thee out of Ur*.
He knew the *sound*; he had not yet entered the *covenant* that name would come to carry.
[pause]
Which is exactly why the argument over the right syllables, earnest as it is, looks straight past the thing itself.
The name is not a sound to recover.
It is a covenant to keep.

[long pause]

Taking It in Vain.

[long pause]

Now — and only now — the commandment opens:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 20:7]
____
Thou shalt not take the *name* of the LORD thy God *in vain*; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his *name* in vain.
____

[pause]
*Vain* is the Hebrew _shav_ — emptiness. *For nothing*.
To take the name in vain is to take the *covenant* for nothing — to wear it, claim it, swear by it, while living as though it asks nothing of you.
It was never first a sin of the mouth; it is a sin of the life.
The cursing man breaks it, yes — but so does the one who cries *Lord, Lord* and works lawlessness, and the one who seals empty prayers with a name he will not keep.
[pause]
Solomon files that exact case:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 28:9]
____
He that turneth away his ear from *hearing the law*, even his *prayer* shall be *abomination*.
____

[pause]
The mouth full of the name, the ear turned from the covenant that fills it — such prayer does not merely go unanswered. It *offends*.
There is the answer to the puzzle this chapter opened with: the prayer sealed *in the name* fails because the ear has left the law.
Most who take His name in vain do it inside the church.

[pause]

The prophets prosecute the same sin under another verb.
Hear Ezekiel: __“When they entered unto the heathen… they *profaned my holy name*, when they said to them, These are *the people of the LORD*, and are gone forth out of his land.”__
Israel did not profane the name by mispronouncing it; they profaned it by wearing it faithlessly in front of the nations — covenant-breakers, introduced everywhere as the covenant people.
God's remedy is not a correction of speech: __“I will *sanctify my great name*, which was profaned among the heathen… when I shall be *sanctified in you* before their eyes.”__
He hallows His own name by making the people who wear it *holy*.

[long pause]

The Lord's Prayer.

[long pause]

The most familiar prayer in the world asks for exactly this — and once the name is understood, the whole prayer opens, line by line.
Its grammar carries more weight than the recitation lets on.
*Hallowed be thy name* is not a description, and not a compliment.
The verb is an imperative — *let thy name be made holy*.
The one praying is demanding the very thing Ezekiel promised: that the name be hallowed in its people, before the eyes of the world.
[pause]
Hear the petitions that march out behind it, each in the same commanding mood.
Thy *kingdom come*. Thy *will be done*, in earth, as it is in heaven.
Name hallowed, kingdom come, will *done* — the covenant kept holy in a people, the King's reign arriving, and His will obeyed on earth the way heaven already obeys it.
The second petition tells us how the first is granted — a name is hallowed when the will of its Bearer is done, beginning with the one praying.

[pause]

The covenant runs through every petition that follows; walk them with the Bible's symbolic language in hand.
*Give us this day our daily bread* — and bread is a defined symbol: the *word* from the mouth of the LORD.
The petition even has a history, for the first daily bread was the manna — rained from heaven one day's portion at a time — and God states His purpose in the giving: __“that I may *prove* them, whether they will *walk in my law*, or no.”__
Luke's wording keeps the manna's rhythm exactly — *give us day by day our daily bread* — and when Luke's Jesus finishes the prayer, He walks straight into a parable of a man begging three loaves at midnight, and a Father who gives to them that ask. The petition, dramatized.
To ask for daily bread is to ask for the day's portion of the word — and to take up the day's test of walking in it.

[pause]

__“And forgive us our *debts*, as *we* forgive our debtors.”__
The release of debts is the law's own mercy. Deuteronomy states it: __“every creditor that lendeth ought unto his neighbour shall *release* it… because it is called *the LORD'S release*.”__
The petition asks God to run His release toward us, as we run it toward our brothers.
Luke's version glosses the symbol as it goes: __“And forgive us our *sins*; for we also forgive every one that is *indebted* to us.”__
Sins in the first clause, debtors in the second — the two gospels set the columns side by side, and the debt is *sin*.
[beat]
It is the one line of the prayer Jesus stops to underline the moment He finishes teaching it: __“For *if ye forgive* men their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you: but *if ye forgive not*…”__
He later stages it as a full drama — the servant forgiven ten thousand talents, who takes his brother by the throat, and is delivered back to the tormentors.
The prayer must be practiced by the man praying it, or it testifies against him.

[pause]

The last petition asks the covenant God for the covenant deliverance: *lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil*.
Both halves repay testing.
[beat]
*Temptation* translates a Greek word that means the *proving* — and James rules out the modern reading in one stroke: __“God cannot be tempted with evil, *neither tempteth he any man*.”__
Yet the same James calls the man blessed who *endures* it: when he is *tried*, he shall receive the *crown of life*.
Beneath the Greek stands a Hebrew word the King James renders both ways — and the wobble is our familiar signpost.
__“God did *tempt* Abraham”__ — the first *tempt* in the Bible, in the very scene that gives us the first *worship*: a discovery the Worship chapter will unfold.
It is the same word as __“that I may *prove* them”__ over the manna, and __“to *prove* thee… whether thou wouldest *keep his commandments*, or no.”__
David even prays *for* it: *prove me*.
[pause]
The petition, then, does not imagine God dangling sin.
It pleads about the furnace-hour of proving — Revelation's __“*hour of temptation*, which shall come upon all the world, to *try* them”__ — the same plea Jesus pressed on His sleeping disciples in Gethsemane: __“Watch and pray, that ye *enter not into temptation*.”__
[beat]
The plea *deliver us from evil* is personal in the Greek — deliver us from the evil *one*: the same wicked one who snatches the sown word, and fathers the tares.
Watch the Name-bearer pray this very petition over a man:

[quote.scripture, Luke 22:31-32]
____
…Simon, Simon, behold, Satan hath desired to have you, that he may sift you as *wheat*:
But *I have prayed for thee*, that thy *faith* fail not: and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren.
____

[pause]
Trial itself is not prayed away — the manna was given to prove.
The plea is to be brought *through* the proving, and out of the destroyer's hand, with the promise standing behind it: God __“will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a *way to escape*.”__
[pause]
Read whole, the prayer Jesus taught is the covenant renewed in miniature: the name hallowed, the kingdom summoned, the will obeyed, the word received daily, the release practiced, the deliverance promised.
Every line of it is prayed *in the name*, because every line of it lives inside the covenant — and its salvation is promised to those who practice what they pray.

[long pause]

Keeping the Name.

[long pause]

How, then, is the name kept full rather than emptied?
By its terms — the law.
Moses binds the two in a single line:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 28:58]
____
If thou wilt not observe to do *all the words of this law* that are written in this book, that thou mayest *fear this glorious and fearful name*, THE LORD THY GOD…
____

[pause]
Keep the law, and you honor the name.
Despise the law, and you empty it.
So at the very end of the story, the people who belong to God are marked by that exact pairing — they that keep the *commandments* of God, and the *faith* of Jesus — with his Father's *name* written in their foreheads.
The covenant owned, and worn where all can see — the *seal* set on God's servants.
Scripture binds the name and the law together, and will not let them be pried apart.

[long pause]

The Counterfeit Name.

[long pause]

In the Bible's symbolic language, many symbols come paired with an opposite — and describing the opposite often sheds light on the meaning of its counterpart.
The name has such an opposite, and it is the oldest counterfeit on record.
[pause]
On the plain of Shinar the builders said: __“let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and *let us make us a name*.”__
Babel — the kingdom whose founding act is a self-made name: an identity manufactured from below, a covenant with no one above.
God's answer stands in the very next chapter, spoken to one man called out of that world: __“I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and *make thy name great*.”__
A made name, or a given one — the whole war of the Bible, in two clauses.
[pause]
The war ends on men's foreheads.
In Revelation, the beast causeth all to receive a mark in their right hand, or in their *foreheads* — the *name of the beast*.
One chapter later, the Lamb's company stands on mount Sion, having his *Father's name* written in their foreheads.
There is no unmarked forehead in that scene.
Everyone wears a name — the one men make for themselves, or the one God gives — and the commandment asks which one you will refuse to carry for nothing.

[long pause]

Called by His Name.

[long pause]

One question remains.
How does a person come to bear a name that is not his own?
Scripture answers with the oldest institution it has — a bride takes her husband's — and Isaiah shows the formula in a single desperate verse:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 4:1]
____
And in that day seven women shall take hold of one man, saying, We will eat our own *bread*, and wear our own *apparel*: only *let us be called by thy name*, to take away our *reproach*.
____

[pause]
To be called by thy name is to be *married* — that is the whole request — and the women's bargain proves it.
The law obliges a husband to supply his wife's food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage — and these seven offer to waive everything but the name itself.
Read in the Bible's symbolic language, the bargain turns chilling.
[beat]
Seven women — seven, the number Scripture gives the assemblies of God's people — asking to wear the Bridegroom's name while eating *their own* bread, and wearing *their own* apparel.
Their own word in place of His; their own righteousness in place of His.
It is the *Lord, Lord* we heard earlier, spoken as a marriage proposal — the name, minus the covenant that fills it.
And the true Husband gives His widowed people exactly the two things these women decline — *food and raiment*: the bread of His word, and the garment of His righteousness; a later chapter opens both in full.

[pause]

The true marriage is enacted through the same covenant mechanics.
When Ruth asks Boaz for marriage, she asks him to spread the corner of his garment over her — and *skirt* there is the Hebrew _kanaph_: the *wing*, the covenant edge.
[beat]
For Ruth is everything the seven women are not, and her story keeps the ledger.
She brings no bread of her own.
She eats at Boaz's table until she was sufficed, and *left*; she gleans his field by the law of the corner, with handfuls *of purpose* dropped in her path; and she is sent home under six measures of barley with the charge, go not *empty*.
And the only raiment the story provides her is the one she asks for.
Boaz had blessed her: __“a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose *wings* thou art come to trust.”__
On the threshing floor she asks him to make good his own blessing in person — the widow's garment laid aside, and one covering requested: his.
[pause]
God describes His own wedding to Israel with the same gesture:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 16:8]
____
Now when I passed by thee, and looked upon thee, behold, thy time was the time of love; and I *spread my skirt* over thee, and covered thy *nakedness*: yea, I sware unto thee, and *entered into a covenant* with thee, saith the Lord GOD, and *thou becamest mine*.
____

[pause]
There the raiment clause is written out in full, in the verses that follow the spread skirt: __“Then washed I thee… I anointed thee… I *clothed thee* also with broidered work… and I covered thee with silk… thy *raiment* was of fine linen… thou didst *eat* fine flour, and honey, and oil.”__
Food and raiment, both from the Husband — exactly the provision the seven women waived.

[pause]

To take the husband's name is to come under the husband's wing — and the wing's corners carry the *tassels*, bound there so that the wearer remembers all the commandments of the LORD, and does them.
The covering a bride enters when she takes the name is stitched at every corner with the reminder of the law of the One who spreads it — and a bleeding woman once took hold of that corner in *faith*, and the name did what no sound can; the Wings chapter tells that story.
The name and the commandments arrive as one garment — which is why the name is kept by keeping the law, and emptied any other way.

[pause]

This grasp is the gesture the nations finally learn.
Isaiah's seven women *take hold* of one man.
Zechariah sees the same verb close on the right thing:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 8:23]
____
…ten men shall *take hold* out of all languages of the nations, even shall take hold of the *skirt* of him that is a Jew, saying, *We will go with you*: for we have heard that *God is with you*.
____

[pause]
The hand closes on the _kanaph_ — the wing-corner where the commandments hang — and the words are Ruth's own, the Moabite bride who said, *whither thou goest, I will go*.

[pause]

Ruth's wedding under the wing leads directly into this chapter's closing point.
The law states the purpose of a kinsman's marriage in name-language.
The son is raised __“that his *name* be not put out of Israel”__ — and the man who refuses is shamed as one who __“will not *build up his brother's house*.”__
In the law's own grammar, building a house and raising a name are one act.
[beat]
So Boaz declares he has taken Ruth __“to *raise up the name of the dead* upon his inheritance.”__
The elders bless her as one come to *build the house*.
And the house that wedding built was the house of *David* — the very house God would answer with a house for His own name.
[pause]
A bride under the wing, a name raised, a house built — and in the fulness of time, a Son of that house who is Himself the temple.
The symbols do not merely repeat; they *compound*.

[pause]

The symbol gathers up the ones before it.
The *gospel* is the covenant *announced*.
The *way*, the *truth*, and the *life* are the covenant *embodied*.
The *name* is the covenant *owned* — taken upon yourself as a bride takes her husband's, to keep or to empty.
Most who cry *Lord, Lord* wear it for nothing.
To bear it in truth is to keep the covenant of the One in whom the name dwells — and so to be *known* by Him in that day.
[beat]
And the name owned is also written — impressed on its bearers like a signet in wax.
Where God sets it, what it says, and what counterfeit stamps the same two places — that is the seal, and it is the Seal chapter.

[long pause]
