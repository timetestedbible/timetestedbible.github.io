Chapter Ten. [beat] The Coin.

[long pause]

Jesus defined this chapter's symbol Himself, in a parable built for the purpose. Let's read Luke 15.

[quote.scripture, Luke 15:8-10, NKJV]
____
Or what woman, having ten silver *coins*, if she loses one coin, does not light a *lamp*, sweep the house, and search carefully until she finds it?
And when she has found it, she calls her friends and neighbors together, saying, “Rejoice with me, for I have found the piece which I lost!”
Likewise, I say to you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over *one sinner who repents*.
____

[pause]
The equation is stated in the last line.
The found coin *is* the repenting sinner — the same celebration that met the prodigal son, told with silver instead of a son.
Identify this symbol correctly, and it unlocks a mystery woven through half a dozen parables.
And, at the far end, it unlocks the wisdom Revelation calls for: the mark of the *beast*, its *image*, its *name*, the *number of its name*, and why no man may buy or sell without them.

[pause]

Decode the parable's own furniture first.
The woman sweeps *the house* — and the lost sheep of this parable's twin are __“the lost sheep of the *house of Israel*.”__ Her ten coins are the house's ten tribes.
She searches by a lit *lamp* — and the lamp, says the proverb, is the commandment.
Its *light* is the commandment lived, as the chapter on Light and Darkness will prove.
By that obedience the coin is found — the lost are sought by the light of the law.

[long pause]

Turn now to the coin of the census.

[long pause]

One coin in the Gospels is named, examined face-up, and ruled on — and the scene opens as a trap.
Let's read Matthew 22.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:15-21, NKJV]
____
Then the Pharisees went and plotted how they might entangle Him in His talk.
And they sent to Him their disciples with the Herodians, saying, “Teacher, we know that You are true, and teach the *way* of God in *truth*; nor do You care about anyone, for You do not regard the *person* of men.
Tell us, therefore, what do You think? Is it lawful to pay *taxes* to Caesar, or not?”
But Jesus perceived their wickedness, and said, “Why do you test Me, you hypocrites?
Show Me the coin of the *census*.” So they brought Him a *denarius*.
And He said to them, “Whose *image* and *inscription* is this?”
They said to Him, “Caesar's.” And He said to them, “*Render* therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's.”
____

[pause]
Where our Bibles say *tax money*, the Greek is literally *the coin of the census* — Rome's registration of persons for the head-tax.
Look at what the coin carries — an image, and a name.
A *beast* is a kingdom.
Daniel says it: __“the fourth beast shall be the fourth *kingdom* upon earth.”__
And a kingdom stamps its money.
Caesar's census coin therefore bears, on its face, the *image* of the beast and the *name* of the beast — the very two things Revelation says the world will be made to carry.
Jesus held the whole question of the mark in His hand, and His ruling was already complete.
Give the beast back its coin.

[pause]

Read without the symbols, __“render unto Caesar”__ is a mirror.
The tax-lover sees a command to pay.
The tax-hater hears that everything is God's.
And the scene shrinks to a clever escape from a trap.
Read with the symbols, the sentence divides the world's whole estate by image and inscription.
Everything stamped by the beast returns to the beast, and everything stamped by God returns to God.
Whose image do *you* bear?
[beat]
God created man in his own *image* — and the *seal* writes His inscription on the forehead.
So what is rendered unto God is the image-bearer himself. Paul says it: __“present your *bodies* a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable unto God, which is your *reasonable service*.”__

[pause]

So the pivot of the chapter is a question the tribute scene forces.
If Caesar has a coin bearing his image, his inscription, struck for his census — what coin bears the *image* of God, the *seal* of God, the *name* of God, and the *number of His name*?
The parable of the woman already answered — we are His coinage.
What follows traces His coin through Scripture — minted, spent, carried in a fish's mouth, counted, and counterfeited.

[long pause]

Every coin begins at the mint.

[long pause]

A coin is the seal made money, and His coin is minted the way all coin is — dug from a field, refined in fire, stamped with the name.
The *field*, Jesus said, is the world — and the treasure hid in it was worth selling everything to buy.
A coin lying loose is simply picked up.
What the buyer found was *ore* — gold scattered through the clay of the field.
Ore is not spent.
It is refined.
The refining is the trial. Job says it: __“he *knoweth* the way that I take — when he hath tried me, I shall come forth as *gold*.”__
Zechariah hears what comes out of the furnace, when the *refined third* is brought through. Listen:

[quote.scripture, Zechariah 13:9]
____
…I will *refine* them as silver is refined, and will try them as *gold* is tried: they shall *call on my name*, and I will hear them: I will say, It is *my people*: and they shall say, The LORD is *my God*.
____

[pause]
Refined, then stamped.
Those who come through the fire call on the Name, and God claims them as His own.
The stamp is the seal the last chapter defined — the Name written on the forehead and the hand, the law sealed in the man.

[long pause]

Now ask what His coin buys.

[long pause]

Caesar's coin moves in Caesar's market.
God's coin has a market of its own, and Scripture posts its prices: __“*Buy* the truth, and *sell it not*; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.”__
Solomon wrote the whole exchange out — and notice, as we read Proverbs 2, what the money is, and where the goods come from.

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 2:3-6]
____
Yea, if thou criest after *knowledge*, and liftest up thy voice for *understanding*; If thou seekest her as *silver*, and searchest for her as for *hid treasures*; Then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God.
For the LORD giveth *wisdom*: out of his *mouth* cometh knowledge and understanding.
____

[pause]
Here is the treasure-hid-in-a-field parable in proverb form, centuries early.
The seeking is priced *as silver*.
The goods are *hid treasures*.
And they issue out of his *mouth*.
The risen Lord still runs the counter: __“I counsel thee to *buy of me* gold *tried in the fire*, that thou mayest be rich.”__
He sells exactly what His mint produces.
[beat]
Mark the vocabulary of this market — silver, treasure, and a *mouth*.
And carry it to the strangest coin-scene in the Gospels.

[long pause]

Next comes the fish that carries it.

[long pause]

At Capernaum the collectors come asking after a very particular coin.
Let's read Matthew 17.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 17:24-27, ASV]
____
And when they were come to Capernaum, they that received the *half-shekel* came to Peter, and said, Doth not your teacher pay the half-shekel? He saith, Yea.
And when he came into the house, Jesus spake first to him, saying, What thinkest thou, Simon? the kings of the earth, from whom do they receive toll or tribute? from their sons, or from strangers?
And when he said, From strangers, Jesus said unto him, Therefore the *sons are free*.
But, lest we cause them to stumble, go thou to the *sea*, and cast a hook, and take up the *fish* that *first cometh up*; and when thou hast opened his *mouth*, thou shalt find a *shekel*: that take, and give unto them *for me and thee*.
____

[pause]
The *fish* is the one symbol even the world still reads.
People put fish on their cars as a sign of their faith — the fish is a believer.
Read the rest of the scene the same way.
Peter, the fisher *of men*, goes to the *sea* — the peoples and nations.
And the *first* fish that comes up out of it carries silver in its *mouth*.
The Son owes no ransom in His Father's house — __“then are the sons free.”__
Yet He pays, and the payment is a parable in miniature.
[beat]
Why a fish, why *first*, and why the *mouth*?
The answers are all in the law the coin was minted under — for the half-shekel the collectors asked for is not a market price.
It is a statute.

[long pause]

Behind the half-shekel stands the census and its ransom.

[long pause]

The law prices persons in silver — not as a figure of speech, but as statute.
Leviticus redeems a man vowed to God __“by thy estimation,”__ in sanctuary shekels.
Whenever Israel is counted, every counted head owes a coin.
Listen to what Moses records:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 30:12, 15]
____
When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel after their number, then shall they give every man *a ransom for his soul* unto the LORD, when thou numberest them; that there be *no plague* among them, when thou numberest them… The *rich* shall not give *more*, and the *poor* shall not give *less* than half a shekel… to make an *atonement* for your souls.
____

[pause]
Every clause carries doctrine.
The ransom is owed *at the counting* — to stand in God's census is to stand ransomed.
The old spiritual asks for exactly this: __“Lord, how I want to be *in that number*, when the saints go marching in.”__
*That number* is the census of the ransomed.
The price is flat — no man's soul outbids another's at that window.
[beat]
The tribute trap opened with this very clause, spoken as flattery — __“You do not regard the *person* of men.”__
And Scripture testifies it of God in the census's own pair. Hear Job:

[quote.scripture, Job 34:19]
____
…that accepteth not the persons of princes, nor regardeth the *rich* more than the *poor*? for they all are the *work of his hands*.
____

[pause]
What was offered as bait was the census price, stated back to its Author — every soul His work, every soul the same half shekel.

[pause]

The money is called a *memorial* — the same word the law uses for the sign bound between the eyes, on the forehead.
And it is not banked, but appointed for the service of the tabernacle.
Appointed how? Exodus records it to the shekel.
Of the hundred talents of silver were *cast the sockets of the sanctuary* — a talent for a socket.
A half-shekel a head, for 603,550 counted men, melted into the foundations.
*God's house stood on the ransom-money of His counted people.*
The house has never changed its engineering. Peter writes: __“ye also, as *lively stones*, are built up a *spiritual house*.”__
And the *stone* is covenant *rock* — a temple of covenant-people, socketed in redemption silver.

[pause]

The census began at the door in Egypt.
The first ransom of the firstborn was the Passover lamb — the firstborn under the marked doors were *bought* with its blood.
And Israel marched out on the *fifteenth* day of the first month.
On the morrow — the *sixteenth*, the day of firstfruits, the very day of the year the Firstborn would rise as the *firstfruits* of them that slept — God stated His claim.
Moses records it:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 13:2]
____
Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever *openeth the womb* among the children of Israel, both of man and of beast: it is *mine*.
____

[pause]
The womb the law watched opens again in Revelation.
The travailing woman __“brought forth a *man child*… and her child was *caught up unto God*, and to his throne”__ — the dragon standing before her to devour him, as Egypt's king stood before the firstborn.
Every redeemed firstborn is a rehearsal of the *firstborn* of every creature.
Under the claim, every firstborn thereafter, *shalt thou redeem* — and the redemption travels under a sign with three locations.
Moses continues:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 13:9]
____
And it shall be for a *sign* unto thee upon thine *hand*, and for a *memorial* between thine *eyes*, that the LORD'S law may be in thy *mouth*: for with a strong hand hath the LORD brought thee out of Egypt.
____

[pause]
There they are — hand, eyes, mouth.
When the claimed were redeemed by silver, the count was a census of *names*: __“Number all the firstborn… take the *number of their names*.”__
There were 22,273 of them, exact to the man where the army musters are round.
And the overage above the Levites' count was ransomed at five shekels a head.
God counts what He redeems by name — and the price attaches to the name counted.

[pause]

Now the fish gives up its riddle.
The redemption sign's third location is the *mouth* — that the LORD's law may be in thy mouth.
And the seal-texts keep the word there.
Moses says: __“the word is very nigh unto thee, *in thy mouth*, and in thy heart.”__
Paul says: __“*confess with thy mouth* the Lord Jesus.”__
A man wears the seal on his forehead.
A fish carries it in its mouth — on a fish they are the same place.
The first man drawn up out of the sea of nations comes up with the ransom-witness in his mouth.
The caught man carries his confession — and the confession is his redemption.
[beat]
The coin itself is exact.
A stater — one full shekel — is precisely *two* half-shekel ransoms, __“for me and thee.”__
The Hebrew Gospel seals the grammar.
The coin is given _be'ad_ us — *on behalf of* us — the very preposition of the atonement.
And Aaron shall make atonement *for* himself, and *for* his house.
The substitution is in the preposition — one pays, another is covered.

[long pause]

All of this converges on the mark of the beast.

[long pause]

Now the piece this symbol unlocks.
Revelation holds two enrollments, and nearly every reader studies the beast's *mark* in isolation. Few ever suspect the mark is an enrollment at all.
Set the two side by side instead.
God counts first. Let's read Revelation 7.

[quote.scripture, Revelation 7:4]
____
And I heard the *number* of them which were *sealed*: and there were sealed *144,000* of all the tribes of the children of Israel.
____

[pause]
This is a census, taken tribe by tribe like Sinai's, of servants sealed in their foreheads before the *four winds* blow.
The Hebrew of the count carries the firstborn census inside it.
The count reads 144 _alaphim_ — thousands.
And the consonants of *thousands* are the consonants of _alluphim_ — *chiefs*, *firsts*.
Revelation names the sealed exactly that — the *firstfruits* unto God and to the Lamb.
God's last census, like His first, is a census of firstborn.
Then the beast counts. Listen to Revelation 13.

[quote.scripture, Revelation 13:16-18]
____
And he causeth *all*, both small and great, *rich and poor*, free and bond, to receive a *mark* in their right hand, or in their foreheads: And that no man might *buy or sell*, save he that had the mark, or the *name* of the beast, or the *number of his name*… Let him that hath understanding *count the number* of the beast: for it is the number of a man…
____

[pause]
The mark of the beast is the counterfeit of the temple census, member for member.
Its *name* sits where the *Name* goes.
Its *number* answers the number of the sealed.
Its enrollment is universal where God's is *of all the tribes*.
And where God's census collects a ransom-coin for the sanctuary, the beast's census issues its coin-mark for the market — *no man might buy or sell* without it.

[pause]

Even the word is mint vocabulary.
The Greek for *mark* is _charagma_ — an impression struck or engraved — the die-strike.
And its only other use in the New Testament is coin-and-idol language: the Godhead falsely imaged in __“gold, or silver… *graven* by art and man's device.”__
The man who takes the mark is *minted* — the beast's image and inscription, struck on flesh.
The census coin, paid in persons.

[pause]

The counterfeit even keeps the ransom's equality clause.
Where the census took the same half shekel of the *rich* and the *poor*, the mark falls on *rich and poor* — with *small and great, free and bond* stacked beside them.
And the Hebrew Revelation reads *the wealthy and the poor* outright.
The beast's census, like God's, is no respecter of persons.
The counterfeit keeps even that clause.
[beat]
Even the registers face each other in the same chapter — the beast's rolls, against the *book of life* of the Lamb.
It is the register of which the psalm says, __“The LORD shall *count*, when he *writeth up* the people.”__

[pause]

The plague-clause inverts with the rest.
The ransom was paid *that there be no plague among them*, when thou numberest them.
The tormentors of the trumpets touch only those men which have *not* the seal of God in their foreheads.
And the first vial falls upon the men which had the *mark of the beast*.
God's counted take no plague.
Babylon's counted are the plague's named address.
Nor can Babylon's market starve the sealed, for the other market never closed. Hear Isaiah:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 55:1]
____
Ho, every one that thirsteth, come ye to the waters, and he that hath *no money*; come ye, *buy*, and eat; yea, come, buy wine and milk *without money* and without price.
____

[pause]
The beast's enrollment is older than Rome.
Its founding charter was spoken on the plain of Shinar.
Listen to the builders at Babel:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 11:4]
____
Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us *make us a name*, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth.
____

[pause]
Babel opened a counter-covenant — a name of man's own minting, for every man to be enrolled under.
The reason the marked cannot be saved is now plain grammar.
They are counted under the wrong name — and there is none other *name* under heaven given among men, whereby we *must be saved*.
Two names, two censuses, two coinages — no man is enrolled in both.

[long pause]

One riddle remains — the number of one man.

[long pause]

Let him that hath understanding *count* the number of the beast — for it is the number of a man.
The Hebrew Revelation reads *one man* — _adam echad_.
Most reach for gematria here, as though Revelation were asking for another candidate whose letters add to 666.
This chapter's claim is different.
Revelation thirteen is not first a gematria problem.
It is a covenant census problem.

[pause]

The words themselves vote that way.
The Greek verb is _psēphizō_ — to tally with a _psēphos_, the pebble ancient counting and voting were done with.
And its only other use in Scripture is a cost-tally: __“which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and *counteth the cost*?”__
John hands the overcomer the very object, four chapters earlier: __“I will give him a *white stone*, and in the stone a *new name written*, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it.”__
And his word is not _lithos_, a rock, but _psēphos_, the counting-pebble.
A psēphos is a vote, and an enrollment-token.
Paul cast one against the saints — __“I gave my *voice* against them.”__
And Matthias was *numbered* with the eleven by one.

[pause]

The Hebrew Revelation names what the white one holds.
The overcomer receives a *good testimony*, and in this testimony a *new name* written — God's vote, cast *for* a man.
It is his sealed entry in the census of the sealed.
The mark is the vote against, carried on the flesh.
The Hebrew's counting verb agrees.
The verb is _chashav_ — the ledger-reckoning of Abraham's __“counted it to him for *righteousness*,”__ and of the law's redemption-price arithmetic.
And its text finishes the instruction as a tally — you shall find the *sum*.

[pause]

Scripture uses the number itself twice — once as money, once as a census.
These are the two things the beast's enrollment controls.
The number 666 is a *money* figure. Consider First Kings 10.

[quote.scripture, 1 Kings 10:14]
____
Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in *one year* was *666 talents* of gold…
____

[pause]
The revenue lands at the very turn where the king began multiplying gold — against Deuteronomy's own law of the king.
It is also a *census* figure, and the roll shows the format of a number-of-a-name exactly. Consider Ezra:

[quote.scripture, Ezra 2:13]
____
The children of *Adonikam*, *666*.
____

[pause]
A count filed under the name that owns it, in the register of those *coming out of Babylon*.
A number of a name is a census entry, and Revelation keeps both ledgers in that grammar.
__“I heard the *number* of them which were sealed”__ stands against the number of *its* name.
And the victors of the sea of glass overcome the beast, his mark, and the *number of his name* — the enrollment itself, refused.

[pause]

So the instruction *count the number* asks for arithmetic, not alphabet-magic.
A census is a thing you *count* — the redeemed, against the enrolled.
The Remnant chapter, still ahead, runs that count down 15 converging lines of evidence, and they settle near 2 persons in 1,000 — beside 1 in 666, which is 1.5 in 1,000.
The wisdom the verse calls for is the verdict the count returns — *few* there be that find it.
How few are ransomed, and how broad the way of the enrolled.
Every man ends in one census or the other — counted and ransomed, or counted and marked.

[pause]

Hosea acted the ransomed side out in one verse.
Commanded to love an adulteress __“according to the love of the LORD toward the children of Israel,”__ he went to market for his own wife: __“So I *bought her* to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for an homer of barley.”__
There is the Husband, paying coin to redeem the wife who left him.
[beat]
The *marriage* itself — what joins it, what can break it, and how far the Husband went to keep it — is the next chapter.

[long pause]
