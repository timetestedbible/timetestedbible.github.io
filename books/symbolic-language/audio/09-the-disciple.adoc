MEAT The Bible's Symbolic Language. [beat] Chapter Nine: The Disciple.

[long pause]

Isaiah, chapter 8: __“Bind up the testimony, *seal the law* among my *disciples*.”__

[long pause]

The gospel's last command is to *disciple* the nations, and everyone thinks he knows what the word means: a follower, a learner, a student of Jesus.
The dictionary is not wrong; it is empty.
A learner of *what*?
[beat]
Scripture does not leave the answer open.
It defines the word exactly once, and it puts the definition in the mouth of the law itself:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 8:16]
____
Bind up the testimony, *seal the law* among my *disciples*.
____

[pause]
The Old Testament's word for disciples is _limmudim_ — the taught ones — and it appears here in the law's own custody clause.
A disciple is where the law is kept when the testimony is bound: the scroll sealed in a living person.
Isaiah uses the word once more, as a promise: __“all thy children shall be *taught of the LORD*”__ — the LORD's own _limmudim_, His own disciples — and Jesus quotes that very line for His hearers.

[long pause]

First, Jesus sets the gates of discipleship.

[long pause]

Jesus did not describe discipleship as a mood of admiration.
He stated its terms, and three times He said *cannot*.
[beat]
__“If any man come to me, and *hate not* his father, and mother… he *cannot* be my disciple.”__
[beat]
__“Whosoever doth not *bear his cross*, and come after me, *cannot* be my disciple.”__
[beat]
__“Whosoever he be of you that *forsaketh not all* that he hath, he *cannot* be my disciple.”__
[pause]
These are gates, not growth curves — and inside the gates He named the marks.
__“If ye *continue in my word*, then are ye my disciples *indeed*.”__
__“By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have *love* one to another”__ — and love is the keeping of the commandments.
__“Herein is my Father glorified, that ye bear much *fruit*; so shall ye be my disciples”__ — and fruit is works.
[beat]
_Indeed_ is the word to weigh: it is spoken to men who had just *believed* — believed as a profession believes: His word accounted factual, and worth trusting.
An assent even the devils manage — they *believe, and tremble*.
Scripture's full *belief* walks.
He that *believeth* on the Son hath eternal life; but he that *obeyeth not* the Son shall not see life.
One verse — and the opposite of believing is disobeying.
So He hands the professors the walking itself as the test: continue in the word — *then are ye my disciples indeed*.

[long pause]

Baptized into the Name.

[long pause]

The commission binds the entry rite to the definition: __“baptizing them *in the name* of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost.”__
The Name is the covenant identity of the house, carrying the Head's Word, covenant law, and fruitful priesthood — and baptism places the disciple *into* it.
__“Buried with him by baptism… even so we also should *walk* in newness of life.”__
__“As many of you as have been baptized into Christ have *put on* Christ”__ — the covenant worn as a *garment*.
The covenant one is baptized into has stated content:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 31:33]
____
this shall be the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel… I will put my *law* in their inward parts, and *write it in their hearts*.
____

[pause]
The new covenant is not the law's retirement; it is the law's change of address — from tables of stone to the tables of the heart.
Jeremiah's promise and Isaiah's seal are one act described twice: the law, written within.
To be baptized into the Name is to enter that house and covenant — the heart handed in as the next table to be written.

[long pause]

As His Master.

[long pause]

The church has a word for the goal of discipleship — *Christlikeness* — and the word is correct; it has simply been left undefined.
Jesus defined it:

[quote.scripture, Luke 6:40]
____
The disciple is not above his master: but every one that is *perfect* shall be *as his master*.
____

[pause]
The disciple's finish line is likeness.
Then everything hangs on one question: what is the Master like?
[beat]
He is the One of whom the Father said, __“obey his voice… for *my name is in him*.”__
The One who kept His own in that name.
And He set the pattern in a single sentence:

[quote.scripture, John 15:10]
____
If ye *keep my commandments*, ye shall abide in my love; even as *I have kept my Father's commandments*, and abide in his love.
____

[pause]
He kept the law — perfectly. __“Which of you convinceth me of sin?”__ [beat] Not to destroy, but to fulfil.
To become like Him is therefore not a sentiment; it has exact content: __“He that saith he abideth in him ought himself also *so to walk, even as he walked*.”__
He walked in the commandments.
Christlikeness *is* the law sealed within — the church's own word, given the Master's own content.

[long pause]

Becoming as the master is a warning before it is a promise — so everything turns on one question: whose works?

[long pause]

Jesus hung the sentence on a question about guides: __“Can the blind lead the blind? shall they not both fall into the ditch?”__
[beat]
Israel had other masters, with disciples of their own — and He split their authority down the middle. Hear Matthew 23:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 23:2-3]
____
The scribes and the Pharisees sit in *Moses' seat*: all therefore whatsoever they *bid you observe*, that observe and do; but do not ye after their *works*: for they say, and do not.
____

[pause]
What they relay from Moses' seat is commanded — observe and do.
Their own *works* are refused in the same breath, for a work is a deed executing a command, and their deeds executed their own legislation.
[beat]
The Hebrew Matthew reads the seeming concession out of the verse — its verb is singular: __“all that *he* says to you, keep and do; but according to their *ordinances* and their deeds, do not do”__ — Moses speaking from the seat, and both columns of the refusal theirs.
__“They say, and do not”__ falls wholly on the men in the seat.
__“Their deeds”__ — _ma'aseihem_, their *works* — stand written beside their ordinances as the deeds that execute them.
[beat]
He read the charge to their face: __“Why do ye also *transgress the commandment of God* by your *tradition*?… in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the *commandments of men*.”__
The Hebrew again writes the legal terms into the verse: your _taqqanot_, your *enactments*, and a *commandment of men* learned by rote.
[beat]
The same scene ends in the ditch: __“they be *blind leaders of the blind*. And if the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch”__ — masters whose disciples, perfected, are as they are.

[pause]
The prophets had already given this fork its legal name.
Micah's indictment of Israel names the crime as keeping — the covenant's own verb — sworn to the wrong legislator.
Listen to Micah:

[quote.scripture, Micah 6:16]
____
For the *statutes of Omri* are *kept*, and all the *works of the house of Ahab*, and ye walk in their counsels; that I should make thee a desolation…
____

[pause]
*Kept* is _shamar_, the verb of keeping the commandments, here spent on a king's statutes.
Evil works are another legislator's statutes kept.
[beat]
Jehoshaphat is the bright mirror: he __“walked in his *commandments*, and not after the *doings* of Israel”__ — one nation, two legislators, and every deed executing one or the other.

[pause]
Moses legislated the audit in advance: the prophet whose sign comes to pass, but who teaches departure from the commandments, is refused — __“ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and *keep his commandments*.”__
[beat]
The judgment scene runs on that statute.
The pleaders of __“many *powers*”__ claim, in the Greek, _dynameis_, and in the Hebrew Gospel _otot_ — *signs*.
No works are pleaded, and works are the verdict: __“depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*”__ — which is a psalm of David, word for word: __“depart from me, all ye *workers of iniquity*.”__
[beat]
Signs prove power; works prove whose command — and whose command, whose disciple.

[long pause]

The Name on the Forehead.

[long pause]

The road that begins in baptism ends where the next chapter — the Seal — ends.
John saw it: __“a Lamb stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having *his Father's name written in their foreheads*.”__
And to the overcomer: __“I will *write upon him the name* of my God.”__
[pause]
Baptized into the Name. Perfected as the Master, in whom the Name is. Sealed with the Name at the last.
[beat]
The disciple's whole arc is one Name carried from water to forehead — and the Name carries the law.
Seal the law among my disciples — the commission, Isaiah's clause, and the 144,000 are one sentence spoken three times.

[long pause]
