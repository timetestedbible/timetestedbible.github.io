Seven times Jesus ends a judgment scene with the same words, and the English hides a detail the Greek insists on.
He does not say there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, as though describing a noise; He says there shall be *the* weeping and *the* gnashing of teeth — definite articles, a known and fixed scene His hearers were expected to recognize.
Luke shows us the scene whole:

[quote.scripture, Luke 13:28]
____
There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, *when ye shall see* Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God, and *you yourselves thrust out*.
____

The weeping and the gnashing are not the soundtrack of a torture chamber.
They happen *at a sight* — the kingdom opened, the patriarchs seated, and the claimants outside looking in.

[discrete]
=== The Psalm That Wrote the Scene

Scripture composed this scene long before the Gospels quoted it, and it runs element for element:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 112:10]
____
The wicked shall *see* it, and be *grieved*; he shall *gnash with his teeth*, and *melt away*: the desire of the wicked shall perish.
____

He sees; he grieves — there is the weeping; he gnashes; and he melts away.
The psalm devotes its first nine verses to what the wicked man is looking at: the man who fears the LORD and delights in His commandments, blessed, established, exalted with honor.
The gnashing is the wicked man's answer to *that* — and its end is not an endless scream but a dissolution: __“the desire of the wicked shall perish.”__

[discrete]
=== What Gnashing Is

Run every occurrence of the Hebrew word, and one definition stands in all of them: gnashing is what the wicked do *at the righteous*.

[quote.scripture, Psalm 37:12]
____
The wicked plotteth against the just, and gnasheth upon him with his teeth.
____

Job's enemy __“gnasheth upon me with his teeth”__ (Job 16:9); the __“hypocritical mockers”__ of the feasts __“gnashed upon me with their teeth”__ (Psalm 35:16); Jerusalem's enemies __“hiss and gnash the teeth”__ over her fall (Lamentations 2:16).
Never once is it the sound of pain.
It is the face of hatred — teeth ground at someone else's standing.

The New Testament stages it in its own narrative, at the trial of Stephen:

[quote.scripture, Acts 7:54-56]
____
When they heard these things, they were cut to the heart, and they *gnashed on him with their teeth*… But he, being full of the Holy Ghost, looked up stedfastly into heaven, and *saw the glory of God*, and Jesus standing on the right hand of God.
____

Hold the two halves of that scene together: the council gnashing, the righteous man seeing the kingdom opened.
It is Luke 13:28 rehearsed in history with the roles' endings not yet dealt — and at the end of the age the same scene stands final, with the sight given to the ones outside.

[discrete]
=== Who Receives the Sentence

Across the seven texts, the one cast out is never the heathen:

* __“the *children of the kingdom* shall be cast out”__ (Matthew 8:12)
* the tares — __“all things that offend, and them which do *lawlessness*”__ — gathered *out of His kingdom* (Matthew 13:41-42)
* the bad fish, severed __“from among the *just*”__ (Matthew 13:49-50)
* the wedding *guest* without the garment (Matthew 22:13)
* the *evil servant*, appointed __“his portion with the *hypocrites*”__ (Matthew 24:51)
* the *unprofitable servant* (Matthew 25:30)
* the ones who __“have eaten and drunk in thy presence”__ (Luke 13:26-28)

Every one is an insider — a claimant of the house, a servant of the master, a guest at the wedding.
And Matthew 24:51 closes a loop the psalms opened: the evil servant's portion is __“with the hypocrites”__ — and Psalm 35:16's gnashers are the __“*hypocritical* mockers.”__
The company he joins is the company that gnashes.

[discrete]
=== The Hebrew Witness

The Hebrew Matthew carries the formula in the Old Testament's own vocabulary — the gnashing is _charaq_, the very root of Psalm 112:10 — and it names the destination without the English fog:

* __“outer darkness”__ in Matthew 8:12 reads *the dark places of Gehinnom*
* in Matthew 22:13 it reads *lowest Sheol* — the _tachtit_ of Deuteronomy 32:22, where the fire of His anger burns down to the grave
* in Matthew 25:30, *the dark places of the depths*

Outer darkness, the furnace of fire, the portion with the hypocrites, sym:sym-gehenna[Gehinnom], lowest sym:sym-hell[Sheol] — five names, one scene, and every Hebrew name is the vocabulary of the grave.
The witness agrees with the psalm's ending: the gnasher *melts away*.

[discrete]
=== The Reading

The weeping is grief over the loss; the gnashing is rage at the blessed — sorrow for self and hatred of the righteous in one face.
The formula does not describe torments inflicted on the excluded; it describes the excluded *unchanged* — confronted with the kingdom they claimed, they do not fall down and repent; they grind their teeth at the ones inside, as the council ground theirs at Stephen.
The gnashing is the evidence read into the verdict: the exclusion is just, and the sentence of Psalm 112 completes it — they melt away, and the desire of the wicked perishes.
