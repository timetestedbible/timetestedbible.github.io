Four words carry most of what people mean by being right with God: to _know_ Him, to _love_ Him, to have _faith_, to _believe_.
And the dictionary has quietly emptied all four.
Knowledge becomes information; love becomes a feeling; faith and belief become assent — things held in the head and the heart, invisible, asking nothing of the hands.
By those definitions a man may know, love, believe, and have faith, and keep not one commandment.

Scripture will not allow it.
It defines all four as the same thing, and that thing is trusting obedience.

[discrete]
=== Love

Begin where the confusion is thickest.
Love, we are told, is what you feel.
Scripture says love is what you do:

[quote.scripture, 1 John 5:3]
____
For this is the *love of God, that we keep his commandments*: and his commandments are not grievous.
____

Jesus says it without remainder: __“If ye love me, keep my commandments”__ (John 14:15).
And John's second epistle runs the whole chain in three verses, and swears none of it is new:

[quote.scripture, "2 John 4-6"]
____
I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children *walking in truth*, as we have received a *commandment* from the Father… not as though I wrote a *new commandment* unto thee, but that which we had *from the beginning*, that we love one another. And *this is love, that we walk after his commandments*…
____

Walking in sym:sym-truth[truth] and walking after His commandments are one walk — for __“thy law is the truth”__ (Psalm 119:142) — and the commandment that defines love is expressly *not new*: it is __“from the beginning.”__
Nor was it new when Moses wrote it: loving Him is __“to keep the commandments of the LORD”__ (Deuteronomy 6:5; 10:12-13).
Love is not a warmth toward God; it is obedience to God.
The feeling may come and go; the keeping is the love.

[discrete]
=== The Love Chapter, Read with the Symbols

Paul's hymn to love is quoted at weddings as if it were a mood; read it with the symbols and it is a legal argument.

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 13:1-3, NKJV]
____
Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not *love*, I have become *sounding brass* or a *clanging cymbal*.
And though I have the gift of prophecy, and sym:sym-understanding[understand] all mysteries and all sym:sym-knowing[knowledge], and though I have all sym:sym-faith[faith], so that I could remove sym:sym-mountain[mountains], but have not love, I am *nothing*.
And though I bestow all my goods to feed the sym:sym-poor[poor], and though I give my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me *nothing*.
____

Tongues, prophecy, mysteries, knowledge, sym:sym-mountain[mountain]-moving faith — every prized gift in the assembly, weighed and found to be *noise* without love.
And this chapter has already defined the love that is missing: __“this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments”__ (1 John 5:3).
Read it so, and the audit is exact: gifts without obedience are a cymbal — sound with nothing inside.

The catalog that follows is the law in motion.
__“Love *does not envy*”__ — the tenth commandment kept; __“does not seek its own… thinks no *evil*”__ — no ill worked to a neighbor; __“does not rejoice in *lawlessness*, but rejoices in the sym:sym-truth[truth]”__ — and __“thy *law* is the truth”__ (Psalm 119:142).
Paul is not decorating; he is itemizing: __“Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the *fulfilling of the law*”__ (Romans 13:10) — and __“the *purpose of the commandment* is love from a pure heart”__ (1 Timothy 1:5, NKJV).
The commandment's own goal is love; love's own shape is the commandments.

Then the hymn turns prophetic:

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 13:8, 10, 12, NKJV]
____
Love *never ends*. But whether there are prophecies, they will fail; whether there are tongues, they will cease; whether there is knowledge, it will vanish away…
But when *that which is perfect* has come, then that which is in part will be done away…
For now we see *in a mirror, dimly*, but then *face to face*. Now I sym:sym-knowing[know] in part, but then I shall know just as I also am sym:sym-knowing[*known*].
____

James supplies the glass: the hearer who is not a doer is __“a man beholding his natural face *in a glass*”__ — and the doer looks __“into the *perfect law* of sym:sym-liberty[liberty]”__ (James 1:23-25).
The mirror we see dimly in is the law; what it shows a man is his own face.
Now the reflection is dim and partial; the day the *perfect* comes, the mediated image gives way to __“face to face”__ — the phrase of Moses, with whom God spoke __“face to face, even apparently, and not in dark speeches”__ (Numbers 12:8).
And mark where the hymn lands: __“I shall *know* just as I also am *known*”__ — the chapter on love ends in the covenant word this chapter opened with.
The gifts are scaffolding and come down; love — the commandments kept — __“never ends,”__ because the law it fulfills does not pass away.

[discrete]
=== Knowing

To _know_ God, we assume, is to know about Him — facts, doctrine, a sound creed.
But Jesus turns away people whose facts are exactly right:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:23]
____
And then will I profess unto them, *I never knew you*: depart from me, ye that *work iniquity*.
____

He does not say _you never knew me_; He says _I never knew you_ — and the disqualifier is __“iniquity,”__ lawlessness.
To be unknown by God is to be what Scripture calls the one not known — a sym:sym-stranger[stranger]; so the whole sentence reads __“I never knew you: depart from me”__ — put out of the household like a man who was never of it.

John gives the test plainly:

[quote.scripture, 1 John 2:3-4]
____
And hereby we do know that we *know him, if we keep his commandments*.
He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a *liar*, and the sym:sym-truth[truth] is not in him.
____

To know God is a covenant bond proven by obedience — the same sense in which God says of Israel, __“You only have I known of all the families of the earth”__ (Amos 3:2).
Knowing is not information held; it is a relationship kept.
__“Adam *knew* Eve his wife; and she *conceived*”__ (Genesis 4:1) — knowing is the union that bears sym:sym-fruit[fruit]; and Hosea hangs a nation's death on its loss: __“My people are destroyed for lack of sym:sym-knowing[knowledge]… seeing thou hast forgotten the *law* of thy God”__ (Hosea 4:6).

Paul says it with a knife: __“They profess that they *know God*; but in *works* they *deny him*”__ (Titus 1:16).
Hosea mourns the same equation from the ruins: __“My people are destroyed for *lack of knowledge*… seeing thou hast *forgotten the law* of thy God”__ (Hosea 4:6) — the knowledge lost was not information; it was the law.
And God defines the knowing by a king’s life: he __“sym:sym-judgment[judged] the cause of the sym:sym-poor[poor] and needy… *was not this to know me?* saith the LORD”__ (Jeremiah 22:16).

And the relationship it names runs deeper than acquaintance.
The first time Scripture uses the word of two persons it means the union of marriage — __“Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived”__ (Genesis 4:1).
To _know_, at root, is the intimate joining of husband and wife; which is why God calls His covenant a sym:sym-marriage[marriage] and His people a bride.

To know God is to be joined to Him as a wife to her husband — and that union keeps an order: __“the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church”__ (Ephesians 5:23), the bride subject to her Head __“in every thing”__ (Ephesians 5:24).
So the intimacy and the obedience are not two things: to know Him is to be His, and to be His is to obey the Head.
The ones He never knew were never joined to Him — they said __“Lord”__ but were no bride of His.

Put the symbols together and the Shepherd says it whole. __“My sym:sym-sheep[sheep] hear my sym:sym-voice[voice]… and they follow me”__ (John 10:27): the sheep are His people, the voice is His words, and to know that voice is to follow it.
And they follow no other — __“a sym:sym-stranger[stranger] will they not follow… for they know not the sym:sym-voice[voice] of strangers”__ (John 10:5): the stranger is the one outside the covenant, so His sheep will not heed the voice, the teaching, of those outside it. __“He calleth his own sym:sym-sheep[sheep] by sym:sym-name[name]”__ (John 10:3), and the sym:sym-name[name] — a symbol whose own chapter is just ahead — will turn out to be the covenant itself.
And to the one who overcomes He gives __“a sym:sym-new[new] sym:sym-name[name] written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it”__ (Revelation 2:17) — _known_ only by the one who receives and keeps it.
Sheep, voice, name, stranger, knowing: each word in its place, and not one of them a thing held only in the head.

[discrete]
=== Faith

Faith, we are taught, is the one thing that needs no works.
James answers head-on:

[quote.scripture, James 2:17]
____
Even so faith, if it hath not works, is *dead*, being alone.
____

He presses it: __“the devils also believe, and tremble”__ (James 2:19) — perfect assent, and it saves none of them.
The faith Scripture honors always moves: __“By faith Abraham… obeyed”__ (Hebrews 11:8).

The Hebrew behind _faith_ is _emunah_, faithfulness — steadiness, reliability — so __“the just shall live by his faith”__ (Habakkuk 2:4) is to live by one’s faithfulness.
The word’s first appearance in Scripture is a picture: Moses’ hands, held up over the battle, __“were *steady*”__ — _emunah_ — __“until the going down of the sun”__ (Exodus 17:12): faithfulness is hands that do not drop.
Faith is not a conviction lodged in the mind; it is the trust that obeys.

[discrete]
=== Belief

Which leaves _believe_, the word on which whole systems are built — and here the ground is already broken: the Gospel chapter caught Paul trading __“obeyed”__ for __“believed”__ in a single breath (Romans 10:16), and pointed at the Greek of John 3:36.
Now read that famous verse whole:

[quote.scripture, John 3:36, ASV]
____
He that *believeth* on the Son hath eternal sym:sym-life[life]; but he that *obeyeth not* the Son shall not see life…
____

The opposite of believing is not doubting; it is disobeying — for belief that is real shows in what a man does.
Economists have a name for the gap: people name grand sums in surveys and spend far less in fact — _hypothetical bias_, the distance between a poll that costs nothing and a choice that costs something.footnote:fn06hausman[Jerry A. Hausman, “Contingent Valuation: From Dubious to Hopeless,” _Journal of Economic Perspectives_ 26, no. 4 (2012): 43–56.]

Scripture keeps the pair everywhere: Israel could not enter the land for __“unbelief”__ — they __“believed not”__ — yet the sin named is disobedience (Hebrews 3:18).
And Abraham, who __“believed in the LORD”__ (Genesis 15:6), is shown to have been __“justified by works, when he had offered Isaac”__ (James 2:21) — the believing and the obeying are one act.

So the four words collapse into one.
To love God is to keep His commandments; to know Him is to keep them; faith obeys because it *trusts* Him; belief obeys because it *knows His word to be true*.
Four doors, one house: the grounds differ — intimacy, trust, conviction, devotion — but every door opens onto the same obedience.
The dictionary pulled them apart and made each one invisible, so a man could claim all four and live as he pleased.
Scripture binds them back together and sets them where they can be seen — in a life that does what God said.

See, then, what the symbols do to the simplest promise of all.

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:13]
____
For whosoever shall *call upon* the sym:sym-name[name] of the Lord shall be *saved*.
____

It is heard as a sentence you say — profess the name, and salvation is done.
But the sym:sym-name[name] is the covenant — the Name chapter, just ahead, will prove it — and we have just seen what it is to _know_ it: the sheep that hear the voice and follow, the bride joined to her Head, the one who receives the new name and keeps it.
To _call upon the name_ is to take up that covenant and live in it — not to pronounce a word, but to belong to the One who bears it.
The many who only say __“Lord, Lord”__ called the name; they never called upon it, and they hear __“I never knew you”__ (Matthew 7:22-23).

This is what knowing the symbolic language does, and why it changes everything: the verse you thought you had mastered turns out to ask for your whole life.
