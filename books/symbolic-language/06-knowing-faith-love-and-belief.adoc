Four words carry most of what people mean by being right with God: to _love_ Him, to _know_ Him, to have _faith_, to _believe_.
The dictionary has quietly emptied all four.
Knowledge becomes information; love becomes a feeling; faith and belief become assent — things held in the head and the heart, invisible, asking nothing of the hands.
By those definitions a man may know, love, believe, and have faith, and keep not one commandment.

Scripture will not allow it.
All four land on one act — the commandments kept — and each holds a distinct office:

.One act, four offices — and the word for holding them
[.prevalence-table%unbreakable, frame=topbot, grid=none, cols="2,7"]
|===
| *Love* | the keeping itself
| *Knowing* | keeps them — as one wed to the Lawgiver
| *Faith* | keeps them — in hope of the promise
| *Belief* | keeps them — because they are the sym:sym-truth[truth]
| *Faithful* | love kept *consistent* — over time, under weight
|===

This chapter proves each office in turn.

[discrete]
=== Love

Begin where the confusion is thickest.
Love, we are told, is what you feel.
Scripture says love is what you do:

[quote.scripture, 1 John 5:3]
____
For this is the *love of God, that we keep his commandments*: and his commandments are not grievous.
____

Jesus says it without remainder: __“If ye love me, keep my commandments”__ (John 14:15).
And John's second epistle runs the whole chain in three verses, and swears none of it is new:

[quote.scripture, "2 John 4-6"]
____
I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children *walking in* sym:sym-truth[truth], as we have received a *commandment* from the Father… not as though I wrote a *new commandment* unto thee, but that which we had *from the beginning*, that we love one another. And *this is love, that we walk after his commandments*…
____

Walking in sym:sym-truth[truth] and walking after His commandments are one walk — for __“thy law is the truth”__ (Psalm 119:142) — and the commandment that defines love is expressly *not new*: it is __“from the beginning”__ — _archē_, the very word that opens Genesis in the Greek Bible, and John's gospel after it.
The commandment to love is as old as creation.
Nor was it new when Moses wrote it: loving Him is __“to keep the commandments of the LORD”__ (Deuteronomy 6:5; 10:12-13).
Love is not a warmth toward God; it is obedience to God.
The feeling may come and go; the keeping is the love.

[discrete]
=== The Love Chapter, Read with the Symbols

Jesus was asked which commandment is greatest, and His answer hangs the whole canon on love:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 22:37-40, NKJV]
____
“You shall *love* the LORD your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.” This is the first and great *commandment*.
And the second is like it: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.”
On these two commandments *hang all the Law and the Prophets*.
____

Two loves, two tables of the same stone: the first four commandments are how one loves God; the last six are how one loves a neighbor.
The Law does not compete with love; it *hangs from it* — and love without the commandments is a hook with nothing on it.

Paul's hymn to love is quoted at weddings as if it were a mood; read it with the symbols and it is a legal argument.

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 13:1-3, NKJV]
____
Though I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not *love*, I have become *sounding brass* or a *clanging cymbal*.
And though I have the gift of prophecy, and sym:sym-understanding[understand] all mysteries and all sym:sym-knowing[knowledge], and though I have all sym:sym-faith[faith], so that I could remove sym:sym-mountain[mountains], but have not love, I am *nothing*.
And though I bestow all my goods to feed the sym:sym-poor[poor], and though I give my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me *nothing*.
____

Tongues, prophecy, mysteries, knowledge, sym:sym-mountain[mountain]-moving faith — every prized gift in the assembly, weighed and found to be *noise* without love.
This chapter has already defined the love that is missing: __“this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments”__ (1 John 5:3).
Read it so, and the audit is exact: gifts without obedience are a cymbal — sound with nothing inside.

Jesus ran the same audit on the same inventory, and passed the same sentence: __“have we not *prophesied* in thy name?… and in thy name done many *wonderful works*?”__ — __“depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*”__ (Matthew 7:22-23).
Paul says *without love*; Jesus says *working lawlessness* — one deficiency named from both sides, for love is the keeping and lawlessness is its absence.

Samuel passed the sentence first: Saul spared the best of the flock *to sacrifice it* — an offering in place of the command — and heard, __“to *obey* is better than *sacrifice*, and to hearken than the fat of rams”__ (1 Samuel 15:22).
Sacrifice, wonders, gifts — the offering never outranks the command.

The catalog that follows is the law in motion.
__“Love *does not envy*”__ — the tenth commandment kept; __“does not seek its own… thinks no *evil*”__ — no ill worked to a neighbor; __“does not rejoice in *lawlessness*, but rejoices in the sym:sym-truth[truth]”__ — and __“thy *law* is the truth”__ (Psalm 119:142).
Paul is not decorating; he is itemizing: __“Love worketh no ill to his neighbour: therefore love is the *fulfilling of the law*”__ (Romans 13:10) — and __“the *purpose of the commandment* is love from a pure heart”__ (1 Timothy 1:5, NKJV).
The commandment's own goal is love; love's own shape is the commandments.

The hymn then turns prophetic:

[quote.scripture, 1 Corinthians 13:8, 10, 12, NKJV]
____
Love *never ends*. But whether there are prophecies, they will end; whether there are tongues, they will cease; whether there is knowledge, it will vanish away…
But when *that which is perfect* has come, then that which is in part will be done away…
For now we see *in a mirror, dimly*, but then *face to face*. Now I sym:sym-knowing[know] in part, but then I shall know just as I also am sym:sym-knowing[*known*].
____

James supplies the mirror: the hearer who is not a doer is __“a man observing his natural face *in a mirror*”__ — and the doer looks __“into the *perfect law* of sym:sym-liberty[liberty]”__ (James 1:23-25).
The mirror we see dimly in is the law; what it shows a man is his own face.
Now the reflection is dim and partial; the day the *perfect* comes, the mediated image gives way to __“face to face”__ — the phrase of Moses, with whom God spoke __“face to face, even plainly, and not in *riddles*”__ (Num 12:8).
Mark where the hymn lands: __“I shall *know* just as I also am *known*”__ — Paul's love chapter ends in the covenant word this chapter defines next.
The gifts are scaffolding and come down; love — the commandments kept — __“never ends,”__ because the law it fulfills does not pass away.

[discrete]
=== Knowing

To _know_ God, we assume, is to know about Him — facts, doctrine, a sound creed.
But Jesus turns away people whose facts are exactly right:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:23]
____
And then will I profess unto them, *I never knew you*: depart from me, ye that *work lawlessness*.
____

He does not say _you never knew me_; He says _I never knew you_ — and the disqualifier is *lawlessness*.
The Greek is _anomia_ — without _nomos_, without *law* — the word the KJV renders __“iniquity,”__ and the word John uses when he defines sin: __“sin is the *transgression of the law*”__ (1 John 3:4).
Read __“iniquity”__ in the New Testament and this is usually the word underneath — law-breaking, wearing a vaguer name.
To be unknown by God is to be what Scripture calls the one not known — a sym:sym-stranger[stranger]; so the whole sentence reads __“I never knew you: depart from me”__ — put out of the household like a man who was never of it.

John gives the test plainly:

[quote.scripture, 1 John 2:3-4]
____
And hereby we do know that we *know him, if we keep his commandments*.
He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a *liar*, and the sym:sym-truth[truth] is not in him.
____

To know God is a covenant bond proven by obedience — the same sense in which God says of Israel, __“You only have I known of all the families of the earth”__ (Amos 3:2).
Knowing is not information held; it is a relationship kept.

Paul says it with a knife: __“They profess that they *know God*; but in *works* they *deny him*”__ (Titus 1:16).
Hosea mourns the same equation from the ruins: __“My people are destroyed for *lack of knowledge*… seeing thou hast *forgotten the law* of thy God”__ (Hosea 4:6) — the knowledge lost was not information; it was the law.
God defines the knowing by a king’s life: he __“sym:sym-judgment[judged] the cause of the sym:sym-poor[poor] and needy… *was not this to know me?* saith the LORD”__ (Jeremiah 22:16).

The relationship it names runs deeper than acquaintance.
The first time Scripture uses the word of two persons it means the union of marriage — __“Adam *knew* Eve his wife; and she *conceived*”__ (Genesis 4:1) — the union that bears sym:sym-fruit[fruit].
To _know_, at root, is the intimate joining of husband and wife; which is why God calls His covenant a sym:sym-marriage[marriage] and His people a bride.

To know God is to be joined to Him as a wife to her husband — and that union keeps an order: __“the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church”__ (Ephesians 5:23), the bride subject to her Head __“in every thing”__ (Ephesians 5:24).
So the intimacy and the obedience are not two things: to know Him is to be His, and to be His is to obey the Head.
The ones He never knew were never joined to Him — they said __“Lord”__ but were no bride of His.

Put the symbols together and the Shepherd says it whole. __“My sym:sym-sheep[sheep] hear my sym:sym-voice[voice]… and they follow me”__ (John 10:27): the sheep are His people, the voice is His words, and to know that voice is to follow it.
And they follow no other — __“a sym:sym-stranger[stranger] will they not follow… for they know not the sym:sym-voice[voice] of strangers”__ (John 10:5): the stranger is the one outside the covenant, so His sheep will not heed the voice, the teaching, of those outside it. __“He calleth his own sym:sym-sheep[sheep] by sym:sym-name[name]”__ (John 10:3) — and the sym:sym-name[name] is a symbol with its own chapter just ahead.
To the one who overcomes He gives __“a sym:sym-new[new] sym:sym-name[name] written, which no man knoweth saving he that receiveth it”__ (Revelation 2:17) — _known_ only by the one who receives and keeps it.
Sheep, voice, name, stranger, knowing: each word in its place, and not one of them a thing held only in the head.

[discrete]
=== Faith

Faith, we are taught, is the one thing that needs no works.
James answers head-on:

[quote.scripture, James 2:17]
____
Even so faith, if it hath not works, is *dead*, being alone.
____

He presses it: __“the devils also believe, and tremble”__ (James 2:19) — perfect assent, and it saves none of them.
The faith Scripture honors always moves: __“By faith Abraham… obeyed”__ (Hebrews 11:8).

The Hebrew behind _faith_ is _emunah_, faithfulness — steadiness, reliability — so __“the just shall live by his faith”__ (Habakkuk 2:4) is to live by one’s faithfulness.
The word’s first appearance in Scripture is a picture: Moses’ hands, held up over the battle, __“were *steady*”__ — _emunah_ — __“until the going down of the sun”__ (Exodus 17:12): faithfulness is hands that do not drop.
Faith is not a conviction lodged in the mind; it is the trust that obeys.

Hebrews states the office in one verse:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 11:1]
____
Now faith is the *substance* of things *hoped for*, the *evidence* of things *not seen*.
____

Evidence is a thing produced and examined — faith is the *seen* member of the pair, and the unseen is proved by it.
Scripture keeps the pattern consistently: hope's object is unseen by definition — __“hope that is *seen* is not hope… but if we hope for that we see not, then do we with *patience* wait for it”__ (Romans 8:24-25) — and the patience is faithfulness holding while the thing stays out of sight.
The unseen things are the eternal ones (2 Corinthians 4:18), and __“we *walk* by faith, not by sight”__ (2 Corinthians 5:7): conduct stands in where sight is denied.
Hebrews 11 itself is a catalog of visible acts — Noah, __“warned of God of things *not seen as yet*… prepared an ark”__ (Hebrews 11:7): unseen warning, visible obedience.
What is hoped for, Scripture also names: the resurrection (Acts 24:15), eternal life __“*promised* before the world began”__ (Titus 1:2), the city and better country Abraham looked for (Hebrews 11:10, 16), His appearing (Titus 2:13).
Faith is what obedience looks like while the promise is still out of sight — works that can be seen (Matthew 5:16), held steady for things that cannot.

[discrete]
=== Belief

Which leaves _believe_, the word on which whole systems are built — and here the ground is already broken: the link:/books/symbolic-language/gospel/[Gospel] chapter caught Paul trading __“obeyed”__ for __“believed”__ in a single breath (Romans 10:16), and pointed at the Greek of John 3:36.
Now read that famous verse whole:

[quote.scripture, John 3:36, ASV]
____
He that *believeth* on the Son hath eternal sym:sym-life[life]; but he that *obeyeth not* the Son shall not see life…
____

The opposite of believing is not doubting; it is disobeying — for belief that is real shows in what a man does.
Economists have a name for the gap: people name grand sums in surveys and spend far less in fact — _hypothetical bias_, the distance between a poll that costs nothing and a choice that costs something.footnote:fn06hausman[Jerry A. Hausman, “Contingent Valuation: From Dubious to Hopeless,” _Journal of Economic Perspectives_ 26, no. 4 (2012): 43–56.]

Scripture keeps the pair everywhere: Israel could not enter the land for __“unbelief”__ — they __“believed not”__ — yet the sin named is disobedience (Hebrews 3:18).
And Abraham, who __“believed in the LORD”__ (Genesis 15:6), is shown to have been __“justified by works, when he had offered Isaac”__ (James 2:21) — the believing and the obeying are one act.

The four words converge, then, on one act — and keep four distinct offices.
*Love* is not a reason to obey; it is the keeping itself: __“this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments”__ (1 John 5:3).
*Faith* keeps them in hope of the promise — __“faith is the substance of things *hoped for*”__ (Heb 11:1); Abraham __“obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went… for he looked for a city”__ (Heb 11:8-10).
*Belief* keeps them because they are the sym:sym-truth[truth]: Thomas believed __“because thou hast seen”__ — and the Lord blessed __“they that have not seen, and yet have believed”__ (John 20:29), grading the two grounds in a single sentence.
*Knowing* keeps them as a bride keeps covenant — __“I will betroth thee unto me in *faithfulness*: and thou shalt *know* the LORD”__ (Hos 2:20); __“He judged the cause of the poor and needy… was not this to know me?”__ (Jer 22:16).
One obedience, four offices — and *faithful* is Scripture's word for that love kept *consistent*: Moses' hands __“steady until the going down of the sun”__ (Ex 17:12), the servant found __“so doing”__ when his lord comes (Matt 24:46), __“faithful unto death”__ (Rev 2:10).
Flatten them into synonyms and each goes invisible — which is what the dictionary did, so a man could claim all four and live as he pleased.
Scripture binds them back together and sets them where they can be seen — in a life that does what God said.

See, then, what the symbols do to the simplest promise of all.

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:13]
____
For whosoever shall *call upon* the sym:sym-name[name] of the Lord shall be *saved*.
____

It is heard as a sentence you say — profess the name, and salvation is done.
Then remember the many this chapter has already met — who prophesied and did wonders *in that name*, and were not known.
They said the name; whatever it is to _call upon_ it, they did not do it.
So what *is* the sym:sym-name[name], that calling upon it saves and merely saying it does not?
The question has a chapter of its own just ahead — link:/books/symbolic-language/the-name/[The Name] — and everything this chapter proved about knowing, loving, and obeying rides on its answer.

This is what knowing the symbolic language does, and why it changes everything: the verse you thought you had mastered turns out to ask for your whole life.
