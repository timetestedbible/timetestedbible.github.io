A few conventions will make the reading easier.

*Symbols and Glossary.*
Words Scripture defines are set in a distinct style — like sym:sym-mountain[mountain] or sym:sym-bread[bread] — wherever they carry that defined meaning.
The Glossary gives each symbol's meaning, defining verse, and proving chapter; its italic _see_ lines point from the short entry to the fuller argument.

*Scripture.*
Quotations are from the King James Version unless noted.
Where it obscures a key word, I use a more literal rendering and say so, sometimes going beneath English to Hebrew or Greek, including unpointed Hebrew consonants where a second meaning stands inside the first.

*Prevalence tables.*
An _Est. %_ column is not a survey result and is not part of the reproducible glossary experiment described in the Introduction.
It is an AI-assisted editorial estimate of the relative prominence of common readings.
Treat the rank and broad shape as useful; do not treat the percentages as measurements.

*The Scripture Index.*
Every cited passage appears in the Scripture Index at the back, with each page where this book discusses it.

*Editions and App.*
This book is offered in print and as a free digital edition.
In digital editions, every symbol links to the Glossary, and each Glossary _see_ line links to the chapter that proves it.
The integrated Bible application at https://TimeTested.Bible[TimeTested.Bible] links every verse and citation to Scripture and includes my other books, such as _Time Tested Tradition_.
However you read, the method is one: let Scripture define its own words — line upon line, precept upon precept.
