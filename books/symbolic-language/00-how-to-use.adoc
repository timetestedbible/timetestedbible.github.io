A few conventions will make the reading easier.

*Symbols.*
Words that Scripture itself defines are set in a distinct style — like sym:sym-mountain[mountain] or sym:sym-bread[bread] — wherever they carry that defined meaning.
Every one is collected in the Glossary at the back, each with a one-line meaning, a defining verse, and the chapter where Scripture establishes it.

*The Glossary “see” line.*
Each entry closes with a short italic line: the abbreviated Scripture references that define the symbol, then _see_ and the chapter that proves it.
Follow the references to test the meaning for yourself; follow the _see_ to the fuller argument.

*Scripture.*
Quotations are from the King James Version unless noted.
Where the King James obscures a key word, we switch to a more literal rendering and say so — and often go beneath the English to the Hebrew or Greek, at times to the unpointed Hebrew consonants, where a second meaning frequently stands inside the first.

*Editions.*
This book is offered in print and as a free digital edition.
In the digital editions every symbol links to its Glossary entry, and each Glossary _see_ line links to the chapter that proves it; in print, the Glossary at the back gives the same definitions.

*The study application.*
For the fullest study, an interactive, integrated Bible application is available at https://TimeTested.Bible[TimeTested.Bible], where every verse and citation is linked to Scripture — and where you will find my other books, such as _Time Tested Tradition_.
However you read, the method is one: let Scripture define its own words, line upon line.
