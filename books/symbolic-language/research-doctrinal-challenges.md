# Research: Doctrinal challenges — Catholic, Protestant, Jewish panels

Author 2026-07-05: "have a background agent read the whole book with the
eyes of Catholic, Protestant, and Jewish doctrine... what doctrine does the
symbolic reading overturn and what scriptures would they use to attempt to
overturn it... and state why those scriptures fail to do what they claim."

Method: per tradition, four readers swept all chapters as that tradition's
best advocate; a synthesizer merged and ranked conflicts, then wrote the
rebuttal for each, verifying verses against the corpus, and graded each
rebuttal honestly: STRONG (counter-texts don't carry the claim), PARTIAL
(a residue stands), EXPOSED (the counter lands a blow the book has not yet
answered).


## Catholic (12 conflicts: {'STRONG': 3, 'PARTIAL': 8, 'EXPOSED': 1})

### [STRONG] Cessation of the Old Law's ceremonial precepts — feasts, sabbath, dietary laws, circumcision, sacred calendar (Council of Florence, Cantate Domino, DS 1348; Trent Sess. VI; CCC 1963-1968, 2175-2176; Aquinas ST I-II q.103 a.4). The most recurrent conflict: raised as CENTRAL against the gospel, signs, name, trees, Lucifer, shadow, pearl, worship, liberty, and path-to-salvation chapters.

**Book:** The new covenant is the Sinai covenant renewed (chadash, Strong's H2318, 'renew, repair'): the same law relocated from stone to heart. Nothing in the law of Moses is retired — the feasts, sabbath, diet, and calendar remain binding signs of belonging, and no fulfilled sign ever licenses lawbreaking (Deut 13); teachers who relax the law are thereby disqualified.

**Their scriptures:** Hebrews 7:12; 8:6-13; 2 Corinthians 3:6-11; Colossians 2:14-17; Ephesians 2:15; Galatians 4:9-11; 5:2-4; Acts 15:5-11, 28-29; Romans 14:5; Hebrews 10:1; Luke 22:20.

**Their argument:** At full strength: the inspired author of Hebrews draws the very conclusion the book forbids — in calling the covenant 'new' God 'hath made the first old... ready to vanish away' (8:13), and the change of priesthood entails 'of necessity a change also of the law' (7:12). Paul forbids judgment over holyday, new moon, or sabbath, which are 'a shadow' whose body is Christ; the ministration 'written and engraven in stones' is 'done away'; the apostolic council of Acts 15, exercising the living authority the book's method bypasses, refused to bind the yoke on the nations and imposed only four abstentions. Florence therefore defined that the legalia ceased at the promulgation of the Gospel and may not be observed as necessary; the moral law abides, but the ceremonial terms are precisely what changed.

**Rebuttal:** Read whole, each counter-text addresses something narrower than 'the Torah expired.' Hebrews 8 defines the new covenant by quoting Jeremiah 31:33 verbatim: 'I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts' (Heb 8:10) — the covenant that supersedes has the SAME laws as its stated content, so what 'vanisheth' is the administration (the fault was 'with them,' 8:8-9), the Levitical priesthood-and-temple apparatus that is the actual subject of Heb 7-10 and that did vanish in 70 AD; Heb 7:12's 'change of the law' sits inside a priesthood argument, not a feasts-and-food argument. Col 2:14's 'handwriting of ordinances' is cheirographon (G5498), by Strong's 'a legal document or bond' — a debt-record, a word never once used of the Torah — and the ordinances Paul dismisses he labels himself three verses later: 'Touch not; taste not; handle not... after the commandments and doctrines of MEN' (2:21-22); God's law cannot be men's doctrine, so what was nailed up is not Sinai. Col 2:17 says the feasts 'ARE a shadow of things TO COME' — present tense, future referent: a shadow of what is still coming has not expired. Gal 4:8-10 is addressed to ex-pagans who 'did service unto them which by nature are no gods' and are warned against turning 'AGAIN' to those elements — they cannot return to a Mosaic calendar they never kept. Gal 5:2-4 frames circumcision twice as a justification mechanism ('justified by the law,' 5:4) — and the same Paul circumcised Timothy (Acts 16:3), raced to keep Pentecost (Acts 20:16), wrote 'let us keep the feast' (1 Cor 5:8), and staged a public proof 'that thou thyself also walkest orderly, and KEEPEST THE LAW' (Acts 21:24) among believers 'all zealous of the law' (21:20). Acts 15 sets an entry floor of four Leviticus 17-18 laws and gives its reason in the next verse: 'For Moses... is read in the synagogues every sabbath day' (15:21) — the council assumes Gentile converts sitting under Moses weekly, a strange assumption for a body abolishing him. Meanwhile Matt 5:18 stands with its own stated expiry ('Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law'), Isa 66:22-23 puts 'from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another' inside the NEW heavens and new earth, Zech 14:16 has the nations keeping Tabernacles after the LORD is King, and Paul's own verdict is 'Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law' (Rom 3:31). The one thing this rebuttal cannot answer from Scripture is Florence itself — a conciliar definition is refused, not refuted, by the concordance; that is a clash of authorities, and it is treated under the rule-of-faith conflict below.

### [PARTIAL] The rule of faith: Scripture authentically interpreted only by the living Magisterium within Tradition, and the indefectibility of the Church in transmitting the deposit (Dei Verbum 8-10; CCC 85-88, 889-890; Trent Sess. IV; Vatican I). Recurs across the introduction, signs, Lucifer, fall-of-Babylon, and path-to-salvation chapters.

**Book:** Scripture is a self-interpreting, deterministic symbol-system any diligent reader can decode by gathering every use of a word; the governing meanings were sealed and lost on the church — still on milk — until knowledge increased in these last days; the visible covenant body can go Babylon-far astray while a remnant holds; apostolic insight was method, not unrepeatable revelation.

**Their scriptures:** 2 Peter 1:20 with 3:15-16; Acts 8:30-31; 1 Timothy 3:15; Matthew 16:18-19; 28:20; John 16:13; Luke 10:16; Jude 3; Nehemiah 8:8; Galatians 1:11-12; Ephesians 3:3-5.

**Their argument:** At full strength: 'no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation' — the Ethiopian needed Philip, not a concordance; the unlearned wrest Paul's letters to their destruction; the church is 'the pillar and ground of the truth.' An 'objective process' whose lone operator selects the parallels and grades the traditions is private judgment in a lab coat. And if the governing meaning of the parables and the gospel lay unread from the apostles until the age of the search engine, the Spirit failed to guide the Church into all truth and the gates of hell prevailed for nineteen centuries — the outcome Christ's promises exclude; by the Vincentian canon, a hermeneutic whose fruit is that no one ever held the true sense is self-condemned.

**Rebuttal:** The interpretation texts do not carry the exclusive-magisterium claim. 2 Pet 1:20 defines itself in its own next clause: 'FOR the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost' (1:21) — the verse concerns prophecy's ORIGIN (epilysis, G1955, an 'explanation, application': no prophecy is the prophet's own unloosing), forbidding private authorship, not lay reading. The Bereans are called 'more noble' precisely because they 'searched the scriptures daily, WHETHER THOSE THINGS WERE SO' (Acts 17:11) — apostolic preaching itself is submitted to the reader's text-check and commended for it; Philip guided the eunuch by 'beginning at the same scripture' (8:35) — the guide's warrant was the text, not an office. Isa 8:20 makes the written word the test of every speaker ('if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them'), and Deut 13 applies that test even to sign-workers. On indefectibility, Scripture's standing model of the covenant body is mass error with a preserved remnant: 'I have reserved to myself seven thousand' (1 Kgs 19:18), which Paul makes the church-age pattern in so many words — 'Even so then AT THIS PRESENT TIME also there is a remnant' (Rom 11:5); the priests whose lips should keep knowledge 'caused many to stumble at the law' (Mal 2:7-8); 'the pen of the scribes is in vain' (Jer 8:8); wolves arise 'of your own selves' (Acts 20:30); many miracle-working name-bearers hear 'I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity' (Matt 7:22-23); and the Lord asks whether the Son of man will find faith on the earth at his coming (Luke 18:8). Matt 16:18 promises the assembly's survival against the gates of death, not the majority's doctrinal accuracy — the same Jesus seats the scribes in Moses' seat and disqualifies their practice in one breath (Matt 23:2-3). The sealed-then-opened claim is Daniel's own oracle, not the book's conceit: 'the words are closed up and sealed TILL THE TIME OF THE END... knowledge shall be increased... the wise shall understand' (Dan 12:4, 9-10). And Rev 18:4's 'Come out of her, MY PEOPLE' presupposes God's people inside a corrupted body — the summons the tradition holds can never sound. The residue is real, though: the book's rhetoric ('more of the sign than sixteen centuries of commentary carried') implies NO remnant held the sense — which contradicts the book's own remnant ecclesiology and hands the Vincentian argument its opening. The book should locate its seven thousand in history — readers in every age who kept sabbath, feast, and symbol — or soften the sixteen-centuries claim; as written, that sentence proves too much.

### [PARTIAL] The content of the Gospel: the Paschal mystery of Christ's death and resurrection stands at the center of the Good News (CCC 422-424, 571, 638-639; 1 Cor 15:1-4 as the transmitted kerygma; Evangelii Gaudium 164). Raised as CENTRAL against both the gospel chapter and the glossary.

**Book:** The gospel is the herald's proclamation that God's kingdom reigns — the renewed covenant and the law of that kingdom going forth from Zion (Isa 52:7; 2:3) — while the death and resurrection are the means that secure it: 'the purchase is not the proclamation,' and the death-and-resurrection summary is 'a true part wearing the name of the whole.'

**Their scriptures:** 1 Corinthians 15:1-4; 1 Corinthians 1:17-18, 23; 2:2; Romans 1:16 with 4:25; Luke 24:46-47; Acts 2:22-38; 10:36-43; 13:26-39; Galatians 1:6-9; 6:14.

**Their argument:** At full strength: Paul defines the gospel he preached in exactly one place and it is the paschal kerygma — 'Christ died for our sins... was buried... rose again the third day' — 'by which also ye are saved'; every apostolic sermon in Acts is structured on the crucified-and-risen Lord; the risen Christ's own summary of what is 'written' is his passion, resurrection, and remission of sins preached to all nations; Acts 13 states the antithesis outright — justification 'from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses'; Galatians anathematizes the 'other gospel' that made law-observance the covenant's content. Making the cross the invisible financing behind a law-proclamation inverts CCC 571.

**Rebuttal:** The counter-texts prove the cross is gospel content — which the book affirms — but they cannot make the death-summary the word's DEFINITION, because Scripture uses 'gospel' of proclamations that contain no cross. Isa 52:7 and Nah 1:15 define the herald's news as 'Thy God reigneth' — and Nahum's herald commands, in the same breath, 'O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts.' Jesus preaches 'the gospel of the kingdom of God' with its content quoted: 'The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the gospel' (Mark 1:14-15). The clinching sequence is Luke's: the twelve are sent 'to preach the kingdom of God... preaching the gospel' (Luke 9:2, 6), yet when Jesus afterward announces his death and rising 'they understood none of these things... neither knew they the things which were spoken' (Luke 18:33-34) — men cannot have been preaching as gospel what was still hidden from them, so 'the gospel' cannot be identical with the passion summary. The gospel was 'preached before... unto Abraham' (Gal 3:8), and Rev 14:6-7's 'everlasting gospel' has its content quoted with no cross in it: 'Fear God... worship him that made heaven, and earth.' Post-resurrection preaching pairs kingdom and King: 'the things concerning the kingdom of God, and the name of Jesus Christ' (Acts 8:12); Acts closes with Paul 'preaching the kingdom of God, and teaching those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ' (28:31). 1 Cor 15:3's 'first of all' (en protois, among the first things) delivers the cross as the gospel's chief certification — the part the book grants. The residue: Paul does not merely finance with the cross, he PREACHES it — 'we preach Christ crucified' (1 Cor 1:23) — and the risen Lord couples his passion with the preached remission of sins (Luke 24:46-47). The formula 'the purchase is not the proclamation' overcorrects against the apostolic record, where the purchase is itself proclaimed as good news; the book would be safer saying the kingdom is the frame and the cross the announced gate, rather than demoting the cross to off-stage financing.

### [PARTIAL] The Real Presence of Christ in the Eucharist and the literal sense of John 6 (Trent Sess. 13, canon 1, anathematizing 'only as in a sign or figure'; CCC 1336, 1374-1377), with the Eucharistic sense of the daily-bread petition (CCC 2837).

**Book:** Eating Christ's flesh and drinking his blood means eating his word: the body is the bread, the word to be eaten and kept; the disciples of John 6:66 erred because 'they took the symbol for the flesh'; daily bread is the day's portion of the word and the manna-test of walking in it.

**Their scriptures:** John 6:51-58 (esp. 6:53-56, with trogo, to gnaw/chew); John 6:60-68; Matthew 26:26-28; Luke 22:19-20; 1 Corinthians 10:16; 11:23-29.

**Their argument:** At full strength: when his hearers took him literally and were scandalized, Jesus did not correct them as he corrected Nicodemus or the disciples about leaven; he escalated the realism — 'my flesh is meat indeed' — shifting to the coarse verb trogo, and let them walk away, even offering the Twelve their leave. Paul's warning that unworthy eaters are 'guilty of the body and blood of the Lord' and judged for 'not discerning the Lord's body' is unintelligible if the bread is only teaching. Trent's canon anathematizes exactly the book's move, and CCC 1336 cites John 6:66 as the Eucharist's first stumbling block.

**Rebuttal:** Eating-the-word is an equation Scripture builds itself: 'man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD' (Deut 8:3, quoted by Jesus at Matt 4:4); 'Thy words were found, and I did eat them' (Jer 15:16); Ezekiel eats the scroll (Ezek 3:1-3); the famine is 'of hearing the words of the LORD' (Amos 8:11). And Jesus once corrected bread-literalism in exactly the book's direction: 'How is it that ye do not understand that I spake it not to you concerning bread... Then understood they how that he bade them... beware of the DOCTRINE' (Matt 16:11-12). Inside John 6 the decode is native: v.35 lays the parallel — 'he that COMETH to me shall never hunger; and he that BELIEVETH on me shall never thirst' — eating is coming, drinking is believing; and the tradition's no-correction narrative misreads the scene's order, because Jesus answers the murmuring disciples BEFORE any of them leaves: 'It is the spirit that quickeneth; THE FLESH PROFITETH NOTHING: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life' (6:61-63), and only then 'from that time many of his disciples went back' (6:66). The explanation was given and refused. Paul's warnings fit the assembly-reading: 'we being many are one bread, and one body' (1 Cor 10:17); the abuse he condemns is shaming that body — 'despise ye the church of God, and shame them that have not?' (11:22), 'tarry one for another' (11:33) — so 'not discerning the Lord's body' (11:29) reads naturally of the congregation; and the rite defines itself as memorial and proclamation: 'this do in remembrance of me' (Luke 22:19), 'ye do shew the Lord's death till he come' (11:26). The residue is John 6:51: 'the bread that I will give is my flesh, WHICH I WILL GIVE FOR THE LIFE OF THE WORLD' — that gloss anchors the bread to the crucified body, not to teaching; the strict flesh-equals-word equation does not absorb it (the eating may be figurative while what is eaten is the sacrificed Messiah, not merely his doctrine), and the deliberate trogo escalation of 6:54-58 remains a genuine foothold. The book's reading survives, but the chapter needs to say how the word eaten includes the death died.

### [PARTIAL] Justification as infused sanctifying grace: righteousness first a received state from which works flow (Trent Sess. 6 ch. 7 and canon 11; Second Council of Orange; CCC 1987-2000). Raised as CENTRAL against the garments chapter and SIGNIFICANT against justice-and-judgment.

**Book:** Righteousness is 'the doing of what God commanded' — 'it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments' (Deut 6:25); the wedding garment 'is simply the law kept, and worn,' against a righteousness 'turned from something you do into a state you merely have.'

**Their scriptures:** Romans 3:20-22, 28; 4:5; Galatians 2:16, 21; Philippians 3:9; Titus 3:5; Ephesians 2:8-10; Romans 5:17; 8:3-4; John 15:4-5.

**Their argument:** At full strength: Trent shares the book's quarrel with an unwoven garment — it condemns bare imputation — but equally condemns righteousness as deeds alone: canon 11 anathematizes whoever excludes the inhering grace and charity poured out by the Spirit. Paul explicitly refuses 'mine own righteousness, which is of the law' for 'that which is through the faith of Christ'; 'to him that worketh not, but believeth... his faith is counted for righteousness'; if righteousness came by the law, Christ died in vain. Defining righteousness as law-keeping without the prior gift is the road of Pelagius, condemned at Carthage and Orange before Trent — and the book's own proof-text betrays the gift-order: 'it was GRANTED unto her' (Rev 19:8).

**Rebuttal:** Scripture defines the word out of its own mouth, and the book has the definitional column: 'it shall be our righteousness, IF we observe to do all these commandments' (Deut 6:25); 'all thy commandments are righteousness' (Ps 119:172); and the New Testament repeats the definition with an anti-deception warning attached — 'Little children, let no man deceive you: he that DOETH righteousness is righteous' (1 John 3:7); 'not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the DOERS of the law shall be justified' (Rom 2:13); Zacharias and Elisabeth are 'righteous before God, WALKING in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless' (Luke 1:6); 'by works a man is justified, and not by faith only' (James 2:24). The book's proof-text carries both halves in a single verse rather than betraying it: the linen is 'granted' AND 'the fine linen IS the righteousness of saints' (Rev 19:8) — dikaioma (G1345), by Strong's 'an equitable deed... a righteous act' — gift and doing welded, which is precisely 'woven and worn.' Against verdict-only imputation, Trent and the book are allies, which blunts the counter's main charge. But the residue is real and double: Rom 4:5 — 'to him that WORKETH NOT, but believeth... his faith is COUNTED for righteousness' (with Gen 15:6) — keeps a second, reckoned use of the word that the book's single equation flattens; and Phil 3:9 has Paul refusing 'mine own righteousness, WHICH IS OF THE LAW' immediately after claiming he had achieved it ('touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless,' 3:6). Scripture uses righteousness both as deed done and as standing granted; the book built its case by gathering every use, and here it has kept only one column of its own concordance. The chapter needs an account of the counted-righteousness texts — entry by faith, walk by doing — or the tradition's charge of definitional amputation stands in part.

### [EXPOSED] Oil and anointing as the scriptural sign of the Holy Spirit, and the absolute gratuity of grace (CCC 695, 1289, 1499-1513; Trent Sess. 14 on unction; Second Council of Orange canons 5-7; CCC 1996-1998). Raised as CENTRAL against the fool-and-the-wise chapter and SIGNIFICANT against the glossary's flat 'Oil: ...not the Spirit.'

**Book:** The oil of the ten virgins cannot be the Spirit, grace, or faith — gifts freely given cannot be bought or run short — but is 'the law bought and kept, the tried doing of the truth, stored in one's own vessel'; the glossary fixes it: 'proven works that make light; not the Spirit.'

**Their scriptures:** Zechariah 4:2-6; 1 Samuel 16:13; Isaiah 61:1 with Luke 4:18; Acts 10:38; 1 John 2:20, 27; 2 Corinthians 1:21-22; Ephesians 2:8-10; Romans 11:6; Philippians 2:13; Isaiah 55:1; Revelation 3:18.

**Their argument:** At full strength: Scripture interprets its own lampstand-oil exactly once, and the angel's answer is 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit' (Zech 4:6). Throughout the canon the anointing oil confers the Spirit — David from the horn of oil 'from that day forward,' Messiah meaning the Anointed-with-the-Spirit (Acts 10:38), the believer's endowment named 'the anointing' (1 John 2:27). The 'buy' of Matthew 25:9 no more excludes gift than 'buy of me gold tried in the fire' or Isaiah 55's 'buy without money.' To define readiness for the Bridegroom as a purchased store of works accumulated apart from the Spirit is semi-Pelagian by the letter of Orange: good works fuel no lamp unless grace first fuels the works.

**Rebuttal:** The book's structural case is genuine and should be stated at full strength: the lamp's fuel is 'pure oil olive BEATEN for the light' (Ex 27:20) — pressed, tried; 'the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light' (Prov 6:23), so what feeds the lamp is what feeds obedience; Scripture's own wise/foolish split is hear-and-do versus hear-and-do-not (Matt 7:24-26); and Prov 21:20 is nearly a caption for the parable: 'There is treasure to be desired and OIL in the dwelling of THE WISE; but A FOOLISH man spendeth it up.' Non-transferability at midnight fits works better than a Spirit the tradition itself says is given for the asking (Luke 11:13). But here the counter lands by the book's own method, and honesty requires saying so. The one time Scripture decodes lampstand-oil, the angel says 'Not by might, nor by power, but by my SPIRIT' (Zech 4:6). The one narrative decode of anointing oil is 'Samuel took the horn of oil, and anointed him... and the SPIRIT OF THE LORD came upon David from that day forward' (1 Sam 16:13). The epistles name the believer's endowment 'the ANOINTING which ye have received of him... [which] teacheth you of all things' (1 John 2:27). Counted by the book's own rules — every use gathered, explicit self-interpretations weighted heaviest — the oil-to-Spirit column is at least as strong as oil-to-works, and stronger at the decisive verses. The glossary's flat 'not the Spirit' therefore denies a cross-text equation of exactly the kind the book elsewhere calls proof of design; this is the book's clearest methodological self-contradiction. A reconcilable reading exists and costs the parable nothing: the Spirit is the source and the wrought obedience the store — 'it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do' (Phil 2:13) — oil as Spirit-wrought doing, unlendable because a life cannot be borrowed. As written, though, the flat denial leaves the semi-Pelagian charge an opening the chapter does not close.

### [PARTIAL] The necessity of baptism and the sacramental economy for salvation (CCC 1129, 1257; Trent Sess. 6 ch. 7, Sess. 7 canons on baptism): raised as CENTRAL against the path-to-salvation chapter's sacrament-free three-step path, and MINOR against the sea chapter's baptism-as-picture.

**Book:** Salvation comes by admitting sin, believing Yeshua paid for it, and confessing him as Lord (Rom 10:9), completed by confessing the law good and attempting obedience; baptism is an enacted symbol — the convert goes under like Jonah while his sins go down like Babylon — never an effective instrument, and the path names no sacrament.

**Their scriptures:** John 3:5; Mark 16:16; Acts 2:38; 22:16; 1 Peter 3:21; Titus 3:5; Romans 6:3-4; John 6:53-54.

**Their argument:** At full strength: when the crowds asked the apostles this book's own question, the answer was never a bare confession formula but 'Repent, and be baptized every one of you... for the remission of sins.' Ananias tells Paul to be baptized and wash away his sins, not to dramatize a removal already accomplished; Peter's conclusion is not that baptism pictures salvation but that 'baptism doth also now save us.' Trent anathematizes reducing the sacraments to bare signs of grace otherwise received; a path that routes entirely around what Christ instituted replaces His economy with a private transaction.

**Rebuttal:** 1 Pet 3:21 defines itself mid-verse, and the definition runs the book's way: baptism saves 'NOT the putting away of the filth of the flesh, BUT the answer of a good conscience toward God... BY THE RESURRECTION of Jesus Christ' — Peter relocates the efficacy from the water to the pledge and the resurrection in his own grammar. The regenerating agent is elsewhere named, and it is the word: born again 'by the WORD of God, which liveth and abideth for ever' (1 Pet 1:23); 'of his own will begat he us with the WORD of truth' (James 1:18); cleansed 'with the washing of water BY THE WORD' (Eph 5:26) — which gives John 3:5's water a scriptural referent that is not the font. The order of events at Caesarea is decisive against any automatic mechanism: the Holy Ghost falls on Cornelius' unbaptized household first, and baptism follows as acknowledgment — 'Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, WHICH HAVE RECEIVED the Holy Ghost?' (Acts 10:44-47). Paul even separates his commission from the rite: 'Christ sent me not to baptize, but to preach the gospel' (1 Cor 1:17) — an impossible sentence if baptism were the instrumental cause of justification. So the counter-scriptures do not establish ex opere operato efficacy. But a residue stands against the chapter as written: in the apostolic record the sign is bound to the entry with commanded immediacy — 'Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins' (Acts 2:38); 'arise, and be baptized, and wash away thy sins' (Acts 22:16) — and no convert in Acts is left unbaptized. A 'path to salvation' that never mentions the commanded sign is thinner than the pattern of the very Scriptures it cites; the tradition's charge of teaching-by-omission lands even though its sacramental metaphysics does not. The chapter should command the enacted sign as the apostles did, while teaching what it enacts.

### [STRONG] The unlimited scope of redemption and the Church's power to forgive every post-baptismal sin — the condemnation of Novatianism (CCC 982, 1446, 1864; Trent Sess. 14 on Penance). Raised as CENTRAL against the path-to-salvation chapter's Numbers 15 reading of Hebrews 10:26.

**Book:** Christ as High Priest presents his blood for unintentional sins and those repented of, but the willful, defiant sinner — the teacher who knows the law and justifies breaking it — 'shall be cut off,' excluded from the atonement: for him 'there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins.'

**Their scriptures:** 1 John 1:9; 2:1-2; John 20:22-23; Matthew 12:31; 2 Samuel 12:13 with Psalm 51; Luke 22:61-62 with John 21:15-17.

**Their argument:** At full strength: David sinned with full deliberation — adultery and murder, the exact category of Numbers 15:30 — and 'The LORD also hath put away thy sin'; Peter's denial was willful and he was restored; 'all manner of sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven unto men.' The Church condemned Novatian for teaching that certain post-baptismal sins cannot be absolved; Hebrews 10:26 describes obstinate apostasy persisted in — unforgivable only because the sinner refuses the forgiveness (CCC 1864) — not a class of sins outside the reach of the Blood. The book's own concession that repented sins are covered dissolves its rule.

**Rebuttal:** The counter-examples all pass through the gate the book itself leaves open, so they strike nothing. The book states that the blood covers 'unintentional sins AND THOSE SINS FOR WHICH WE HAVE REPENTED' — and every case the tradition cites is a case of repentance: David is forgiven precisely upon 'I have sinned against the LORD' (2 Sam 12:13), pleading the very distinction at issue — 'thou desirest not sacrifice... The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit' (Ps 51:16-17); Peter wept and was restored; and Ezekiel states the book's rule twice as God's own: 'if the wicked will TURN from all his sins... he shall surely live' — 'all his transgressions... shall not be mentioned unto him' (Ezek 18:21-22; 33:14-16). What Heb 10:26 targets is continuing defiance, glossed three verses later as having 'trodden under foot the Son of God... and done despite unto the Spirit of grace' (10:29); and Num 15:31 defines the presumptuous soul by an ongoing posture — 'he hath DESPISED the word of the LORD.' Even the tradition's own proof-verse concedes the category: Matt 12:31's 'all manner of sin... shall be forgiven' carves out, in the same sentence, the blasphemy that 'shall not be forgiven' — so Scripture itself, on the tradition's chosen ground, holds a sin that stands outside forgiveness exactly as Heb 10:26 does. And the Catholic gloss on that sin (CCC 1864: unforgivable because the sinner refuses forgiveness to the end) is materially the book's position in different vocabulary. The Novatian charge misfires on the history too: Novatian refused absolution TO THE REPENTANT; the book refuses covering to the UNREPENTANT — which Trent also does, since unrepented mortal sin damns. The two systems converge; only the book's phrase 'excluding him from the atonement' invites the misreading, and a clarifying sentence — that the exclusion lasts exactly as long as the defiance — would close the gap entirely.

### [PARTIAL] Nicene consubstantiality: the Son as 'Light from Light, true God from true God,' not a creature of borrowed light; the Word of John 1 as the divine person, not the codified Torah (Nicaea I, DS 125; CCC 240-242, 446-451; CCC 65). Raised as CENTRAL against sun-moon-and-stars and SIGNIFICANT against the glossary, the-way-the-truth-and-the-life, wings, and path-to-salvation.

**Book:** The Father is the sun who alone shines of himself; the Son is the moon, the faithful witness, who 'has no light of his own — all it gives is the sun's light turned back'; and Yeshua is 'the Torah embodied — the standard walking,' the word-and-law made flesh, with 'the truth' defined as the law (Ps 119:142).

**Their scriptures:** John 1:1-4, 9, 14, 17; John 5:26; John 8:58; John 8:12; Hebrews 1:3; John 10:30; 17:5; Colossians 2:9; Revelation 21:23; Philippians 2:9-11; Romans 10:9-13.

**Their argument:** At full strength: Nicaea formulated 'Light from Light' precisely to exclude the moon-model — the Fathers' image is the apaugasma, the radiance of the same nature as its source, whereas moonlight is extrinsic reflection off a lightless body; John 5:26 gives the Son life 'in himself,' the property the moon lacks by definition; John's prologue makes what became flesh God the creating Word, expressly contrasted with 'the law given by Moses'; the apostles transfer YHWH-texts to Jesus (Joel 2:32 in Rom 10:13, Isa 45:23 in Phil 2:10); and a triad of Father-sun, Son-moon, righteous-stars ranks the Son among derivative light-bearers differing in degree — the shape of the Arian ladder. Scripture symbolizes Christ as the Sun of righteousness and the city's lamp, never as the moon.

**Rebuttal:** The moon-decode is not free association; it is Scripture's own collocation, found by the exact structural matching the book teaches: the Davidic throne 'shall be established for ever as the moon, and as A FAITHFUL WITNESS in heaven' (Ps 89:36-37), and Revelation opens by naming Jesus 'THE FAITHFUL WITNESS' (Rev 1:5) — the canon hangs the same title on the moon and on the Son. And the derivation the symbol pictures is the Son's relentless self-description: 'The Son can do nothing of himself' (John 5:19); 'I can of mine own self do nothing' (5:30); 'My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me' (7:16); 'I have not spoken of myself; but the Father... gave me a commandment, what I should say' (12:49); 'All power is GIVEN unto me' (Matt 28:18); and the tradition's own key text says GIVEN — 'so hath he GIVEN to the Son to have life in himself' (5:26). A symbol of received light says what these verses say, and Nicaea's own formula, 'Light from Light,' is a derivation image; a figure is not a metaphysics, in a book where the Lamb is also a lion and a door. As for 'the truth is the law': 'thy LAW is the truth' (Ps 119:142) and 'thy WORD is truth' (John 17:17) are the definitional verses, and Ps 33:6 ('by the WORD of the LORD were the heavens made') lets the dabar be older and larger than its Sinai codification — the creating Word who later legislates. But the residue must be stated plainly. John 5:26's gift is to have life IN HIMSELF — possessed, not continuously borrowed — which is exactly where 'no light of His own' overshoots its texts; John 1:4 locates the light in him ('In him was life; and the life was the light of men'); Heb 1:3's apaugasma is radiance streaming from the glory, not reflection off an alien surface; and the canon's final light-scene retires the moon while keeping the Lamb burning — 'the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon... the Lamb is the light thereof' (Rev 21:23), with Mal 4:2 giving Messiah the SUN's role. Likewise 'Torah made flesh' inverts John 1:3: the Word that made all things contains the law; the law does not contain him. The Ps 89/Rev 1:5 witness-link is sound and worth keeping; the glossary's definitional sentences ('no light of His own,' the Son sharing the moon-office with the bride) teach more than the derivation texts teach and should be re-fenced before a reader builds a Christology out of an astronomy.

### [STRONG] The souls of the purified just are in heaven now and see the divine essence before the general resurrection — Benedictus Deus (DS 1000-1002), a dogmatic definition; CCC 1023-1026 — against the book's 'heaven comes down, man does not go up' (raised as CENTRAL against the way-truth-life chapter, SIGNIFICANT against the glossary, MINOR against the clouds appendix).

**Book:** In Scripture the hope was never to climb up to heaven but for heaven to come down to earth; John 14:6 leads 'unto the Father,' not 'to heaven'; the glossary defines heaven as 'the dwelling and government of God; the hope is heaven coming down, not man going up.'

**Their scriptures:** John 14:2-3; Philippians 1:23; 2 Corinthians 5:1-8; Luke 23:43; Colossians 3:1-2; 1 Peter 1:4; Hebrews 12:22-23; Acts 7:55-59; Revelation 6:9-11; 7:9-15.

**Their argument:** At full strength: three verses before 'I am the way,' Christ promises to take the disciples where he is — 'I go to prepare a place for you... that where I am, there ye may be also'; Paul desires 'to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better' and is 'willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord'; the thief is promised paradise 'to day'; John sees souls before the throne now. Benedictus Deus defined that the just even now behold God's essence; Catholic eschatology holds both movements — the blessed ascend now, and at the end heaven and earth are united — and striking the first half removes the intermediate state on which the communion of saints depends.

**Rebuttal:** The book's claim here rests on flat apostolic assertion, not on a decoded symbol. At Pentecost — after the Ascension — Peter states: 'David is NOT ascended into the heavens' (Acts 2:34); Jesus: 'NO MAN hath ascended up to heaven, but he that came down from heaven' (John 3:13); 'The heaven, even the heavens, are the LORD'S: but the earth hath he given to the children of men' (Ps 115:16). The tradition's chief proof-text carries its own mechanism one verse before the clause it quotes: 'I WILL COME AGAIN, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also' (John 14:3) — the reunion is placed at his return, not at the believer's death; 1 Thess 4:16-17 runs the same order, 'ever with the Lord' only after descent and resurrection, while the interim is named sleep ('them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake,' Dan 12:2; 'fallen asleep in Christ,' 1 Cor 15:18). Luke 23:43's 'to day' hangs on a comma no Greek manuscript contains, and the Lord himself adjudicates it three days later: 'I am NOT YET ASCENDED to my Father' (John 20:17) — wherever paradise was that Friday, it was not the Father's presence above. 2 Cor 5, read whole, runs the book's way: the longed-for house is the resurrection body 'from heaven,' and Paul explicitly refuses disembodiment — 'NOT for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life' (5:2-4); on sleep's terms, Phil 1:23 is fully satisfied when the sleeper's next waking moment is Christ. And the canon's finale states the direction twice: the city 'coming DOWN from God out of heaven,' 'the tabernacle of God is WITH MEN' (Rev 21:2-3) — as the Lord's prayer always asked ('Thy will be done IN EARTH,' Matt 6:10) and the meek were always promised ('they shall inherit THE EARTH,' Matt 5:5). The residue: Rev 6:9-11's souls under the altar and Heb 12:23's 'spirits of just men made perfect' give the tradition real imagery of the conscious departed (answerable as vision-symbol — blood crying like Abel's — and as the registry of those 'written in heaven,' but not effortlessly); and Benedictus Deus itself, being a definition, is contradicted rather than answered. As exegesis, though, the counter-scriptures do not overturn Acts 2:34 and John 20:17.

### [PARTIAL] The distinction of the theological virtues: faith as graced assent, charity as infused friendship with God, fear as the Spirit's gift — not one behavior under four names (Dei Filius ch. 3; Trent Sess. 6 canon 28; CCC 143-155, 1813-1831, 2090). Raised as SIGNIFICANT against both the knowing-faith-love-belief and fear-of-the-LORD chapters.

**Book:** The four words collapse into one: love is not warmth but obedience; faith is not conviction lodged in the mind but the trust that obeys; belief is the obedience itself; fear is not a feeling about God but obedience to God — knowing, loving, fearing, and believing simply are commandment-keeping.

**Their scriptures:** 1 Corinthians 13:2-3, 13; Hebrews 11:1, 6; Romans 4:5; James 2:19, 22; 1 John 4:18; Mark 12:30; Romans 10:9-10.

**Their argument:** At full strength: Paul separates what the book fuses — mountain-moving faith can exist without charity, and a body given to be burned can exist without charity, which is impossible if love simply is the deeds; 'now abideth faith, hope, charity, THESE THREE.' The devils' belief is genuine assent without obedience, so belief cannot simply be obedience; James says faith is made perfect by works — completed, not constituted. John distinguishes the servile fear love casts out from the filial fear that endures. Collapsing the virtues into behavior renders the interior life dispensable and 1 Corinthians 13 unintelligible.

**Rebuttal:** For love and fear the collapse is not the book's invention; it is Scripture's own copula, stated as definition: 'THIS IS the love of God, THAT we keep his commandments' (1 John 5:3); 'this is love, that we walk after his commandments' (2 John 6); 'them that love me AND keep my commandments' (Deut 5:10); 'Fear God, AND keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man' (Eccl 12:13); Deut 10:12-13 stacks fear, walk, love, serve, keep as one demand with five verbs. Faith as fidelity is the Hebrew word itself: Hab 2:4's emunah (H530) is by Strong's 'firmness... fidelity' — the just live by faithfulness — and Paul's own summary is 'faith which WORKETH by love' (Gal 5:6). So the tradition's suggestion that these definitions are a modern flattening fails: the definitional verses exist and say what the book says. But the residue is genuine. 1 Cor 13:2-3 requires faith and even heroic deeds to be separable from love — 'though I have ALL FAITH, so that I could remove mountains, and have not charity, I am nothing... though I give my body to be burned, and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing' — which is impossible if love and faith are strictly identical with the doing; 13:13 counts 'these three,' not one; and James can call the devils' bare assent 'believing' (2:19), so the word does carry an assent-sense in Scripture's own usage that the flat equation 'belief is the obedience itself' excludes. The defensible synthesis is one covenant-loyalty root with distinguishable members — love the allegiance, faith its persevering trust, fear its guarded reverence, obedience their common body. The book proves the root and the direction (against feeling-first religion) but overstates identity where Scripture keeps enumeration; a paragraph conceding the distinction-within-union would cost the argument nothing and would take 1 Cor 13 off the table.

### [PARTIAL] The rejection of millenarianism and of any fixed census of the elect: one general resurrection at the visible Parousia, the kingdom consummated beyond history, and the Church's hope that embraces the numberless multitude (CCC 676, 998, 1001, 1038-1041; Holy Office 1944, DS 3839; CCC 851, 1821). Raised as SIGNIFICANT against the remnant chapter (twice) and the sea chapter's reading of 1 Thess 4:17.

**Book:** The first resurrection yields bodily kings and priests reigning with Christ a thousand years in the land while mortal nations still marry, build, and plant; converging scriptural lines fix the saved remnant near 0.2 percent; and the catching-up of 1 Thess 4:17 is the vapor rising — peoples lifted out of the sea of nations when Babylon falls.

**Their scriptures:** John 5:28-29; 18:36; Matthew 22:30; 1 Corinthians 15:23-26, 50-54; 2 Peter 3:10-13; 1 Thessalonians 4:14-17; Acts 1:9-11; Revelation 7:9; Luke 13:23-24; 1 Timothy 2:4; Romans 11:25-32.

**Their argument:** At full strength: since Augustine the Church reads the thousand years as the age of the Church and the first resurrection as baptismal; the bodily resurrection is one, general, and simultaneous with judgment; an interim kingdom of marrying mortals contradicts 'in the resurrection they neither marry'; Paul's comfort is anchored to dead bodies rising, which no sociological exodus accomplishes; asked whether few are saved, the Lord refused the census — 'strive' — and John's final vision is a multitude no man could number, the canon's deliberate reversal of every remnant count; the Holy Office ruled even mitigated millenarianism unsafe, and CCC 676 names intra-historical realizations the Antichrist's deception.

**Rebuttal:** On the millennium the counter-texts do not survive their own context. Rev 20:5 — 'the REST OF THE DEAD lived not AGAIN until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection' — is unintelligible on the baptismal reading: if the first resurrection is the soul's rising in baptism, who are 'the rest of the dead' who live AGAIN at the era's end, and why does one verb govern both groups? Two risings separated by the thousand years is the sentence's plain machinery, and 1 Cor 15:23-26 runs the same sequence — 'every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's AT HIS COMING. THEN cometh the end... he must REIGN, TILL he hath put all enemies under his feet. The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death' — an interval of reign between the coming and the end, with death destroyed last, exactly where Rev 20:14 puts it. Mortal nations beside the raised is likewise Scripture's own picture, not the book's: 'the child shall die an hundred years old... they shall build houses... plant vineyards' (Isa 65:20-22), nations keeping Tabernacles on pain of drought (Zech 14:16-17); Matt 22:30's no-marriage clause governs those 'in the resurrection' and says nothing of nations left in flesh; and the saints' venue is stated — 'we shall reign ON THE EARTH' (Rev 5:10), thrones over tribes and cities (Matt 19:28; Luke 19:17). Even the census-refusal is overstated: Jesus answered in substance — 'strait is the gate, and narrow is the way... and FEW there be that find it' (Matt 7:14) — and Luke 13:24's 'many... shall not be able' is an answer, not a deflection. But two counters land and must be owned. First, Rev 7:9's 'great multitude, WHICH NO MAN COULD NUMBER' is the canon's deliberate reversal of counting, and a derived 0.2 percent — assembled from ore grades, lion demographics, and surveyed acreage — is precisely a man numbering what the text says no man could; the ratio outruns every verse it leans on and should be presented as illustrative convergence, not census. Second, whatever symbolic freight 1 Thess 4:17 carries, verse 16 anchors it to bodies — 'the DEAD IN CHRIST shall rise first' — and Paul offers the passage as comfort to the bereaved (4:13, 18); a reading in which the catching-up is only peoples-out-of-nations evaporates that comfort and cannot be the governing sense. The millennium rebuttal is strong; the census and the vapor-reading are where the tradition draws blood.


## Protestant (reformed/evangelical confessional tradition: westminster standards, heidelberg catechism, belgic confession, canons of dort, second helvetic) (12 conflicts: {'PARTIAL': 9, 'STRONG': 1, 'EXPOSED': 2})

### [PARTIAL] Justification by faith alone: faith as the empty hand that receives Christ, distinct from obedience (WCF 11.1-2, 14.2; Heidelberg Q60-61; WSC Q86). The panel's most recurrent CENTRAL charge, raised against chapters 7 (Gospel), 9 (The Name), 20 (Knowing/Faith/Belief), 30 (Fear of the LORD), 31 (Path to Salvation), and the glossary's Belief/Faith entries.

**Book:** To believe IS to obey: Romans 10:16 makes 'obeyed' and 'believed' one act under two names; John 3:36's apeitheo shows the unbeliever is the disobeyer; the glossary defines belief as 'to obey' and faith as 'faithfulness — trusting obedience that acts.' Calling on the name, fearing God, loving God, and believing collapse into one covenant posture: trusting obedience to the King's terms.

**Their scriptures:** Rom 4:4-5; Rom 3:28; Gal 2:16; Gal 3:12; John 6:28-29; Eph 2:8-9; Titus 3:5; Rom 11:6; Acts 16:30-31

**Their argument:** At full strength: the confession is surgical — God justifies 'not by imputing faith itself... or any other evangelical obedience,' faith being 'the alone instrument.' Paul's antithesis is deliberate: 'to him that WORKETH NOT, but believeth on him that justifieth the ungodly, his faith is counted for righteousness' (Rom 4:5); 'the law is not of faith' (Gal 3:12); when asked what works to do, Jesus answers that the work of God is to believe (John 6:29). The tradition concedes the lexical point (unbelief is disobedience to the command to believe; true faith always fruits in obedience) but denies the collapse: if believing simply IS obeying the law of the kingdom, then law-keeping becomes the instrument of justification, 'grace is no more grace' (Rom 11:6), assurance is destroyed, and what Paul tore down is built again.

**Rebuttal:** Paul writes the book's equation himself: 'But they have not all OBEYED the gospel. For Esaias saith, Lord, who hath BELIEVED our report?' (Rom 10:16, verified KJV) — obeyed glossed by believed inside one verse. Hebrews swaps the words within two: they 'believed not' (3:18, apeitheo, G544 — Strong's own kjv_def: 'not believe, disobedient, obey not') / 'could not enter in because of unbelief' (3:19). The judgment texts require the fusion: vengeance falls on 'them that obey not the gospel' (2 Thess 1:8; 1 Pet 4:17), and Christ is 'the author of eternal salvation unto all them that OBEY him' (Heb 5:9); 'obedience of faith' bookends Romans (1:5; 16:26). Rom 4:5's 'worketh' is defined by its own preceding verse — working for wages, 'the reward not reckoned of grace, but of DEBT' (4:4): what is excluded is the wage-claim, not the obedience-shape of trust. The exhibit of Romans 4 is the man of whom God says 'Abraham OBEYED my voice, and kept my charge, my commandments, my statutes, and my laws' (Gen 26:5), and Hebrews defines his faith by the act: 'By faith Abraham... obeyed' (11:8). John 6:29 — the tradition's own proof-text — calls believing 'the WORK of God,' refusing the work/faith wall in the very sentence quoted to build it. James closes the loop on the same Gen 15:6 the tradition cites: 'by works a man is justified, and not by faith only' (Jas 2:24).

### [PARTIAL] The newness of the new covenant and the abrogation of the ceremonial law — feasts, new moons, sabbaths as expired administration (WCF 7.5-6, 19.3, 21.7; WLC Q33-35; Heidelberg Q103). The panel's most widespread cluster: chapters 5, 7, 24, 25, 26, 28, 29, 31, all CENTRAL or SIGNIFICANT.

**Book:** Chadash means renew: the 'new' covenant is the same covenant renewed — same law, same terms; what changes is the writing surface (stone to heart) and the priesthood. Matt 5:17's 'fulfil' means fill full, not retire. Col 2:16's 'let no man judge you' protects the feast-keepers from the ascetic judges, and the feasts 'ARE' (present tense) a shadow of things still to come. The covenant signs are declared perpetual; Isa 66:23 and Zech 14:16 put new moons, sabbaths, and tabernacles in the age to come.

**Their scriptures:** Jer 31:32; Heb 8:13; Heb 7:11-19; Heb 9:9-10; Heb 10:1-18; 2 Cor 3:7-11; Col 2:14-17; Gal 4:9-11; Acts 15:10, 28-29; Rom 14:5-6

**Their argument:** At full strength: Jeremiah's own sentence asserts discontinuity — 'NOT according to the covenant that I made with their fathers' — and the inspired commentary draws the conclusion the book refuses: 'In that he saith, A new covenant, he hath made the first old. Now that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away' (Heb 8:13). The newness is a changed priesthood necessitating 'a change also of the law' (7:12), meats, drinks, and carnal ordinances 'imposed until the time of reformation' (9:10), a stone-engraven ministration 'done away' (2 Cor 3:7-11), and shadows whose office ends when the body arrives (Col 2:17). Paul fears for those who 'observe days, and months, and times, and years' (Gal 4:10-11), and the council laid no calendrical yoke on the nations.

**Rebuttal:** Ask the promise itself what is new: 'I will put MY LAW in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts' (Jer 31:33) — the covenant Hebrews calls better is quoted with Torah as its first clause (Heb 8:10, verified). 'Not according to' is explained by the next words: 'which my covenant they BRAKE' — Heb 8:8 finds fault 'with THEM,' not with the law. Chadash (H2318, Strong's: 'renew, repair') is the word of Lam 3:23's mercies 'new every morning' and Ps 104:30's 'thou renewest the face of the earth' — the same thing, renewed. Heb 7:12's changed law sits inside an argument about priestly descent (7:13-14, Judah barred from the altar), and Heb 9:10's 'meats and drinks and divers washings' are the tabernacle's own cultic ordinances (9:9, 'gifts and sacrifices') — grant every word, and the feasts and sabbath stand untouched, for the sabbath predates Levi (Gen 2; Ex 16). Col 2 names its own judges: 'touch not; taste not; handle not... after the commandments and doctrines of MEN' (2:21-22) — ascetic intruders, not Moses — and calls the feasts a shadow of things TO COME, still future when Paul wrote. Apostolic practice agrees: 'I must by all means keep this feast' (Acts 18:21 KJV); 'let us keep the feast' (1 Cor 5:8); flight prayed against 'on the sabbath day' for a generation forty years past the cross (Matt 24:20); Moses read 'every sabbath day' as the council's assumed school (Acts 15:21). And the prophets seat the calendar in the new creation: 'from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship' (Isa 66:23); tabernacles for the nations on pain of no rain (Zech 14:16-17). Residue conceded: Heb 8:13's 'old... ready to vanish away' remains the tradition's strongest sentence, and the Gal 4:8-10 pagan-calendar reading (v. 8, 'ye did service unto them which by nature are no gods') is plausible but not compelled by the letter's Judaizing frame.

### [PARTIAL] The definition of the gospel as Christ's death and resurrection for sinners, distinguished from the law (1 Cor 15:1-4; Second Helvetic ch. 13; WLC Q72; Machen against liberalism). Raised as CENTRAL against chapters 7, 24, 29, and the glossary's Gospel entry.

**Book:** The gospel is the herald's cry that God reigns — the message Jesus taught, not merely the message about Jesus; its content is the kingdom and its terms, the renewed covenant's law. The cross purchases the kingdom, 'but the purchase is not the proclamation,' and the standard evangelical summary is 'a true part wearing the name of the whole.' Nahum 1:15, twin of Isa 52:7, tells the hearers what to do with the news: 'keep thy solemn feasts.'

**Their scriptures:** 1 Cor 15:1-4; 1 Cor 1:17-18, 23; 1 Cor 2:2; Rom 1:16-17; Gal 1:6-9; Luke 24:46-47; Acts 20:24; Mark 10:45

**Their argument:** At full strength: Paul names the death-burial-resurrection message 'the gospel which I preached... by which also ye are saved,' delivered 'first of all'; he resolves to know nothing but Christ crucified and equates the preaching of the cross with the gospel's saving power. The risen Lord defines the message for all nations as his death, resurrection, and remission of sins in his name (Luke 24:46-47). Making Torah the gospel's terms is precisely the 'other gospel' Galatians anathematizes, and the 'gospel Jesus taught vs. gospel about Jesus' antithesis is Harnack's move, which Machen answered: the NT knows no gospel of Jesus that is not the gospel about Him.

**Rebuttal:** The corpus will not let 'gospel' shrink to the passion summary. Jesus preached 'the gospel of the kingdom of God... repent ye, and believe the gospel' (Mark 1:14-15) before one word about the cross had been spoken — whatever Galilee was commanded to believe, it was not yet death-burial-resurrection. The same 'gospel of the kingdom' runs to the end of the age (Matt 24:14); it was 'preached... as well as unto them' — the wilderness generation (Heb 4:2); preached 'before... unto Abraham' (Gal 3:8); and the everlasting gospel of Rev 14:6-7 is 'Fear God... worship him that made heaven, and earth.' Paul's own summaries hold both: 'the gospel of the grace of God' and 'preaching the kingdom of God' describe one ministry in adjacent verses (Acts 20:24-25), and the NT's history closes with him 'preaching the kingdom of God, AND teaching those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ' (Acts 28:31); Philip preached 'the things concerning the kingdom of God, AND the name of Jesus Christ' (Acts 8:12). Even 1 Cor 15:3's 'first of all' marks leading facts, not the whole circumference — and 15:2 conditions salvation on 'IF ye keep in memory,' terms attached in the tradition's own proof-text. The Galatian anathema targets a different basis of justification ('except ye be circumcised... ye cannot be saved,' Acts 15:1), not the King's law as the kingdom's constitution: 'do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we establish the law' (Rom 3:31). Residue conceded: 1 Cor 1:17-18 does bind the cross to the gospel's saving power, Luke 24:46-47 is the risen Lord's own commission-summary, and no apostle is recorded preaching the calendar as gospel content — the Nahum 1:15 inference outruns its NT attestation.

### [PARTIAL] Justification by the imputed righteousness of Christ, not righteousness performed (WCF 11.1; Heidelberg Q60-62; WSC Q33). Raised as CENTRAL against chapters 15 (Garments), 22 (Light and Darkness), 27 (Justice and Judgment), 21 (oil as works), 1 (Introduction), and the glossary's Righteousness entry ('not a verdict only received').

**Book:** The wedding garment is righteousness performed — 'the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints' (Rev 19:8); righteousness is defined by Deut 6:25 as observing all the commandments; the KJV's narrowed 'righteousness of saints' bred the confusion in which righteousness became a state merely had. Isa 64:6 does not disqualify the garment: it is not the righteous act that is filthy but the sin it is worked in — so the answer to a dirty robe is to wash it (in the Lamb's blood), not to take it off; those who neither weave nor wash are found bare (Rev 3:4; 16:15; 22:14).

**Their scriptures:** Phil 3:8-9; Rom 4:4-8; Rom 3:21-28; 2 Cor 5:21; Isa 61:10; Isa 64:6; Zech 3:3-5; Gal 3:27; Rev 7:14; Luke 15:22

**Their argument:** At full strength: Rev 19:8's own grammar says the linen 'was GIVEN unto her' — adornment of a standing already conferred, not the ground of admission. Deut 6:25's law-righteousness is precisely what Paul renounces as 'mine own righteousness, which is of the law' to be found in Christ's (Phil 3:9); 'God imputeth righteousness without works' (Rom 4:6); our best works are 'defiled and mixed with so much weakness' that Heidelberg Q62 draws its conclusion from Isa 64:6 itself. Joshua's filthy garments are TAKEN AWAY and replaced, not laundered (Zech 3:4-5); the prodigal's robe is brought forth before his confession ends; the saints' robes are made white in the blood of the Lamb, not in the wearer's diligence. 'To be clothed is to have done it' is justification by performed works — the covenant of works rebuilt.

**Rebuttal:** Rev 19:8 defines its own linen: dikaiomata ton hagion (G1345 — Strong's: 'an equitable deed'; Thayer: 'a righteous act or deed') — the saints' righteous acts, as the ASV renders. 'Granted that she should ARRAY HERSELF' holds gift and deed in one clause — the exact grammar of Phil 2:12-13 ('work out your own salvation... for it is God which worketh in you') and Eph 2:10 (good works God 'before ordained that we should walk in them'), and of Ezek 16:9-10, where God both washes and clothes the covenant wife with the fine linen she then wears. Scripture defines the word the tradition leaves undefined: 'it shall be our righteousness, IF we observe to do all these commandments' (Deut 6:25); 'all thy commandments are righteousness' (Ps 119:172); and it shows the garment worn: Zacharias and Elisabeth 'righteous before God, walking in all the commandments and ordinances of the Lord blameless' (Luke 1:6). Isa 64:6 is read from its own context: v. 5 blesses him 'that rejoiceth and WORKETH RIGHTEOUSNESS' while vv. 5-7 describe a nation in unrepented sin with 'none that calleth upon thy name' — a rag-verdict on that condition, or the chapter contradicts itself in adjacent verses. Zech 3 is iniquity REMOVED ('I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee') — which is washing, not wardrobe-swap — followed immediately by terms: 'IF thou wilt walk in my ways, and if thou wilt keep my charge' (3:7). And Rom 4:6-8 glosses its own 'imputeth righteousness' by Ps 32: iniquities forgiven, sin not imputed — imputation, on Paul's own definition, is the cleansed record, not a substitute robe. 1 John 3:7 posts the warning in advance: 'let no man deceive you: he that DOETH righteousness is righteous.' Residue conceded: Phil 3:9 is not yet answered at full force — Paul, blameless by Luke 1:6's own standard (Phil 3:6), still counts that righteousness loss to gain 'the righteousness which is of God by faith'; the book needs a printed reading of Philippians 3 before this grade can rise.

### [PARTIAL] Solus Christus and eternal life not attainable by law-keeping: Christ the only mediator by his person and atoning work, not the law personified (WCF 8.1-5; Heidelberg Q29-30; Gal 3:21; Rom 10:5-8). Raised as CENTRAL against chapter 8 (Way, Truth, Life) and SIGNIFICANT against 16 (Wings) and 31 (priesthood 'established by the law').

**Book:** Jesus' three titles are three Torah-symbols — the way is covenant-conduct, the truth is the law, the life is covenant-existence — so He is the Torah embodied, the standard walking; 'no man cometh unto the Father, but by me' names the one road there has ever been, now walked perfectly and thrown open. The narrow way is Torah-shaped conduct that leads to life; the bleeding woman reached for the law made flesh and laid hold of its corner (the tzitzit), for Malachi puts healing 'in his wings' (kanaph).

**Their scriptures:** John 14:1-6 in context; John 10:9; Heb 10:19-20; 1 Tim 2:5-6; Gal 2:21; Gal 3:21; Rom 10:5-8; Mark 5:30, 34; Matt 23:5

**Their argument:** At full strength: in context Jesus answers Thomas about where He goes and how they will come — through His death, resurrection, and return; Heb 10:19-20 names the way as 'the blood of Jesus' and 'his flesh' — atonement, not exemplified law-keeping. The one mediator is such because he 'gave himself a ransom,' and 'if righteousness come by the law, then Christ is dead in vain.' Paul expounds the very Deut 30 text the book cites for the law's walkability AGAINST that use (Rom 10:5-8). At the healing, Jesus adjudicates on the spot: 'virtue is gone out of ME... thy faith hath made thee whole' — the hem was contact, not reservoir; the Pharisees' enlarged borders carried no healing. Making the mediator a model converts grace into law.

**Rebuttal:** The triad is Torah's before it is anyone's, by cross-text definition: 'thy LAW is the TRUTH' (Ps 119:142); 'I have chosen the WAY of truth' (119:30); 'the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the WAY of LIFE' (Prov 6:23); 'he is thy LIFE' said of YHWH (Deut 30:20) in the same breath as 'it is your life' said of the law's words (Deut 32:47). John 1:14 — the Word made flesh — is the claim itself; embodiment does not compete with mediation, it grounds it: 'my doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me' (John 7:16); 'his commandment IS life everlasting' (12:50). Heb 10:20's blood-opened way is 'new and LIVING' — opened, then walked: 'let us draw near.' And the gate-saying quoted against the book convicts the book's target instead: those refused at the narrow way's end are 'ye that work INIQUITY' — anomia, G458, Strong's: 'transgression of the law' — men who cried 'Lord, Lord' but did not do 'the will of my Father' (Matt 7:21-23). Rom 8:3-4 supplies the order the tradition says the book lacks: God condemned sin in the flesh 'THAT the righteousness of the law might be FULFILLED IN US, who walk not after the flesh.' On the fringe: faith and tassel are not rivals — Num 15:39 says the fringe exists that a man may 'remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them,' and Mal 4:2 hangs healing 'in his WINGS' (kanaph, H3671, the garment-corner word); her faith is what reached, the kanaph is where it reached (Matt 9:21; 14:36). Residue conceded: Mark 5:30 locates the power in His person, and Matt 23:5's enlarged borders healed no one — the chapter should print the guard that the tassel is where faith touched, not what healed; and John 14:2-3's immediate context (departure and return) is read past rather than absorbed.

### [STRONG] The perfection of Christ's one sacrifice for all sins of His people, and the perseverance of the saints (WCF 8.5, 11.3, 17.1-2; Canons of Dort V; Heidelberg Q1). Raised as CENTRAL twice against chapter 31 (Path to Salvation): blood covers only unintentional and repented sins; the saved can be condemned by willful, unrepentant lawbreaking.

**Book:** Via Numbers 15:27-31, Yeshua's high-priestly blood covers unintentional sins and sins repented of; the presumptuous, high-handed sinner who despises the word is cut off from the atonement while he so stands. Hebrews 10:26 is willful sin after knowledge, and 'the penalty for sinning after being healed is worse than if we had never been healed' — the saved can be condemned by willful, unrepentant breach and denial of the commands.

**Their scriptures:** 2 Sam 12:13 with Ps 51; 1 John 1:7-9; 1 John 2:1-2; Heb 10:14; Col 2:13-14; John 10:27-29; Rom 8:33-39; 1 John 2:19; Phil 1:6; Jer 32:40

**Their argument:** At full strength: David's adultery and murder were high-handed sins in full knowledge of the law, yet 'the LORD hath put away thy sin'; the blood 'cleanseth us from ALL sin' and 'all unrighteousness'; 'by one offering he hath perfected FOR EVER them that are sanctified' (Heb 10:14). Hebrews 10:26-29 describes final apostasy — trampling the Son — not a Levitical grid limiting the cross to inadvertencies. Those given to the Son 'shall never perish'; the golden chain of Rom 8:30 loses no one; apparent apostates 'were not of us' (1 John 2:19); warnings are means by which God preserves the elect; and the book's own citation of Jer 32:40 — 'I will put my fear in their hearts, that they shall not depart from me' — is the tradition's proof that God secures perseverance.

**Rebuttal:** List the tradition's exhibits and watch them file into the book's own two categories. David: 'I have sinned against the LORD' (2 Sam 12:13), a broken and contrite heart (Ps 51:17) — forgiven UPON repentance, the book's second class exactly; so Manasseh, Peter, the Corinthian. 1 John 1:9's cleansing is governed by its own IF — 'IF we confess our sins' — and 2:1's Advocate follows 1:9's confessors. Not one counter-text shows the blood covering an unrepented high hand — and the tradition does not actually claim it does; it claims the elect will always repent, which is a perseverance thesis, not a statement about the atonement's scope, so the scope-counter dissolves on inspection. On perseverance, the book's contested sentence is Scripture nearly verbatim: 'the latter end is worse with them than the beginning. For it had been better for them not to have known the way of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment' (2 Pet 2:20-21) — said of those who had 'escaped the pollutions of the world through the knowledge of the Lord and Saviour.' Heb 10:26-29 builds its a fortiori on Moses: 'he that despised Moses' law died without mercy... of how much sorer punishment' — spoken of one 'sanctified' by the blood he tramples, which is the Numbers 15 frame in apostolic hand. John 10:28's 'neither shall any MAN pluck them' guards against plunder, and v. 27 defines the sheep as those who 'hear my voice... and follow me'; Rom 8:38-39's list is external powers — neither text addresses the man who stops following. Ezek 33:13 states the doctrine in God's first person: 'When I shall say to the righteous, that he shall surely live; if he trust to his own righteousness, and commit iniquity... he shall die for it.' 'He that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved' (Matt 24:13). The counters, read in context, either presuppose repentance (conceding the grid) or answer a different question; the sole live residue is Heb 10:14's 'perfected for ever,' which its own chapter qualifies twelve verses later.

### [PARTIAL] The full deity and aseity of the Son — 'Light of Light, very God of very God' (Nicene Creed; WCF 2.3, 8.2; Calvin's autotheos). Raised as CENTRAL against chapter 23 (Sun, Moon, and Stars) and the glossary's Moon entry.

**Book:** The moon is the Son: 'the faithful witness' (Ps 89:37; Rev 1:5) 'who shines with no light of His own but the Father's reflected' — 'light reflected, not self-made, the Father's light carried faithfully into the dark' — while the sun-Father 'alone shines of Himself; all other light is His light, handed on.'

**Their scriptures:** John 1:4, 9; John 5:26; John 8:12; John 17:5; Heb 1:3; Col 2:9; Mal 4:2; Rev 21:23

**Their argument:** At full strength: Nicene orthodoxy confesses the Son as begotten light of the same essence, not a creature-like reflector of light foreign to its nature. Scripture assigns Christ the sun's own titles — 'the Sun of righteousness' (Mal 4:2), the apaugasma of the Father's glory as a sunbeam of the sun (Heb 1:3), having 'life in himself' (John 5:26), the true Light in whom was life (John 1:4, 9), the Lamb who replaces the sun as the city's light (Rev 21:23). John 5:19 describes economic obedience in the mediatorial office; pressed as ontology, the moon-figure is the graded, borrowed-light cosmology 'Light of Light' was written to exclude.

**Rebuttal:** The counters prove less than claimed at the very word level they cite. Heb 1:3's apaugasma (G541) is glossed by Thayer — the tradition's own lexicon — FIRST as 'reflected brightness... of Christ in that he perfectly reflects the majesty of God'; the effulgence-versus-reflection wall is not in the lexica. Rev 21:23's Greek distinguishes source from carrier in one verse: the city's light is 'the glory of God' and the Lamb is its luchnos (G3088, 'a portable lamp') — the KJV's 'the Lamb is the light thereof' translates 'lamp': glory as source, the Lamb bearing it — the moon-figure's exact structure, in the tradition's own proof-text. John 5:26 says the Son's life-in-himself is GIVEN ('so hath he GIVEN to the Son to have life in himself'), and given-ness is what 'Light of Light, begotten not made' itself confesses — received light of the same essence. The Son's self-descriptions carry the moon's grammar throughout John: 'The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do' (5:19); 'my doctrine is not mine' (7:16); 'I have not spoken of myself' (12:49-50). And Ps 89:37 with Rev 1:5 hands the identical title — 'faithful witness' — to the moon and to the Son. Residue conceded plainly: the glossary's flat 'no light of His own' is broader than John will bear — 'In HIM was life; and the life was the LIGHT of men' (1:4), 'the TRUE Light' (1:9), 'the glory which I HAD with thee before the world was' (17:5). Moonlight is a created surface's extrinsic reflection; apaugasma is radiance of the same essence. One printed guard sentence — that the Son receives as the only-begotten receives, of the Father's own essence, not as a mirror receives — would close the Arian door the current wording leaves ajar. This is the book's most cheaply fixable CENTRAL vulnerability.

### [PARTIAL] The abrogation of the food laws and Christian liberty in meats (WCF 19.3, 20.1). Raised as CENTRAL against chapter 13 (Trees): 'the name is eternal, the law is eternal, and the diet is eternal with them.'

**Book:** Isaiah 66:17 places judgment on eaters of swine's flesh at the end of the age, so the clean/unclean line holds to the end; Acts 10's vision is about nations, not menu — Peter never eats, and Peter himself supplies the interpretation.

**Their scriptures:** Mark 7:15, 18-19; Acts 10:15 with 11:9; Acts 15:10, 28-29; Rom 14:14, 20; 1 Tim 4:1-5; Heb 9:9-10; Gal 2:11-16; Eph 2:14-15; Isa 65:3-4

**Their argument:** At full strength: Jesus declared all foods clean ('purging all meats,' Mark 7:19); 'What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common' is spoken of the animals in the sheet — the sign works only because the food-symbol itself is cleansed; 'commanding to abstain from meats' is a doctrine of apostasy since 'every creature of God is good' (1 Tim 4:3-5); the council refused the yoke; 'there is nothing unclean of itself' (Rom 14:14); meats were 'imposed until the time of reformation' (Heb 9:10); Isa 66:17's swine-eating is an idolatrous graveyard cult (65:3-4), not Leviticus 11 binding the church; an eternal diet rebuilds the middle wall Christ abolished.

**Rebuttal:** Each flagship text answers a different question than the one asked of it. Mark 7's controversy is stated twice in its own frame: disciples 'eat bread with... UNWASHEN HANDS' (7:2, 5) — the tradition of the elders, not Leviticus; the KJV's received text reads the participle as the body's process ('goeth out into the draught, purging all meats,' 7:19 verified), and 'thus he declared all foods clean' rests on the later critical text's masculine participle — a translation-layer verdict, not a red-letter one. In Acts 10 the interpretation is given on the page by the man who saw the vision: 'God hath shewed me that I should not call any MAN common or unclean' (10:28) — Peter never eats, and a vision's furniture is not legislation (Jeremiah's boiling pot regulated no cookware). 1 Tim 4 bounds itself: meats 'which God hath created TO BE RECEIVED... sanctified by the WORD OF GOD' (4:3, 5) — the word is what marks the receivable, and the forbidders also forbid marriage: ascetics, not Moses, who never commanded abstinence from what God gave. Acts 15's yoke is quoted in its own v. 1 — 'except ye be CIRCUMCISED... ye cannot be saved' — and the council's four abstentions are themselves Leviticus 17-18's stranger-laws, with 'Moses... read in the synagogues every sabbath day' (15:21) as the assumed continuing school. Rom 14's weak eat 'HERBS' (14:2) — a scruple Leviticus never taught, but idol-markets did (1 Cor 8) — and 14:14's word is koinos (G2839, 'common'), not akathartos (G169, 'unclean'): Peter keeps the two words distinct in one sentence (Acts 10:14, 'common OR unclean'), so the verse waives contact-defilement, not the creation-categories. Isa 66:17 then stands where the book put it: swine-eaters 'consumed together' at the end of the age. Residue conceded: Mark 7:15's own universal — 'there is NOTHING from without a man, that entering into him can defile him' — is a dominical principle the hand-washing frame narrows but does not erase; the book's reading of 'nothing' is arguable but is doing heavy lifting, and should be argued in print rather than assumed.

### [PARTIAL] The perspicuity of Scripture against esoteric keys, the single sense of Scripture, and the Spirit's necessary illumination (WCF 1.6-1.9; Belgic 7; Luther's external clarity). Raised as SIGNIFICANT against chapters 1, 3, 5, and 16 — the method itself: Scripture deliberately encoded, recoverable by a deterministic process, with salvation-weighted threads legible only beneath the English.

**Book:** Scripture is encoded line by line so that those who will not hear (obey) will hear (sound) but not understand; the church is on milk while the meat is the symbolic language 'kept secret from the foundation of the world'; the decoding process is deterministic and objective — demonstrated on an AI — and threads like kanaph bind covenant, commandment, garment, and healing beneath the English.

**Their scriptures:** WCF 1.7 with Ps 19:7; Ps 119:130; 2 Tim 3:15; 2 Cor 4:2-4; Rom 16:25-26; 1 Cor 2:14; Luke 24:45; Acts 16:14; WCF 1.9

**Their argument:** At full strength: all things necessary for salvation are so clearly propounded that the unlearned, in due use of ordinary means, may attain sufficient understanding; Paul renounced hidden things, commending himself 'by manifestation of the truth'; Matt 13's concealment was judicial hardening of that generation, and the mystery 'kept secret since the world began' is expressly NOW made manifest to all nations. Saving understanding is the Spirit's gift, not a procedure's output — a hermeneutic executable by a machine catalogues correlations but cannot yield what the psalmist prays for. A standing key the whole church lacked for two millennia re-hides what God unveiled — the structural move of esotericism the Reformation always rejected; and the many-layered symbol-senses collide with the one-sense rule that makes sola scriptura functional.

**Rebuttal:** The concealment is not the book's theory but the Lord's stated practice: 'without a parable spake he not unto them: and when they were alone, he expounded all things to his disciples' (Mark 4:34) — a standing two-tier delivery; 'unto them that are without, all these things are done in parables: THAT seeing they may see, and not perceive' (Mark 4:11-12); 'I will utter things which have been KEPT SECRET from the foundation of the world' (Matt 13:35, quoting Ps 78:2's 'dark sayings of old'). Isaiah says the word was made 'precept upon precept; line upon line... THAT they might go, and fall backward, and be broken' (28:13) — encoding as judgment, in the prophet's own grammar. 'It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter' (Prov 25:2); words 'closed up and sealed till the time of the end,' when 'none of the wicked shall understand; but the wise shall understand' (Dan 12:9-10) — hiddenness with an expiry date and a moral filter, the book's claim exactly. The two tiers survive the resurrection: 'we speak wisdom among them that are PERFECT... the wisdom of God in a MYSTERY' (1 Cor 2:6-7); milk against strong meat for 'them that are of full age' (Heb 5:12-14); 'if our gospel be hid, it is hid to them that are lost' (2 Cor 4:3). Rom 16:25-26's 'now made manifest' is manifest 'BY THE SCRIPTURES OF THE PROPHETS... for the OBEDIENCE of faith' — through the prophets, to the obedient: the book's method and its condition in one verse. And WCF 1.7 itself confesses 'all things in Scripture are not alike plain,' claiming clarity only for things necessary to salvation — the milk-tier the book affirms. Residue conceded: the tradition's sharpest thrust is unmet where the book makes symbolic content salvation-weighted (the name, the garment, the diet); to the degree salvation hangs on what only the decoding recovers, WCF 1.7 and 2 Cor 4:2 press hard, and the book should state in print which tier salvation lives on — and that the method's determinism concerns verification (anyone can check the cross-texts), not a substitute for the Spirit-opened heart it elsewhere assumes.

### [PARTIAL] The authentic text: the Greek NT as immediately inspired and final, and the Masoretic pointing as bound (or at least its force) into the authentical Hebrew (WCF 1.8; Helvetic Consensus Canons I-III). Raised as SIGNIFICANT against chapters 1, 24, and 29 — the NT as bearing a Hebrew substrate, vowel points as a late human overlay, and a late Hebrew Revelation cited as second witness.

**Book:** The NT's Greek has passed through one layer of translation from a Hebrew-speaking world; the vowel points are late and human, so consonant-permitted readings and deliberate double meanings may stand against the received pointing (kisse'/keseh; helel as bringer of darkness); the Cochin Hebrew NT serves as a second witness for symbol-words.

**Their scriptures:** 2 Tim 3:16; Matt 5:18; 2 Pet 3:15-16; WCF 1.8-1.9; the ancient versions (LXX, Targum, Vulgate) reading 'throne' and 'I will be like' centuries before the pointing

**Their argument:** At full strength: final appeal lies in the Greek NT 'kept pure in all ages... therefore authentical'; the apostles wrote Greek to Greek churches and Peter ranks Paul's Greek letters with 'the other scriptures'; the extant Hebrew Matthews are medieval; treating the canonical Greek as lossy strips the church of an authentical text. And once the reading tradition may be overridden wherever bare consonants permit a preferred symbol, the text loses its one determinate sense and becomes a wax nose with the interpreter as sovereign — every ancient version read kisse' as throne before the Masoretes pointed it.

**Rebuttal:** On the points, the New Testament itself takes the book's side: Heb 11:21 has dying Jacob worshipping 'upon the top of his STAFF' where Gen 47:31's Masoretic pointing reads 'BED' — the same consonants (matteh H4294 / mittah H4296, both from natah), vocalized two ways — an inspired author reading the consonants against what later hardened into the received pointing (both verses verified in the KJV corpus, which translates each context's choice). The pointing is post-apostolic by centuries as public fact; Matt 5:18's jot and tittle are consonantal strokes — the promise of preservation runs precisely to the letters the book studies; and the Helvetic Consensus's vowel-canon was vacated by the Reformed tradition's own house within a century, so the counter enforces a confession its authors' heirs abandoned. On the substrate, Scripture flags it internally: the risen Lord speaks to Paul 'in the HEBREW tongue' (Acts 26:14); Paul stills Jerusalem in Hebrew (22:2); John pauses to give the key names 'in the Hebrew tongue' (John 19:13, 17, 20) — and Revelation, the very book at issue, names its own terms in Hebrew inside a Greek text (Rev 9:11 'in the Hebrew tongue Abaddon'; 16:16 'in the Hebrew tongue Armageddon'). Reading the Greek with the Hebrew beneath it is not conjectural emendation of the text; it is using a substrate as the church has always used versions — as witnesses to sense, with the canonical text retained. Residue conceded: the tradition's structural warning stands — 'consonant-permitted' is a far weaker constraint than 'NT-attested' (Heb 11:21 is warranted by an inspired author; a re-pointed helel is warranted by the method alone), and the book has not printed its stopping rule. Until it states what disciplines a re-vocalization (ancient-version support, contextual necessity, cross-text convergence), the wax-nose charge will keep finding purchase; and a c. 1700 Hebrew Revelation should be cited as an interpretive echo, never seated as textual authority.

### [EXPOSED] The innumerable multitude of the redeemed, the warrant of assurance, and the canon (Rev 7:9; WCF 18.1-2; WCF 1.3 on the Apocrypha). Raised against chapter 19 (The Remnant): the ~0.21% converged ratio, 'the deadliest thing in the world is the comfortable assumption,' and 2 Esdras tabulated as a witness.

**Book:** Scripture's own figures converge on a saved remnant near one in five hundred — Elijah's seven thousand, tribute arithmetic, gleaning ratios, ore grades, land capacity — roughly seventeen million of eight billion; the numbers are given to produce urgency, and comfortable assumption of safety is the deadliest thing in the world.

**Their scriptures:** Rev 7:9; Rev 5:9; Gen 15:5 and 22:17 with Gal 3:29; Rom 11:26, 32; 1 John 5:13; Rom 8:15-16, 35-39; 2 Pet 1:10; WCF 1.3 with Luke 24:44

**Their argument:** At full strength: the consummated church is deliberately presented as beyond census — 'a great multitude, which no man could number' — and the star-and-sand promise belongs, per Gal 3:29, to the believing seed, so the very figure the book assigns to the lost majority is in Genesis a promise about the saved. Elijah's 7,000 and Jeremiah's one-of-a-city are remnants inside apostate Israel at historical low ebbs, not a quota across all ages. Deriving a divine percentage from tribute rolls, olive yields, gold assays, and lion demography is arithmetic imposed on Scripture, not exegesis; tabulating 2 Esdras as a numbered doctrinal witness breaches sola scriptura at the methodological level. And a piety in which every believer must presume himself probably lost empties 1 John 5:13 and the Spirit of adoption of their intended comfort.

**Rebuttal:** The few-texts are dominical and quantitative in form: 'strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and FEW there be that find it' (Matt 7:14), given as the answer-shape to the direct question 'Lord, are there few that be saved?' — 'many... will seek to enter in, and shall not be able' (Luke 13:23-24); 'many are called, but few chosen' (Matt 22:14; 20:16); and it is Paul, not the book, who sets the remnant against the sand-multitude: 'Though the number of the children of Israel be as the sand of the sea, A REMNANT shall be saved' (Rom 9:27, quoting Isa 10:22); 'I will take you one of a city, and two of a family' (Jer 3:14). Rev 7:9's 'no man could number' states what MAN can count — the same vision numbers the sealed exactly in v. 4, and seventeen million of every nation, kindred, and tongue in one place is precisely a crowd no man could number. On assurance, the urgency is apostolic idiom: 'give DILIGENCE to make your calling and election sure' (2 Pet 1:10), and 1 John's 'that ye may KNOW' (5:13) rests on the tests the book preaches — 'hereby we do know that we know him, IF we keep his commandments' (2:3); 'we know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren' (3:14): Johannine assurance is diagnostic, not presumptive, which is the chapter's point against comfortable assumption. But the honest verdict: these texts carry 'few' — they do not carry a PERCENTAGE. The step from few to 0.21% runs through tribute rolls, ore grades, land density, and lion demography, none of which Scripture offers as census instruments; Gal 3:29's assignment of the star-sand seed to those in Christ goes unengaged; and seating 2 Esdras as a numbered line in the convergence table hands a sola-scriptura reader a methodological breach the chapter itself flags ('extra-canonical') and then commits. The pastoral core survives; the published arithmetic is the book's most exposed flank, and the tradition's blow lands.

### [EXPOSED] The lawfulness of divorce for the innocent party in adultery (WCF 24.5-6), against chapter 10's controlling premise that the porneia exception cannot mean adultery because adultery carried death, not divorce — so that reading it otherwise makes Jesus commute a death penalty against Matt 5:18.

**Book:** Jesus uses two words in one sentence — porneia the exception, moicheia the consequence — so the exception covers Joseph's betrothal case and unions unlawful at the root, not a spouse's adultery; if it meant adultery, Jesus 'quietly commuted a death penalty' twelve verses after 'one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass'; divorce is evil or obedience depending on which union it severs (Ezra 10).

**Their scriptures:** Jer 3:8; Matt 1:19; Matt 19:3-9; 1 Cor 5:1; John 8:11; John 18:31; 1 Cor 7:12-14

**Their argument:** At full strength: porneia is the genus of which moicheia is a species — Greek usage calls a wife's unfaithfulness porneia (Sirach 23:23) and incest porneia (1 Cor 5:1) — so Jesus chose the wider word to cover adultery, betrothal-unchastity, and unlawful unions together. The Pharisees' question concerns divorcing WIVES under Deut 24, and Jesus' exception answers that question. The alleged collision with Leviticus fails on the book's own evidence: Jewish courts in Jesus' day did not execute (John 18:31), Jesus Himself did not (John 8:11), and God, the Judge, answered His wife's ADULTERY with 'a bill of divorce' (Jer 3:8) — a verse the book quotes — while Joseph, expressly called 'a just man,' chose quiet divorce over prosecution (Matt 1:19), showing the law left death or divorce in the wronged party's hand. WCF 24.5 rests on Jesus' words, not a commutation of Moses.

**Rebuttal:** The two-word datum stands and is real evidence: in one sentence Jesus makes porneia the exception and moicheia the consequence (Matt 5:32; 19:9, verified) — He had the narrower word in His mouth and reserved it for the other clause. Deut 22:22 is unambiguous that adultery's statutory answer is death, Matt 5:18 stands twelve verses before the exception, and Joseph's case shows the clause working exactly where the book places it: a betrothal found defiled before the union, answered by putting away 'privily' (Matt 1:19) — porneia, not moicheia. Ezra 10:3 shows commanded putting-away of root-unlawful unions 'according to the law.' John 8:11 is a judge declining to convict when every lawful witness has filed out, not a commutation. But the honest verdict: Jeremiah 3:8 breaks the chapter's controlling premise on the book's own page — 'for all the causes whereby backsliding Israel committed ADULTERY I had put her away, and given her A BILL OF DIVORCE.' God, the wronged Husband and the Judge, answers adultery with divorce, not death; if divorce-for-adultery entailed commuting Lev 20:10, the LORD's own writ would convict Him. Matt 1:19 compounds it: Joseph is called JUST while choosing divorce over prosecution, so the law evidently left the remedy in the injured party's hand — which dissolves the death-or-nothing dilemma on which the chapter's reductio rests. The betrothal-and-unlawful-union reading of porneia may still be right, but the argument 'adultery carried death, therefore the exception cannot mean adultery' is refuted by the very verse the chapter cites, and the chapter as written does not answer it. The premise needs rebuilding (e.g., on the porneia/moicheia lexical split alone) or the tradition wins this exchange.


## Judaism (rabbinic) (12 conflicts: {'PARTIAL': 9, 'STRONG': 3})

### [PARTIAL] Absolute unity and incorporeality of God; worship, prayer, and divine prerogatives (stilling seas, sending winds, healing, pardoning) belong to HaShem alone, and directing any of them through a man is avodah zarah (Shema; Rambam's Principles 2, 3, 5; Deut 4:15-19; Deut 13; Sanhedrin 38b). Recurs as CENTRAL in chs. 4, 8, 9, 12, 16, 20, 23, 25, 26, 29, 31 — the panel's most frequent objection.

**Book:** Jesus performs what the Tanakh reserves to God (treading the sea, commanding winds, healing in his wings), bears the Name as the Angel of Exodus 23:20-21, is the Torah embodied and the one road to the Father — the celestial household even mapping Father/Son onto sun and moon.

**Their scriptures:** Deut 4:15-19; Deut 6:4; Num 23:19; Hos 11:9; Isa 42:8; Isa 44:6; Deut 13:1-4; Ex 33:15, 20; Ps 145:18; Deut 30:11-14

**Their argument:** At Horeb Israel saw no form precisely so that no figure could ever be pointed to as God; 'God is not a man' and His glory is given to no other. Deuteronomy 13 stipulates that even a wonder that comes to pass cannot warrant transferring worship to a new object — the sign-worker is the test, not the proof. Sanhedrin 38b already answers Exodus 23: the Name-bearing angel is the created Metatron, whom Israel must not receive in God's place, and 'he will not pardon' proves him less than God. No mediator was ever appointed: the word is near, and every penitent comes to the Father by words and turning alone.

**Rebuttal:** The Tanakh itself carries a second, Name-bearing figure it both distinguishes from and identifies with YHWH — and this is textual, not imported. Exodus 23:21: 'my name is in him.' Jacob blesses in one breath, with one verb: 'God, before whom my fathers... walked... The Angel which redeemed me from all evil, bless the lads' (Gen 48:15-16); Hosea says the one Jacob wrestled was 'the angel... even the LORD God of hosts' (Hos 12:4-5); the angel in the bush speaks as 'I am the God of thy father' and Moses hides his face 'for he was afraid to look upon God' (Ex 3:2-6); the captain of the LORD's host accepts Joshua's worship and repeats Sinai's shoe-command (Josh 5:14-15); the elders 'saw the God of Israel, and did eat and drink' (Ex 24:10-11); Ezekiel sees on the throne 'the likeness as the appearance of a man' (Ezek 1:26) — so a flat 'no form ever' reading of Deut 4:15 is contradicted by the canon's own theophanies. Decisive word-level check: in Daniel 7:14 the son of man receives pelach (H6399, 'to serve or worship') — verified against every occurrence in the corpus, this Aramaic verb is used only of cultic service: serving gods or God (Dan 3:12, 14, 17-18, 28; 6:16, 20; Ezra 7:24). Daniel deliberately gives the son of man the service his own book reserves for deity. Even Sanhedrin 38b concedes the category — a Name-bearer exists — and disputes only the identification. As for Deut 13: verified text disqualifies one who says 'let us go after OTHER gods, which thou hast not known' (13:2); the book's claimant points to the God of the fathers and His commandments, so whether honoring the canonical Name-bearer is 'another god' is the very question at issue — Deut 13 cannot settle it without assuming its own answer.

### [PARTIAL] No vicarious atonement by human blood: each soul answers for its own sin, teshuvah atones completely and directly, and human sacrifice is abomination (Deut 24:16; Ezek 18; Ps 49:7; Mishnah Yoma 8:8-9; Rambam Hilchot Teshuvah 1:3; Deut 12:31). Recurs as CENTRAL in the Introduction and chs. 5, 7, 10, 15, 17, 18, 21, 31, and the glossary.

**Book:** The debt of sin is settled at the cross: the kingdom is 'purchased,' the covenant 'ratified in blood,' robes 'washed white in the blood of the Lamb,' the Akedah a rehearsal of the Father offering the Son; keeping the commandments only stops digging the debt deeper.

**Their scriptures:** Deut 24:16; Ezek 18:20-23; Ps 49:7-8; Ex 32:32-33; Micah 6:7-8; Jonah 3:10; Hos 14:2-3; Isa 1:16-18; Deut 12:31

**Their argument:** God refused Moses' own offer to be blotted out for Israel; 'the soul that sinneth, it shall die' and 'none of them can by any means redeem his brother.' Nineveh is forgiven upon works of repentance with no blood; Hosea substitutes 'the calves of our lips' for bulls; Micah asks the book's question outright — 'shall I give my firstborn for my transgression?' — and answers with justice, mercy, and humility. The Akedah's climax is the knife stopped; a kingdom purchased by innocent human blood is the Molech-principle the Torah came to abolish.

**Rebuttal:** Vicarious bearing of iniquity is not foreign to Torah — it is a legislated Torah category. Lev 17:11 (verified): blood is given 'upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls.' Lev 10:17: the priests eat the sin offering 'to bear the iniquity of the congregation, to make atonement for them' — one party bears another's iniquity by God's own institution; likewise Ex 28:38, 'that Aaron may bear the iniquity of the holy things.' Substitution is Moriah's own vocabulary: Abraham offers the ram 'in the stead of his son' (Gen 22:13) after saying 'God will provide himself a lamb' (22:8) — the stopped knife teaches that God supplies the substitute, not that substitution is abolished. Ps 49:7 read whole answers itself: no MAN can redeem his brother — 'But GOD will redeem my soul from the power of the grave' (49:15); the psalm denies human ransom and asserts God's, which is precisely the book's claim about whose act the cross is. Deut 24:16 governs human courts ('the fathers shall not be PUT TO DEATH for the children'), and Ezek 18 answers the sour-grapes proverb — involuntary inherited guilt — not a voluntary, God-appointed bearing; indeed the same historian records that David's sin was 'put away' yet a death was still exacted (2 Sam 12:13-14). Micah 6:7 rejects the Molech grammar — a worshipper giving HIS OWN child for HIS OWN sin; Isa 53 (verified) is its inversion: 'the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all' (53:6), 'when thou shalt make his soul an offering for sin [asham]... he shall prolong his days' (53:10), 'he shall bear their iniquities... he bare the sin of many' (53:11-12) — God, not man, makes the offering, and the offerer is willing. Jonah 3:10 granted Nineveh a temporal reprieve (Nahum records the city's later fall), not entry into the kingdom. Residue conceded: Ezek 18:21-22 and Hos 14:2 do promise life to the one who turns without naming a substitute, and the whole vicarious weight finally rests on Isaiah 53 being an individual who dies as an asham — a reading the counter-texts do not refute, but which the tradition contests with its corporate-Israel servant. The book should acknowledge that its case here stands or falls with Isaiah 53.

### [STRONG] Torah she-be'al peh: interpretation is vested in the transmitted chain of the Sages, the Pharisees are the commanded court of Deut 17, and branding their doctrine 'leaven' rejects lo tasur (Deut 17:8-11; Avot 1:1; Bava Metzia 59b; Rambam Hilchot Mamrim 1:1-2). Recurs as CENTRAL in the Introduction and chs. 2, 14, 19, 27, 29, and the glossary.

**Book:** Scripture is a self-decoding cipher any diligent reader can recover by letting Scripture interpret Scripture; the Pharisees' doctrine is the leaven Jesus warned against — the word embittered and puffed up — and worship shaped by human precept is vain (Isa 29:13 / Matt 15:9).

**Their scriptures:** Deut 17:8-11; Deut 30:11-14; Mal 2:7; Lev 7:13; Lev 23:17; Neh 8:8; Matt 23:2-3 (the book's own witness)

**Their argument:** Moses commanded obedience to the judges of each generation — 'thou shalt not decline from the sentence' — and Malachi sends Israel to seek Torah at the priest's mouth; 'it is not in heaven' means meaning is settled by the received courts, not private pattern-matching. Without the Pharisees' transmission there is no canon, no preserved text, no practicable Written Torah. And the leaven claim fails on the book's own method: the Torah commands leavened bread before God — the todah loaves and the Shavuot wave-loaves — so the alleged uniform pattern is false in Moses himself. Even the book's Jesus concedes: 'the scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses' seat.'

**Rebuttal:** Read in place, Deut 17:8-11 (verified) establishes a judiciary — 'matters of controversy... between blood and blood, between plea and plea' — at the chosen place, bounded by 'according to the sentence of THE LAW'; it is a court for cases, not a grant of interpretive monopoly over meaning, and Deut 30:11-14 in context says the word is near 'that thou mayest DO it' — doability, not court ownership (Bava Metzia 59b's use of it is the innovation). Decisively, Scripture itself repeatedly shows the commissioned teachers corrupting the deposit while God's prophets indict them: the tradition quotes Mal 2:7 but the same oracle continues 'But ye are departed out of the way; ye have caused many to stumble at the law; ye have corrupted the covenant of Levi' (Mal 2:8, verified); 'the pen of the scribes is in vain' (Jer 8:8); 'her priests have violated my law' (Ezek 22:26); 'their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men' (Isa 29:13) — the office never immunized the officeholders, so Jesus rebuking the tradents stands in the prophetic pattern, not outside it. The leaven counter-example dissolves on inspection: the wave loaves are indeed baked with leaven (Lev 23:17), but the same code bars leaven from the altar-fire — 'ye shall burn no leaven... in any offering of the LORD made by fire' (Lev 2:11), firstfruits oblations 'shall not be burnt on the altar for a sweet savour' (Lev 2:12), 'thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leaven' (Ex 34:25) — the leavened loaves are waved and eaten by the priest (Lev 23:20), never ascending the altar; and the book's claim concerned leaven as a FIGURE, which Moses and the prophets never once deploy positively. Preservation is not interpretive authority: the scribes faithfully preserved the very texts that indict scribes. Residue: Matt 23:2-3 remains the tradition's best card, but it is bounded in its own sentence ('do not ye after their works') and by the leaven warning two chapters earlier.

### [PARTIAL] The Messiah is verified by completed public outcomes — exiles gathered, Temple built, war abolished; a claimant killed before accomplishing them is thereby disqualified, and Tanakh knows no second coming (Rambam Hilchot Melachim 11:3-4; Deut 18:21-22; Ezek 37:24-28; Isa 2:4). Recurs as CENTRAL in chs. 2, 6, 9, 18, 27, 31.

**Book:** Jesus' resurrection is THE sign vindicating him; the unfulfilled prophecies belong to his return — enthronement now (Ps 110), open reign later.

**Their scriptures:** Deut 18:21-22; Jer 23:5-6; Ezek 37:24-28; Isa 2:4; Isa 11:6-12; Zech 14:9; Rambam Hilchot Melachim 11:4

**Their argument:** The prophets define the Branch by verifiable results: in his days Judah saved, Israel dwelling safely, the sanctuary in their midst for evermore, war abolished, the earth full of the knowledge of God. None followed the first century — within a generation the Temple burned and Israel was scattered, the exact inverse. A private resurrection attested by partisans replaces the Torah's falsifiable test with an unfalsifiable one, and the two-stage advent is a post-hoc rescue with no source in Tanakh: he who did not accomplish and was killed 'is not the one,' and one does not wait for him a second time.

**Rebuttal:** The interval is not a church invention; the Tanakh builds it into the portrait. Ps 110:1 (verified): 'The LORD said unto my Lord, Sit thou at my right hand, UNTIL I make thine enemies thy footstool' — enthronement first, subjugation deferred, an interim session in David's own words. Dan 9:26 (verified): 'after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off... and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city' — on any reading, a dated anointed one dies inside the Second Temple window BEFORE the destruction; a rule that a dead claimant is disqualified collides with Daniel's own dying, dated Mashiach. Zech 12:10 (verified): at the hour the spirit is poured on Jerusalem 'they shall look upon me whom they have PIERCED, and they shall mourn' — the piercing precedes the national recognition. Hos 5:15-6:2: 'I will go and return to my place, TILL they acknowledge their offence... after two days will he revive us' — withdrawal-and-return is God's stated pattern. Isa 53:10-12: the servant pours out his soul unto death and AFTERWARD 'shall prolong his days' and 'divide the spoil' — death then triumph in one figure. Rambam's disqualification rule is codified nowhere in Tanakh; Deut 18:22 tests whether a spoken word comes to pass, and Daniel's timeline did. Residue conceded honestly: the publicly verifiable outcomes — regathering, universal peace, knowledge of God — remain outstanding, so the book's verification is deferred to the return; until it happens, the tradition is entitled to call that unfalsifiable, and the book has no answer to that except the same trust Daniel was told to seal up.

### [PARTIAL] The irrevocable election of Israel and Jerusalem: the seed of Israel never ceases, Zion's rebukes end in restoration and never in transfer, and identifying Jerusalem with Babylon-the-harlot is the supersessionist inversion (Jer 31:35-37; Lev 26:44-45; Ps 132:13-14; Sanhedrin 44a; Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:1). Recurs as CENTRAL in chs. 6, 10, 13, 18, 33, 34, and the glossary.

**Book:** Jerusalem is the great city 'spiritually called Sodom and Egypt,' the harlot who kills the prophets; the kingdom is taken from the unfaithful generation and given to a fruit-bearing nation; branches were broken from the olive tree and wild branches grafted in; 'Israel was never a bloodline to be born into' but a name to be given.

**Their scriptures:** Jer 31:35-37; Lev 26:44-45; Ps 132:13-14; Isa 1:26-27; Jer 31:38-40; Gen 17:7-8; Isa 62:1-7; Zech 2:16

**Their argument:** God stakes Israel's seed on the fixed order of sun and moon and swears Jerusalem is His rest for ever; the prophets rebuke her harshly but always within the covenant and unto restoration — 'afterward thou shalt be called, The city of righteousness.' Jeremiah swears rebuilt Jerusalem 'shall not be plucked up, nor thrown down, any more for ever' — the direct negation of 'shall not rise.' Redefining Israel as a character-class open to any believer dissolves the nation the oath guarantees; a gentile joins only by joining the nation itself.

**Rebuttal:** The harlot vocabulary for Jerusalem is the prophets' own, verified: 'How is the faithful city become an harlot!' (Isa 1:21); Zion's rulers addressed as 'rulers of Sodom' after 'we should have been as Sodom' (Isa 1:9-10); Ezekiel makes Sodom Jerusalem's SISTER and Jerusalem worse — 'Sodom thy sister hath not done... as thou hast done' (Ezek 16:46-48). Revelation 11:8 walks a road Isaiah and Ezekiel paved; adopting the prophets' own indictment is not the destroyers' taunt. And the book's shape is exactly Isa 1:26-28's: purge then restoration — 'Zion shall be redeemed with judgment' while 'they that forsake the LORD shall be consumed' — two destinies inside one city; the fallen harlot-city and the restored city of righteousness are both in the oracle, so Jer 31:40's rebuilt Jerusalem is the book's New Jerusalem, not its refutation. On the seed-oath: the book affirms Jer 31:35-37 and reads the continuity the way Isaiah does — through a remnant that IS Israel: 'the holy seed shall be the substance thereof' (Isa 6:13), 'a remnant of them shall return' (Isa 10:22), 'I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob... mine elect shall inherit it' against 'ye shall leave your name for a curse unto my chosen: for the Lord GOD shall slay thee, and call his servants by another name' (Isa 65:9, 15, verified) — Scripture itself divides within Israel and even names a renaming. Gentile grafting is Torah's own mechanism, enlarging rather than replacing: the mixed multitude under one law (Ex 12:38, 48-49), the joined stranger given tribal inheritance 'as born in the country' (Ezek 47:22-23), strangers cleaving to the house of Jacob (Isa 14:1; 56:3-7). Where the counter genuinely lands: ch. 34's sentence 'Israel was never a matter of descent... never a bloodline' overstates against Gen 17:7 and Jer 31:36-37, which tie the covenant to zera — the book's own proof-texts (Isa 65:9; Ezek 47:22) keep the seed and graft INTO it. The sentence should read 'never a bloodline alone'; as written, it hands the tradition a real point.

### [STRONG] The canon closed with the last prophets; Christian books are sifrei minim with no sanctity, so a symbol-lexicon whose defining entries stand in Revelation has no evidentiary force (Tosefta Sotah 13:3; Mishnah Yadayim 3:5; BT Shabbat 116a; Deut 4:2). Recurs as CENTRAL in chs. 3, 19, and pervasively per the ch. 25 finding.

**Book:** The 66 books share one symbolic vocabulary; Revelation 17:9-10, 15 states the keys (mountains = kingdoms, waters = peoples) that Daniel, Isaiah, and Jeremiah corroborate, and NT texts seal each chapter's derivation.

**Their scriptures:** Deut 4:2; Prov 30:5-6; Tosefta Sotah 13:3; Zech 13:2-5; Mal 3:22

**Their argument:** Prophecy departed with Haggai, Zechariah, and Malachi; the canon closed, and nothing may be added to His words. A lexicon whose defining entries stand outside the canon proves the Tanakh from a book Israel never received; it demonstrates only that a later author reused Daniel's imagery — which no one disputes.

**Rebuttal:** The objection mistakes order of presentation for order of proof. Every load-bearing key is defined inside the Tanakh, verified: mountain = kingdom in Daniel's own gloss — 'the stone... became a great mountain' interpreted as 'the God of heaven [shall] set up a KINGDOM' (Dan 2:35, 44) — and in Jeremiah's address to Babylon, 'O destroying mountain... I will make thee a burnt mountain' (Jer 51:25); waters = peoples/nations in 'the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria' (Isa 8:7) and 'the nations shall rush like the rushing of many waters' (Isa 17:12-13). Remove Revelation entirely and the lexicon stands; Revelation's agreement is then evidence that its author read the same dictionary — a corroborating witness, not the foundation (2 Esdras in ch. 19 is likewise corroboration, not proof). Meanwhile 'prophecy ceased with Malachi' is itself an extra-biblical tradition-claim: the Tanakh's own canons are Deut 18:22 (does the word come to pass) and Isa 8:20 (accordance with the law and the testimony), and Joel 2:28 (verified) promises prophecy poured out on all flesh AFTERWARD — a future the cessation doctrine must itself defer. Deut 4:2 and Prov 30:6 forbid adding to God's words; whether these books are His words is the very question, which a prohibition cannot adjudicate. Honest limit: for a reader who rules the NT out of court, any conclusion whose only witness is NT (the specific christological fulfillments) remains unproven — but that is a verdict about the reader's canon, not about the lexicon's demonstrability within Tanakh, which is what the counter-argument claimed to defeat.

### [PARTIAL] Kiddush HaChodesh: the month begins at the renewed crescent, keseh means the covered moon (Rosh Hashanah at the dark of the month), and the court's proclamation binds even against the sky (Ex 12:2 with RH 20a; RH 8a; RH 25a; Rambam Hilchot Kiddush HaChodesh). Recurs as CENTRAL in chs. 23, 24, 25 (and MINOR in ch. 6).

**Book:** Keseh in Ps 81:3 is the full moon, spelled letter-for-letter as 'throne' in Prov 7:20; with no 'and' between chodesh and keseh, the full moon is the head of the month, and the dark-conjunction reckoning is the counterfeit — the enemy's seizure of the appointed times.

**Their scriptures:** Ex 12:2; Ps 81:4-5 as read in RH 8a and Beitzah 16a; 1 Sam 20:5, 18; Lev 23:2, 4 as read in RH 25a; Isa 66:23

**Their argument:** Chodesh is built on ch-d-sh, renewal — the month begins when the moon emerges renewed from concealment. The gemara derives keseh from kasah, 'covered': the one feast at the hidden moon is Rosh Hashanah, the first of the month — the exact inverse of the book's reading; the full moon is conspicuous on the 15th because Pesach and Sukkot fall mid-month. And Psalm 81 calls this a statute administered by Israel's courts, whose sanctification binds even if astronomically mistaken; the calculated calendar carries that delegated Sinai authority. The keseh/kisse letter-identity rests on one anomalous spelling.

**Rebuttal:** On keseh the philology is verified and runs the book's way: Strong's H3677 — 'properly, fulness or the FULL MOON, i.e. its festival' — and the tradition's own JPS joins NKJV, ESV, NASB in rendering Ps 81:3 'at the full moon,' against the gemara's covered-moon derash. Prov 7:20 (verified) breaks the 'concealed moon' reading outright: the goodman 'will come home at the keseh' — a homecoming timed by the month's one conspicuous marker, not by the night the sky shows nothing — and the WLC confirms it is there spelled with aleph (הַכֵּסֶא), letter-for-letter kisse', 'throne,' in the received consonantal text itself; Strong's derives BOTH H3677 and H3678 from the same root kasah ('covered, i.e. a throne (as canopied)'), and Job 26:9 holds both senses in one verse — 'He holdeth back the face of his throne, and spreadeth his cloud upon it.' So the 'single anomalous spelling' is canonical attestation, not an accident. On court authority: 'atem afilu mut'im' must be read against the verse's own words — 'the feasts of the LORD... these are MY feasts... which ye shall proclaim IN THEIR SEASONS' (Lev 23:2, 4, verified): proclamation is commanded to track the season, not to constitute it, and the Torah's clock for moadim was hung in the sky at creation — 'let them be for signs, and for seasons' (Gen 1:14); 'He appointed the moon for seasons' (Ps 104:19). A court that binds 'even against the sky' is human precept enthroned over the statute (Isa 29:13; Deut 4:2). Honest residue, stated plainly: chodesh really is built on chadash 'to be new' (Strong's H2320/H2318 — 'the new moon'), and Ex 12:2's renewal most naturally names the moon's re-emergence, not its fullness; 1 Sam 20:5's foreknown chodesh fits several reckonings. The full-moon month-head must still overcome the word's own root, and the book has deferred that answer to the companion volume — until it delivers, the tradition's strongest philological card stands unanswered.

### [PARTIAL] God never divorced Israel: Isaiah 50:1 is a rhetorical denial (no get exists), the separation is a hiding of the face within a standing marriage, and a Husband who dies to remarry is a contradiction of God's immutability (Sanhedrin 105a; Rashi on Isa 50:1; Mal 3:6; Num 23:19). CENTRAL in chs. 10 and 17 and the glossary.

**Book:** God did hand Israel a bill of divorce (Jer 3:8), the covenant people really are the widow and the fatherless, and the no-return statute of Deut 24:4 was answered at the cross: the Husband took the wife's death penalty and rose free to wed her anew.

**Their scriptures:** Isa 50:1; Lam 1:1 ('as a widow'); Isa 54:6-8; Jer 3:1 with Deut 24:1-4; Hos 2:19-20; Mal 3:6; Num 23:19

**Their argument:** Isaiah's question expects the answer 'there is none': produce the bill — the separation is disciplinary ('for your iniquities'), forsakenness 'for a small moment.' The clinching argument is halachic: Jeremiah 3:1 invokes Deuteronomy 24's no-return rule and God nonetheless says 'yet return again to me' — were the divorce real, His own invitation would violate His own Torah; therefore no get ever issued. And Hosea's re-betrothal 'for ever' needs no legal miracle and no dying Husband — 'God is not a man,' and 'I change not.'

**Rebuttal:** The 'no get exists' claim is contradicted in terms by the verified text of Jer 3:8: 'for all the causes whereby backsliding Israel committed adultery I HAD PUT HER AWAY, AND GIVEN HER A BILL OF DIVORCE; yet her treacherous sister Judah feared not' — a report, not a question, and the same verse distinguishes the two houses: Israel divorced, Judah not (yet). Isa 50:1's challenge is addressed to Zion's children — Judah — in Isaiah's day, so the two texts cohere exactly as the book reads them; Hosea concurs for the northern house: 'she is not my wife, neither am I her husband' (Hos 2:2), 'ye are not my people' (Hos 1:9). Sanhedrin 105a must read Jeremiah's explicit get as rhetoric against its own grammar. Second, the legal impasse is raised by Scripture itself, not imposed by the book: Jer 3:1 (verified) quotes the Deut 24:4 bar — 'shall he return unto her again? shall not that land be greatly polluted?' — and then says 'yet return again to me': the text itself demands a resolution the tradition leaves as bare paradox. And Isaiah hands the restored wife the vocabulary of a husband's death: 'thou... shalt not remember the reproach of thy WIDOWHOOD any more. For thy Maker is thine husband' (Isa 54:4-5) — widowhood, not quarrel. Honest residue: the mechanism — the Husband Himself taking the death — stands on the NT and the incarnation; Mal 3:6 and Num 23:19 are not answered from Tanakh alone, and Hos 2:19's re-betrothal is not explicitly routed through a death in the prophets. The divorce's reality is won; the cross as its legal answer remains a claim the Tanakh permits the book to propose but does not itself state.

### [PARTIAL] Isaiah 7:14: ha-almah is a young woman of Ahaz's day, the sign was spent on the Aram-Ephraim crisis (v. 16 dates it), and betulah is the word Hebrew would have used for virgin (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Radak ad loc.). CENTRAL in chs. 5 and 26.

**Book:** The virgin conception is the sign — the word stood seven centuries before its fulfillment arrived to testify, continuing into Revelation 12.

**Their scriptures:** Isa 7:1-16, esp. 7:14-16; Isa 8:3-4; Gen 24:16; Prov 30:19

**Their argument:** A sign given to certify deliverance from Rezin and Pekah, fulfilled 700 years after both kings were dust, certifies nothing to the man it was given to — failing the book's own definition of a sign. Ha-almah with the article is a young woman present at court; had virginity been meant, Hebrew possessed betulah. The 'virgin' reading rests on a Greek translation artifact torn from a dated context.

**Rebuttal:** Three verified checks blunt the counter. (1) The addressee: after Ahaz refuses to ask (7:12), the oracle deliberately widens — 'Hear ye now, O HOUSE OF DAVID... the Lord himself shall give YOU a sign' (7:13-14, plural) — a dynastic sign can outlive the man who forfeited his own; and the offered sign's scale was 'in the depth, or in the height above' (7:11) — the register of the miraculous, not of a court pregnancy. (2) The lexical claim cuts the other way: betulah is NOT the unambiguous virgin-word — 'Lament like a VIRGIN [betulah] girded with sackcloth for THE HUSBAND OF HER YOUTH' (Joel 1:8, verified; Strong's H1330: 'sometimes (by continuation) a bride') — while almah is never used of a married woman anywhere in Tanakh: Rebekah is the almah of Gen 24:43 and explicitly the untouched betulah of 24:16. The LXX's parthenos is pre-Christian Jewish translation, not a church artifact. (3) The oracle-cycle itself overshoots Ahaz: Isaiah's own sign-children carry the near timetable ('I and the children whom the LORD hath given me are for signs,' 8:18; the Maher-shalal child of 8:3-4 duplicates 7:16's before-the-boy-knows clock), while Immanuel owns the land — 'the breadth of THY land, O Immanuel' (8:8) — and the child of 9:6 carries names no Judean court child bears: 'The mighty God, The everlasting Father.' Honest residue: 7:16's 'the child' most naturally continues 7:14's child, and 'the almah is with child' reads most simply of a woman then present; the dual-horizon reading is defensible and textually motivated, but not compelled — this remains a contested reading, not a demonstration.

### [STRONG] 'All Israel have a share in the world to come' (Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:1 on Isa 60:21); the sparse reading of Jer 3:14 was rejected (Sanhedrin 111a), and the gates of teshuvah never close — a fixed ~0.2% of the saved would make repentance a fraud. CENTRAL in ch. 19 (twice).

**Book:** Scripture fixes the saved at a fraction of one percent by converging censuses — Elijah's 7,000, the gleanings, the refined third, the land's carrying capacity — and the comfortable assumption of salvation is the deadliest thing in the world.

**Their scriptures:** Isa 60:21; Jer 3:14 as read in Sanhedrin 111a; Ezek 18:21-23, 32; Ezek 33:11; Jonah 3:10; Micah 7:18-20

**Their argument:** The Mishnah opens the chapter on final judgment with the opposite census: all Israel has a share, grounded on the very inheritance verse the book runs arithmetic on. Rabbi Yochanan protested the sparse reading — the one of a city brings merit to the whole city. Elijah's 7,000 are a floor under Ahab's apostasy, not an eternal ore grade; Nineveh shows a whole city spared in forty days; God has no pleasure in the death of the wicked.

**Rebuttal:** The Mishnah's proof-text, read where Isaiah put it, is remnant-conditioned — every verse verified. Isa 60:21's 'Thy people also shall be all righteous: they shall inherit the land' stands at the end of the refining sequence Isaiah himself narrates: 'Zion shall be redeemed with judgment... and the destruction of the transgressors and of the sinners shall be together' (1:27-28); 'he that is LEFT in Zion... shall be called holy' — after the filth is washed 'by the spirit of judgment, and by the spirit of burning' (4:3-4); 'the Redeemer shall come to Zion, and unto THEM THAT TURN from transgression in Jacob' (59:20); 'I will bring forth a seed out of Jacob... mine elect shall inherit it' while 'the Lord GOD shall slay thee' (65:9, 12-15). 'All righteous' describes all of the survivors — which is the book's exact claim. The wider census agrees: 'though thy people Israel be as the sand of the sea, yet A REMNANT of them shall return' (Isa 10:22); 'all the sinners of my people shall die by the sword' (Amos 9:10); 'I will purge out from among you the rebels... they shall not enter into the land' (Ezek 20:38); two parts cut off, the third refined (Zech 13:8-9); deliverance 'in the remnant whom the LORD shall call' (Joel 2:32); and the Torah's own enacted ratio — of the counted generation, two men entered the land (Num 26:65). Nor does a sparse count make teshuvah a fraud: the book's fraction is a prophecy of how few will turn, not a quota barring anyone — Ezek 33:11's open door stands, and the fewness measures how few walk through it, exactly as 1 Kgs 19:18's seven thousand measured Ahab's generation. Caveat the book should own: the specific ~0.2% is an inferred convergence, not a stated number of Scripture — but the counter-scriptures' claim, that Scripture forbids a sparse census, is refuted by Scripture's own censuses.

### [PARTIAL] The Masoretic vocalization and reading tradition is received transmission from Sinai (mikra soferim, Nedarim 37b-38a on Neh 8:8); reading against the pointing makes the text infinitely plastic and dissolves the shared Scripture. SIGNIFICANT in the Introduction and chs. 16 and 24.

**Book:** The vowel points are a late, human overlay 'not above question'; a reading permitted by the bare consonants can stand even against the pointing, and consonantal wordplays are part of the intended meaning.

**Their scriptures:** Neh 8:8; Deut 31:9-13; Ps 81:4 (heh) against Isa 14:13 (aleph); Deut 17:8-11

**Their argument:** Ezra's Levites 'gave the sense' — the reading itself is Sinaitic transmission the pointing merely notates. Detach text from mesorah and every verse becomes malleable: the license that yields 'I will silence the Highest' could yield anything. The method even trips on its showcase — keseh and kisse' are spelled differently where it matters, and the Strong's lemmas the book leans on are themselves generated from the very pointing it dismisses.

**Rebuttal:** God Himself puns beneath the pointing — verified: Jeremiah is shown 'a rod of an ALMOND tree [shaqed]' and told 'I will HASTEN [shoqed — watch over] my word to perform it' (Jer 1:11-12); Amos is shown 'a basket of SUMMER FRUIT [qayitz]' and told 'THE END [qetz] is come upon my people' (Amos 8:1-2). Both oracles turn on consonantal identity that different vowels split — the prophetic text is built to carry a second reading in its consonants, which is all the book claims. The Masorah's own ketiv/qere apparatus concedes in hundreds of places that the received consonants and the received reading diverge — so 'one unbroken Sinaitic vocalization' is a tradition about the tradition, not a property of the text. On the showcase: the objection inverts. The WLC itself spells the full moon כסא in Prov 7:20 — letter-for-letter 'throne' — so the identity has canonical consonantal attestation, and Strong's derives both H3677 and H3678 from the one root kasah; the 'anomalous spelling' is the witness, not the embarrassment. Against infinite plasticity, the book's stated control is not the reader's wish but cross-text confirmation: a re-pointing stands only where the resulting sense matches the symbol's independent usage elsewhere — the same multi-witness discipline the tradition itself applies in al-tikrei derash, differing only in refusing the pointing a veto. Honest residue, twofold: Neh 8:8 fairly establishes that SOME reading tradition accompanied the text from Ezra, so the pointing is presumptively old and each departure bears its own burden of proof; and the tradition scores a clean hit on tool-consistency — the Strong's lemmas the book cites are artifacts of the very pointing it discounts, which the book should say aloud when it uses them.

### [PARTIAL] Jeremiah 31's covenant is future and self-dated by its effects (no one teaches his neighbor, all know HaShem); the Sinai covenant stands unmodified and needs no re-ratification in blood (Jer 31:33-37; Deut 29:13-14; Rambam's 9th Principle). SIGNIFICANT in ch. 7 and the glossary.

**Book:** Chadash means renew: the new covenant is the same Torah-covenant renewed at the cross, in force now, written on hearts, carried to the nations as the gospel of the kingdom.

**Their scriptures:** Jer 31:33-34; Jer 31:35-37; Deut 29:13-14; Ps 111:9

**Their argument:** The philology is granted — chadash renews. But Jeremiah dates the renewal by its effects: no man teaches his neighbor, all Israel knows HaShem, sin remembered no more. Nothing of this obtained in the first century or since — teachers multiplied — therefore the covenant was not renewed at the cross. And the covenant already binds all generations without re-ratification; Torah knows no sealing of God's covenant by a man's execution.

**Rebuttal:** The dating test assumes what no covenant in Tanakh exhibits: that ratification and consummation are one moment. Verified cases: the covenant with Abram was CUT on a single day — 'In the same day the LORD made a covenant with Abram' (Gen 15:18) — while its own terms scheduled four hundred years of affliction before the land-clause matured (15:13); Sinai was ratified in blood in Exodus 24 while Deut 28-30 spreads its blessings, curses, exile, and return across centuries; David received the oath in 2 Sam 7 with the throne's fullness deferred. Jer 31:33's 'I will put my law in their inward parts' begins where Jeremiah himself begins it with the exiles — 'I will give them an heart to know me... for they shall return unto me with their whole heart' (Jer 24:7, verified) — a work inaugurated in a remnant and consummated when 'they shall ALL know me' (31:34). So 'the effects are not yet total' proves the covenant is not yet consummated — which the book affirms — not that it was never cut. The book keeps the parties and the content intact: the same Torah (Strong's H2318, chadash: 'to be new... renew, repair'), the houses of Israel and Judah, gentiles entering by Torah's own gate of joining (Isa 56:6-7; Ezek 47:22). Honest residue: 'ratified in blood at the cross' is Jeremiah nowhere — it is NT; and the covenant's named parties are the two houses, so the book's extension to the nations rides on its Ephraim-among-the-nations identification, itself a contested further claim. The tradition's timing argument fails, but its party-clause observation still requires the book's larger framework to answer.
