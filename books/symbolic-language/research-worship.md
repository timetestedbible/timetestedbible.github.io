## The compression

Modern usage: worship ≈ the song service ("praise and worship" as a
music genre). Scripture's usage: a body bowed and a life in service.
Scripture itself keeps worship and singing distinct **inside single
scenes**:

- 2 Chr 29:28 — "all the congregation WORSHIPPED, **and** the singers
  SANG, and the trumpeters sounded" — three different acts, one verse.
  Then 29:30: "they sang praises with gladness, and they BOWED THEIR
  HEADS and worshipped."
- 2 Chr 20:18-19 — Jehoshaphat "bowed his head with his face to the
  ground: and all Judah… FELL before the LORD, worshipping"; the
  Levites "STOOD UP to praise the LORD… with a loud voice." Worship
  falls down; praise stands up.

## What the word is

*Shachah* = to bow oneself down, prostrate. The SAME word is rendered
"worship" (Gen 22:5), "bow down" (Ex 20:5), "did obeisance," "did
reverence" — Abraham *shachah*s to the Hittites (Gen 23:7), Absalom to
David (2 Sam 14:33), Mordecai refuses it to Haman (Est 3:2). The word
is the posture of homage to a superior; worship of God is that homage
directed at Him. Greek *proskuneō* = "kiss toward": the subject's kiss
to his king. The Hebrew homage-kiss is real: "KISS the Son, lest he be
angry" (Ps 2:12); the 7000 = "every mouth which hath not KISSED" Baal
(1 Kgs 19:18); "let the men that sacrifice KISS the calves" (Hos 13:2);
Job clears himself of sun-worship: "or my mouth hath KISSED my hand…
I should have denied the God that is above" (Job 31:26-28).

## The fixed pair: bow + serve

The second commandment: "thou shalt not BOW DOWN thyself (shachah) to
them, nor SERVE (avad) them" (Ex 20:5) — worship IS the commandment's
subject matter. The pair everywhere: Dan 3:12/18/28 "we will not SERVE
thy gods, nor WORSHIP the golden image"; Rom 1:25 "WORSHIPPED and
SERVED the creature more than the Creator"; Rev 13 the beast crisis.
Jesus: "Thou shalt WORSHIP (proskuneō) the Lord thy God, and him only
shalt thou SERVE (latreuō)" (Matt 4:10).

## Worship = the fear of the LORD (Jesus' own rendering)

Matt 4:10 quotes Deut 6:13 — which reads "Thou shalt FEAR (yare) the
LORD thy God, and serve him." Jesus renders *fear* as *worship*: the
two are one thing. Parallels: 2 Kgs 17:36 "him shall ye FEAR, and him
shall ye WORSHIP, and to him shall ye do sacrifice"; Ps 96:9 "WORSHIP
the LORD in the beauty of holiness: FEAR before him, all the earth";
Rev 14:7 "FEAR God… and WORSHIP him"; Rev 15:4 "who shall not FEAR
thee… all nations shall come and WORSHIP before thee." And the vain
counterfeit is defined through the same equation: Isa 29:13 "their FEAR
toward me is taught by the PRECEPT OF MEN" = Matt 15:9 "in vain they do
WORSHIP me, teaching for doctrines the COMMANDMENTS OF MEN." → hands
directly to ch. 32 (fear of the LORD = keep His commandments,
Eccl 12:13).

## First occurrence

The first "worship" in the Bible is Gen 22:5 — Abraham on the way to
bind Isaac: "I and the lad will go yonder and WORSHIP." No song, no
service of music: the costliest obedience in Genesis is what Scripture
first calls worship. (Cf. 1 Sam 15:22 "to OBEY is better than
sacrifice"; Mic 6:6-8.)

## Worship at the appointed times and place

Worship is something Israel GOES UP to do: Elkanah "went up out of his
city YEARLY to worship and to sacrifice… in Shiloh" (1 Sam 1:3);
firstfruits set down, "and worship" (Deut 26:10); "worship at his
footstool" (Ps 99:5; 132:7). NT keeps the idiom: Greeks "came up to
WORSHIP at the FEAST" (John 12:20); the eunuch "had come to Jerusalem
for to worship" (Acts 8:27); Paul "went up to Jerusalem for to worship"
(Acts 24:11) and defines his worship: "so WORSHIP I the God of my
fathers, believing all things which are written in the LAW and in the
prophets" (24:14). Forward: "from one new moon to another, and from one
SABBATH to another, shall all flesh come to WORSHIP" (Isa 66:23); the
nations go up "to WORSHIP the King… and to KEEP THE FEAST of
tabernacles," on pain of no rain (Zech 14:16-17).

## Rev 14:6-7 — the everlasting gospel's worship call

"WORSHIP him that MADE HEAVEN, AND EARTH, THE SEA, and the fountains of
waters" — the Creator-formula of the SABBATH commandment: "in six days
the LORD MADE HEAVEN AND EARTH, THE SEA, and all that in them is"
(Ex 20:11). The angel's call to worship quotes the fourth commandment's
ground clause. Cross-check, Hebrew Revelation (data/hg-chapters/
Revelation-14.json; bibles/hg.txt): "Fear YHWH and make your PRAYER
(tephillah) to him… and make prayer to the one who CREATED the heavens
and the earth and the sea and the wells" — where the Greek says
worship, the Hebrew witness says prayer made TO a person; neither
witness describes music. Counter-worship: the dragon/beast/image
worshipped (Rev 13:4, 8, 15); the angel refuses John twice — "worship
God" (Rev 22:8-9; cf. Acts 10:25-26).

## Whole-life service

Rom 12:1 "present your BODIES a living sacrifice… your reasonable
SERVICE (latreia)"; John 4:23-24 "worship him in spirit and IN TRUTH"
(truth = God's law, Ps 119:142); Nehemiah's assembly read the law a
quarter of the day, then "confessed, and WORSHIPPED" (Neh 9:3). Exodus'
liberation-formula: "let my people go, that they may SERVE me"
(Ex 8:1) — released FROM Pharaoh's service INTO God's (ties to ch. 30
Liberty).

## Meaning

Worship = homage in the body and allegiance in the life: to bow down
to God and serve Him only — the fear of the LORD acted out, at His
appointed times, on His terms. Singing can accompany it; it is not it.
The counterfeit is worship re-defined by the precepts of men — and the
end-time issue (Rev 13-14) is precisely whom you bow to and serve.

## Creature vs Creator (Rom 1:25) — author query, tested 2026-07-03

Is "creature" the beast symbol? Lexically NO: ktisis G2937 (from ktizo,
create) = the made thing — same word as "whole CREATION groaneth"
(Rom 8:22), "new CREATURE" (2 Cor 5:17). Revelation beast = therion
G2342, different word. Structurally YES, one direction: Rom 1:23 quotes
Moses own idolatry catalog item for item (Deut 4:16-18, running on to
sun/moon/stars in 4:19); the made thing men serve has a fixed political
face — the beast, a kingdom of men — and creature-worship heads up at
Rev 13 (all the world worships the beast). Every beast is a creature;
not every creature is the beast. Handled in ch. 31 prose with sym-beast
link; no glossary alias (would overclaim the lexical identity).
