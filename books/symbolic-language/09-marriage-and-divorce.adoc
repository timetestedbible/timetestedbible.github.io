Ask an AI whether Jesus allows divorce — the same test we ran on the gospel — and the machine returns the consensus of the modern church in one sentence:

[quote, Grok, asked whether Jesus allows divorce]
____
Yes, Jesus permits divorce on the grounds of sexual immorality (Greek _porneia_), as He explicitly states in Matthew 5:32 and 19:9 that divorcing for any other reason and remarrying constitutes adultery.
____

Ask it what _porneia_ means and the answer is just as confident: __“the broad New Testament term for illicit sexual activity or ‘sexual immorality’ — any form of sexual intercourse or behavior outside the covenant of monogamous marriage.”__
Press further and the scholarship divides into three camps — most read the exception as any serious sexual sin; some confine it to sin discovered during betrothal, like Joseph’s dilemma; a few tie it to the forbidden unions of Leviticus 18 — and the survey closes the way surveys do: the majority view prevails.
Notice what every camp shares.
All three read the exception as a _permission_ about _literal_ sex, and not one of them opens the Torah to ask what the law itself does with each case.
This chapter will run that test — the law first, then the words, then the Bible’s symbolic language — and the common interpretation will not survive it.

[discrete]
=== The Collision with the Law

Here is the problem the common view never faces.
Under the law, adultery is not a divorce ground; it is a capital crime:

[quote.scripture, Leviticus 20:10]
____
And the man that committeth *adultery* with another man’s wife, even he that committeth adultery with his neighbour’s wife, the adulterer and the adulteress shall surely be *put to death*.
____

Deuteronomy repeats it for the married and the betrothed alike — __“they shall *both* of them die… so shalt thou put away evil from Israel”__ (Deuteronomy 22:22-24) — and the scribes could still quote it to Jesus’ face: __“Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be *stoned*: but what sayest thou?”__
(John 8:5).
A dead spouse needs no bill of divorcement.
If the exception clause means __“divorce is permitted for adultery,”__ then Jesus quietly commuted a death penalty — amended the law — a dozen verses after declaring that __“one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law”__ (Matthew 5:18), in the same sermon.
The reading that everyone holds makes Jesus break the rule He had just laid down.
Either He contradicted Himself in a single chapter, or the exception is not about adultery at all.

[discrete]
=== Two Words in One Sentence

The Greek settles which, because Jesus used two different words — in the same breath:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 5:32]
____
But I say unto you, That whosoever shall put away his wife, saving for the cause of *fornication*, causeth her to commit *adultery*: and whosoever shall marry her that is divorced committeth adultery.
____

The exception is _porneia_ — fornication; the consequence is _moicheia_ — adultery.
He had the adultery-word in His mouth and did not use it for the exception; and when He lists the sins of the heart, He lists them separately: __“*adulteries*, *fornications*”__ (Matthew 15:19) — two words, two things.
Mark and Luke record the saying with no exception at all — __“Whosoever shall put away his wife, and marry another, committeth adultery”__ (Mark 10:11; Luke 16:18) — the absolute form.footnote:hebnt[The Hebrew text of Mark and Luke (the Cochin manuscript tradition, _Ele Ha-Sodot_, c. 1700) carries the same absolute form, with no exception clause, using the very verb of Leviticus 20:10; the manuscripts are presented at https://TimeTested.Bible[TimeTested.Bible].]
Only Matthew carries the clause — and Matthew has already shown his readers the case it covers.
In his first chapter: Mary, __“espoused to Joseph,”__ is found with child, and Joseph — called __“her *husband*”__ and __“a *just* man”__ in the same verse — is __“minded to *put her away* privily”__ (Matthew 1:18-19).
A betrothed man in Israel is a husband; ending a betrothal takes a putting-away; and the text calls Joseph _just_ — law-keeping — in the very act of resolving suspected fornication by a quiet bill rather than a public stoning.
The exception clause covers Joseph; it does not commute Leviticus.

[discrete]
=== What God Hath Joined

Before the divorce question can be answered, marriage itself must be defined — and Scripture defines it as one would expect.
__“They twain shall be *one flesh*.
What therefore *God hath joined together*, let not man put asunder”__ (Matthew 19:5-6, quoting Genesis 2:24).
What kind of joining?
The last chapter watched it enacted: the husband spreads his wing over the bride — __“I spread my skirt over thee… yea, I sware unto thee, and *entered into a covenant* with thee… and thou becamest mine”__ (Ezekiel 16:8) — and she is called by his name.
Malachi names the wife by the bond: __“she is thy companion, and *the wife of thy covenant*”__ (Malachi 2:14).
And Proverbs names the bond by its Owner — the adulterous woman is one __“which forsaketh the guide of her youth, and forgetteth *the covenant of her God*”__ (Proverbs 2:17).
sym:sym-marriage[Marriage] is the covenant in miniature: a one-flesh union that God Himself joins, sworn, witnessed, and worn like a sym:sym-name[name].
The joining is real whichever way it runs — __“he which is *joined to an* sym:sym-harlot[harlot] is one body… but he that is *joined unto the Lord* is one spirit”__ (1 Corinthians 6:16-17) — which is why the question _who joined this union?_ will turn out to be the whole divorce question.

[discrete]
=== The Nakedness of a Thing

Now open the statute the Pharisees waved at Jesus:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 24:1]
____
When a man hath taken a wife, and married her, and it come to pass that she find no favour in his eyes, because he hath found *some uncleanness* in her: then let him write her a *bill of divorcement*, and give it in her hand, and send her *out of his house*.
____

__“Some uncleanness”__ is, in the Hebrew, _the nakedness of a thing_ — and __“nakedness”__ here is the exact word of the forbidden-union chapter: __“None of you shall approach to any that is near of kin to him, to uncover their sym:sym-naked[nakedness]”__ (Leviticus 18:6), the catalog of unions that were never lawful to make.
The statute’s own vocabulary points away from adultery — which Deuteronomy has already sentenced to death two chapters earlier — and toward the union with something unlawful at its root: the sym:sym-naked[nakedness] uncovered, the covenant covering absent.

Read the rest of the statute’s shape.
Divorce is regulated, never celebrated: the bill in her hand protects the woman (a hard-hearted man could otherwise simply cast her out and leave her bound); she is free to remarry (Deuteronomy 24:2); and one door is bolted — her first husband __“may not take her again… after that she is defiled: for that is *abomination*”__ (Deuteronomy 24:4).
Hold that no-return rule; the whole gospel will hang on it before this chapter ends.
The law also forbids divorce outright in two cases (Deuteronomy 22:19, 29), and guards the wife’s provision — __“her food, her raiment, and her duty of marriage, shall he not diminish”__ (Exodus 21:10), the very rights the seven women of the last chapter offered to waive.
When the Pharisees asked Jesus, __“Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife *for every cause*?”__footnote:everycause[Their phrase quotes a standing rabbinic dispute: the school of Hillel allowed divorce “for any cause” — a spoiled dish would do — while the school of Shammai confined it to unchastity (Mishnah, Gittin 9:10).
Jesus’ answer sides with neither school; it goes back to the joining.] — He answered past the whole debate: Moses __“*suffered*”__ divorce __“because of the hardness of your hearts: but *from the beginning it was not so*”__ (Matthew 19:8).
The statute manages the wreckage of hard hearts; it does not bless it.
__“The LORD… hateth putting away”__ (Malachi 2:16) — when what is put away is __“the wife of thy covenant.”__

[discrete]
=== The Commanded Divorce

But there is a union the law refuses to bless — and there, Scripture does not permit divorce; it commands it.
Watch how the law describes marrying outside the covenant:

[quote.scripture, Exodus 34:15-16]
____
Lest thou make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and they *go a whoring after their gods*… And thou take of their daughters unto thy sons, and their daughters go a whoring after their gods, and *make thy sons go a whoring* after their gods.
____

The out-of-covenant marriage is not at risk of leading to fornication; the law calls the union itself a going-a-whoring — sym:sym-fornication[fornication] in the Bible’s symbolic language is covenant-unfaithfulness, and Israel commits it __“with *stones* and with *stocks*”__ (Jeremiah 3:9), with high places (2 Chronicles 21:11), with kingdoms (Revelation 17:2), and with the daughters of strange gods (Deuteronomy 7:3-4).
Now read Ezra with that definition in hand.
The returned exiles had __“taken of their daughters… so that the *holy seed* have mingled themselves with the people of those lands”__ (Ezra 9:2), and the remedy was a covenant:

[quote.scripture, Ezra 10:3]
____
Now therefore let us make a covenant with our God to *put away all the wives*, and such as are born of them… of those that *tremble at the commandment* of our God; and let it be done *according to the law*.
____

__“Put away… according to the law”__ — divorce, not as concession but as obedience, demanded by the men who trembled at the commandment; and Nehemiah enforces the same, holding up Solomon as the proof of where the strange wives lead: __“even him did outlandish women cause to sin”__ (Nehemiah 13:26).

And here Malachi’s oracle shows its two edges, for the same prophet who thundered __“the LORD… hateth putting away”__ thunders first against the marriage Ezra dissolved: __“Judah… hath *married the daughter of a strange god*.
The LORD will *cut off* the man that doeth this”__ (Malachi 2:11-12).
Putting away the wife of the covenant is treachery; keeping the daughter of the strange god is treachery.
Divorce in Scripture is evil or obedience depending entirely on _which union it severs_ — because __“what God hath joined together”__ is a covenant clause, and the union God never joined is the one He commands undone.

Paul keeps both edges for the assemblies — and marks whose voice is whose.
To the married he gives the absolute rule and names its source: __“I command, yet *not I, but the Lord*, Let not the wife depart from her husband”__ (1 Corinthians 7:10).
Then, turning to the man called while already married to an unbeliever, he changes the attribution: __“But to the rest speak *I, not the Lord*”__ (1 Corinthians 7:12) — the absolute belongs to the Lord; the mixed-marriage counsel is the apostle’s own judgment, for a case Ezra’s courts never faced: a man converted mid-marriage, his household half in and half out.
And that judgment runs the opposite direction from Ezra’s: __“if she be pleased to dwell with him, let him *not* put her away… and the unbelieving wife is *sanctified* by the husband”__ (1 Corinthians 7:12, 14).
Paul writes the rule in both directions — the believing wife likewise does not leave her unbelieving husband, who is sanctified by her (7:13-14) — for a reason Ezra’s generation did not have.
The covenant door now stands open to every nation, and the out-of-covenant spouse is no longer a pull toward strange gods but a soul within reach of the salvation the sym:sym-gospel[gospel] now publishes to all peoples: __“For what knowest thou, O wife, whether thou shalt *save* thy husband?”__
(1 Corinthians 7:16).
In Ezra’s day the strange wives drew the holy seed out; in Paul’s day the believing spouse may draw the sym:sym-stranger[stranger] in.
New marriages are still made __“*only in the Lord*”__ (1 Corinthians 7:39), and if the unbeliever departs, the believer __“is not under bondage”__ (1 Corinthians 7:15).

[discrete]
=== The Deposed Queen

Scripture also stages a divorce where no sexual sin appears at all — and the grounds are the symbol laid bare.
Queen Vashti is summoned by the king, __“but the queen Vashti *refused to come* at the king’s commandment”__ (Esther 1:12).
The case is heard __“according to *law*”__ (Esther 1:15), and the concern of the court is not jealousy but obedience across the whole realm: __“this deed of the queen shall come abroad unto *all women*, so that they shall *despise their husbands*”__ (Esther 1:17).
The verdict:

[quote.scripture, Esther 1:19]
____
…That Vashti *come no more* before king Ahasuerus; and let the king give her *royal estate unto another that is better than she*.
____

Mark that sentence, because it is the Bible’s standing formula for a deposed covenant partner.
Samuel to Saul: __“The LORD hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, *that is better than thou*”__ (1 Samuel 15:28).
Jesus to the husbandmen of the vineyard: __“The kingdom of God shall be *taken from you, and given* to a nation bringing forth the sym:sym-fruit[fruits] thereof”__ (Matthew 21:43).
And Solomon — the very case Nehemiah cited — hears it over his strange wives: __“I will surely *rend the kingdom* from thee, and will *give it* to thy servant”__ (1 Kings 11:11).
One formula, one lesson: the King’s wife must obey, or the estate passes — not because the King is petty, but because the whole land learns from the queen; the covenant marriage is public, and its breach teaches rebellion to every house in the empire.
Then comes the replacement, and she is the deposed queen’s exact inversion.
Esther — a bride taken from the scattered exiles — is __“made queen *instead of* Vashti”__ (Esther 2:17); and where Vashti refused to come when summoned, Esther goes in _unsummoned_, at the peril of her life, to intercede for her people: __“I will go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and *if I perish, I perish*”__ (Esther 4:16).
The queen who would not come, and the bride who comes at the cost of her life: the two wives of the whole symbolic story, side by side in one Persian court.

[discrete]
=== God’s Own Case File

Now open the greatest divorce case in Scripture — God’s own — and watch Him keep His own law at every step.
His wife committed adultery, endlessly, __“with stones and with stocks.”__
The law says death; He is the Judge; and He does not stone her:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 3:8]
____
And I saw, when for all the causes whereby backsliding Israel committed *adultery* I had *put her away*, and given her a *bill of divorce*; yet her treacherous sister Judah feared not, but went and played the sym:sym-harlot[harlot] also.
____

The bill of divorce is the exile — the wife sent __“out of his house,”__ scattered among the nations — and God holds the paperwork up to her children (Isaiah 50:1).
But now the Husband has a legal problem, and He states it Himself, citing His own statute:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 3:1]
____
They say, If a man put away his wife, and she go from him, and become another man’s, *shall he return unto her again?* shall not that land be greatly polluted? but thou hast played the sym:sym-harlot[harlot] with many lovers; *yet return again to me*, saith the LORD.
____

Deuteronomy 24:4 bars the road home: the first husband may not take her again.
God announces His intention to do the one thing His own law forbids — and He does not waive the law to do it; the whole testimony of this book is that He never does.
Listen to Paul find the door:

[quote.scripture, Romans 7:2-4]
____
For the woman which hath an husband is bound by the law to her husband *so long as he liveth*; but *if the husband be dead*, she is loosed from the law of her husband…
Wherefore, my brethren, ye also are become *dead to the law by the body of Christ*; that ye should be *married to another*, even to him that is *raised from the dead*…
____

Death dissolves the bond — and the death that dissolves this one is the _Husband’s_.
The wife earned the stoning; the Husband takes the stroke; and rising, He is free to wed her again — not under the old contract she shattered, but new.
That is why the renewed covenant is announced in remarriage language: __“I will make a new covenant… not according to the covenant… which my covenant they brake, *although I was an husband unto them*”__ (Jeremiah 31:31-32).
That is why Hosea, God’s sign-and-similitude marriage, must not only endure his wife’s adultery but _buy her back_ — __“So I *bought her* to me for fifteen pieces of silver”__ (Hosea 3:2) — and why the reunion is a fresh betrothal in the vocabulary this book keeps finding at the center: __“I will *betroth thee* unto me for ever; yea, I will betroth thee unto me in sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness], and in sym:sym-judgment[judgment]… and thou shalt sym:sym-knowing[know] the LORD”__ (Hosea 2:19-20).
That is why the forsaken wife is told her shame is over: __“thou shalt forget… the reproach of thy sym:sym-widow[widowhood]… For thy *Maker* is thine *husband*… For a small sym:sym-moment[moment] have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I *gather* thee”__ (Isaiah 54:4-7).
And that is why, when they dragged a woman taken __“in the very act”__ of adultery before Jesus and quoted the death penalty at Him (John 8:4-5), the only Man present with the right to cast the first stone stooped, wrote in the dust, and let her accusers file out — __“Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sym:sym-sin[sin] *no more*”__ (John 8:11).
And mark that the trap was lawless on its face: the statute they quoted requires *both* — __“they shall *both* of them die, both the *man* that lay with the woman, and the woman”__ (Deuteronomy 22:22; Leviticus 20:10) — and a woman taken in the very act was taken with a man they knew and did not bring.
To stone her alone would have broken the very law they invoked; He could not lawfully condemn her on their case.
He was not waiving the penalty.
He was on His way to pay it.

[discrete]
=== The Burning and the Wedding

The Bible closes its marriage file the way the law requires — with an execution and a wedding, in that order.
The false wife, __“the great sym:sym-harlot[whore]”__ who corrupted the earth with her sym:sym-fornication[fornication], is judged at last: __“true and righteous are his judgments: for he hath sym:sym-judgment[judged] the great sym:sym-harlot[whore]”__ (Revelation 19:2), and she is __“utterly *burned with fire*”__ (Revelation 18:8) — the law’s own sentence for the priest’s daughter who plays the whore (Leviticus 21:9).
And in the very next breath:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 19:7-8, ASV]
____
Let us rejoice and be exceeding glad, and let us give the glory unto him: for the *marriage of the* sym:sym-lamb[Lamb] is come, and his *wife hath made herself ready*.
And it was given unto her that she should array herself in *fine linen, bright and pure*: for the fine linen is the sym:sym-righteousness[righteous acts] of the saints.
____

The counterfeit wife burned; the true bride wed, dressed in _righteous acts_ — plural and active in the Greek, where the King James flattens it to __“the righteousness of saints”__ — the commandments kept, worn: the sym:sym-garment[garment] whose full reading has its own chapter ahead.
Paul had named the mystery long before: __“For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and shall be joined unto his wife, and they two shall be one flesh.
This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning *Christ and the church*”__ (Ephesians 5:31-32) — every marriage since Eden has been a similitude of this one, which is why the law guards marriage so fiercely and why the prophets grieve its breaking in the language of heaven’s own wounds.
__“For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste sym:sym-virgin[virgin] to Christ”__ (2 Corinthians 11:2).

So the symbols return their verdict on the sentence the AI gave us.
Divorce for adultery is not the law’s remedy and was never Jesus’ concession — adultery’s wage is death, and the Husband paid it.
The putting-away Scripture allows is the one it commands: the union that is itself sym:sym-fornication[fornication], the marriage to the daughter of a strange god, the covenant God never joined.
And the marriage Scripture will not let go of is the covenant one — defended in the law, mourned by the prophets, kept by God Himself through His wife’s adultery, His own bill of divorce, His death, and the wedding feast He has already announced.
The gospel, read through this symbol, is a Husband who would not quit: __“Turn, O backsliding children, saith the LORD; *for I am married unto you*”__ (Jeremiah 3:14).
The marriage is the covenant between one God and one people — and the wing the bride came under, the covenant’s own hem, is the next symbol.
