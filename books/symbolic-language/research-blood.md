# Research: Blood — dual-meaning evaluation (TODO item, drafted for review)

Question posed (TODO): one symbol with two poles, or two distinct uses?

## The two poles

- LIFE GIVEN (covenant): "the LIFE of the flesh is in the BLOOD…
  I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement" (Lev
  17:11); "the BLOOD OF THE COVENANT" (Ex 24:8; Matt 26:28; Heb 13:20);
  "by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy PRISONERS out of
  the pit" (Zech 9:11 — blood as release-instrument, ties ch. 25
  Liberty); the Passover token (Ex 12:13, in chs. 7/8); washed robes
  (Rev 7:14).
- LIFE TAKEN (death upon the peoples): waters→blood (first plague; Rev
  16:3-6 "became as the blood OF A DEAD MAN: and every living soul
  DIED"); "they have shed the blood of saints… thou hast given them
  BLOOD TO DRINK"; blood in the skirts (Jer 2:34, ch. 15); the winepress
  (Rev 14:20).

## Verdict proposed

ONE substance, ONE meaning — LIFE (Lev 17:11 states it) — read in two
directions by context: life POURED OUT TO GOD seals covenant and
releases; life POURED OUT BY VIOLENCE indicts and returns on the head
of the shedder ("blood for blood"). Same pattern as lion (royal
predator: rightful in Judah's King, wicked in Babylon) and shadow
(whose covering). So: not two symbols, and not strictly a symbol at
all by the ch. 1 test (its dictionary meaning IS life-in-death) — but
a glossary entry is still useful for the waters→blood equation.
