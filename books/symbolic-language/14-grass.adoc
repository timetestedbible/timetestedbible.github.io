The last chapter set a sym:sym-mountain[mountain] over a sym:sym-tree[tree] — a kingdom over a nation.
Beneath them both is the smallest of the three figures, and the shortest-lived:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 40:6-8]
____
…All *flesh is grass*, and all the goodliness thereof is as the *flower of the* sym:sym-field[field]… The *grass withereth*, the flower fadeth… but the *word of our God shall stand for ever*.
____

Grass is a man — a single mortal, up in the morning and gone by evening. __“As for man, his days are as grass… the wind passeth over it, and it is gone”__ (Psalm 103:15-16); __“in the morning it flourisheth… in the evening it is cut down, and withereth”__ (Psalm 90:5-6).
So the three figures of a people are complete: a _mountain_ is the kingdom, a _tree_ the nation, and the _grass_ the individual who springs up and dies within it.
And the New Testament simply hands the definition back: __“all flesh is as grass… The grass withereth”__ (1 Peter 1:24).

[discrete]
=== Grain Is Grass

But grass is not only the picture of frailty; it is also the field God farms.
The grains that feed the world — barley and wheat — are grasses, and with them the symbol turns from the withering blade to the standing crop.
The field is sown, and Jesus names both the seed and the soil: __“The seed is the word of God”__ (Luke 8:11), scattered on the hearts of men — some hard, some shallow, some good ground that bears.
And the seed must die to live: __“Except a *grain* of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit”__ (John 12:24, ASV).
The word buried in a man is a seed that dies and rises into a harvest.

[discrete]
=== The Harvest

Grain is grown to be reaped, and the reaping is judgment.
The prophets fixed that meaning long before the parables — __“Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe… for their wickedness is great”__ (Joel 3:13); the nations gathered __“as the sheaves into the floor”__ to be threshed by Zion (Micah 4:12-13).
So when Jesus says the same, He is not inventing an image but citing one:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:39]
____
…the *harvest is the end of the world*; and the *reapers are the angels*.
____

The sym:sym-field[field] is the world, the crop is mankind, and the sym:sym-harvest[harvest] is the day it is cut down.
Revelation reaps the same field with the same words — __“Thrust in thy *sickle*, and reap… for the *harvest of the earth* is ripe”__ (Revelation 14:15) — Joel’s command reissued at the end: one chain from the Prophets through the Gospels to the last book.
And the harvest has a present tense too: __“Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are *white already to harvest*”__ (John 4:35); __“The harvest truly is plenteous, but the *labourers* are few”__ (Matthew 9:37).

[discrete]
=== Wheat, Chaff, and Tares

Two different partings happen at the harvest, and the whole vocabulary is Old Testament.
The first is the grain parted from its own husk.
When the threshed wheat is thrown up into the wind, the light, empty *chaff* is carried off while the heavy grain falls back to the floor — and this is what the wicked are likened to, weightless and rootless: __“like the chaff which the sym:sym-wind[wind] driveth away”__ (Psalm 1:4), __“chased as the chaff of the sym:sym-mountain[mountains] before the wind”__ (Isaiah 17:13) — the very kingdoms of this book’s first chapter, blown off like husks.
John the Baptist says it in one breath and expects to be understood: __“he will throughly purge his floor, and gather his wheat into the garner; but he will burn up the chaff with unquenchable fire”__ (Matthew 3:12).

The second parting is between two different plants.
The *tares* are not the wheat's own husk but a weed sown among it — darnel, which *mimics* the wheat so closely that the two cannot be told apart until the ear forms.
That is the enemy's work, and Jesus decodes it plainly:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 13:38]
____
The sym:sym-field[*field*] *is the world*; the good *seed are the children of the kingdom*; but the *tares are the children of the wicked one*…
____

So the two are gathered by different means to one end.
The chaff is winnowed off by the wind; the tares are bound __“in bundles to burn them”__ (Matthew 13:30) — the counterfeit gathered with the counterfeit — while the wheat alone is kept: __“gather the wheat into my barn”__ (Matthew 13:30).
The threshingfloor is the day of judgment, and it misses nothing: neither the empty husk nor the planted lie.
But mark the mercy in the machinery: __“I will *sift* the house of Israel among all nations, like as *grain* is sifted in a *sieve*, yet shall *not the least grain fall* upon the earth”__ (Amos 9:9, ASV) — the promise the Lord repeated to Peter under the same figure: sifted as sym:sym-wheat[wheat], but prayed for, and kept (Luke 22:31-32).
The shaking is real; the grain is not lost.

[discrete]
=== The Firstfruits

One grain is reaped before all the rest.
Barley ripens first, in the spring, and the law commanded that its first sheaf be brought and waved before the LORD — __“a sheaf of the firstfruits of your harvest”__ (Leviticus 23:10) — the pledge of the whole harvest to come.
That sheaf is Christ: __“now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept”__ (1 Corinthians 15:20), raised in the very season of the wave sheaf, the first of a harvest that rises after Him.
Barley is the humble, early grain — the loaf a soldier dreams, which is Israel, tumbling into the enemy camp and overturning it (Judges 7:13); the five barley loaves a boy carries, from which a multitude is fed (John 6:9).
The firstfruits are few and lowly, but they carry the whole field in promise.

[discrete]
=== The Leaven

Grain ground and baked becomes bread, and __“man doth not live by sym:sym-bread[bread] only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD”__ (Deuteronomy 8:3) — the bread is the word.
What, then, is *leaven*?
It is bread soured.
The very consonants that spell it also spell vinegar, an embittered heart, a cruel man; leaven is the loaf turned impure — fermented, puffed up, gone off.
So the word that comes near God must carry none of it: leaven is swept out before Passover (Exodus 12:15) and forbidden on the altar — __“ye shall burn no leaven… in any offering of the LORD made by fire”__ (Leviticus 2:11).
The bread offered to God is _unleavened_, a pure and unsoured word.

This is why Jesus warned, __“beware… of the leaven of the Pharisees”__ (Matthew 16:6), and why His disciples, supposing He meant ordinary loaves, missed Him — until they understood __“that he bade them not beware of the leaven of bread, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees”__ (Matthew 16:12).
Their doctrine was the word gone sour: the true bread embittered and puffed up, its corruption working through the whole lump.
Paul keeps the figure exactly: __“Know ye not that a *little leaven* leaveneth the *whole lump*? *Purge out* therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a *new lump*, as ye are unleavened… let us keep the feast… with the *unleavened bread of sincerity and truth*”__ (1 Corinthians 5:6-8).
Sound teaching is the unleavened word; corrupt teaching is the word leavened.

[discrete]
=== Ears to Hear

Set it together and the parables come clear.
When Jesus told of a sower, of wheat among tares, of a harvest and a burning, He was not speaking a new tongue; He was speaking the oldest one.
A hearer who knew that flesh is grass, that the harvest is the day of the LORD, that the wicked are chaff and tares and the firstfruits the LORD's own, needed no interpreter — the meaning was written already in the Law and the Prophets.
That is why He could say __“Who hath ears to hear, let him hear”__ (Matthew 13:9), and then wonder that His own disciples asked what the parable meant — __“Know ye not this parable? and how then will ye know all parables?”__
(Mark 4:13).
The words were sown centuries before; the parable only brought in the sheaves.
To read the New Testament by the Old is to reap what was long since planted.
