# Donkey / Ass — symbol study (2026-07-11)

Hebrew chamor (H2543, he-ass): 93 verses, 96 tokens (Judg 15:16, 1 Kgs 13:13,
13:28 carry it twice). She-ass: athon (H860), 28 verses, 34 tokens (Num 22
carries it three times in v23, twice in vv27, 30; 1 Sam 9:3 and 10:2 twice).
Colt: ayir (H5895), 8×. Wild ass: pere (H6501), 10× — INCLUDING Gen 16:12,
where KJV "a wild man" hides the noun: pere adam, a wild-ass man; the tally
counts the tag, not the English. Sibling: arod (H6171), 1× (Job 39:5b,
parallel to pere in the same verse); Aramaic arad (H6167), 1× (Dan 5:21).
Greek: onos (G3688), 6×; onarion "young ass" (G3678), 1× (John 12:14); polos
"colt" (G4454), 11×; hypozygion (G5268), 2× — Matt 21:5 and 2 Pet 2:16, and
the word itself is the Greek NT's gloss of the animal: hypo-zygion, "the
beast UNDER THE YOKE." Excluded neighbor: pered (H6505, mule) — the hybrid,
David's royal mount (1 Kgs 1:33) — a different lexeme, flagged wherever a
thematic point touches it. All tallies verified verse-by-verse against
kjv_strongs.txt.

## Candidate definition

**The ass is the burden-bearer — man in his laboring flesh, defined by whose
yoke he is under: brought into the household it knows its master's crib,
bows the shoulder to the load, rests on the sabbath, and — alone among the
unclean — its firstling is redeemed with a lamb or dies by the neck;
un-yoked it is the pere, the wild-ass man, a "disciple of the wilderness,"
alone.** The same beast is the seat of Israel's native, lowly rule — judges'
sons on colts where the nations ride horses — until the King Himself comes
"meek, sitting upon the foal of the yoke-beast," and the horse is cut off.

**Author's refinement (2026-07-11): the stiffnecked people = the donkey,
whether native or foreign.** The fork of the yoke is no bloodline test —
the Passover chapter legislates it in place: "One law shall be to him that
is HOMEBORN, and unto the STRANGER that sojourneth among you" (Exod 12:49),
said of who may eat the redeeming lamb. The home-born stiffneck faces the
same araph as the unredeemed firstling; Prov 29:1 runs the sentence at
individual scale ("hardeneth his neck… destroyed without remedy" — maqsheh
oreph, the stiffnecked anatomy); and the offer side is equally unrestricted:
the King who rides the yoke-beast addresses "ALL ye that labour and are
heavy laden… take my yoke upon you" (Matt 11:28-29) — burden-bearers as
such, not a nation. Israel in Egypt (the firstborn son under sivlot and
mas, redeemed by the lamb) is the pattern's chief national instance, not
its boundary.

## The defining statute — the unclean bearer redeemed by the lamb

- **Exod 13:13 — "Every firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem (padah) with a
  lamb (seh); and if thou wilt not redeem it, then thou shalt break his neck:
  and all the firstborn of MAN among thy children shalt thou redeem."** The
  ass and the son stand in one verse under one verb — padah (H6299), three
  times. Repeated verbatim at the covenant renewal (Exod 34:20).
- **The ass is the only unclean animal the statute names.** The frame makes
  the singling-out visible: clean firstlings are NOT redeemed — "they are
  holy: thou shalt sprinkle their blood upon the altar" (Num 18:17); generic
  unclean firstlings are redeemed at estimation-plus-a-fifth or simply SOLD
  (Lev 27:27; Num 18:15). The ass alone gets the personal sentence: no
  altar (unclean), no price, no sale — a substitute from the flock dies, or
  it dies. That is man's column of the ledger: the firstborn of man in the
  same breath, redeemed by substitution, never sacrificed.
- **The lamb is the book's sym-lamb**: seh, "one of the flock — a covenant
  person… the Lamb of God the perfect lamb of the flock." The statute's
  grammar: the unclean burden-bearer lives only by the substitution of one
  of the flock.
- **Israel's own exodus runs the statute at nation scale.** "Israel is my
  son, even my FIRSTBORN" (Exod 4:22); the firstborn-nation serves under
  Egypt's BURDENS — sivlot (H5450, Exod 6:6), the noun of saval "to bear,"
  under "taskmasters" who are literally sarei MAS, princes of the levy (H4522,
  Exod 1:11) — and is redeemed by the Passover lamb the night the unredeemed
  firstborn die. Exod 13:13 sits inside that very pericope; v15 states the
  exchange ("all the firstborn of my children I redeem").
- **MESH WITH THE COIN CHAPTER (ch. 10, "The Census and Its Ransom" region):**
  the chapter's architecture is claim → ransom (firstborn claimed at Exod
  13:2; ransomed by lamb at Passover, by silver at census; God's house
  socketed in the redemption money). The donkey supplies the portrait the
  architecture assumes: WHAT the claimed one is before redemption — the
  unclean burden-bearer who cannot go to the altar and cannot pay, standing
  in the statute shoulder to shoulder with "the firstborn of man." The Coin
  chapter says God counts what He redeems; Exod 13:13 shows the counted one:
  a beast of burden under a lamb's blood.
- **The neck seals it.** "Break his neck" is araph (H6202), the denominative
  verb of oreph (H6203), NECK — the word inside STIFFNECKED (qesheh-oreph,
  Exod 32:9), and Moses pleads that very word eleven verses before the
  statute's repeat: "it is a stiffnecked people; and pardon our iniquity"
  (Exod 34:9 → 34:20). Prov 29:1 runs the sentence in proverb form: "He,
  that being often reproved HARDENETH his NECK (oreph), shall suddenly be
  destroyed, and that WITHOUT REMEDY." Refuse the redemption, and the
  stiff neck is the broken neck. (Lexeme-grade for oreph; araph's other
  ritual uses keep the register — the never-yoked heifer of the unsolved
  murder, Deut 21:4, 6; the anti-sacrifice "as if he cut off a dog's neck,"
  Isa 66:3 — araph is what is done to the unclean, never to the seh.)

## Scripture's self-decode — Issachar the strong ass (Gen 49:14-15)

- "Issachar is a strong ass couching down between two burdens: and he saw
  that rest was good… and BOWED HIS SHOULDER TO BEAR (saval, H5445), and
  became a SERVANT UNTO TRIBUTE (mas, H4522)." A tribe decoded in-text: the
  ass = the servant who bows the shoulder to the load. The oracle's two key
  words are Egypt's two words — mas (the taskmasters' levy, Exod 1:11) and
  saval (the burden-root of Egypt's sivlot, Exod 6:6).
- **saval is the Servant's verb**: "my righteous SERVANT [shall] justify
  many; for he shall BEAR (yisbol, H5445) their iniquities" (Isa 53:11). The
  burden-bearer's verb runs from Issachar's saddlebags through Egypt's
  brick-loads to the sin-bearing Servant — lexeme-grade all three.
- Name note: Issachar = "man of hire/wages" (Gen 30:18, sachar) — the
  wage-servant tribe wears the wage-servant beast.
- **Isa 46:1-7 makes carrying the test of a god.** Babylon's idols are
  loads — "your carriages were heavy loaden; they are a BURDEN to the weary
  beast" (46:1) — but Israel's God carries His people: "even I will CARRY
  (esbol, H5445 again), and will deliver you" (46:4). Idols must be borne;
  God bears. Who-bears-whom is the donkey question, and Isaiah asks it of
  every religion.

## The ass that knows its Master (a recurring shame-structure)

Three scenes run the same inversion — the yoke-beast's knowing service set
against covenant-men who will not know. Stated as structure, not proof:

- **Isa 1:3 — "The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his MASTER'S (baal)
  CRIB: but Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider."** The
  unclean beast knows the hand that feeds it; the covenant son does not.
  sym-knowing is the covenant verb, and the ass has it.
- **Num 22 — Balaam's athon** sees the messenger (malak) three times, saves
  the seer's life, absorbs his blows, and when the LORD opens her mouth she
  argues from covenant service: "Am not I thine ass, upon which thou hast
  ridden ever since I was thine unto this day?" (22:30). 2 Pet 2:16 names
  her by function: "the DUMB ASS (aphonon hypozygion — the voiceless
  under-yoke) speaking with man's voice forbad the madness of the prophet."
  The submissive bearer sees what the prophet-for-hire cannot.
- **1 Kgs 13 — the old prophet's ass** stands by the slain man of God, the
  lion beside it: "the lion had not eaten the carcase, nor torn the ass"
  (13:28) — judgment measured to the man, the beast of burden untouched —
  and then the ass carries the dead prophet home to burial.

## The wild fork — pere, the un-yoked

- **Job 11:12 — the hinge verse: "vain man would be wise, though MAN be BORN
  like a WILD ASS'S COLT" (ayir pere adam yivvaled).** One clause welds all
  three words: man is born the pere's colt; heart comes only by coming
  under a yoke.
- **Gen 16:12 — Ishmael, pere adam,** "a wild-ass man; his hand against
  every man, and every man's hand against him" (KJV "wild man" hides the
  noun; the tag carries it). He grows up exactly to the word: "he dwelt in
  the wilderness" (21:20).
- **Hos 8:9 — "they are gone up to Assyria, a wild ass alone by himself:
  Ephraim hath hired lovers."** The covenant tribe that slipped the yoke is
  the lone pere. (Wordplay, flagged as wordplay: pere / Ephraim ring on the
  same consonants.)
- **Jer 2:24 — the study's sharpest find: "a wild ass USED TO the
  wilderness, that snuffeth up the wind at her pleasure" — "used to" is
  limmud (H3928), THE DISCIPLE WORD.** "Seal the law among my DISCIPLES
  (limmudai)" (Isa 8:16 — the sym-disciple anchor); "all thy children shall
  be TAUGHT of the LORD (limmudei YHWH)" (Isa 54:13); "ye… that are
  ACCUSTOMED (limmudei) to do evil" (Jer 13:23). The pere is a limmud too —
  a disciple of the wilderness, schooled by no-master, appetite for teacher.
  Every man is somebody's limmud; the fork is the school. Lexeme-grade,
  H3928 in all four.
- **Job 39:5-8 — the boundary stone.** God's own speech celebrates the
  pere: "Who hath sent out the wild ass free? or who hath loosed the bands
  of the wild ass (arod)?… he scorneth the multitude of the city, neither
  regardeth he the crying of the driver." In a BEAST, wildness is God's own
  gift (cf. Ps 104:11, God waters the wild asses). The indictment texts are
  all covenant-MEN wearing the pere's portrait. The wild pole is therefore
  not "the wicked"; it is THE UN-YOKED STATE — glory in a beast, tragedy or
  treason in a son.
- **Job 24:3, 5 — both poles in one oppression scene**: the wicked "drive
  away the ass of the fatherless" (v3) — strip the orphan's one bearer —
  and the dispossessed poor themselves are driven out "as wild asses in the
  desert… the wilderness yieldeth food for them and for their children"
  (v5). Un-house a man and you make him a pere.
- **Dan 5:21 — Nebuchadnezzar's cure.** The king un-yoked from heaven's
  Master is sent to the pere's address: "his dwelling was with the wild
  asses (arad)… TILL HE KNEW that the most high God ruled" — Isa 1:3's
  missing verb, learned at the wild ass's crib-less table.
- Jer 14:6 (wild asses on the high places, snuffing wind, eyes failing for
  no grass) keeps the register: the un-yoked starve when the wind is all
  there is to eat.

## The King's mount — the ass against the horse

- **In Israel the ass is the seat of native rule; the horse is imported
  war-power.** The judges' line rides colts: Jair's thirty sons on thirty
  ass-colts over thirty cities (Judg 10:4 — the text puns ayarim/colts with
  ayarim/cities), Abdon's seventy (12:14); Deborah sings to "ye that ride
  on white asses" as the class who "walk by the way" (Judg 5:10 — KJV's
  "sit in judgment" rides the disputed middin; the nobility reading stands
  on the verse's own rider/walker contrast, disclosed below); "the asses be
  for the KING'S HOUSEHOLD to ride on" (2 Sam 16:2). Meanwhile the law
  forbids the king multiplied horses (Deut 17:16); "an horse is a vain
  thing for safety" (Ps 33:17); woe to them that "trust in chariots" and
  "ride on horses" (Isa 31:1). sym-horse is the conqueror's mount; the ass
  is the servant-ruler's.
- **Gen 49:10-11 — the colt waits at the vine.** "Until Shiloh come; and
  unto him shall the gathering of the people be. BINDING his FOAL (ayir)
  unto the VINE, and his ASS'S COLT (beni athono) unto the CHOICE VINE
  (soreq)" — the soreq is the very planting-word of God's vine-nation
  (Isa 5:2; Jer 2:21, same H8321). Judah's royal colt stands hitched to
  the vine-nation until the one comes whose it is.
- **Zech 9:9 gathers all three domestic lexemes into one verse** — chamor,
  ayir, ben-athonot: "thy King cometh unto thee: he is just, and HAVING
  SALVATION (nosha — saved); LOWLY (ani, H6041 — the sym-poor/meek word
  family), and riding upon an ass, and upon a colt the foal of an ass."
  The next verse cuts off the counter-mount: "I will cut off the chariot
  from Ephraim, and the HORSE from Jerusalem, and the battle bow shall be
  cut off: and he shall speak PEACE unto the heathen" (9:10) — and the
  mounted King looses prisoners: "by the blood of thy covenant I have sent
  forth thy prisoners out of the pit" (9:11).
- **Matt 21 / Mark 11 / Luke 19 / John 12 — the colt TIED and LOOSED.**
  "Ye shall find an ass TIED (deo), and a colt with her: LOOSE (lyo) them…
  the Lord hath need of them" (Matt 21:2-3); "a colt tied, WHEREON NEVER
  MAN SAT" (Mark 11:2; Luke 19:30) — the unbroken colt, no man's beast,
  submits at first mounting to its Owner (the never-yoked grade Scripture
  reserves for sacred first use: the red heifer, Num 19:2; the araph
  heifer, Deut 21:3; the ark's kine, 1 Sam 6:7). And the borrowed servant
  is returned: "straightway he will send him hither" (Mark 11:3) — the
  Master takes no man's ass (the taking-test below).
- **Matthew's Greek welds mount to Rider.** 21:5 reads "thy King cometh
  unto thee, MEEK (praus), sitting… upon a colt the SON OF THE UNDER-YOKE
  (huios hypozygiou)" — and Matthew has already fixed both words on the
  Rider Himself: "Take my YOKE (zygos) upon you… for I am MEEK (praos) and
  lowly in heart… my BURDEN is light" (11:29-30). The meek King who offers
  His yoke rides the yoke-beast's son. (Disclosure: Strong's splits one
  adjective's spellings, G4239 at 21:5 / G4235 at 11:29; zygos is the noun
  inside hypo-zygion.)
- **The loosed colt joins the loosed daughter.** Luke 13:15-16 — "doth not
  each one of you on the sabbath LOOSE (lyo) his ox or his ASS from the
  stall…? ought not this woman… whom Satan hath BOUND (deo)… be LOOSED
  (lyo) from this bond on the sabbath day?" Jesus' own a-fortiori runs from
  the ass's tether to the person's bond — the beast-law read of persons,
  exactly the hermeneutic Paul states for plow-beast law: "Doth God take
  care for oxen? or saith he it altogether FOR OUR SAKES?" (1 Cor 9:9-10).
  Same deo/lyo pair as the colt. And the loosing is never into the wild —
  sym-liberty's "change of masters, never the absence of one": the colt is
  loosed BECAUSE "the Lord hath need of him."
- **HEBREW-GOSPEL SECOND WITNESS (data/hg-chapters/):** Matthew 21 reads
  athon + ayir throughout — "you will find ONE SHE-ASS (athon) and one
  COLT (ayir)" (21:2), "riding upon a she-ass and upon a colt, the FOAL OF
  A SHE-ASS (ayir ben athon)… righteous and SAVED is he, LOWLY (ani)"
  (21:5). Mark 11 uses ayir in every verse (2, 4, 5, 7). Luke 19:30 —
  where the Greek has only polos — the Hebrew expands to "a colt, SON OF A
  SHE-DONKEY (ayir ben athon), tied, that never a person rode upon it."
  That is Gen 49:11's exact pair (ayir + ben-athon) under all three
  synoptics: the Hebrew witness reads the entry through Judah's oracle,
  not only Zechariah's. Luke 13:15 and 14:5 use chamor for the household
  sabbath-texts, matching the OT statute vocabulary.

## Other facets, same structure

- **Jer 22:19 — the burial of an ass.** Jehoiakim "shall be buried with the
  burial of an ass, drawn and cast forth beyond the gates of Jerusalem" —
  sentenced six verses after the indictment: "Woe unto him… that useth his
  neighbour's SERVICE WITHOUT WAGES" (22:13). The king who yoked men
  without pay is dragged out like a dead yoke-beast — measure for measure
  in donkey-grammar. A dead ass gets no burial because it was never more
  than its load; the unredeemed bearer's end.
- **Deut 22:10 — "thou shalt not plow with an ox and an ass together."**
  The clean altar-beast and the unclean redeemed-beast may not share one
  yoke. Paul's verb for the forbidden pairing is the law's own picture:
  "be not UNEQUALLY YOKED (heterozygountes) with unbelievers" (2 Cor 6:14)
  — thematic-grade (no donkey lexeme in Paul; the yoke carries the link),
  licensed by his stated for-our-sakes hermeneutic (1 Cor 9:9-10).
- **The ass keeps the stranger's company in the mercy-clauses.** The
  enemy's straying ass returned, the hater's ass raised under its burden
  (Exod 23:4-5); the sabbath rest named FOR it: "that thine ox and thine
  ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid, and the STRANGER, may be
  refreshed" (Exod 23:12; Deut 5:14) — the outsider inside the household's
  rest. Its inverse is Neh 13:15, "lading asses" on the sabbath — the
  breach measured on the beast the law rested.
- **The bearer carries the broken home.** Samaria's men set "all the feeble
  of them upon ASSES" and returned the captives to Jericho (2 Chr 28:15);
  the Samaritan "set him on his own beast" (Luke 10:34 — generic ktenos,
  thematic echo only). Jesse's ass carries bread to Saul (1 Sam 16:20),
  Abigail's carries the peace-offering down to David (1 Sam 25:18-23),
  Joseph's ten asses carry "the good things of Egypt" home to Jacob (Gen
  45:23): the ass is provision's carrier from first to last.
- **Saul and the lost she-asses (1 Sam 9-10).** The asses of Kish are LOST
  (avad, H6) and Saul is sent to SEEK (baqash, H1245) — the exact grammar
  of the lost flock: "I have gone astray like a LOST (avad) sheep (seh!);
  SEEK (baqash) thy servant" (Ps 119:176); "my people hath been LOST sheep"
  (Jer 50:6). Inside the errand the kingdom arrives: "the asses are found.
  And on whom is all the desire of Israel?" (9:20). Samuel has just warned
  what the asked-for king does with asses: "he will TAKE… your asses, and
  put them to HIS work" (8:16) — against the servant-leaders' oath, "whose
  ass have I taken?" (Samuel, 1 Sam 12:3; Moses, Num 16:15). The people's
  burden-bearers are the people's service: the false shepherd takes them;
  the true takes none; the Owner only borrows — "the Lord hath need of
  him" — and sends him back. (Lexeme-grade on avad/baqash; the Saul-errand
  reading itself is association-grade and claimed as structure only.)
- **The ass stops below the mount.** "Abide ye here WITH THE ASS; and I and
  the lad will go yonder and worship" (Gen 22:5) — the bearer carries the
  wood toward Moriah, and the service ends where worship begins; sonship
  goes up. (Already quoted in ch. 21, Worship.) Exod 4:20 is its far echo:
  Moses sets wife and sons on the chamor to ride TOWARD Egypt for the
  first redemption; the last Redeemer rides the chamor INTO Jerusalem for
  the greater one. Both association-grade.
- **The flesh-texts are the wild pole seen from inside.** Oholibah dotes on
  Egypt's paramours "whose FLESH is as the flesh of ASSES" (Ezek 23:20);
  Jer 2:24's pere is in heat, "in her occasion who can turn her away?"
  Appetite is the master where the Master is not — the slave-house's flesh
  is beast-flesh. 2 Kgs 6:25 belongs here as covenant collapse: in the
  siege famine an ASS'S HEAD — unclean, inedible — sells for eighty pieces
  of silver.

## Consonantal and wordplay notes (second dimension, never the definition)

- **chamor (חמור) rides the consonants of chomer (חמר) — clay, mire, the
  potter's material, the homer-measure**: "we are the CLAY (chomer), and
  thou our potter" (Isa 64:8; Job 10:9). The burden-beast and the potter's
  clay share one skeleton, as adam shares adamah — the red-clay beast for
  the red-earth man. Wordplay only, but it says what the texts say: the
  ass is man's laboring flesh.
- **Judg 15:16 puns it in-text, tag-visible**: "with the jawbone of an ASS
  (chamor, H2543), HEAPS upon HEAPS (chamor chamoratayim, H2565)" — Samson
  stacks the Philistines in donkey-piles.
- **Judg 10:4** puns ayarim (colts) with ayarim (cities, spelled like
  colts) — thirty sons, thirty colts, thirty cities.
- **Hamor (H2544), prince of Shechem** — "serve the men of HAMOR" (Judg
  9:28): Shechem's citizens are "sons of Donkey." Name-note only.
- **pere / Ephraim** (Hos 8:9) — see the wild fork above.
- **"Ass = fool" is Latin, not Hebrew.** Scripture's donkey texts are about
  service, never stupidity: Prov 26:3 disciplines ass and fool in one verse
  but as distinct instruments (bridle for the one, rod for the other), and
  Ps 32:9's no-understanding beast is the horse and the MULE (pered), not
  the chamor. The modern sneer imports a dictionary meaning the text
  refutes — Scripture's ass is the knowing servant (Isa 1:3).

## Counter-texts weighed

- **Most occurrences are plain livestock and stay literal**: wealth ledgers
  (Gen 12:16; 24:35; 30:43; Job 1:3; 42:12), plunder and herem inventories
  (Gen 34:28; Num 31; Josh 6:21; 7:24; 1 Sam 15:3; 27:9; 1 Chr 5:21), the
  murrain (Exod 9:3), theft/pit/deposit statutes (Exod 21:33-22:10), travel
  and provisioning scenes (Gen 42-45; Josh 9:4; 2 Sam 16-17; 19:26; 1 Kgs
  2:40; 2 Kgs 4; Judg 19), the returning exiles' 6,720 (Ezra 2:67). The
  symbol never dissolves the animal. Yet even the literal texts hold one
  shape: the ass is always SOMEBODY'S, named with the ox as the household's
  working pair (covet-law, sabbath-law, fallen-beast law) — and Jesus keeps
  the pair alive in both His sabbath appeals (Luke 13:15; 14:5).
- **Judg 5:10 disclosure**: "sit in judgment" renders middin (H4055),
  elsewhere garments/carpets; the nobility point rides the verse's own
  contrast (they that ride vs they that walk), not the KJV gloss.
- **Isa 21:7's "chariot of asses"** in the Babylon-watch oracle is a
  caravan detail the watchman logs; no claim made.
- **Overclaim check 1 — the ass is not "Israel" by identity.** Issachar is
  called an ass; Israel is shamed BY the ass (Isa 1:3); Ishmael and
  Nebuchadnezzar wear the pere. The constant is the RELATION — burden-bearer
  under, or out from under, a master — not any nation's identity. Identity
  claims would need identity-grade evidence the texts do not give.
- **Overclaim check 2 — the wild pole is not simply "the wicked."** Job 39
  is God celebrating the freedom He gave the beast; Job 24:5's pere-life is
  inflicted on the innocent poor. The symbol's wild pole is the un-yoked
  state as such; the verdict depends on whose neck left the yoke, and why.
- **Overclaim check 3 — the statute's scope is kept.** Exod 13:13 redeems
  the FIRSTLING of the ass, not every ass; the man-ward reading rides the
  verse's own pairing (ass + son, one verb) and the Num 18:15-17 /
  Lev 27:27 frame that singles the ass out — not on a claim that every
  donkey in Scripture is a redemption figure.
- **Riding is not automatically royal.** Abigail, the Shunammite, Achsah,
  the Levite's concubine, and half of Genesis ride asses as ordinary
  transport; the rule-thread stands on the texts that state it (Judg 5:10;
  10:4; 12:14; 2 Sam 16:2; Zech 9:9-10), not on the act of riding.

## Open questions

- Placement: the firstling statute is print-grade and lands inside ch. 10's
  existing "Redemption of the Firstborn" region — does the donkey join the
  Coin chapter as a section, or carry its own chapter with the wild/tame
  fork and the King's mount? The limmud find (Jer 2:24) also offers the
  Disciple chapter a dark twin ("disciple of the wilderness").
- The tower, RESOLVED NEGATIVE (2026-07-15): all three Hebrew synoptics
  read tower/fortress for the Greek's "village" at the colt scene — but
  the systematic survey showed Shem Tov and Vat. Ebr. 100 render EVERY
  village as tower (the Vulgate's castellum read classically), so the
  reading is translation habit, not tradition. Hebrew Mark is weakly
  selective (2-vs-1) — insufficient. Closed unless new manuscript
  evidence reopens it. See Addendum 2.
- Isa 32:20 — "blessed are ye that sow beside all waters, that send forth
  thither the feet of the ox and the ass" — sits directly after the Spirit
  poured out (32:15): clean and unclean laborers sent forth (not yoked
  together) to the sowing among the waters/peoples? Attractive; needs an
  ox-side counter-sweep before claiming. Isa 30:24's restoration colt
  eating "clean provender… winnowed" belongs to the same question.
- Whether Gen 49:11 should be cited as the entry's root text in print: the
  evangelists' explicit citation is Zech 9:9, but the HG's ayir-ben-athon
  vocabulary in all three synoptics reads the entry through Judah's oracle.
  Currently held as structure (the colt bound to the soreq until Shiloh
  comes; loosed when He comes), association-grade.

## Addendum (author, 2026-07-15): Zech 9:9 — is He riding on BOTH?

The question: does the language of Zech 9:9 suggest the King rides both
animals? The witnesses, in order:

1. **The Hebrew doubles the preposition.** WLC: רֹכֵב עַל־חֲמֹור
   וְעַל־עַיִר בֶּן־אֲתֹנֹות — rokhev AL-chamor VE-AL-ayir ben-atonot,
   "riding UPON an ass and UPON a colt, son of she-asses (plural)."
   Simple apposition ("a donkey, that is, a colt") needs no second עַל;
   the text takes the marked construction. Grammar alone leaves both
   readings open — poetic doubling of one animal, or coordination of two.
2. **The oracle it echoes coordinates two real objects with the same
   syntax.** Gen 49:11: "binding TO THE VINE (la-gefen) his foal, and TO
   THE CHOICE VINE (ve-la-soreqah) his ass's colt" — doubled preposition,
   two kindred-but-distinct plants, two animal terms (iro / beni atono).
   Nobody reads gefen and soreqah as one vine; by the source-oracle's own
   model, chamor and ayir read most naturally as two kindred mounts.
3. **The LXX — pre-Christian — reads two.** Brenton: "riding on an ass
   AND a young foal" (ἐπὶ ὑποζύγιον καὶ πῶλον νέον). The two-animal
   reading cannot be Matthew's invention; the Greek synagogue read it
   that way centuries before him.
4. **Matthew mirrors the doubled preposition exactly** — ἐπιβεβηκὼς ἐπὶ
   ὄνον ΚΑΙ ἘΠῚ πῶλον (21:5) — and his narrative supplies two animals
   throughout: "they brought the ass and the colt, and laid on THEM
   (ἐπ᾽ αὐτῶν) the garments, and he sat upon THEM (ἐπάνω αὐτῶν)" (21:7).
   The pronoun's first use in the verse is indisputably the two animals;
   the second is usually deflected to "the garments," but Matthew wrote
   the same word twice in one sentence.
5. **The Hebrew Matthew answers the question directly — and surprises.**
   HG 21:7 word-by-word: "they brought the she-ass (athon) and the colt
   (ayir), and Yeshua rode UPON HER (עליה, feminine singular — the
   MOTHER); and the others placed their vessels and their garments on
   THEM (עליהם, both)." The Hebrew witness has Him on the she-ass, the
   colt alongside, the company's gear on both.
6. **Mark, Luke, and John name only the colt** — "whereon never man sat"
   (Mark 11:7 ἐκάθισεν ἐπ᾽ αὐτόν; Luke 19:35 set Jesus ἐπὶ τὸν πῶλον;
   John 12:14 the onarion).

**Assembly.** The witnesses harmonize if the doubled עַל is literal: He
rode BOTH — the mother for one leg (the Hebrew Matthew's "upon her"), the
never-ridden colt for the entry itself (Mark/Luke/John's leg). Matthew,
quoting the prophecy whole, reports the whole: both brought, both
saddled, "he sat upon them." Each single-mount Gospel names one leg;
none contradicts the other. The alternative harmonization (rider on the
colt only, the dam alongside to steady her unbroken foal) satisfies
Mark-Luke-John but leaves the LXX, the doubled preposition, Matthew's
ἐπάνω αὐτῶν, and HG's "upon her" all unexplained.

**Symbolic register (candidate structure, author to weigh):** Gen 49:11
binds the two animals to two vines — the foal to the gefen, the she-ass's
son to the soreqah, the choice-vine word of God's planted nation (Isa
5:2; Jer 2:21). The mother is the athon that has long borne the yoke; the
colt "whereon never man sat" has borne no yoke but His. The King who
comes lowly rides them both into the city — the seasoned bearer and the
newly claimed — one Rider over both, as the one law covers the homeborn
and the stranger (Exod 12:49, the study's yoke-fork refinement). Held as
structure pending the author's ruling.

## Addendum 2 (author, 2026-07-15): "straightway he will send them" — moment-family?

The question: does Matt 21:3's "right away" connect to "immediately" or
"in a moment"?

- **Lexically, no — and the distinction is clean.** "Straightway" is
  euthys/eutheōs (G2117/G2112). "In a moment" (1 Cor 15:52) is en atomō
  (G823, the uncuttable instant) with "the twinkling (rhipē) of an eye";
  Hebrew's moment is rega (H7281, Job 34:20; Isa 26:20). Different word
  families; no tag-level weld.
- **But euthys carries its own freight: it is the STRAIGHT-word.** The
  adverb of promptness is the adjective of the prepared road — "make his
  paths STRAIGHT (eutheias)" (Matt 3:3; John 1:23, Isa 40:3's highway).
  Greek promptness is straightness: no crooked delay. The colt is
  released and returned by the straight way of the Lord.
- **The Hebrew Matthew's bridge: u-MIYAD — "and immediately," letters:
  "and FROM THE HAND."** HG Matt 21:3: "the master has need of them, and
  miyad he will LET THEM GO (ya'azov)"; HG Mark 11:3: "the Lord needs
  him, and miyad they will let him go (yanichuhu)." The Hebrew witness
  reads the promptness as the OWNER'S instant release — the claim of the
  Master opens the hand that held them, at once. (Wordplay note, held
  lightly: Job 34:20 removes the mighty "WITHOUT hand" — lo b'yad — in a
  moment at midnight; the needed colt is released FROM the hand — miyad —
  straightway. The hand opens for the claimed; no hand avails for the
  doomed.)
- **Who sends whom: both directions are instant.** Greek Mark 11:3 adds
  palin — "straightway he sends him BACK here" — the Borrower's promise
  of prompt return; the HG reads the owner's prompt release. The verse
  holds both: released from the hand the moment the Kyrios is named,
  returned straightway when the need is served.
- **Structural kinship to the moment-complex (structure, not lexeme):**
  the shape of the scene — bound, loosed at the Master's claim, carried
  into service, returned — is the gathering's shape: the trumpet sounds
  and the dead are raised "in a moment" (1 Cor 15:52); the colt is
  loosed "because the Lord hath need of him." The vocabulary differs;
  the grammar of instant claim-and-release is one.

**The immediacy is DOUBLED — the finding is also "at once" (2026-07-15):**
Greek Matt 21:2: __“Go into the village… and STRAIGHTWAY (eutheōs) ye
shall find an ass tied”__ — the same eutheōs as v3's sending. HG Matt
21:2 renders it with the same miyad: __“Go to the fortress… and miyad you
will find one she-ass and one colt.”__ At the Master's word the bearer is
found at once and released at once — one errand, two instants, one word
pair (eutheōs / miyad). And the same chapter carries the instant's dark
twin in a third Greek immediacy word: the fruitless fig tree withers
parachrēma, __“presently… How soon is the fig tree withered away!”__
(Matt 21:19-20) — in one chapter the claimed bearer is loosed at once and
the fruitless tree dies at once: the Exodus-midnight grammar (one stroke
frees and judges) in miniature.

**The other Hebrew Gospels (same question, checked):**

- **Hebrew Mark 11:3** matches Hebrew Matthew: __“the Lord needs him, and
  miyad (from-the-hand) they will let him go (yanichuhu).”__ Both
  witnesses read the promptness as the owner's instant release.
- **Hebrew Luke has no immediacy clause** (faithful to the Greek, which
  lacks euthys) — but it pays for the absence three times over:
  1. **The colt stands "at the tower" — but the reading DISSOLVED under
     the systematic check (2026-07-15).** HG Luke 19:30 reads migdol,
     HG Matthew mivtsar, HG Mark migdal, where the Greek has kōmē,
     "village." Village-to-tower is indeed unnatural FROM GREEK — but
     the Vulgate renders kōmē as castellum (Jerome's regular village
     word, classically "fort"), and the rendering survey shows Shem Tov
     Matthew and Vat. Ebr. 100 Luke write tower for EVERY village
     (Matt 9:35; 10:11; 14:15; Luke 9:56; 10:38 — Martha's village;
     17:12; 24:13 — Emmaus). Their colt-tower is translator habit
     (castellum read classically), not a preserved reading. Hebrew Mark
     alone shows possible selectivity (arim, "towns," at 6:6, 36;
     migdal at 11:2; ch. 8's village references paraphrased away) — a
     2-vs-1 sample in a free text, too thin to build on. The Vulgate is
     no rescue (author's follow-up, same day): Jerome renders kōmē as
     castellum SYSTEMATICALLY — Bethany (John 11:1), Emmaus (Luke
     24:13), Bethlehem (John 7:42), Martha's village, the Galilean
     circuits — late Latin's ordinary village-word; a witness that
     calls every village a castle cannot testify that this one was
     special. VERDICT: the manuscript tower is a rendering artifact at
     every level; no claim stands on the versions.
     WHAT SURVIVES, textually: the chapter supplies its own tower —
     Matt 21:33 __“built a TOWER”__ in the vineyard, quoting Isa 5:2
     letter-for-letter (Shem Tov: u-vayin migdal betocho), thirty
     verses after the colt; and Judah's oracle binds the colt TO THE
     VINE (Gen 49:11). The colt at the vine, the vine with its tower —
     one chapter, both from the same two source-oracles. That is the
     defensible route to the tower, needing no manuscript.
  2. **The answer is wage-grammar.** HG Luke 19:31: say __“you have done
     as our lord DESIRES (chafets) his PE'ULAH (work).”__ Pe'ulah
     (H6468) is precisely the word Scripture pairs with sachar in the
     coming-King texts — __“behold, his REWARD (sachar) is with him, and
     his WORK (pe'ulah) before him”__ (Isa 40:10; 62:11, the line Rev
     22:12 takes up) — and the word whose wage is promised in Issachar's
     name-verse: __“thy WORK (pe'ulah) shall be rewarded (yesh sachar)”__
     (Jer 31:16). All tags verified. In the Hebrew Luke, loosing the
     colt IS the lord's wage-work: the King comes with His sachar, and
     His pe'ulah walks before Him on four feet.
  3. **Two adons.** The colt's __“adon”__ (HG 19:33) questions the
     untying and yields to __“adoneinu”__ — the lesser lord releases the
     beast at the greater Lord's claim, the taking-test of 1 Sam 12:3
     passed at the tower.
- **Hebrew John 12:15** carries John's famous rendering — __“Do not
  fear, daughter of Tsiyon”__ for Zechariah's "rejoice greatly" — with
  the same ayir-ben-atonot vocabulary, and closes: the disciples
  understood __“when Yeshua was exalted in the light of the Most High”__
  (HG 12:16).

## Glossary entry

Candidate [[sym-donkey]]. Definition: "The stiffnecked burden-bearer —
man in his laboring flesh, home-born or stranger alike, defined at the
fork of the yoke: in the household it knows its master's crib, bears the
load, rests on the sabbath, and — alone among the unclean — its firstling
is redeemed with a lamb or dies by the neck the stiffnecked harden;
un-yoked it is the wild ass, a 'disciple of the wilderness,' alone. The
judges rode the colts of Israel's peace, and the King comes lowly on the
foal of the yoke-beast while the horse and chariot are cut off."
