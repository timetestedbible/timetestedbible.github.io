# The Palm (kaph) — research notebook

Occasioned by the author's question (2026-07-18): *when God shields Moses, is the
Hebrew word palm? If so that is a Proverbs 31 connection* — and the follow-on chain
*palm = shield = wall = salvation*. Author has ruled the symbol into the glossary,
beside Wall, with the translation note: English "hand" flattens two Hebrew words.

## Vocabulary — the two hands

| word | Strong's | gloss | range |
|------|----------|-------|-------|
| kaph כף | H3709 | open palm, hollow of the hand | 180 occurrences; also sole of the foot (kaph regel), palm FROND, pan/spoon of the altar vessels |
| yad יד | H3027 | hand | the hand of power, agency, possession |

English Bibles print "hand" for both. Scripture itself keeps them apart — twice in a
single verse:

- **Isaiah 62:3** — "a crown of glory in the **hand (yad)** of the LORD, and a royal
  diadem in the **hand (kaph)** of thy God." Two words, one KJV gloss.
- **Proverbs 31:20** — "She stretcheth out her **hand (kaph)** to the poor; yea, she
  reacheth forth her **hands (yad)** to the needy." The poet alternates deliberately.

The pattern of the split: *yad* wields; *kaph* covers, carries, offers, receives. The
palm is the hand OPEN — nothing gripped, nothing hidden.

## Occurrences — the palm's range (all verified in kjv_strongs.txt; 180 total)

**The covering palm (the founding scene).** Exodus 33:22 — "I will **cover** (sakak)
thee with my **hand (kaph)** while I pass by": God's open palm over Moses in the cleft
of the rock. The verb is the booth-root (sakak → sukkah), the same verb as the cherubim
"covering the mercy seat with their wings" (Ex 25:20) and "He shall cover thee with his
feathers" (Ps 91:4). Psalm 139:5 repeats the posture: "Thou hast beset me behind and
before, and laid thine **hand (kaph)** upon me."

**The engraved palm.** Isaiah 49:16 — "I have graven thee upon the **palms (kaph)** of
my hands; thy **walls** are continually before me" — palm and wall in one verse, spoken
to Zion (49:14). The bride is written on the open palm.

**The providing palm (Proverbs 31).** The virtuous woman works with her kaph (31:13),
plants a vineyard with the fruit of her kaph (31:16), holds the distaff with her kaph
(31:19), stretches her kaph to the poor (31:20). Her palm does what God's palm does —
covers and provides. (She is also "like the merchants' ships," 31:14 — the redemptive
face of the ship symbol.)

**The praying palm.** Prayer is the palm spread open toward heaven: Solomon "spread
forth his **hands (kaph)** toward heaven" (1 Kings 8:22); "stretch out thine **hands
(kaph)** toward him" (Job 11:13); "I will lift up my **hands (kaph)** in thy name"
(Ps 63:4); "let us lift up our heart with our **hands (kaph)**" (Lam 3:41). And the
rejected prayers are the same posture defiled: "when ye spread forth your **hands
(kaph)**, I will hide mine eyes… your hands are full of blood" (Isa 1:15).

**The palm branch.** The same word names the fronds: "branches (**kappot**) of palm
trees" in the Tabernacles command (Lev 23:40). The palm of the hand and the palm of the
tree are one word — so Exodus 33:22 holds the Tabernacles pair in one clause: sakak +
kaph, the booth and the palm. Even the trees keep the idiom: "the trees of the field
shall clap their **hands (kaph)**" (Isa 55:12).

**The dove's palm.** Genesis 8:9 — "the dove found no rest for the **sole (kaph)** of
her foot" (see research-dove.md): the palm that found no resting place on the
death-waters.

## The chain the author proposed: palm = shield = wall = salvation

Four different lexemes (kaph, magen, chomah, yeshuah) — not one word, but one figure,
welded pairwise by Scripture itself:

- **Wall = Salvation, named:** "thou shalt call thy **walls Salvation** (yeshuah)"
  (Isa 60:18); "salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks" (Isa 26:1).
  Identity-grade — already the glossary's Wall entry.
- **Shield of salvation:** Ps 18:35. God Himself the shield (Gen 15:1) and the wall
  ("a wall of fire round about," Zech 2:5).
- **Palm ↔ wall:** Isa 49:16, one verse.
- **Cover → salvation in one psalm:** Ps 91 opens with sakak-covering and the shield
  (91:4) and closes "and shew him my **salvation** (yeshuah)" (91:16).

Statement with house precision: palm, shield, and wall are the images — three postures
of one encompassing presence — and **Salvation (yeshuah) is what Scripture names the
encompassment**. The meaning, not a fourth symbol.

## Counter-texts weighed

- *Kaph* is a common anatomical word; most of the 180 hits are plain usage (soles of
  feet, dishes/pans of the tabernacle service, clapping). The symbol lives in the
  covenant scenes (covering, engraving, provision, prayer, the feast branches), not in
  every occurrence. Not every palm is freighted.
- The wing-covering verses (Ps 91:4; Ruth 2:12) use *kanaph* (wing), a different word —
  the shared element is the covering verb *sakak*, with palm and wing as its two
  instruments. Do not conflate kaph/kanaph (structural, not lexical).
- Clapping of palms carries both joy (Ps 47:1, "clap your hands, all ye people" —
  verify tag: that is kaph) and scorn (Nah 3:19); the open palm's meaning is set by its
  direction — spread toward God, opened to the poor, or struck in derision.

## Approved definition (author's ruling 2026-07-18 — entered in 49-glossary.adoc)

**Palm (kaph)** — the open, covering hand of God (and of His bride after Him): the palm
that shielded Moses in the cleft, that Zion is graven upon beside her walls named
Salvation, that the virtuous woman stretches out to the poor; the same word names the
palm branches of Tabernacles. Often translated "hand," but Hebrew separates *kaph*, the
open palm of covering and provision, from *yad*, the hand of power — Isaiah 62:3 holds
both in one verse.
