# Hate — word recovery study (2026-07-11)

WORD-RECOVERY class (like Grace and Mercy): the meaning was flattened in
translation; no image transfer. Hypothesis under test: Scripture's "hate" is
not the emotion (bitterness, loathing) but the un-choosing — the repudiation
of a covenant partner or claim — with feeling at most a variable overtone.

## Tallies (verified verse-by-verse against kjv_strongs.txt)

- **sane (H8130, verb)**: 139 verses, 146 tokens. KJV renderings: hate/Hate 68,
  hated 42, hateth 20, hatest 5, hating 1, haters 1, hateful 1 (Ps 36:2),
  "utterly" 1 (Judg 15:2's infinitive absolute), **odious 1 (Prov 30:23)** —
  and **enemies 3, enemy 2, foes 1**: the KJV itself renders the participle as
  a status word, not a feeling word, in Exod 1:10; 2 Sam 19:6; 2 Chr 1:11;
  Esth 9:16; Prov 25:21; 27:6 ("the kisses of an ENEMY [sone] are deceitful").
- **sin'ah (H8135, noun)**: 16 verses, 16 tokens (incl. Num 35:20 the
  manslayer statute; 2 Sam 13:15; Ps 139:22; Ezek 23:29 "hatefully").
- **seni'ah (H8146, adj)**: 1× — Deut 21:15, the unique spelling לַשְּׂנִיאָה,
  "the firstborn son be hers that was HATED" — the word exists only as the
  legal label of the un-favored wife.
- **sene (H8131, Aramaic)**: 1× — Dan 4:19 "the dream be to them that hate
  thee" ∥ "thine enemies" — the enemy-parallel again.
- **miseo (G3404)**: 38 verses, 42 tokens in the NT.
- **Excluded neighbors, flagged wherever touched**: satam (H7852, 6×) — the
  seething GRUDGE that plots blood: "Esau HATED (satam) Jacob… then will I
  SLAY my brother" (Gen 27:41), "in WRATH they hate me" (Ps 55:3), Job 16:9
  (tearing, gnashing). Hebrew has a word for the hot murderous grudge, and it
  is NOT sane — see the election section. eyvah (H342) — ENMITY (Gen 3:15;
  Num 35:21-22, interchanging with sin'ah inside one statute; Ezek 25:15;
  35:5 "the old hatred"). mas'emah (H4895, 2×, Hos 9:7-8). Greek: echthra
  (G2189, enmity, Gal 5:20; Rom 8:7; Jas 4:4), theostyges "haters of God"
  (G2319, Rom 1:30), stygetos "hateful" (G4767, Titus 3:3).

## The traditional definition (stated fairly)

Hate is taught as the emotion at maximum: intense hostility, detestation,
loathing — anger hardened into malice. Its opposite is love-the-feeling.
The hard texts are then handled case by case: Luke 14:26 becomes "Semitic
hyperbole — it really means love less by comparison"; "Jacob have I loved,
Esau have I hated" becomes anthropomorphism or "loved less"; "God is love"
and "the LORD hates" are held in tension as a paradox of the divine
character. Note what the tradition concedes under pressure: whenever a text
cannot bear the emotion, the accepted escape hatch is precisely the
comparative/rejection reading — but only locally, as a figure of speech,
never as the word's own meaning.

## Candidate definition

**To hate is to un-choose: the repudiation of a covenant partner, claim, or
thing — the wife un-favored, the master not served, the King refused, the
son unchastened, the brother unrebuked, the worship not accepted — enmity as
a standing (provable in court, rendered "enemy" by the KJV itself), enacted
as sending away, driving out, not sitting with, not choosing. Feeling, when
present, rides the act; the burning grudge has its own Hebrew word (satam),
and it is not this one.** Love's other pole on one axis: love keeps and
cleaves (1 John 5:3; ch. 6); hate un-chooses and casts off — both covenant
verbs, both enacted, neither a temperature.

## The legal core — senu'ah, the un-chosen wife

- **Deut 21:15-17 — "two wives, one beloved (ahuvah), and another hated
  (senu'ah)."** WLC: הָאַחַת אֲהוּבָה וְהָאַחַת שְׂנוּאָה. The hated wife is
  still a wife, still in the house, still bearing him sons (v15: "they have
  born him children, both the beloved and the hated"). The law never rebukes
  the hating; it regulates its consequences — "hated" is a civil status like
  "widowed" or "divorced," and the statute exists to protect the senu'ah's
  firstborn: "he may NOT make the son of the beloved firstborn before the son
  of the hated… the right of the firstborn is his." If hate meant loathing,
  Torah would here be administering a household run on disgust; what it is
  administering is RANK — the un-favored one whose legal rights survive her
  un-choosing.
- **Gen 29:30-31 — Scripture's own comparative gloss, in consecutive
  verses**: "he LOVED also Rachel MORE THAN Leah" (v30) → "when the LORD saw
  that Leah was HATED (senu'ah), he opened her womb" (v31; so v33). Leah is
  manifestly cohabiting, bearing son after son — hated = loved-less, the
  second-ranked, the not-chosen-first. This is the exact equation of
  Luke 14:26 ∥ Matt 10:37 (below), already installed in Genesis. And the
  LORD's response to the senu'ah is the Deut 21 statute enacted in person:
  He opens her womb, and the priesthood (Levi) and the scepter (Judah) both
  come through the hated wife.
- **Prov 30:23 — the KJV hides the same word**: "an ODIOUS woman when she is
  married" is senu'ah ki tibba'el — the HATED woman when she gets MARRIED
  (ba'al, H1166), one of the four things the earth cannot bear. The
  un-chosen woman chosen at last is Isaiah's whole restoration arc in one
  proverb: "thou hast been FORSAKEN (azuvah) and HATED (senu'ah)" (Isa 60:15
  — hate ∥ forsake, the verse's own pair); "a woman forsaken… a wife of
  youth, when thou wast REFUSED (ma'as)" (Isa 54:6); "thou shalt no more be
  termed Forsaken… thy land Beulah (H1166): for… thy land shall be MARRIED
  (tibba'el)" (Isa 62:4). Same vocabulary end to end: senu'ah → be'ulah,
  the hated wife married.
- **Deut 24:3 — hate is the divorce ground**: "if the latter husband HATE
  her, and write her a bill of divorcement… and SEND her (shalach) out of
  his house." Deut 22:13-19 runs the procedure: "take a wife, and go in unto
  her, and HATE her, and give occasions of speech against her" — hate moves
  immediately to a legal action to void the covenant; if the charge is
  false, the penalty is the permanent loss of the divorce right ("she shall
  be his wife; he may not put her away all his days," 22:19).
- **Judg 14:16-15:2 — hate operating as de facto divorce**: Samson's wife:
  "Thou dost but HATE me, and LOVEST me NOT: thou… hast not told it me" —
  hate = the withheld confidence of one flesh, not a scowl. Her father then
  treats the standing as legally effective: "I verily thought that thou
  hadst UTTERLY HATED her (sano seneatah); therefore I GAVE HER to thy
  companion" (15:2) — hated = repudiated, so re-givable.
- **Mal 2:16 — sane and shalach welded**: כִּי־שָׂנֵא שַׁלַּח. The
  translation dispute is real — KJV "he hateth putting away" (God the
  subject); the other grammar "he who hates — send away!" / "who hates and
  divorces" (the husband the subject, as LXX and the consonants equally
  permit). Weigh it and it does not matter for THIS study: on either
  reading the verse holds hate and divorce-sending in one breath, in a
  chapter indicting the man who "dealt treacherously" with "the wife of thy
  covenant" (2:14). The pair IS the point: in Malachi's Hebrew, hating and
  sending away are one motion. (Ch. 11 quotes the KJV reading with the
  covenant-wife qualifier; nothing here contradicts it.)

## The self-glossing expulsions — hate defined by its enactment

Scripture repeatedly names the hate and the act in the same breath, and the
act is always a putting-out or a not-taking-in:

- **Gen 26:27 — Isaac's own gloss**: "Wherefore come ye to me, seeing ye
  HATE me, and have SENT ME AWAY (shalach — Deut 24's divorce verb) from
  you?" (וְאַתֶּם שְׂנֵאתֶם אֹתִי וַתְּשַׁלְּחוּנִי).
- **Judg 11:7 — Jephthah**: "Did not ye HATE me, and EXPEL me (garash — the
  divorce/drive-out verb) out of my father's house?"
- **Hos 9:15 — God as subject, the full formula**: "there I HATED them…
  I will DRIVE THEM OUT (garash) of MINE HOUSE, I will LOVE THEM NO MORE."
  Hate = expulsion from the house plus the withdrawal of covenant love —
  divorce grammar exactly.
- **Jer 12:7-8 — the order of operations**: "I have FORSAKEN mine house,
  I have LEFT mine heritage… [she] crieth out against me; THEREFORE have I
  HATED it." The forsaking is the hating.
- **Isa 66:5**: "your brethren that HATED you, that CAST YOU OUT (nadah)
  for my name's sake" — hate ∥ excommunication (already quoted in ch. 47).
- **Ps 5:4-5**: "neither shall evil DWELL with thee. The foolish shall not
  STAND in thy sight: thou HATEST all workers of iniquity" — hate = no
  residence, no standing, in the house.
- **Ps 26:5**: "I have HATED the congregation of evil doers; and WILL NOT
  SIT with the wicked" — hate = refused table.
- **Ps 101:3**: "I HATE the work of them that turn aside; it shall NOT
  CLEAVE (davaq — the marriage verb, Gen 2:24) to me" — hate is the
  anti-cleaving.
- **Ps 50:17**: "thou HATEST instruction, and CASTEST my words BEHIND thee."
- **Prov 1:29 — the sharpest single gloss**: "they HATED knowledge, and DID
  NOT CHOOSE (bachar) the fear of the LORD." Hate = the un-choosing, stated
  as a poetic equation.
- **2 Sam 5:8**: "the lame and the blind, that are HATED of David's soul…
  Wherefore they said, The blind and the lame SHALL NOT COME INTO THE
  HOUSE" — the narrative supplies the meaning as a house-exclusion.
- **Amos 5:21 — God refusing worship**: "I HATE (saneti), I DESPISE
  (ma'asti — REJECT, H3988) your feast days, and I WILL NOT SMELL in your
  solemn assemblies." The parallel word is ma'as — the rejection verb of
  1 Sam 15:23, 26 ("thou hast REJECTED the word of the LORD, he hath also
  REJECTED thee from being king"), Hos 4:6 ("thou hast rejected knowledge,
  I will also reject thee"), Isa 54:6 (the refused wife). Isa 1:13-15 runs
  the same refusal in full: "my soul HATETH… I cannot away with… I will
  hide mine eyes… I will not hear" — non-acceptance itemized.

## The manslayer law — hate as a standing provable in court

- Deut 4:42; 19:4, 6; Josh 20:5 — the refuge cities turn on one finding:
  "he HATED him NOT in time past" (לֹא־שֹׂנֵא לֹו מִתְּמֹל שִׁלְשֹׁם).
  Deut 19:11 gives the guilty form: "if any man HATE his neighbour, and LIE
  IN WAIT for him… and smite him mortally." Num 35:20-23 legislates the
  same line with sin'ah and eyvah (enmity) as interchangeable terms, and
  v24 assigns the question to a court: "the CONGREGATION shall JUDGE between
  the slayer and the revenger of blood according to these judgments." What
  the elders of a city can adjudicate from testimony is a documented
  standing — prior threats, quarrels, a known enmity — not the inside of a
  man's chest. Hate here is legal enmity: the difference between
  manslaughter and murder.
- **1 John 3:15 reads that statute**: "Whosoever HATETH his brother IS a
  murderer" — the standing of Deut 19:11 already counts as the crime it
  ripens into; the chapter's own case is Cain (3:12).
- **Ps 139:21-22 — the psalmist's self-gloss**: "I hate them with perfect
  hatred: I COUNT THEM MINE ENEMIES" — the second line translates the
  first: hating = reckoning as enemies, taking God's side of the line
  ("Do not I hate them… that hate THEE?"). Honest note: v21b adds "am not
  I GRIEVED with those that rise up" — feeling stands alongside, named by
  its own word, distinct from the stance.
- **Ps 109:3-5**: "words of HATRED… For my love they are my ADVERSARIES
  (satan, H7853)… they have rewarded me… HATRED for my LOVE" — sin'ah ∥
  the satan-verb: accusation, standing opposition.
- **Eccl 3:8**: "a time to love, and a time to hate; a time of WAR, and a
  time of PEACE" — the verse's own second pair locates hate on the
  war/allegiance axis.
- **Hidden hatred proves the point**: "He that HATETH dissembleth with his
  LIPS… his HATRED is COVERED by deceit" (Prov 26:24-26); "the KISSES of an
  ENEMY (sone)" (Prov 27:6). Hate can wear warm affect and stay hate —
  impossible if hate WERE the affect. The stance is the substance; the face
  is detachable.
- **2 Chr 19:2 — hate as camp-membership**: "Shouldest thou HELP the
  ungodly, and LOVE them that HATE the LORD?" — Jehoshaphat's "love" is a
  military alliance with Ahab; the LORD's haters are the opposing camp, and
  Ps 81:15 has them "SUBMIT themselves unto him" — enemies capitulating,
  not feelings changing. Exod 20:5-6 ∥ Deut 7:9-10 draw the covenant axis:
  "them that HATE me" against "them that LOVE me, and KEEP my
  commandments" — the hater of God is the covenant-refuser (so Rom 1:30's
  "haters of God" sit in a list of the disobedient).

## Election — "Esau have I hated"

- **Mal 1:2-3**: "Was not Esau Jacob's brother?… yet I LOVED Jacob, and I
  HATED Esau, and laid his MOUNTAINS and his HERITAGE waste." The objects
  of the hate are inheritance-objects; loved/hated = chosen/un-chosen
  lines. **Rom 9:10-13** quotes it precisely about the un-choosing:
  "the children being NOT YET BORN, neither having done any good or evil,
  that the purpose of God according to ELECTION might stand… Jacob have I
  loved, but Esau have I hated." A responsive emotion toward a person who
  has done nothing is unintelligible; an election between two lines before
  either exists is exactly what the passage says it is.
- **The Esau arc glosses itself with rejection vocabulary**: Esau "DESPISED
  (bazah) his birthright" (Gen 25:34) — un-chose it for a bowl of pottage —
  and Hebrews closes the loop: "as Esau, who for one morsel of meat SOLD
  his birthright… when he would have inherited the blessing, he was
  REJECTED (apodokimazo)" (Heb 12:16-17). The un-chooser un-chosen.
- **The grudge-word check**: when Scripture wants Esau's hot murderous
  hatred it uses a different lexeme — "Esau HATED (satam) Jacob… then will
  I SLAY my brother" (Gen 27:41). God's word over Esau in Malachi is sane,
  not satam. The KJV renders both "hated"; the Hebrew keeps the emotion
  and the election in separate words.

## The Gospel's own gloss

- **Luke 14:26 ∥ Matt 10:37 — Scripture translating its own idiom**: "If
  any man come to me, and HATE NOT his father, and mother… he CANNOT be my
  disciple" is, in its Matthean twin, "He that LOVETH father or mother MORE
  THAN ME is not worthy of me." The same equation Genesis already made:
  loved-more-than ↔ hated (Gen 29:30-31). Hate = renounce the prior claim —
  which is why the list ends "yea, and his own LIFE also," and why
  Luke 14:33 states the whole demand without the word: "whosoever…
  FORSAKETH NOT ALL that he hath, he cannot be my disciple."
- **Matt 6:24 ∥ Luke 16:13 — the verse glosses itself**: "either he will
  HATE the one, and LOVE the other; or else he will HOLD TO (antechomai)
  the one, and DESPISE (kataphroneo) the other." Hate/love = despise/hold-to:
  a servant's choice between masters — allegiance, service, not feeling.
  "Ye CANNOT SERVE God and mammon."
- **John 12:25**: "he that HATETH his life in this world shall keep it unto
  life eternal" — the synoptic parallels run "let him DENY himself…
  whosoever will LOSE his life for my sake shall find it" (Matt 16:24-25);
  Rev 12:11 "they LOVED NOT their lives unto the death." Hate the life =
  renounce the claim on it.
- **Luke 19:14 — hate enacted as a formal repudiation**: "his citizens
  HATED him, and SENT AN EMBASSY (presbeia, G4242) after him, saying, WE
  WILL NOT HAVE THIS MAN TO REIGN OVER US." The hate IS the refusal of
  kingship — 1 Sam 8:7 in Greek dress: "they have REJECTED (ma'as) me,
  that I should NOT REIGN over them."
- **Luke 6:22 — the beatitude unpacks its own verb**: "when men shall HATE
  you, and when they shall SEPARATE you from their company, and shall
  reproach you, and CAST OUT your name as evil" — hate itemized as
  excommunication: separation, name struck off.
- **John 15:25**: "they HATED me WITHOUT A CAUSE" quotes the sin'at chinnam
  of Ps 35:19; 69:4 — where the haters are, in the psalms' own parallel
  lines, "mine ENEMIES wrongfully." John 15:18-24 defines the world's hate
  operationally: not receiving ("ye are not of the world"), rejecting the
  works and the testimony; 7:7 "me it hateth, BECAUSE I TESTIFY of it,
  that the works thereof are evil" — hate as the refusal of reproof (the
  Lev 19:17 axis, below).
- **Matt 5:43 — what is being corrected**: "Ye have heard that it hath been
  SAID… hate thine enemy." No Torah text says it; Torah says the opposite —
  the sone's fallen ass must be helped up (Exod 23:5), the oyev's straying
  ox returned (23:4), the brother not hated in the heart (Lev 19:17).
  Hebrew Matthew reads the tradition as "hate the ONE WHO HATES YOU" —
  reciprocal enmity — equally absent from Torah. Jesus is not softening the
  law; He is deleting an addition to it.

## Hate as withheld duty — the office refused

- **Prov 13:24**: "He that SPARETH his ROD HATETH his son: but he that
  LOVETH him CHASTENETH him." No emotion on either side of the proverb —
  both poles are defined by acts of the father's office. Heb 12:7-8 gives
  the meaning of the withheld rod: "what son is he whom the father
  chasteneth not? But if ye be without chastisement… then are ye BASTARDS,
  and NOT SONS." To spare the rod is to treat him as no son of yours — the
  quiet un-sonning. (God's love runs the same axis: "whom the LORD loveth
  he correcteth," Prov 3:12.)
- **Lev 19:17 — the commanded rebuke**: "Thou shalt NOT HATE thy brother IN
  THINE HEART: thou shalt in any wise REBUKE thy neighbour, and not suffer
  sin upon him" — then v18: "thou shalt LOVE thy neighbour as thyself."
  The alternative to heart-hate is not warm feeling; it is the spoken
  rebuke. Silent unrebuke — letting the brother carry his sin unchallenged
  — IS the heart-hate: the inward un-brothering. "In thine heart" does not
  make hate an emotion; it locates where the casting-off can hide (cf. the
  covered hatred of Prov 26:26). The statute itself keeps the grudge in a
  separate word: v18 continues "thou shalt not avenge, NOR BEAR ANY GRUDGE
  (natar, H5201)" — Leviticus distinguishes sane from grudge-bearing in
  consecutive verses, exactly as Genesis keeps satam for Esau's. 1 John
  develops both halves: the brother-hater "is in darkness" (2:9-11) and
  "is a murderer" (3:15).
- **The reproof cluster**: "they HATE him that REBUKETH in the gate"
  (Amos 5:10); "he that HATETH REPROOF is brutish… shall die"
  (Prov 12:1; 15:10); "every one that doeth evil HATETH the light, NEITHER
  COMETH to the light, LEST his deeds should be REPROVED" (John 3:20 — the
  verse's own gloss: hating = not coming, to avoid the rebuke).
- **Officers "hating covetousness"** (Exod 18:21) ∥ "he that HATETH GIFTS
  shall live" (Prov 15:27) — the refusal of the bribe: an enacted
  non-acceptance, the same grammar as God refusing the feasts.
- **2 Sam 19:6 — Joab to David**: "thou LOVEST thine ENEMIES, and HATEST
  thy FRIENDS. For thou hast declared this day… thou REGARDEST NEITHER
  princes nor servants" — hate = the disregard of loyal covenant men,
  read off David's public conduct.
- **Eph 5:29**: "no man ever yet HATED his own FLESH; but NOURISHETH and
  CHERISHETH it" — the antonym pair is feeding and warming, not liking:
  to hate would be to cast off and cease sustaining — said inside the
  marriage passage, of the wife who is "his own flesh."

## God as subject — the same grammar throughout

Deut 12:31; 16:22 ("which the LORD thy God hateth" — practices banned from
His worship); Prov 6:16-19 ("six things doth the LORD hate… an ABOMINATION
unto him" — to'evah, the exclusion category: what may not come near,
Deut 7:26); Ps 5:5; 11:5 (excluded from His presence); Isa 1:14; Amos 5:21
(worship refused — "I will not smell… I will not hear"); Isa 61:8 ("I hate
robbery for burnt offering" — the offering not accepted); Amos 6:8 ("I
abhor the excellency of Jacob, and hate his palaces: therefore will I
DELIVER UP the city" — enacted as handing over); Hos 9:15 (driven from the
house, love withdrawn); Jer 12:7-8 (house forsaken); Zech 8:17; Mal 1:3;
2:16. In every text the divine hate is a refusal to accept or a putting
out — never a portrait of God seething. Rev 2:6, 15 keep the grammar ("thou
hatest the DEEDS of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate" — the practice
refused), and Rev 17:16 enacts hate in full: the ten horns "shall HATE the
whore, and shall make her DESOLATE and NAKED… and BURN her with fire" —
the adulteress's judicial sentence of Ezek 16:37-41 (stripped, left naked
and bare, houses burned), executed by her own former lovers, "them that
thou hast LOVED, with all them that thou hast HATED" (16:37).

## Second witness — the Hebrew Gospels (bibles/hg.txt, data/hg-chapters/)

Not lexeme-uniform, and honesty requires saying so. The flagship
divergences run TOWARD rejection (John 15:18 "rejected me first"; Luke
14:26's departure construction) — but the witness is mixed, not uniform:
Cochin John also renders miseo with "angry" four times (7:7; 15:19, 23,
24), and the persecution texts reach for wrath-vocabulary (qatsaph, Mark
13:13; Luke 19:14). The second witness supports the rejection reading at
its decisive points and adds an affect-coloring at others; it is weighed
as support, not proof:

- **sane (H8130) where allegiance is the point**: Matt 6:24 "without HATING
  (yisna) the one and LOVING the other, or HONORING (kabed) the one and
  DESPISING (bazah, H959) the other" — and bazah is the very verb of Esau
  despising the birthright (Gen 25:34); Hebrew Matthew glosses the
  two-masters hate with the un-choosing of the Esau arc. Matt 5:43-44
  (sane three times); Luke 16:13 (yisna); Luke 1:71 "from those hating us
  (mesone'einu)."
- **Luke 14:26 — no sane at all**: the Hebrew reads a departure
  construction — "if any man comes with me and does not WALK [away] with
  me [from] his father and his mother… and also his own soul, he will not
  be my disciple" — the translator's note: "the idiom 'hate'… rendered as
  a form of departure," i.e., leave behind, renounce. The Hebrew witness
  read Luke's miseo exactly as Matt 10:37 does: the renounced prior claim.
- **Cochin John renders miseo as rejection outright**: John 15:18 "you must
  know that it REJECTED me first"; 12:25 "he who CONDEMNS his life in this
  world"; 15:19-24 and 7:7 "angry with you/me"; 3:20 "curses the lamp."
- **The persecution texts use covenant-curse vocabulary instead**:
  Matt 10:22 "ye shall be hated of all men" reads "you will be a DERISION
  (la'ag) and a HORROR (za'avah, H2189)" — za'avah is Deut 28:25's own
  curse-word (KJV "shalt be REMOVED into all the kingdoms of the earth" —
  the horror-status of the covenant curses, so Jer 24:9; 29:18); Matt 24:9
  "a reproach (cherpah)"; Mark 13:13 "all shall RAGE (qatsaph)";
  Luke 21:17 "you will be CURSED (arurim) by all humanity"; Luke 19:14
  "they ANGERED him (hiqtsiphuhu)"; Luke 6:22 "provoke… separate… curse…
  cast out your name."

Verdict: the continuity claim is lexeme-grade only for the two-masters and
love-your-enemy sayings; elsewhere the Hebrew witnesses translate the
Greek's one flattened verb back OUT into its components — renounce, reject,
curse, cast out — which is itself evidence that miseo was carrying more
than a feeling.

## Counter-texts — the sweep, with verdicts

- **2 Sam 13:15 — Amnon, the strongest counter-text**: "Then Amnon HATED
  her EXCEEDINGLY; so that the HATRED wherewith he hated her was GREATER
  than the LOVE wherewith he had loved her." Emotional heat is undeniably
  in view. But watch what the hate IS in the text: "And Amnon said unto
  her, ARISE, BE GONE" — and Tamar's protest names the act, not the mood:
  "this evil in SENDING ME AWAY (shalach) is greater than the other" —
  and v17: "PUT (shilchu) now this woman OUT from me, and BOLT THE DOOR
  after her." Shalach twice in as many verses (v16 leshalcheni, v17
  shilchu; v18's "brought her out" is yatsa): the divorce-expulsion verb
  is the hate's entire content. The law had one remedy for what Amnon did —
  marry her and never put her away (Deut 22:29; Tamar even offers it,
  13:13); his hate is precisely the refusal of that duty, and it leaves
  Tamar "DESOLATE (shomemah) in her brother Absalom's house" — the
  cast-off-wife status word (Isa 54:1). His "love" (v1-14) was seizure;
  his "hate" is discard — both are enacted claims, and the emotion is
  the temperature of the act, not its definition. VERDICT: self-glosses
  as repudiation; heat present as intensity, not as content.
- **2 Sam 13:22 — Absalom**: "Absalom spake unto his brother Amnon NEITHER
  GOOD NOR BAD: for Absalom HATED Amnon" — hate = relations severed (the
  same sign as Joseph's brothers, who "could not speak PEACEABLY unto him,"
  Gen 37:4). It then ripens exactly along Deut 19:11's track — the
  standing enmity, the lying in wait (13:28-29). VERDICT: status, then
  the statute's escalation.
- **Ps 25:19**: "they hate me with CRUEL hatred (sin'at chamas — hatred of
  violence)." The modifier proves sin'ah admits degrees and can arm
  itself; an enmity can be violent. VERDICT: intensity is a variable ON
  the standing — which is the claim.
- **Prov 10:12** ("hatred stirreth up strifes: but love covereth all
  sins"), **15:17** ("better a dinner of herbs where love is, than a
  stalled ox and hatred therewith") — household enmity vs covenant
  loyalty; both proverbs work as stance, neither requires feeling.
  VERDICT: compatible.
- **Prov 26:24-26** — covered hatred: actively SUPPORTS the status reading
  (see above). VERDICT: supports.
- **Ezek 23:28-29; 16:37**: "them whom thou HATEST… they shall deal with
  thee HATEFULLY (b'sin'ah), and shall TAKE AWAY all thy labour, and shall
  LEAVE THEE NAKED" — the lovers-turned-haters' sin'ah is itemized as
  spoiling and stripping: the cast-off executed. VERDICT: enacted.
- **Ps 105:25**: "He TURNED THEIR HEART to hate his people, TO DEAL
  SUBTILLY with his servants" — the infinitive glosses the hate.
  VERDICT: enacted.
- **Eccl 2:17-18**: "I HATED life… I hated all my labour… because I should
  LEAVE it unto the man that shall be after me" — Qoheleth renounces the
  worth of the thing ("I went about to cause my heart to DESPAIR," 2:20).
  VERDICT: repudiation of a claim — his own stake in his works.
- **Deut 1:27; 9:28**: "Because the LORD HATED us, he hath brought us
  forth out of Egypt… to destroy us" — the murmurers construe the exodus
  itself as an expulsion-to-slaughter: even the slander uses hate as a
  casting-out, not a mood. VERDICT: supports.
- **Ps 139:21** pairs the hate-stance with a named feeling ("am not I
  GRIEVED") — the one place the sweep found feeling explicitly alongside,
  and it arrives under its own word (qut), not inside sane. VERDICT: the
  lexicon keeps them separate.

**Range statement**: sane runs from the un-favored wife (a protected legal
status), through the divorce ground, the court-provable enmity of the
manslayer law, the national enemy (KJV "enemies/foes"), the withheld office
(rod, rebuke, sustenance), the refused claim (worship, bribes, reproof,
kingship, birthright), to God's own election between lines. One sense
covers the whole range: the un-choosing — repudiation of a covenant
partner, claim, or thing — with emotional intensity as a free variable
(zero in Deut 21:15; white-hot in 2 Sam 13:15) and never the content. No
occurrence requires "hate" to mean a feeling with no enacted stance; the
feeling-words of the field are others (satam the grudge, qut grief, af
wrath). Identity is not claimed beyond this: hate is not "divorce" (it is
the ground of it), not "murder" (it is the standing that ripens into it),
not "enmity" simply (eyvah is its twin, not its spelling) — it is the
un-choosing those enactments execute.

## Coherence with the book

- **Ch. 6 (Love, Knowing, Faith, and Belief)**: love = keeping the
  commandments — "this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments"
  (glossary: sym-love, NOVEL). Hate recovered as the un-choosing gives the
  axis its other pole: both covenantal, both enacted, neither a feeling —
  "them that LOVE me, and KEEP my commandments" against "them that HATE
  me" (Exod 20:5-6) becomes a single coherent sentence instead of a
  feelings/works hybrid.
- **Ch. 5x (The Disciple)** quotes Luke 14:26 as a gate but leaves "hate
  not" undecoded; this study supplies Matt 10:37 + Gen 29:30-31 and
  the HG's departure-rendering. Luke 14:33 ("forsaketh not all") is
  already the chapter's third gate — the decode is native to the passage.
- **Ch. 11 (Marriage and Divorce)** quotes Mal 2:16 (KJV) twice with the
  covenant-wife qualifier; compatible as-is. The senu'ah material
  (Deut 21:15-17; 22:13-19; 24:3; Isa 60:15/54:6/62:4) would deepen its
  legal spine, and Rev 17:16 = Ezek 16:37-41 is already its register.
- **Ch. 22 (Fear of the LORD)** quotes "the fear of the LORD is to HATE
  evil" (Prov 8:13) and already glosses it with departure texts ("by the
  fear of the LORD men DEPART from evil," Prov 16:6) — the chapter
  translated hate as turning-away before this study was written.
- **Ch. 20 (Light and Darkness)** quotes John 3:20 — hateth the light =
  cometh not, lest reproved; the reproof-refusal cluster joins it.
- **Ch. 15x (Weeping and Gnashing)** uses "hatred" in prose for the
  gnashing-rage; no citation conflict (gnashing's texts are wrath-words),
  but the prose word could be sharpened to "rage" if the glossary defines
  hate as rejection.
- **Glossary**: sym-divorce already says "HATED when it betrays the wife
  of the covenant"; sym-love (NOVEL), sym-mercy and sym-grace (WORD) are
  the entry-format models.

## Consonantal / wordplay notes (second dimension only, never the definition)

- Unpointed שנא carries "hate" in Hebrew and "change / alter" in Aramaic
  (H8133): Dan 6:17's seal "that the purpose be NOT CHANGED" is לָא־תִשְׁנֵא
  — the same consonants as Lev 19:17's "thou shalt NOT HATE" (לֹא־תִשְׂנָא).
  To hate is to change out — the senu'ah is the exchanged one, the
  replaced. Wordplay, flagged as wordplay.
- In-text near-pun: the senu'ah's son receives פִּי שְׁנַיִם (pi shnayim,
  "double portion," Deut 21:17) — the hated (senu'ah) wife's boy gets the
  "mouth of two"; the un-chosen line carries the doubled inheritance.
  Observation only.
