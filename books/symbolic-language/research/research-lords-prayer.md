# Research: The Lord's Prayer — behind the ch. 8 section

Done 2026-07-02. Matt 6:9-13 ∥ Luke 11:1-4, read through the book's
symbols (name = covenant; bread = word; debt = sin; peirasmos = the
proving).

## "Temptation" (peirasmos G3986, 21×; peirazō G3985, 42×)

- The word means the PROVING/trial, not enticement. James rules the
  modern sense out: "God cannot be tempted with evil, NEITHER TEMPTETH
  HE ANY MAN" (Jas 1:13) — yet "blessed is the man that ENDURETH
  temptation: for when he is TRIED, he shall receive the crown of life"
  (Jas 1:12; 1:2-3 "count it all joy… the trying of your faith");
  1 Pet 1:6-7 manifold temptations = "the TRIAL of your faith… tried
  with fire."
- Beneath it, Hebrew *nasah* H5254, which the KJV renders BOTH ways
  (translators' wobble = signpost):
  - "God did TEMPT Abraham" (Gen 22:1) — the FIRST "tempt" in the
    Bible, same scene as the first "worship" (22:5).
  - "that I may PROVE them, whether they will walk in my law"
    (Ex 16:4 — the manna); "to PROVE thee… whether thou wouldest keep
    his commandments, or no" (Deut 8:2); "the LORD your God PROVETH
    you" (Deut 13:3).
  - David prays FOR it: "PROVE me" (Ps 26:2).
  - The reverse: Israel TEMPTS God at Massah (Ex 17:2, 7; Deut 6:16 —
    quoted by Jesus in His own wilderness proving, Matt 4:7).
- So "lead us not into temptation" = a plea about the furnace-hour of
  proving, not about God dangling sin: "the HOUR OF TEMPTATION, which
  shall come upon all the world, to TRY them" (Rev 3:10); Gethsemane's
  "watch and pray, that ye ENTER NOT INTO TEMPTATION" (Matt 26:41);
  balanced by 1 Cor 10:13 — "with the temptation also make a WAY to
  escape."

## "Deliver us from evil" — personal in the Greek

τοῦ πονηροῦ (Matt 6:13) = "the evil one," the "WICKED ONE" who snatches
the sown word (Matt 13:19) and fathers the tares (13:38). The petition
performed by the Name-bearer: "Simon, Simon… Satan hath desired to have
you, that he may SIFT YOU AS WHEAT: but I HAVE PRAYED FOR THEE, that
thy faith fail not" (Luke 22:31-32).

## Matthew vs Luke deviations

- Setting: Luke's is answer to "Lord, TEACH US TO PRAY" (11:1).
- Luke 11:2 abbreviates but keeps name → kingdom → will.
- Bread: Matt "this day" (sēmeron); Luke "DAY BY DAY" (kath' hēmeran,
  11:3) — Luke keeps the manna's rhythm, the daily rate of Ex 16:4.
- Forgiveness: Matt "DEBTS… debtors" (opheilēmata); Luke GLOSSES the
  symbol in one verse — "forgive us our SINS; for we also forgive every
  one that is INDEBTED to us" (11:4): sins in the first clause, debtors
  in the second. Cross-gospel parallel columns: the debt IS sin.
  (Deut 15:2 supplies the institution: "the LORD'S release.")
- Matt carries the doxology (6:13b); KJV Luke 11:4 keeps the
  deliverance clause.
- Luke's continuation dramatizes the bread petition: the friend at
  midnight begging THREE LOAVES (11:5-8) and the Father who gives to
  them that ask (11:9-13).
- Matt's continuation underlines the forgiveness petition (6:14-15) —
  the only petition glossed with a condition; staged fully in
  Matt 18:23-35 (tormentors).

## Rule noted (author correction)

Not "every symbol meets its counterfeit": many symbols have an
OPPOSITE, and describing the opposite often sheds light on the meaning
of its counterpart (e.g., name/Babel's self-made name, poor/rich,
liberty/bondage, light/darkness, shadow of the Almighty/shadow of
Egypt). Phrase such transitions accordingly.
