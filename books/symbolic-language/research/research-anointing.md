# Research: Anointing

Working record for the short study "The Anointing." The question is the
religious use of the English word, not every ordinary application of oil.

## Candidate definition

To anoint is to mark a person or thing as God's own and appoint it to a stated
office or work. Oil may carry the visible mark; the Spirit may furnish the
power for the assignment; neither is the lexical definition of the act. Lamp
oil is the fruit and evidence of the Spirit's work made manifest in proven
obedience, not the Spirit treated as consumable fuel.

This is a recovered word rather than a symbol. The concrete act is smearing,
but the sacred use is appointment, installation, and assignment.

- **H4888, mishchah** — 24 verses. The act or material of anointing, especially
  the holy compound.
- **H4899, mashiach** — 38 verses. An anointed person: priest, king, covenant

- **G5545, chrisma** — 1 John 2:20, 27. The received anointing or unction.
- **G5547, Christos** — the Anointed, the Greek title corresponding to

## Pattern by object and office

### Objects

Jacob anoints the pillar at Bethel (Genesis 31:13). Moses anoints the
tabernacle, ark, table, lampstand, altars, laver, and vessels to hallow them
(Exodus 30:26-29; 40:9-11). These objects cannot receive a spiritual gift.
The act removes them from ordinary use, marks them as the LORD's, and assigns
them to sacred service.

### Priests

Exodus 28:41 supplies purpose inside the sentence: "anoint them, and
consecrate them, and sanctify them, that they may minister unto me in the
priest's office." Leviticus 8:12 likewise says Aaron is anointed "to sanctify
him." The result is office and holy use.

### Kings and prophet

Saul is anointed "to be captain" and save Israel (1 Samuel 9:16; 10:1).
David is anointed as king (1 Samuel 16:12-13; 2 Samuel 2:4; 5:3). Hazael and
Jehu are anointed as kings; Elisha is anointed "to be prophet" (1 Kings
19:15-16). The infinitive or office following the verb names the assignment.

### Covenant servants without recorded oil

Psalm 105:9-15 calls Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob God's "anointed" while recounting
the covenant and God's protection of them among kings. Isaiah 45:1 calls Cyrus
God's anointed, although verses 4-5 twice say Cyrus did not know Him. The title
therefore rests on divine choice and commission, not necessarily a ritual
oiling or indwelling Spirit.

## Spirit relation

### David and Saul

In 1 Samuel 16:13 the oiling and the Spirit's arrival are adjacent but distinct
clauses. Verse 14 says the Spirit departed from Saul. David nevertheless calls
Saul "the LORD's anointed" in 1 Samuel 24:6 and 26:9. Saul's appointment still
governs David's conduct after the empowerment has gone. Anointed cannot mean
"currently possessing the Spirit."

### Jesus

Acts 10:38 provides the full grammar: God anoints Jesus **with** the Holy Ghost
and power; Jesus then does good and heals. God appoints, Jesus is appointed,
the Spirit and power equip, and the works execute the commission. Isaiah 61:1
and Luke 4:18 attach the assignment: anointed **to preach**. Acts 4:27 names
Jesus as the one God anointed without mentioning literal oil.

### Believers

Second Corinthians 1:21-22 says God anointed, sealed, and gave the earnest of
the Spirit. The acts are joined but not synonyms. First John 2:20, 27 describes
an anointing received from the Holy One that abides and teaches truth. These
passages support a Spirit-given endowment, but do not reverse the larger usage
and make anointing itself mean Spirit.

## Lamp oil and anointing oil

Exodus 25:6 lists "oil for the light" and "spices for anointing oil" as
separate provisions. Exodus 27:20 specifies pure beaten olive oil for the
lamp. Exodus 30:22-33 specifies a restricted compound of olive oil, myrrh,
cinnamon, calamus, and cassia for anointing people and sanctuary objects.
Shared olive material does not erase distinct preparation, use, or meaning.

The distinction is directly relevant to Matthew 25. Evidence drawn from royal
or priestly anointing does not automatically define oil stored as lamp fuel.
The lamp-oil question must be answered from the lamp's action, storage, buying,
nontransferability, and light.

## Countertexts weighed

- **1 Samuel 16:13:** Strong association between oil and the Spirit in David's
  installation. The following verse and Saul's retained title prove association
  without identity.
- **Zechariah 4:1-14:** The vision promises Zerubbabel will finish the temple
  "by my spirit." The Spirit is the cause behind the completed work. When the
  olive branches are interpreted, they are called "the two anointed ones," not
  "the Spirit." The passage joins Spirit, lamp, olive, appointed servants, and
  work but never states an identity formula "oil is the Spirit." Ezekiel 36:27
  supplies the causal bridge: God puts His Spirit within His people and causes
  them to walk in His statutes and do His judgments. Lamp oil can therefore be
  the fruit and evidence of the Spirit's work without being the Spirit Himself.
- **Acts 10:38:** Strongest New Testament association. Its syntax distinguishes
  the act of anointing from the Holy Spirit and power with which Jesus is
  furnished.
- **1 John 2:20, 27:** The anointing abides and teaches, naturally supporting
  the Spirit or a Spirit-given endowment. It defines the received endowment in
  that context, not all oil and not the verb's full scriptural range.
- **James 5:14 and Mark 6:13:** Physical oil is applied to the sick with
  _aleipho_, the ordinary oiling verb, not _chrio_. Healing oil does not define
  sacred appointment or lamp oil.

## Verdict

Recovered word. In religious usage, **anointing is God's appointment of a
person or thing to His service: marking it as His, authorizing its office, and
equipping it for its assigned work.** Oil can be the visible sign, and the
Spirit can be the empowering gift, without either becoming the definition of
the word.
