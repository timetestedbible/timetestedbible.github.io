# Works (good works, evil works) — word recovery study (2026-07-11)

Author's hypothesis, tested (not assumed): the consensus hears "good works"
as charity, kindness, generic moral effort; the author suspects works =
actions measured against the law — deeds executing God's commandments
versus deeds executing the commandments of men, the fork being WHOSE
command the action obeys, not how the action looks. Every tally and
same-word claim below verified against kjv_strongs.txt; Hebrew from
wlc.txt; Greek from greek_nt.txt; second witness from data/hg-chapters/.

## The corpus

Hebrew: **ma'aseh (H4639)** "work, deed, workmanship" — 221 verses, 235
tokens (Psalms 38, Exodus 37, Isaiah 24, Ecclesiastes 19). **po'al
(H6467)** "work, deed" — 38 verses. **pe'ullah (H6468)** "work; WAGES" —
14 verses. **avodah (H5656)** "service, labor, bondage" — 125 verses, 141
tokens. **alilah (H5949)** "doings" — 24 verses. Siblings disclosed:
**ma'alal (H4611)** "doings" — 41 verses (the "amend your ways and your
doings" word); **melakah (H4399)** "occupation, workmanship" — 149 verses
(the sabbath command's word, Exod 20:9-10, and God's own creation-work,
Gen 2:2-3) — a neighbor lexeme, bucketed literal below. The verb **pa'al
(H6466)** supplies the fixed phrase **po'alei aven, "workers of
iniquity" — 19 verses** (Job 31:3; 34:8, 22; Ps 5:5; 6:8; 14:4; 28:3;
36:12; 53:4; 59:2; 64:2; 92:7, 9; 94:4, 16; 125:5; 141:9; Prov 10:29;
21:15) — and its bright twin "worketh righteousness," po'el tsedeq
(Ps 15:2).

Greek: **ergon (G2041)** — 161 verses, 176 tokens (John 25, Revelation
21, Romans 15, James 12, Hebrews 11); "good work(s)" with ergon — 27
verses; **erga nomou "works of the law" — 8 verses, 10 tokens** (Rom
3:20, 28 KJV "deeds"; 9:32; Gal 2:16 ×3; 3:2, 5, 10 — the tally corrects
research-dead-works.md's count of 5). **ergates (G2040)** "workers,
labourers" — 15 verses. **ergazomai (G2038)** "to work" — 37 verses.
**praxis (G4234)** "deeds" — 6 verses (incl. Matt 16:27, Rom 8:13,
Col 3:9).

## Candidate definition

**A work is a deed that executes a command, and Scripture audits every
work by whose command it executes — God's or man's — never by its size,
kindness, or religious shine. Good works are the commandments done where
men can see them; evil works are men's enactments (or the flesh's
appetites) done in the commandments' place; and the wage rides inside the
deed — one Hebrew noun, pe'ullah, is both "work" and "wages," so to be
judged "according to works" is simply to be paid what the deed already
carries.** Charity is a bright commanded subset (the Hebrew idiom for
almsgiving IS tzedaqah, "righteousness"), not the definition.

## Scripture's self-gloss — works stand in the commandments' column

- **Exod 18:20 — the defining first use: "thou shalt teach them the
  STATUTES (chuqqim) and the LAWS (torot), and shalt shew them the WAY
  wherein they must walk, and THE WORK (ha-ma'aseh) that they must do."**
  One sentence, one chain: law taught → way walked → work done. The
  ma'aseh is what the taught law becomes when hands perform it — definite
  article, the work, an assigned thing, not self-invented effort.
- **Ps 111:7-8 — "The WORKS of his hands are verity and judgment; all his
  COMMANDMENTS are sure… they stand fast for ever."** Hebrew: ma'asei
  yadav emet u-mishpat / ne'emanim kol piqqudav — works and precepts are
  the two halves of one poetic line; God's works and God's commandments
  are one register, stated as parallel by the psalm itself. Man's side of
  the weld: **Ps 78:7 — "not forget the WORKS of God, but KEEP his
  COMMANDMENTS"** (works there is ma'alal, H4611 — disclosed), and
  **Deut 11:7-8 — "your eyes have SEEN all the great WORKS (ma'aseh) of
  the LORD which he did. THEREFORE shall ye keep all the commandments."**
  Seeing His works obligates keeping His commandments; the two nouns pull
  one wagon. Even the angels are defined this way: "his angels… THAT DO
  HIS COMMANDMENTS, hearkening unto the voice of his word" (Ps 103:20).
- **Eccl 12:13-14 — the standard, then the audit: "Fear God, and KEEP HIS
  COMMANDMENTS: for this is the whole of man. For God shall bring every
  WORK (ma'aseh) into JUDGMENT, with every secret thing, whether it be
  good, or whether it be evil."** The book's last two verses are one
  hinge: v13 names the measure, v14 announces the measuring. "Good or
  evil work" is defined by the verse before it — kept or broken
  commandment.
- **Deut 32:4 — "He is the Rock, his WORK (po'al) is perfect: for all his
  ways are JUDGMENT."** The for-clause glosses work by mishpat, the same
  equation as Ps 111:7.

## The fork stated — whose statute the deed keeps

- **Mic 6:16 — the sharpest verse in the study: "the STATUTES (chuqqot) of
  OMRI are KEPT (yishtammer — shamar, the commandment-keeping verb), and
  all the WORKS (ma'aseh) of the house of AHAB, and ye walk in their
  counsels."** Man's statutes, kept with the very verb that keeps
  mitzvot, produce man's works — statute → work, in one breath. Its
  mirror: **2 Chr 17:4 — Jehoshaphat "walked in his COMMANDMENTS, and not
  after the DOINGS (ma'aseh) of Israel."** Commandments walked versus
  works followed: the fork is not works-versus-faith but
  command-versus-command.
- **Lev 18:3-4 runs both sides in one statute: "After the DOINGS (ma'aseh)
  of the land of Egypt… shall ye not do… neither shall ye walk in their
  ORDINANCES (chuqqot). Ye shall DO my JUDGMENTS, and KEEP mine
  ORDINANCES, to walk therein."** Doings execute ordinances on both sides
  of the fork; the only question is whose. So Exod 23:24: "thou shalt not
  bow down to their gods… nor DO AFTER THEIR WORKS."
- **Isa 29:13 — the fear of God itself can become a man-made work: "their
  fear toward me is taught by the PRECEPT of men" — and "precept" is
  H4687, MITZVAH.** A commandment of men, verbatim. Jesus reads the verse
  at exactly this pressure point: "Why do ye also TRANSGRESS THE
  COMMANDMENT OF GOD by your TRADITION?… in vain they do worship me,
  teaching for doctrines the COMMANDMENTS OF MEN" (Matt 15:3, 9; Mark
  7:7), and names the mechanism: "full well ye REJECT the commandment of
  God, that ye may KEEP your own tradition" (Mark 7:9) — keep-verb again,
  aimed at the wrong lawgiver.
- **Matt 23:3-5 — works done "to be seen": "whatsoever they bid you
  observe [from Moses' seat], that observe and do; but do not ye after
  their WORKS… all their works they do for to be SEEN of men."** The
  Greek keeps Moses' word commanded (poiēsate kai tēreite) and their
  works refused — the same men relay God's law and manufacture their own
  works.
- **Col 2:20-23 — Paul's name for the counterfeit register: "ordinances
  (dogmatizesthe)… after the COMMANDMENTS AND DOCTRINES OF MEN… a shew of
  wisdom in WILL WORSHIP (ethelothrēskeia — self-willed religion)."**
  Religious, ascetic, impressive — and man-legislated, therefore vain.
- **avodah forks the same way: 2 Chr 12:8 — "they shall be his servants;
  that they may know MY SERVICE, and the SERVICE OF THE KINGDOMS OF THE
  COUNTRIES."** One noun serves Egypt's brick-quota (Exod 1:14; 2:23) or
  the LORD's house (1 Chr 23:28); the Passover ordinance is itself "this
  SERVICE" (Exod 12:25-26; 13:5), and the Levites' avodah runs "according
  to the COMMANDMENT of the LORD" (Num 4:49). Work is always work under
  someone; the fork is the master.

## Good works = the law done where men can see

- **Matt 5:16 inside 5:17-19.** "Let your light so shine before men, that
  they may SEE your GOOD WORKS, and glorify your Father" — and three
  verses later the frame: "think not that I am come to destroy the law…
  whosoever shall DO and TEACH them [these commandments], the same shall
  be called great." The book has already fixed the optics: the
  commandment is the lamp and the law is the light (Prov 6:23); light =
  the law lived visibly (sym-light, ch. 20). Good works are what the
  watching world sees when the commandments burn.
- **The seen-fork (weighed, not dodged): Matt 6:1 "do not your
  righteousness before men, TO BE SEEN of them" and Matt 23:5 "all their
  works they do for to be SEEN of men"** look like counter-texts to 5:16
  and are actually its calibration: in 5:16 men see and glorify YOUR
  FATHER; in 6:1/23:5 the deed is engineered so men glorify YOU (HG 6:1:
  "so that they PRAISE YOU… no reward from your Father"). Visibility is
  constant; the glory's address changes with the command's address — a
  deed executing God's command points at God, a deed executing
  self-display pays out in the only coin it sought ("they HAVE their
  reward").
- **Eph 2:10 — the works pre-written: "we are his WORKMANSHIP (poiēma —
  the 'work of thy hands' register of Isa 64:8; 29:23; 60:21), created in
  Christ Jesus unto GOOD WORKS, which God hath BEFORE ORDAINED
  (proētoimasen, prepared beforehand) that we should WALK IN THEM."**
  Whose path was prepared beforehand for walking in? The covenant idiom
  answers itself — "walk in my statutes" (Lev 26:3) — and the
  new-covenant promise is precisely God preparing that walk: **Ezek 36:27
  "I will put my spirit within you, and CAUSE you to WALK IN MY STATUTES,
  and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them"**; **Isa 26:12 "thou also
  hast WROUGHT (pa'al) all our WORKS (ma'aseh) IN US."** Eph 2:10 is
  Ezek 36:27 + Isa 26:12 in Greek — and it meshes verbatim with the
  Garments chapter's granted-then-worn righteousness (Rev 19:8).
- **Titus 2:11-14 — grace's curriculum ends in works: "the grace of God…
  TEACHING us that, denying ungodliness… that he might redeem us from all
  ANOMIA (lawlessness), and purify unto himself a peculiar people,
  ZEALOUS OF GOOD WORKS."** Redeemed FROM lawlessness UNTO good works —
  the two poles of the works-fork inside one sentence; good works are
  lawlessness's stated opposite, therefore law-keeping.
- **James 1:22-25 — "be ye DOERS of the word… whoso looketh into the
  perfect LAW of liberty… being not a forgetful hearer, but a DOER OF THE
  WORK (poiētēs ergou), this man shall be blessed in his deed."** The
  work a man does by continuing in the law is called THE work — Exod
  18:20's grammar in Greek. James then runs the axis both ways: "faith
  without WORKS is dead" (2:17-26), Abraham's altar and Rahab's
  hospitality the living works.
- **Rev 2:26 — "he that overcometh, and KEEPETH MY WORKS (ho tērōn ta
  erga mou) unto the end."** Tēreō is the commandment-keeping verb
  (Rev 12:17; 14:12 "keep the commandments of God") — Jesus' works can be
  KEPT because works are commandment-shaped; and Mic 6:16's shamar of
  Omri's statutes is the same keeping sworn to the wrong lord.
  1 John 3:22-24 and 2 John 6 close the loop: "we keep his commandments,
  and DO those things that are pleasing in his sight… this is love, that
  we WALK AFTER HIS COMMANDMENTS" — the book's ch. 6 equation (love =
  keeping the commandments) is the works equation.
- **Works are the glossary's Fruit.** Fruit is already fixed as "a
  nation's works, by which it is judged," and the texts weld the nouns:
  "fruitful in every good WORK" (Col 1:10), good works "that they be not
  UNFRUITFUL" (Titus 3:14), "the UNFRUITFUL works of darkness" (Eph
  5:11), "the fruit of his DOINGS" (Jer 17:10), and the oil that burns in
  the virgins' lamps is proven works (sym-oil). One organism: command →
  work → fruit → judgment.

## The work of God — John 6:28-29

- The crowd asks a taskmaster's question: "What shall we do, that we
  might WORK THE WORKS OF GOD?" (ta erga tou Theou — HG translation: "do
  good works to El"). Jesus answers with a singular: **"This is THE WORK
  OF GOD, that ye BELIEVE on him whom he hath SENT (apesteilen)."** With
  the book's recovered belief (obey because it is the truth) and the
  vineyard chapter's sent-drumbeat, the verse is not works dissolved into
  sentiment — it is the law's own next commandment: **Deut 18:18-19 "I
  will raise them up a Prophet… whosoever will not HEARKEN unto my words
  which he shall speak in my name, I will REQUIRE it of him."** John
  glosses it exactly so: "this is his COMMANDMENT, That we should BELIEVE
  on the name of his Son" (1 John 3:23). The work of God is obedience to
  the Sent One — the one command that orders all the rest, demanded of a
  generation refusing it.
- **Even the Son's works are received orders.** "My Father worketh
  hitherto, and I work… the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he
  seeth the Father do" (John 5:17, 19); "the works which the Father hath
  GIVEN me to finish… bear witness that the Father hath SENT me" (5:36);
  "I must work the works of HIM THAT SENT ME" (9:4); "I have FINISHED the
  work which thou GAVEST me to do" (17:4). Works are assigned by the
  Sender — never self-designed projects — and Mark 13:34 states it as
  parable: the departing master gives "to every man HIS WORK."
- **Works are the credential signs cannot be.** "If I do not the works of
  my Father, believe me not" (John 10:37) — works authenticate because
  they are testable against the Father's known will, and Deut 13:1-4 is
  the statute behind the test: a prophet whose SIGN comes to pass but who
  leads away from the commandments is refused — "ye shall… KEEP HIS
  COMMANDMENTS." Signs prove power; works prove alignment.

## Judged by works — the wage rides inside the deed

- **pe'ullah, the study's sharpest lexical find: one noun is both the
  deed and its pay.** "The WAGES (pe'ullat) of him that is hired shall
  not abide with thee all night" (Lev 19:13); "let this be the REWARD
  (pe'ullat) of mine adversaries" (Ps 109:20); "the LABOUR (pe'ullat) of
  the righteous tendeth to LIFE" (Prov 10:16); "your work (pe'ullah)
  shall be rewarded" (2 Chr 15:7; so Jer 31:16). Twice Isaiah announces
  the coming LORD with it: **"behold, his REWARD (sakhar) is with him,
  and his WORK (pe'ullah) BEFORE HIM" (Isa 40:10; 62:11) — and Rev 22:12
  quotes the line as the judgment formula: "behold, I come quickly; and
  my REWARD is with me, to give every man according as his WORK shall
  be."** To be judged by works is to be paid the wage the deed already
  contains — which is why "the WAGES of sin is death" (Rom 6:23) is
  works-grammar, not an arbitrary sentence.
- **The render-according-to formula is Scripture's spine**: "thou
  renderest to every man according to his WORK" (Ps 62:12 — and MERCY
  stands in the same verse: "unto thee, O Lord, belongeth mercy: FOR thou
  renderest…" — mercy and works-judgment are one sentence, not rivals);
  "shall not he render to every man according to his WORKS?" (Prov
  24:12); "the WORK of a man shall he render unto him" (Job 34:11); "I
  the LORD search the heart… to give every man according to his ways,
  and according to the FRUIT OF HIS DOINGS" (Jer 17:10); "by him ACTIONS
  are WEIGHED" (1 Sam 2:3, alilah); Ezek 24:14 and 36:19 "according to
  thy ways and according to thy DOINGS shall they judge thee." The NT
  repeats it verbatim: Matt 16:27 (praxis); Rom 2:6-13 — ending **"the
  DOERS OF THE LAW shall be justified"** — the formula's own gloss of
  what the audited works are; 1 Pet 1:17; 2 Cor 11:15 (false ministers
  "whose end shall be according to their works"); 2 Tim 4:14; Rev 2:23;
  18:6; and the assize itself: **"the dead were judged out of those
  things which were written in the books, ACCORDING TO THEIR WORKS…
  every man according to their works" (Rev 20:12-13).**
- **Works are repentance enacted.** "God SAW THEIR WORKS, that they
  TURNED from their evil way" (Jonah 3:10) — Nineveh's works are the
  turning made visible; "do WORKS MEET FOR REPENTANCE" (Acts 26:20; so
  Matt 3:8 "fruits"); "AMEND your ways and your DOINGS, and OBEY THE
  VOICE of the LORD your God" (Jer 26:13 — amending doings glossed as
  obeying, in-verse); "repent, and do the FIRST WORKS" (Rev 2:5). The
  seven letters open "I KNOW THY WORKS" seven times (Rev 2:2, 9, 13, 19;
  3:1, 8, 15): works are what the returning King reads.
- **"Blessed are the dead… their WORKS do follow them" (Rev 14:13)** —
  spoken one verse after "here are they that KEEP THE COMMANDMENTS of
  God" (14:12): the works that follow are the keeping just named.

## The counterfeit register — man's works executing man's design

- **"Work of men's hands" = idols — roughly two dozen ma'aseh verses**
  (Deut 4:28; 27:15; 31:29; 1 Kgs 16:7; 2 Kgs 19:18; 22:17; 2 Chr 32:19;
  34:25; Ps 115:4; 135:15; Isa 2:8; 17:8; 37:19; Jer 1:16; 10:3, 9, 15;
  25:6-7; 32:30; 44:8; 51:18; Ezek 6:6; Hos 13:2; 14:3; Mic 5:13; Hag
  2:14), carried into the NT by **Acts 7:41 — "they made a calf… and
  REJOICED IN THE WORKS OF THEIR OWN HANDS"** — and Rev 9:20 ("repented
  not of the works of their hands, that they should not worship
  devils"). The calf is the register's defining case: Aaron proclaims "a
  feast TO THE LORD" (Exod 32:5) — the right God, sought through man's
  workmanship — and heaven files it under works-of-own-hands idolatry.
  The counterfeit is not usually another god; it is God approached by
  man's design. And the maker-verses add the sentence: "they that make
  them are like unto them" (Ps 115:8; 135:18) — a man becomes what his
  works execute.
- **"Works of the flesh" (Gal 5:19-21) — every item catalogued lands on a
  statute:** adultery (Exod 20:14); fornication (Lev 19:29; Deut 22:21);
  uncleanness — akatharsia, Lev 18's defilement register (18:24-30);
  lasciviousness — aselgeia, restraint thrown off, the license Jude 4
  says grace gets turned into; idolatry (Exod 20:3-5); witchcraft —
  pharmakeia (Exod 22:18; Deut 18:10-12); hatred (Lev 19:17 "thou shalt
  not hate thy brother in thine heart"); variance and seditions (Lev
  19:16 talebearing; Prov 6:16-19 — sowing discord closes the list of
  what the LORD hates); emulations and envyings (Exod 20:17); wrath and
  strife (Lev 19:18 "thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge");
  heresies — the self-chosen party (Num 15:39 "seek not after your own
  heart and your own eyes"); murders (Exod 20:13); drunkenness (Deut
  21:20-21; Prov 23:20-21); revellings — kōmoi, the calf-feast itself
  ("rose up to play," Exod 32:6, cited as the idol-feast at 1 Cor 10:7).
  The catalog is a lawbreaking list, and its sentence is a works-
  judgment: "they which DO such things shall not inherit the kingdom."
  **Paul then closes the opposite list with the law as measure: "against
  such there is NO LAW" (Gal 5:23)** — in Paul's own antithesis the law
  audits both columns; the fruit of the Spirit passes, the works of the
  flesh fail. Gal 5:14 ("all the law is fulfilled in… love thy
  neighbour") stands five verses before the catalog.
- **The rest of the register keeps the same grammar**: "works of
  darkness" (Rom 13:12; Eph 5:11 — "unfruitful": no fruit because no
  command executed); "dead works" (Heb 6:1; 9:14 — see
  research-dead-works.md: works whose wages is death, never used of
  obedience); "wicked works" (Col 1:21); **"unlawful deeds" (2 Pet 2:8) —
  the Greek is anomois ergois, literally LAWLESS WORKS, the tag KJV
  hides**; "ungodly deeds" (Jude 15); "his own works were evil, and his
  brother's righteous" (1 John 3:12 — Cain); "the works of the devil"
  destroyed by the Son (1 John 3:8, where sin has just been defined as
  anomia, 3:4). **John's own gloss of the light/dark works fork: "men
  loved darkness… because their DEEDS WERE EVIL… but he that DOETH TRUTH
  cometh to the light, that his deeds may be made manifest, that they
  are WROUGHT IN GOD" (John 3:19-21)** — truth is the law (Ps 119:142),
  so doing-truth is law-doing, and "wrought in God" is Isa 26:12's works
  wrought in us. **Titus 1:16 states the audit in one line: "they profess
  that they know God; but IN WORKS they DENY him, being… DISOBEDIENT, and
  unto every good work REPROBATE (adokimos — failing the assay)."**
  Profession is tested by works; the works' content is obedience.
- **Works reveal paternity.** "If ye were Abraham's children, ye would DO
  THE WORKS OF ABRAHAM… ye do the DEEDS OF YOUR FATHER" (John 8:39, 41)
  — whose child, whose works, whose command: one question three ways.

## Matt 7:21-23 — the capstone, sharper in the original than in English

- **The pleaders never claim erga.** KJV "have we not… done many
  WONDERFUL WORKS?" hides the noun: the Greek is **dynameis (G1411),
  POWERS** — prophecy, exorcism, mighty acts. The Hebrew Gospel reads
  **otot, SIGNS** ("in your name we did many SIGNS"). The only "works"
  in the scene are in the verdict: **"depart from me, ye that WORK
  (ergazomenoi) LAWLESSNESS (anomia)."** They plead powers; He judges
  works — and Deut 13:1-4 already legislated the gap: a sign that comes
  to pass certifies nothing when the sign-worker leads away from the
  commandments. Signs prove power; works prove whose command.
- **The verdict is a Psalm quotation, verbatim in the Hebrew Gospel:**
  HG Matt 7:23 reads **suru mimmenni kol PO'ALEI AVEN — "depart from me,
  all ye WORKERS OF INIQUITY," Ps 6:8 word for word** — placing the
  pleaders inside the OT's standing 19-verse category ("thou hatest all
  workers of iniquity," Ps 5:5; "all the workers of iniquity do
  flourish… shall be destroyed for ever," Ps 92:7).
- **The fork inside the passage:** "not every one that saith… Lord, Lord…
  but he that DOETH THE WILL OF MY FATHER" (7:21), and the parable that
  follows: "whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and DOETH them"
  (7:24). The works were many, loud, supernatural, and in His name; the
  alignment was to another will. Wonderful works are not good works.

## Paul without anti-law — what "works of the law" excludes, and what it cannot

- **What is excluded: works as WAGE, MERIT, RANSOM-PRICE.** Paul's own
  definition of the working he refuses: **"to him that WORKETH is the
  reward (misthos) reckoned not of grace, but OF DEBT" (Rom 4:4)** —
  working-for-hire, obligating God as debtor. The pe'ullah find gives
  this teeth: deeds do carry wages — and that is exactly why they cannot
  buy back a forfeited life. Sin's works already earned death (Rom 6:23,
  opsōnia — soldier's pay); the ransom is fixed and unpayable by the
  debtor (Ps 49:7-8; the Coin chapter); "when ye shall have done ALL
  those things which are commanded you, say, We are unprofitable
  servants: we have done that which was OUR DUTY" (Luke 17:10) — perfect
  subsequent obedience earns no surplus to pay old debt with. Boasting is
  the tell Paul names twice (Rom 3:27; 4:2; Eph 2:9): a wage can be
  boasted of; a gift cannot.
- **What is not excluded: the doing of the law.** The same letters:
  "the DOERS OF THE LAW shall be justified" (Rom 2:13); "do we then make
  void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we ESTABLISH the law"
  (Rom 3:31); "that the righteousness of the law might be FULFILLED IN
  US, who walk not after the flesh" (Rom 8:4). Gal 3:10's curse falls on
  "as many as are of the works of the law" precisely FOR NOT DOING it —
  "cursed is every one that CONTINUETH NOT in all things written in the
  book of the law TO DO THEM" (quoting Deut 27:26). Rom 9:31-32 diagnoses
  the manner, not the deeds: Israel pursued the law's righteousness "not
  by faith, but AS IT WERE (hōs) by works" — as-if-by-wage. Scripture
  never calls obedience a dead work, and never sets "works of the law"
  against good works — it sets wage-logic against gift-logic, then
  commands the walk: **Eph 2:8-10 holds both hands in three verses — "not
  OF works" (source) / "created UNTO good works… that we should WALK IN
  THEM" (purpose).** Titus does the same: "not by works of righteousness
  which we have done… he saved us" (3:5) in the epistle that demands a
  people "ZEALOUS of good works" (2:14) and "careful to MAINTAIN good
  works" (3:8) — three verses after 3:5.
- **Rom 9:11 and 2 Tim 1:9** ("not of works… before the world began")
  exclude works from ELECTION AND CALLING for the plainest of reasons —
  the works did not exist yet ("the children being not yet born, neither
  having done any good or evil"). Timing, not hostility: what precedes
  birth cannot be earned by deeds; what follows calling is commanded
  deeds (2 Tim 2:21; 3:17 — "throughly furnished unto ALL GOOD WORKS").
- **Extra-biblical flag (support, not proof):** the Qumran letter 4QMMT
  is titled in its own text miqsat MA'ASEI HA-TORAH, "some works of the
  law" — and it is a party's list of disputed halakhic RULINGS, the
  taqqanot register. The one known Hebrew use of Paul's exact phrase
  names sectarian rule-lists, not obedience as such.
- **What stays hard, flagged honestly:** Gal 3:11-12 sets Hab 2:4 ("the
  just shall live by faith") against Lev 18:5 ("the man that doeth them
  shall live in them") with "the law is not of faith" — the study's
  reading (Lev 18:5 states the law's true life-in-doing; Hab 2:4 names
  the root that produces the doing; the opposition is between two GROUNDS
  of standing, not two lifestyles) coheres with Rom 3:31 and Jas 2, but
  Gal 3:12's blunt wording remains the tradition's strongest card and
  should be quoted, not paraphrased around, wherever the book handles it.

## The charity texts — the subset folds into the genus

- **In Hebrew, almsgiving IS "righteousness."** HG Matt 6:1-4 reads
  TZEDAQAH (H6666) throughout: "take heed lest you do your RIGHTEOUSNESS
  before men… when you do RIGHTEOUSNESS, do not sound a trumpet… let your
  left hand not know." The idiom exists because the law commands the open
  hand: **"I COMMAND thee, saying, Thou shalt open thine hand wide unto
  thy brother" (Deut 15:11)**; the gleaning statutes (Lev 19:9-10; Deut
  24:19); the returned pledge — "it shall be RIGHTEOUSNESS unto thee
  before the LORD" (Deut 24:13); "he hath given to the poor; his
  RIGHTEOUSNESS endureth for ever" (Ps 112:9); Isa 58:6-10, the chosen
  fast. **Deut 15:10 even seats the alms-command inside the works-blessing
  formula: "for this thing the LORD thy God shall bless thee IN ALL THY
  WORKS."** Charity is law.
- **Acts 9:36 — Dorcas "full of GOOD WORKS AND almsdeeds"**: the kai
  distinguishes genus and species; her alms are Deut 15 obedience, one
  bright column of the works, not their definition. **1 Tim 5:10's list
  maps item-for-item onto statutes**: brought up children (Deut 6:7),
  lodged strangers (Lev 19:34; Deut 10:19), washed the saints' feet (the
  servant's office made a dominical command, John 13:14 "ye also OUGHT
  to"), relieved the afflicted (Isa 58:10). 1 Tim 6:18 and **Heb 13:16
  put alms in the SACRIFICE register — "with such sacrifices God is well
  pleased"** — commanded offering, not sentiment; and the wage rides
  inside this deed too: "he that hath pity upon the poor LENDETH UNTO THE
  LORD; and that which he hath given will he PAY HIM AGAIN" (Prov 19:17).
- Verdict: the charity counter-texts fold in whole. Tradition mistook the
  brightest commanded species for the genus — and then lost the genus's
  measuring rod, leaving "good works" as unmeasured niceness.

## Hebrew Gospels — second witness, consolidated

- **Matt 5:16**: "your GOOD DEEDS" = ma'aseikhem ha-TOVIM (H4639 +
  H2896); "light" is ma'or (H3974), the sanctuary-luminary word (Exod
  35:14) — the lamp register built into the verse.
- **Matt 23:3**: "all that HE says to you, keep and do; but according to
  their ORDINANCES (taqqanot) and their DEEDS (ma'aseihem) do not do" —
  the Hebrew names what the Greek leaves implicit: taqqanah is the
  technical term for a man-made enactment; works execute enactments, and
  the second witness writes the legislative source into the verse.
  **Matt 23:5**: "all their deeds they do for the SIGHT OF EYES
  (le-mar'eh einayim)."
- **Matt 15:3, 6, 9**: "why do you transgress the sayings of El for the
  sake of YOUR ENACTMENTS (taqqanoteikhem)?… you despise the sayings of
  El by your enactments… their fear of me is a COMMANDMENT OF MEN
  (mitzvat anashim) learned by rote (melummadah)" — Isa 29:13's exact
  consonants.
- **Matt 7:22-23**: the plea is SIGNS (otot); the sentence is Ps 6:8
  verbatim — po'alei aven.
- **Matt 6:1-4**: alms = tzedaqah, with sakhar (wage) and shalem (repay)
  carrying the pay-register: "your Father who sees the hidden things will
  REPAY you."
- **John 6:28-29** (translation only; no word-tags in this stretch, and
  versification runs a verse off the Greek): "What should we do that we
  may be able to do GOOD WORKS TO EL?… This is THE WORK OF EL, that you
  believe in he whom he sent." The works-to-God phrasing keeps the
  service register.

## Counter-sweep — the literal range, and why it reinforces the fork

- **Craftsmanship ma'aseh (~70 of the 221)**: tabernacle needlework and
  "cunning work" (Exod 26-39, ~28 verses), temple lily-work and bases
  (1 Kgs 7; 2 Chr 3-4), apothecary's art, bakemeats, jewels, Tyre's wares
  (Ezek 27:16). Plain workmanship — and yet the bucket's own climax runs
  the audit: **"according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so the
  children of Israel made all the work. And Moses did LOOK UPON ALL THE
  WORK, and, behold, they had done it AS THE LORD HAD COMMANDED… and
  Moses BLESSED them" (Exod 39:42-43)** — Scripture's first works-
  inspection: deed examined against command, then blessing; Rev 20:12 in
  miniature. Its dark twin is the calf — equally skilled craftsmanship,
  executing man's design, inspected and ground to powder (Exod 32).
  The same hands, two commands, two verdicts: even the "literal" bucket
  teaches the definition.
- **Occupation and livelihood (~25)**: "what is your OCCUPATION?" (Gen
  46:33); the old man "from his WORK out of the field" (Judg 19:16); the
  works Solomon made and hated under the sun (Eccl 2). The sabbath frame
  governs this bucket and reinforces work-under-command: "six days shalt
  thou labour, and do all thy WORK (melakah)" (Exod 20:9) — ordinary
  labor is itself commanded, and bounded: "he that is entered into his
  rest hath CEASED FROM HIS OWN WORKS, as God did from his" (Heb 4:10) —
  the weekly enacted confession that works stop where the Sender says
  stop. The livelihood-blessing formula is attached to obedience clauses:
  "that the LORD thy God may bless thee in ALL THE WORK OF THINE HAND"
  follows the tithe shared with the Levite and stranger (Deut 14:29), the
  forgotten sheaf left (24:19), the open hand (15:10).
- **God's works (~60 across the lexemes)**: creation and exodus doxology
  (Ps 8; 19:1; 104; 111; 145; Deut 3:24; 11:3-7). Weighed above: this
  bucket is where the works∥commandments weld is stated (Ps 111:7; 78:7;
  Deut 11:7-8), so it feeds the definition rather than escaping it.
- **Honest residue**: some tokens are bare narrative — "the ACTS of his
  power" (Esther 10:2), Benaiah "who had done many acts" (2 Sam 23:20),
  wheels "and their work" (Ezek 1:16). The definition claims the range,
  not one fitted sense: (1) plain product or deed of the hands; (2) an
  assigned task under a master (avodah; Mark 13:34); (3) the audited
  deed — and wherever Scripture states the audit's standard, the standard
  is a command and the question is whose. No text audits a work by its
  kindness-quotient.

## The traditional definition (stated fairly)

Commonly taught: "good works" are acts of kindness, charity, and service
— visiting the sick, feeding the poor, being a good neighbor — valuable
as gratitude and as evidence of saving faith, but emphatically not the
ground or measure of standing before God; "works" in Paul is heard as any
human doing whatsoever, so that earnest law-keeping itself becomes the
paradigm danger ("works-righteousness," "legalism"), and the sabbath,
diet, and feast commandments are the first works retired. The tradition
does teach a judgment/reward according to works (2 Cor 5:10 is preached)
and does quote Eph 2:10 — but it leaves "good works" undefined beyond
niceness, has no operating category for RELIGIOUS works executing men's
enactments as the Bible's chief counterfeit (church tradition reads
"works of the law" as the threat and church activity as safe — Scripture
reads God's law done as the good works and men's rules kept as the vain
ones), and hears any works-audit as a threat to grace, where Scripture
puts mercy and the works-render in one verse (Ps 62:12). Divergence is
therefore on the definition itself, the counterfeit's identity, and the
wage-logic — not on whether judgment by works exists.
