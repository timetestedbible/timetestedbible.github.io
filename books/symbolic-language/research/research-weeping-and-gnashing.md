# Weeping and gnashing of teeth — the rage of the excluded (2026-07-08)

Full-corpus study. Coverage: every occurrence of the Gospel formula (Greek
*ho klauthmos kai ho brygmos tōn odontōn*, 7×), every OT occurrence of the
Hebrew root *charaq* H2786 (5×, confirmed consonantally in the WLC), the two
remaining NT gnashing texts, and the Hebrew Gospels as second witness
(data/hg-chapters/). Fourteen occurrences in all; nothing dropped.

---

## The finding

**The formula is not a torture soundtrack; it is a defined scene.** The Greek
carries definite articles — THE weeping and THE gnashing — a known tableau,
and Luke states it in full:

> "There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, **when ye shall see**
> Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the prophets, in the kingdom of God,
> and **you yourselves thrust out**." (Luke 13:28)

The weeping and gnashing happen **at the sight** of the kingdom given to
others. And the Old Testament wrote that scene first, element for element:

> "The wicked **shall see it**, and **be grieved**; he shall **gnash with his
> teeth**, and **melt away**: the desire of the wicked shall **perish**."
> (Psalm 112:10)

Sees → grieves (the weeping) → gnashes → melts away (the end). Psalm 112 is
the Gospel formula's template — and its ending is *perishing*, not endless
torment, exactly where the book's Gehenna study already stands.

## What gnashing IS (the OT definition)

In every Hebrew occurrence, gnashing is what the wicked do **at the
righteous** — rage, not pain:

- "he **gnasheth upon me** with his teeth; mine enemy sharpeneth his eyes
  upon me" (Job 16:9)
- "With **hypocritical mockers** in feasts, they **gnashed upon me** with
  their teeth" (Ps 35:16)
- "The wicked plotteth against the just, and **gnasheth upon him** with his
  teeth" (Ps 37:12)
- "All thine enemies… hiss and **gnash the teeth**… We have swallowed her
  up" (Lam 2:16)

And the New Testament keeps the definition in its own narrative: at Stephen's
trial the council, "cut to the heart… **gnashed on him with their teeth**"
(Acts 7:54) — **while Stephen sees the heavens opened and the Son of man in
glory** (7:55-56). The wicked gnashing, the righteous seeing the kingdom:
Acts stages the very tableau Luke 13:28 makes final, with the roles' outcomes
reversed at the end of the age — then, it is the excluded who see.

## Who receives the sentence

Run the seven formula texts and one fact stands: **every subject is an
insider-claimant, never a heathen.**

| Text | The one cast out |
|---|---|
| Matt 8:12 | "the **children of the kingdom**" |
| Matt 13:41-42 | tares — "them which **do iniquity**" gathered *out of his kingdom* |
| Matt 13:49-50 | bad fish — "the wicked… from **among the just**" |
| Matt 22:13 | the wedding **guest** without the garment |
| Matt 24:48-51 | the **evil servant** — "portion with the **hypocrites**" |
| Matt 25:30 | the **unprofitable servant** |
| Luke 13:26-28 | "we have **eaten and drunk in thy presence**… workers of iniquity" |

Matt 24:51's "portion with the *hypocrites*" closes a lexical loop: Ps 35:16's
gnashers are the "*hypocritical* mockers" — the same word family (chanef; the
Hebrew Matthew writes H2611 at 24:51). The gnashers of the psalm and the
portion of the servant are one company.

## The Hebrew Gospels as second witness

The Hebrew Matthew (data/hg-chapters/) renders the formula with the OT's own
root — and unifies the destinations:

- **13:42, 50; 24:51; 25:30** — "gnashing" = **charaq H2786**, the very root
  of Ps 112:10, 35:16, 37:12: the lexical bridge from the formula to its OT
  template, in the Hebrew witness's own hand. (8:12 alone reads *chazaq*
  H2388, "clenching" — the grip-word — a resh/zayin variant worth noting,
  not leaning on.)
- **8:12** — "outer darkness" reads "the **dark places of Gehinnom**"
- **22:13** — "outer darkness" reads "**lowest Sheol**" (*tachtit* — Deut
  32:22's "lowest hell," where the fire of His anger burns *to the grave*)
- **25:30** — "the dark places of **the depths** (*tachtit*)"

Outer darkness, the furnace, the portion with the hypocrites, Gehinnom,
lowest Sheol — one scene under five names, and every Hebrew name is
grave-and-destruction vocabulary. The witness stands with Ps 112:10's ending:
the gnasher **melts away**.

## The reading

The weeping is grief over the loss; the gnashing is rage at the blessed —
sorrow for self and hatred of the righteous, in one face. The formula does
not describe torments inflicted; it describes **the character of the excluded
persisting at the sight of what they forfeited** — they do not repent even
then; they rage, as the council raged at Stephen. The exclusion is thereby
shown just: the gnashing IS the evidence. And per Ps 112:10, it ends — the
gnasher melts away; the desire of the wicked perishes.

**Edge case (carried for completeness):** Mark 9:18 — the possessed boy
"gnasheth with his teeth, and **pineth away**" (*xērainō*, withers — the
grass-word). Even here the pair holds: gnashing joined to wasting, rage
joined to perishing.

## Common teaching (for the comparison line)

Commonly taught: the anguish/pain of the damned under torment in hell. This
study: the rage of excluded claimants at the sight of the kingdom given to
others — insiders, not pagans; ending in destruction, not endless torture.
**Likely DIVERGENT — run the blind delta grading before any glossary entry.**

## Glossary candidate

> **Gnashing of teeth:: The rage of the wicked at the righteous — what the
> excluded do at the sight of the kingdom they forfeited, sorrow and hatred
> in one face, and then they melt away. Scripture defines it on the wicked
> against the just, stages it at Stephen's trial, and hands the formula its
> template: he shall see, be grieved, gnash, and perish. +
> (Ps 112:10; Ps 37:12; Luke 13:28; Acts 7:54; Matt 8:12)**
