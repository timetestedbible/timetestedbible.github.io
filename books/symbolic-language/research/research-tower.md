# Tower (migdal) — symbol study (2026-07-10)

Hebrew migdal (H4026, from gadal "to be great"), 45 occurrences; Greek pyrgos
(G4444), 4 NT uses. Sibling word: bachan/bachon (Isa 32:14; Jer 6:27) — the
assay-tower, from bachan "to test metal."

## Candidate definition

**A tower is the covenant as stronghold — what a NAME builds: the name in
defensible, runnable-into form.** God's Name builds the tower of salvation;
man's name builds Babel; the disciple who takes the Name builds a tower he
must finish.

## The defining verse

- **Prov 18:10 — "The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous
  runneth into it, and is safe."** Explicit equation, Name = tower; the book's
  established Name = covenant completes it: tower = covenant stronghold.
- Support: Ps 61:3 "a strong tower from the enemy." 2 Sam 22:51 "tower
  (migdol) of salvation" rides a Kethiv/Qere: the WRITTEN consonants (MGDWL)
  say tower; the pointed tradition and the Ps 18:50 parallel read "great
  deliverance" (yagdil). Under this book's older-witnesses rule the kethiv
  stands, but the verse is support-grade only, and the variance is disclosed.

## The counterfeit lock (the strongest structural evidence)

- **Gen 11:4 — "let us build us a city and a tower… and let us make us a
  name."** The tower/name pairing recurs exactly: every tower is built to
  house a name. God's Name is a tower you run INTO; Babel builds its own tower
  to hoist its own name. Two towers, two names, two covenants.
- Judgment levels "every high tower" (Isa 2:15; cf. 30:25 "when the towers
  fall").
- **THE FALLEN TOWER'S NAME (author's find, 2026-07-10): Siloam = "Sent."**
  Scripture itself interprets the name — John 9:7: "the pool of Siloam (which
  is by interpretation, SENT)." The tower that fell on eighteen men of
  Jerusalem (Luke 13:4) was the tower of the SENT — and Isaiah had already
  defined refusing Shiloah: "this people refuseth the waters of Shiloah that
  go softly… now therefore the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the
  river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria" (Isa 8:6-7 — the same
  chapter as "seal the law among my disciples," 8:16, and the flood=armies
  figure). Refuse the gentle sent-waters, and the imperial flood comes; the
  tower of Sent falls on the city that refused the Sent One — and Jesus'
  twice-repeated "except ye repent, ye shall all LIKEWISE perish" (Luke
  13:3, 5) was cashed within the generation: "they shall not leave in thee
  one stone upon another" (Luke 19:43-44) — the vineyard's own tower down.
  The name's two poles hold: the blind man SENT to wash in Sent "came
  seeing" (John 9:7); the men who refused it died under its stones. Luke
  even pairs the number inside the chapter: eighteen crushed by the tower
  (13:4) against the daughter of Abraham "whom Satan hath bound, lo, these
  EIGHTEEN years," loosed (13:11, 16) — judgment and release, side by side.
- Narrative echo (flagged as echo, not proof): Shechem's men financed
  Abimelech from the house of BAAL-BERITH — "lord of the covenant" — and their
  tower-refuge burned over them (Judg 9:4, 46-49); the hold they died in was
  the house of EL-BERITH, "god of the covenant" (9:46). Refuge in a
  counterfeit covenant's tower does not hold.

## The vineyard tower

- Isa 5:2: the vineyard (self-decoded v7 = house of Israel) has a tower built
  in its midst; Jesus keeps it in the retelling (Matt 21:33; HG confirms
  מגדל migdal, same word as Babel and Prov 18:10).
- What stood in Israel's midst bearing the Name: the house built "for my name"
  (2 Sam 7:13). The vineyard's tower = the sanctuary, the Name's stronghold
  among the vines.

## The disciple's tower (joins the psephos study)

- Luke 14:28-30: "intending to build a tower… counteth the cost (psephizo —
  the pebble-tally, the verb's only other NT use is Rev 13:18)… lest, after he
  hath laid the foundation, and is not able to finish, all that behold begin
  to MOCK him." The unfinished, mocked tower echoes Babel's outcome — Gen 11:8
  "they left off to build the city" (the tower project abandoned with it,
  v. 4-5). COUNTER-TEXT WEIGHED: the adjacent twin parable (the king counting
  10,000 before war, Luke 14:31-32) keeps a generic cost-counting reading
  live; the tower-specific signature (psephizo + unfinished + mocked) makes
  Luke 14 a CONSISTENT EXTENSION of the symbol, not a proof of it. Between the three cannot-be-my-disciple gates: taking
  the covenant on is a tower-build; count before founding. Ties: faithful =
  the keeping KEPT (ch. 6); Babel as the cautionary tower.

## Other facets, same structure

- **Micah 4:8** — "O tower of the flock (migdal-eder), the strong hold of the
  daughter of Zion, unto thee shall it come, even the first dominion; the
  kingdom" — the shepherd's watchtower over the sheep receives the kingdom;
  sited near Bethlehem by Gen 35:21.
- **The watch-post** — 2 Kgs 9:17 (genuine migdal). Hab 2:1's "tower" is a
  THIRD lexeme, matsor (H4692) — cited for the watch-post picture only,
  flagged like bachon. Jer 6:27 (bachon): "I have set thee for a tower and a
  fortress among my people, that thou mayest know and TRY their way" — the
  prophet as assay-tower; joins the refining/coin vocabulary (try as gold,
  Zech 13:9).
- **The bride's towers** — Song 8:10 "I am a wall, and my breasts like towers:
  then was I in his eyes as one that found favour" (cf. 4:4 neck as David's
  tower hung with shields): covenant fidelity unbreached, the bride fortified.

## Counter-texts weighed

Many towers are plain military architecture and stay literal: Uzziah's
(2 Chr 26:9-10), Nehemiah's wall towers (Neh 3), Tyre's (Ezek 26-27),
Penuel's (Judg 8). The symbol does not dissolve the architecture; the
symbolic freight rides the defined uses (Name-tower, Babel-tower, vineyard-
tower, flock-tower, watch-tower) and the judgment texts that level the
self-built.
