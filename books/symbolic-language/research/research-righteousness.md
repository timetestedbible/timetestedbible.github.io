# Righteousness correction (2026-07-12)

The earlier glossary formula, "the doing of God's commandments," accurately
described righteousness in a covenant subject but presented that application
as the category itself. It therefore left God's own righteousness and the
Pauline reckoning texts under-explained.

The common category is **right-living**. God obeys no superior; He is
Right-Living by definition, and His ways and works are the measure of right
(Deut 32:4; Ps 145:17). For a creature under His authority, the commandments
specify right-living (Deut 6:25; Ps 119:172), which is why the one who does
righteousness is righteous (1 John 3:7) and the wedding linen is the saints'
righteous acts (Rev 19:8).

Romans 4 concerns the source and reckoning of righteousness rather than a
contrary substance. Its controlling distinction is not work versus obedience
but wage-work versus faithful obedience. Verse 4 defines the excluded work as
labor traded for a wage that would put God in debt. Luke 17:7-10 proves that
even complete obedience creates no such wage claim: the servant has only done
his duty and remains unprofitable.

The believer in Romans 4:5 receives God's word as true and governing and sets
himself to obey it. Abraham supplies Paul's own example: "By faith Abraham...
obeyed" (Heb 11:8), and James 2:22 says his faith worked with his works and was
brought to maturity by them. His faithful obedience was counted for
righteousness; it did not earn righteousness as wages. Romans 4:6-8 then names
forgiveness and covered sin, not the renaming of wrong-living as right.

Philippians 3:9 likewise contrasts righteousness claimed as one's own with
righteousness sourced in God through faith. The contrast is source and claim,
not right-living against lawlessness; Paul immediately resumes the language of
pressing forward and walking by the same rule (Phil 3:12, 16).

The resulting formula is: **Righteousness is God's gift in source and
right-living in substance: granted, then worn.**
