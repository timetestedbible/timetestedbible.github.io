# Research: Net — the enclosure that takes the living

Question (author, 2026-07-13): a full research pass on the uses of "net" in
scripture.

## Findings

**1. Scripture defines the image itself: men are the fish, the conqueror's
net is his power over them.** Habakkuk 1:14-17 — "makest men as the fishes
of the sea… They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in
their net (cherem), and gather (asaph, H622) them in their drag (mikmereth)…
Therefore they sacrifice unto their net… Shall they therefore empty their
net, and not spare continually to slay the nations?" The sea is the defined
symbol (the waters "are peoples, and multitudes, and nations," Revelation
17:15; MEAT ch. 40); the Chaldean's net is the apparatus that hauls living
multitudes out of it — and worshiping the net (1:16) is the empire crediting
its own machinery with its portion.

**2. God's net is enemy armies — and it takes its catch ALIVE.** Ezekiel
32:3 puts both net-words in one sentence and names the mesh: "I will spread
out my net (resheth) over thee *with a company of many people*; and they
shall bring thee up in my net (cherem)" (WLC: וּפָרַשְׂתִּי אֶת־רִשְׁתִּי …
וְהֶעֱלוּךָ בְּחֶרְמִי; LXX reads "the nets of many *nations*"). So Zedekiah:
"My net (resheth) also will I spread upon him, and he shall be taken in my
snare (metsudah): and I will bring him to Babylon" (Ezekiel 12:13; 17:20) —
capture and deportation, not the sword; the prince of Ezekiel 19:8-9 is
netted by the nations and carried to Babylon in chains. Hosea 7:12 (fowl
brought down), Job 19:6, Lamentations 1:13, Psalm 66:11-12 — where the net
of God is a passage, not an end: "thou broughtest us INTO the net… thou
broughtest us OUT into a wealthy place."

**3. The wicked spread nets too — hidden ones, for the innocent.** The
Psalms cluster (9:15; 10:9; 25:15; 31:4; 35:7-8; 57:6; 140:5); "a man that
flattereth his neighbour spreadeth a net for his feet" (Proverbs 29:5); the
priests and king's house "have been… a net spread upon Tabor" (Hosea 5:1 —
rulers AS the net over worshipers); the woman "whose heart is snares and
nets (cherem)" (Ecclesiastes 7:26); "they hunt every man his brother with a
net (cherem)" (Micah 7:2). The reflex judgment is fixed: their own foot is
taken in the net they hid (Psalm 9:15; 35:8; 141:10; Job 18:8).

**4. The kingdom fishes the same sea — with the empires' own two nets, for
the opposite end.** Jeremiah first: "I will send for many fishers… and they
shall fish them" — the REGATHERING of Israel from all lands (Jeremiah
16:15-16). Then Matthew 4:18-19: the cast-net (amphiblēstron) by the sea of
Galilee, "I will make you fishers of men"; and Matthew 13:47-50: "the
kingdom of heaven is like unto a net (sagēnē), that was cast into the sea,
and gathered of every kind" — drawn to SHORE when full, sat down, sorted:
"the good into vessels, but cast the bad away… so shall it be at the end of
the age." In the LXX of Habakkuk 1:15-17 the empire's two nets are these
same two words — amphiblēstron (Brenton "casting net") and sagēnē
("drag") — so Babylon's net and the kingdom's net are lexical twins run in
reverse: Babylon empties its net "continually to slay the nations" and
fattens itself; the kingdom's net gathers unto vessels. (Local lxx.txt is
Brenton's English; the Greek wording is Rahlfs, flagged as an external
check. The NT side is tag-verified: G293, G4522.)

**5. The cherem weld: what the net brings up is DEVOTED.** The same
consonants חרם are the dragnet (Habakkuk 1; Ezekiel 26:5,14; 32:3; 47:10;
Micah 7:2; Ecclesiastes 7:26) and the ban: "every devoted thing is MOST HOLY
unto the LORD" (Leviticus 27:28), it cannot be ransomed (27:29), it goes to
the priests (Numbers 18:14) — or it is wholly destroyed (Jericho, Joshua
6:17; Achan, Joshua 7; Agag, 1 Samuel 15:21; "the people of my curse,"
Isaiah 34:5). Two outcomes, no third: consecrated or burnt — exactly the
parable's shore: vessels or furnace (Matthew 13:48-50). Achan is the man who
takes privately from the net's catch and becomes cherem himself; Habakkuk's
Chaldean "sacrifices unto his net" — claims the devoted catch as his own
portion. The Tanakh's last written word is this word: "lest I come and smite
the earth with a cherem" (Malachi 4:6) — and the age to come is defined by
its absence: "there shall be no more cherem: but Jerusalem shall dwell
safely" (Zechariah 14:11) = "no more curse" (katathema, G2652) before the
throne (Revelation 22:3). No more ban because no more net: nothing left in
the sea to haul out.

**6. The metsudah range: net and stronghold are one enclosure.** The root
*tsud* (hunt) yields both: "God… hath compassed me with his net (metsudah)"
(Job 19:6) — and "the LORD is my rock, and my fortress (metsudah)" (2 Samuel
22:2); David took "the strong hold (metsudah) of Zion: the same is the city
of David" (2 Samuel 5:7). Ecclesiastes 9 holds both senses two verses apart:
fish "taken in an evil net (metsodah)" (9:12) and the great king who "built
great bulwarks (metsodim)" against the little city (9:14). One enclosure:
refuge to those inside with the king, capture to the prey it closes on.

**7. The sanctuary's nets (noted, not pressed).** The altar of burnt
offering carries "a grate of network (resheth)… even to the midst of the
altar" (Exodus 27:4-5; 38:4) — the devoted offering burns on a net; and the
two pillars wear "nets of checker work" (sebakah) crowned with pomegranates
(1 Kings 7:17-42; Jeremiah 52:22-23). These are craft-uses of the mesh
words; the symbol rides the capture-uses. The altar case is listed because
cherem law (find 5) burns the devoted on that netted hearth — a coherence,
not a proof.

**8. The two boat-scenes frame the age.** At the call, the catch at His word
overwhelms the tool: "they inclosed a great multitude of fishes: and their
net brake (diarrēssō)" (Luke 5:6). After the resurrection, the count is
taken and the tool holds: "full of great fishes, an hundred and fifty and
three: and for all there were so many, yet was not the net broken (schizō,
G4977)" (John 21:11). The finished net is not torn.

## Second witness (Hebrew Gospels)

- Matthew 4:18-21 — the brothers' nets are **mikmoreth** (H4365), Isaiah and
  Habakkuk's fisherman's drag; "fishers (H1771) of men" answers Jeremiah
  16:16's dayagim.
- Mark 1:16-18 — the nets are **resheth** (H7568).
- Matthew 13:47 — "the kingdom of heaven is like a net (לרשת, H7568) in the
  midst of the sea, into which all kinds of fish are **gathered (asaph,
  H622)**" — the HG uses Habakkuk 1:15's own gathering-verb (both
  tag-verified locally), tying the kingdom net to the Chaldean net
  text-to-text.

## Counter-texts weighed

- **Proverbs 1:17** ("in vain the net is spread in the sight of any bird")
  — states the mechanism, not an exception: nets work by unseen enclosure.
- **Exodus 27:4 / 1 Kings 7** — mesh as craft, not capture. The range of the
  WORDS includes fabric; the SYMBOL is carried by the capture-uses. Listed
  honestly rather than forced (the altar coherence noted at find 7).
- **Individual catches** (David in Psalm 25:15; 31:4; the wild bull of
  Isaiah 51:20; Zedekiah; Pharaoh) — the net scales to great prey: princes
  are the "great fish" hauled singly. The constant is enclosure-capture of
  the living, not stealth (snare, pach) or the hook (angle) — Psalm 140:5
  and Hosea 5:1 list snare and net side by side as different tools.
- **God's net vs the wicked's net** — not a contradiction but the range:
  the instrument is capture-of-the-living; whose hand spreads it decides
  whether the catch is deported to Babylon, devoured by the fisher-empire,
  or drawn to shore for the vessels.

## Wordplay dimension (flagged as wordplay, not definition)

Strong's derives resheth from *yarash*, "to take possession / inherit /
dispossess." The consonants of Hebrew Matthew's kingdom-net phrase — דומה
לרשת, "like a net" — are letter-for-letter the Torah's possession-infinitive
לרשת, *lareshet*, "to inherit" (e.g. Deuteronomy 2:31, WLC-verified). Read
aloud, Matthew 13:47 carries a second line: the kingdom of heaven is like —
*to take possession* — in the midst of the sea. The net is how a kingdom
takes possession of its inheritance out of the peoples.

## Verdict

A net is **the enclosure by which a power takes living multitudes out of
the sea — out of the mass of peoples — alive, whole, and of every kind, for
sorting and devotion.** Empires spread it and worship it (Habakkuk); God
spreads it as armies and it carries to Babylon (Ezekiel); the kingdom
spreads it as the gospel and it gathers to the shore (Matthew). What comes
up in it is cherem — the same word — wholly consecrated into vessels or
wholly given to the fire, never ransomed, never split. The snare takes one
by the foot in secret; the net takes the many by enclosure. At the end
there is no more net and no more ban (Zechariah 14:11; Revelation 22:3):
the catch is landed, the count stands, and the net is not torn.
