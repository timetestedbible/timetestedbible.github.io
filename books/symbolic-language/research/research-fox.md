# Research: Fox (shu'al / alōpēx) — the breach-dweller (2026-07-28)

Complete sweep, verified against kjv_strongs.txt and wlc.txt: Hebrew
*shu'al* H7776, **6 occurrences** (Judg 15:4; Neh 4:3; Ps 63:10; Song
2:15; Lam 5:18; Ezek 13:4); Greek *alōpēx* G258, **3 occurrences**
(Matt 8:20; Luke 9:58; Luke 13:32). The LXX renders *shu'al* with
*alōpēx* at every checked site (Song 2:15; Lam 5:18; Ezek 13:4), and
the Hebrew Gospels render *alōpēx* back as *shu'al* (Matt 8:20; Luke
13:32) — one lexeme across both testaments, round trip confirmed.
Place-names (Shual, Hazar-shual) set aside. *Shu'al* may cover the
jackal (the pack-scavenger of Ps 63:10); the distinct word *tannim*
(H8577, KJV "dragons") is not part of this study.

## Verdict up front: Scripture defines it out loud

**Ezekiel 13:4 is the in-text decode** — "O Israel, thy prophets are
like the FOXES **in the ruins**" (*ka-shu'alim ba-choravot* — the KJV's
"deserts" flattens *choravot*, ruins). The next verse states the failure
by structural contrast: "ye have **not gone up into the breaches**
(*peratsot*), neither made up the **hedge** (*gader*) for the house of
Israel to stand in the battle in the day of the LORD" (13:5). The fox
is the creature *of* the breach — it dens in gaps, walls, and ruins.
The prophet's calling is the exact opposite: "make up the hedge, and
**stand in the gap**" (Ezek 22:30). So the false prophet is a fox: the
one who *inhabits* the breach he was sent to repair. The breach is his
habitat and his livelihood — mending it would evict him. The same
oracle finishes the portrait: these prophets build the counterfeit
repair, the wall daubed with untempered mortar that falls in the storm
(13:10-16).

## The occurrences — one pattern

- **Ezek 13:4** — the decode, above. Prophets = foxes in the ruins.
- **Song 2:15** — "Take us the foxes, the little foxes, that **spoil**
  (*mechablim* — ruin, corrupt) **the vines: for our vines have tender
  grapes** (*semadar* — in blossom)." The vineyard is the covenant
  people by Scripture's own equation ("the vineyard of the LORD of
  hosts is the house of Israel," Isa 5:7); the fox ruins it **at
  blossom** — before fruit can set. Even the *little* ones. The command
  is to catch them.
- **Lam 5:18-19** — "the mountain of Zion, which is desolate, the foxes
  walk upon it. Thou, O LORD, remainest for ever; **thy throne** from
  generation to generation." Foxes pace the ruined throne-mount; the
  contrast drawn in the very next line is the throne that endures.
- **Luke 13:32** — "Go ye, and tell **that fox**" — Herod: an Idumean
  on Judah's ruined throne, the killer of the Baptist. And the pericope
  seals the identification: it ends "your **house is left unto you
  desolate**" (13:35) — Lam 5:18's picture, foxes walking the
  desolation, replayed with a name attached.
- **Neh 4:3** — Tobiah mocks the builders: "even that which they build,
  **if a fox go up, he shall even break down their stone wall**." The
  wall's enemy assigns the fox its known power — breach-making — and
  invokes it against the gap-menders. The wall stood anyway.
- **Ps 63:9-11** — those who seek the king's soul "shall fall by the
  sword: they shall be **a portion for foxes**" — and the psalm names
  them in its closing line: "the mouth of them that **speak lies**
  shall be stopped." The liars end as the fox's food: ruin feeds the
  ruin-creature, and the false take their portion with the false.
- **Judg 15:4-5** — Samson catches 300 foxes, ties them **tail to
  tail**, sets a firebrand between the tails, and looses them into
  Philistia's standing corn, vineyards, and olives. Read with the
  repository's own approved decode of Tail — "the prophet that teacheth
  lies" (Isa 9:15) — the image locks: breach-creatures joined lie to
  lie, fire between them, burning a nation's harvest, vintage, and oil.
  Samson turns the spoiler-class loose on the spoilers' own fields. (At
  the plain level the verse still testifies to the range: what foxes do
  to a land's produce is destroy it.)
- **Matt 8:20 / Luke 9:58** — "The foxes have **holes** (HG: *chorim*;
  Luke's HG: *me'arot*, dens), and the birds of the air have nests; but
  the Son of man hath not where to lay his head." The fox is *housed*
  in the land — the hole-dweller is at home precisely where things lie
  in ruins — and it is paired with the fowls of the air, the
  seed-snatchers of the sower parable (Matt 13:4, 19). The squatters
  have dens and the heir has nowhere — spoken, pointedly, to a scribe
  offering to follow.

## The wordplay dimension (flagged as wordplay, not the definition)

Ezekiel plays the fox's own name inside the oracle: the prophets are
*shu'alim* (foxes, 13:4, spelled שעלים) who pollute souls "for
**handfuls** of barley" — *b'sho'alei* se'orim (13:19, בשעלי) — the
same consonantal skeleton. *Shu'al* is the hollow-creature, the
burrower of holes; *sho'al* is the hollow of a hand (the measure in
which God holds the waters, Isa 40:12). The fox-prophets work for what
a hollow hand can hold, and the Gospel saying preserves the name's
meaning in translation: the foxes have *holes*.

## Counter-texts weighed

- **Judg 15** — instrumental use: the fox's nature is harnessed for
  judgment on Philistia; nothing in the passage honors the fox itself.
- **Matt 8:20 / Luke 9:58** — readable as plain naturalism (animals
  have homes; the itinerant Son does not). Noted honestly: the saying
  does not *require* the symbol — but the fowls-pairing and the
  homeless-heir contrast sit fully inside the range, and the HG's
  *shu'alim/chorim* carries the Hebrew resonance.
- **Ps 63:10** — property-use (the scavenger of the fallen); consistent
  with, not additional to, the range.
- No occurrence casts the fox positively; none requires a second sense.

## Candidate definition (pending author approval)

**Fox** — the breach-dweller: the false prophet or usurper at home in
the ruins he was sent to repair. Scripture decodes it aloud — "thy
prophets are like the foxes in the ruins… ye have not gone up into the
breaches" (Ezek 13:4-5) — and keeps the figure everywhere the word
appears: spoiling the vineyard at blossom (Song 2:15), walking desolate
Zion while the true throne endures (Lam 5:18-19), breaking down walls
in the mouths of the wall's enemies (Neh 4:3), eating the fallen liars
(Ps 63:10-11), and wearing Herod's face inside a desolation oracle
(Luke 13:32-35). The foxes have holes; the Son of man, nowhere to lay
his head (Matt 8:20) — the squatters are housed in the ruin, and the
heir is not.

Related: Tail (the lying prophet — Judg 15's tail-to-tail fire), Wall
(the gap the fox lives in and the prophet should stand in), Grapes /
the vineyard (what the fox spoils), Swine and Dog (the book's other
unclean teacher-figures), Mountain / Zion / Throne (what lies desolate
under the fox's feet), Harvest (what burns).
