# Paul's "works of the law" — occurrence and vocabulary audit (2026-07-12)

## Corpus

The exact Greek phrase _erga nomou_ occurs in 7 verses and 9 tokens in the
critical Greek text: Romans 3:20, 28; Galatians 2:16 (3 times); 3:2, 5, 10.
The Received Text behind the KJV also reads "works of the law" at Romans 9:32,
making 8 verses and 10 tokens. The critical text there has _ex ergōn_, "by
works," without _nomou_, "of law."

The phrase itself does not identify which law, which application of law, or
which claim is being made for the deed. Paul's surrounding vocabulary must do
that work.

## Occurrence table

| Reference | Immediate vocabulary | Classification | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romans 3:20 | "whatsoever the law saith"; "by the law is the knowledge of sin"; the law and prophets witness God's righteousness in v.21 | **God's law — clear** | The law speaks to the world, identifies sin, and stands with the prophets. No human statute is in view. The excluded claim is justification by its deeds, not the law's identity. |
| Romans 3:28 | boasting excluded; Jew and Gentile; circumcision and uncircumcision justified by faith; "we establish the law" in v.31 | **God's law — clear** | Paul denies the law as the ground of boasting or justification and immediately denies that faith abolishes it. |
| Romans 9:32, KJV/TR | Israel pursues "the law of righteousness" but seeks it "not by faith, but as it were by works" | **God's law — clear; textual variant** | The referent is Israel's law of righteousness. The critical text lacks "of the law," strengthening the focus on the attempted manner, "as if by works," rather than identifying a different code. |
| Galatians 2:16, 3 tokens | men "of the circumcision"; Peter separates from Gentiles; Paul says he was not walking uprightly; "why compellest thou the Gentiles to Judaize?" | **Circumcision-party application — context clear; source mixed** | Circumcision is a God-given sign, but the demand that Gentiles Judaize and the table separation are the party's application. The dispute is not safely labeled either "all Torah obedience" or simply "commandments of men." |
| Galatians 3:2 | Spirit received; hearing of faith; begun in Spirit, perfected in flesh | **Ambiguous, with circumcision controversy controlling the letter** | The verse names no particular deed. Galatians 2 and 5 make circumcision the live controversy, but the phrase itself does not specify a statute. |
| Galatians 3:5 | Spirit supplied; miracles worked; hearing of faith | **Ambiguous, with circumcision controversy controlling the letter** | Same antithesis as 3:2 and still no named act. It contrasts the source of the Spirit with "works of law," not God's commands with man's in the abstract. |
| Galatians 3:10 | "written in the book of the law"; quotation of Deuteronomy 27:26; "continue in all things... to do them" | **God's law — explicit** | The explanatory quotation fixes the referent. ## Paul's vocabulary key

Paul has clear vocabulary when he wants to identify the source.

### God's law and commands

- _nomos Theou_, "law of God" — Romans 7:22, 25; 8:7.
- "the law is holy, and the commandment holy, and just, and good" — Romans
  7:12.
- _tērēsis entolōn Theou_, "keeping of the commandments of God" —
  1 Corinthians 7:19.
- "the law and the prophets" — Romans 3:21.
- "the law of Christ" — Galatians 6:2.

### Human and sectarian enactments

- _patrikai paradoseis_, "traditions of my fathers" — Galatians 1:14.
- _ioudaizein_, "to Judaize / live as do the Jews" — Galatians 2:14.
- _paradosis tōn anthrōpōn_, "tradition of men" — Colossians 2:8.
- _dogmatizesthe_, "subject to ordinances," followed by "Touch not; taste
  not; handle not" — Colossians 2:20-21.
- _entalmata kai didaskalias tōn anthrōpōn_, "commandments and doctrines of
  men" — Colossians 2:22.
- _Ioudaikoi mythoi_ and _entolai anthrōpōn_, "Jewish fables" and
  "commandments of men" — Titus 1:14.

### Boundary-marker vocabulary

- _peritomē_ / _akrobystia_, circumcision / uncircumcision — Romans 3:30;
  Galatians 2:7-12; 5:2-6; 6:12-15.
- Paul separates the boundary marker from obedience explicitly:
  "Circumcision is nothing, and uncircumcision is nothing, but the keeping of
  the commandments of God" (1 Corinthians 7:19).

### Wage and merit vocabulary

- _misthos_, wage/reward, and _opheilēma_, debt — Romans 4:4.
- boasting — Romans 3:27; 4:2; Ephesians 2:9.
- the servant who completes every command remains "unprofitable" and has only
  done his duty — Luke 17:7-10.

## Findings

1. _Erga nomou_ cannot be assigned a single hidden definition such as
   "obedience," "human traditions," or "boundary markers" in every occurrence.
2. Romans 3:20, 28 and Galatians 3:10 clearly concern God's law. Romans 9:32
   does also in the KJV/TR, though its phrase has a textual variant.
3. Galatians 2:16 is governed by the circumcision party's separation and demand
   that Gentiles Judaize. A divine sign has been turned into a universal entry
   requirement and ground of standing.
4. Galatians 3:2 and 3:5 inherit that controversy but remain lexically
   ambiguous; the named contrast concerns the source of the Spirit.
5. Paul distinguishes God's commands from men's rules with explicit vocabulary.
   An interpretation that calls every command "works" and then treats every
   work as what Paul rejected erases distinctions Paul himself maintains.
6. The uniform element is not the code's source but the claim made for the
   deed: works cannot generate justification, the Spirit, election, or salvation
   as wages. The same Paul establishes God's law, commands its keeping, and
   insists that even complete obedience leaves God owing no debt.

## Compact conclusion

"Works of the law" is not Paul's synonym for obedience. In clear contexts it
can name deeds related to God's Torah; in the Galatian controversy it can name
the circumcision party's imposed boundary practices; in several verses the act
is left unstated. The phrase consistently denies deeds as the purchase price or
entry claim. It does not consistently identify the deeds themselves as evil,
human, or disobedient.
