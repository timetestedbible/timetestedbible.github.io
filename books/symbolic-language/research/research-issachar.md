# Research: Issachar — there is a wage

Question (author, 2026-07-15): full study of Issachar. Thesis under test —
the glossary entry: "The hireling who owns the field: strong as the donkey
to carry, humble enough to bow, settled in tents, discerning the appointed
times, rich enough to feed a king, and willing to walk into the valley when
Israel must be saved. In the heavenly camp he is Virgo in the east… and is
identified with sapphire, the full moon."

Occurrences: H3485, 40 verses (all listed in the tiers below); one
same-named PERSON, a porter's son (1 Chr 26:5), excluded from the tribe's
pattern. All tags verified against kjv_strongs.txt.

## The name — yesh sachar, "there is a wage"

- **Born of a hire, named a wage.** Jacob comes __“out of the field”__ and
  Leah meets him: __“Thou must come in unto me; for surely I have hired
  thee (sachor secharticha, H7936 doubled) with my son's mandrakes”__ (Gen
  30:16); __“God hath given me my hire (sechari, H7939)… and she called
  his name Issachar”__ (30:18). The tribe begins in a field, in a hire,
  at wheat harvest (30:14).
- **The wage was a child — and Scripture says that in its own proverb:**
  __“the fruit of the womb is his reward (sachar, H7939)”__ (Ps 127:3).
  Leah's naming enacts the psalm's word exactly.
- **The name's own text.** Jeremiah's promise to weeping Rachel carries the
  name spelled out, tag for tag: __“Refrain thy voice from weeping… for
  thy work shall be rewarded ({H3426}{H3426? — yesh}{H7939} — yesh
  sachar), saith the LORD; and they shall come again from the land of the
  enemy”__ (Jer 31:16). THERE-IS-A-WAGE is the LORD's answer to the
  mother's tears, and the wage is children come back from the enemy's
  land — the return and the resurrection wearing Issachar's name. (Note
  the cross: the promise is to RACHEL, spoken in the name of LEAH's
  wage-son — the hire-son's name comforts the barren-line's weeping.)
- The same idiom seals covenant labor elsewhere: __“your work shall be
  rewarded (yesh sachar)”__ (2 Chr 15:7); __“his reward (sachar) is with
  him”__ (Isa 40:10; 62:11) — the line Revelation gives the returning
  King: __“behold, my reward is with me, to give every man according as
  his work shall be”__ (Rev 22:12). The wage-name points at the day wages
  are paid.

## The oracle — the strong ass who saw the rest (Gen 49:14-15)

__“Issachar is a strong ass (chamor garem, an ass of bone) couching down
between two burdens (mishpetayim): and he saw that rest (menuchah) was
good, and the land that it was pleasant; and bowed his shoulder to bear
(saval), and became a servant unto tribute (mas).”__

- The donkey study carries the lexemes: saval is the sin-bearing Servant's
  verb (Isa 53:11), mas is Egypt's levy (Exod 1:11). Issachar bows the
  shoulder BECAUSE HE SAW — the rest and the land are the wage he works
  for. The yoke's pay is rest: __“Take my yoke upon you… and ye shall find
  rest”__ (Matt 11:29) — the hire-tribe's bargain offered to all.
- **Deborah's song inverts the couching — and swaps the tribes.** The
  oracle's rare word mishpetayim (H4942) occurs again only in Judg 5:16
  (and its twin moshvot? — no: the word itself, twice total): in the
  Kishon war __“the princes of Issachar were with Deborah; even Issachar,
  and also Barak: he was sent on foot into the VALLEY”__ (5:15) — while
  __“Why abodest thou among the mishpetayim (sheepfolds), to hear the
  bleatings?”__ is asked of REUBEN (5:16). The donkey ROSE from between
  the saddlebags and walked into the valley; the firstborn lay down in
  his place. Couching is Issachar's posture, not his boundary — when
  Israel must be saved, the burden-bearer stands up.

## Tents, times, and the king's table

- **Tents:** __“Rejoice, Zebulun, in thy going out; and, Issachar, in thy
  tents. They shall call the peoples unto the mountain; there they shall
  offer sacrifices of righteousness: for they shall suck of the abundance
  of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand”__ (Deut 33:18-19). The
  pair splits one vocation: the goer-out and the tent-dweller together
  call the peoples to the mountain. Issachar's riches come TO his tents.
- **Times:** __“the children of Issachar, which were men that had
  understanding (binah) of the times (ittim), to know what Israel ought
  to do; the heads of them were two hundred; and all their brethren were
  at their commandment”__ (1 Chr 12:32) — said AT HEBRON, in the muster
  that came __“to turn the kingdom of Saul to David”__ (12:23). The tribe
  that discerns the appointed times is the tribe that knows WHEN to
  enthrone the king — and the rest of Israel takes its cue from them.
  This is the calendar-tribe verse: understanding of times is not
  stargazing, it is knowing what Israel ought to DO, and its fruit is a
  kingdom turned to its rightful king.
- **The feast:** the same muster ends with provision streaming in __“on
  asses, and on camels, and on mules, and on oxen… meat, meal, cakes of
  figs, and bunches of raisins, and wine, and oil… for there was joy in
  Israel”__ — the near tribes __“even unto Issachar and Zebulun and
  Naphtali”__ (1 Chr 12:40): the coronation fed on the burden-bearers'
  backs. Under Solomon the arrangement is formalized: twelve officers,
  each a month — __“Jehoshaphat the son of Paruah, in Issachar”__ (1 Kgs
  4:17, under the 4:7 month-scheme): the times-tribe keeps a month of the
  king's table.

## The land — a field called "God sows"

Issachar's lot (Josh 19:17-23) is the great arable valley: Jezreel —
__“God sows”__ — with Shunem, Chesulloth under Tabor, En-gannim ("spring
of gardens"). The hire-son born of a field-bargain inherits the
breadbasket where the nation's decisive battles muster (Deborah's Kishon,
Judg 5; Gideon's Midian camp __“in the valley of Jezreel,”__ Judg 6:33;
Saul's last field at Gilboa, 1 Sam 29:1, 11). In Issachar's Shunem the
prophet's chamber is built and the Shunammite's son is raised from death
(2 Kgs 4) — the land of the wage-name hosts a firstfruits of the
resurrection the name promises (yesh sachar — the child comes back). And
Hosea hangs the last harvest on the valley's name: __“great shall be the
day of Jezreel… I will sow her unto me in the earth”__ (Hos 1:11; 2:23).

## The camp — east, under Judah's standard

- __“On the east side toward the rising of the sun… the standard of the
  camp of Judah… and those that do pitch next unto him shall be the tribe
  of Issachar”__ (Num 2:3-5): the donkey-servant marches under the lion-
  king's banner, on the sunrise side — the place of beginnings and
  returns (sym-east). His prince offers on DAY TWO, immediately after
  Judah (Num 7:18); at the covenant antiphon he stands on Gerizim, the
  mountain of BLESSING (Deut 27:12).
- **The books' system identifications** (held as the books' own
  derivations, not free-standing lexical claims): the camp-east standard
  group maps to the sign-group containing Virgo, and TTT ch. 7 sets the
  aviv full moon in Virgo at the head of the year — the times-tribe under
  the sign where the year's first full moon stands. On the breastplate,
  birth order fills row two with Judah, Issachar, Zebulun (Exod 28:18):
  Issachar's stone is the SAPPHIRE — the stone the Pearl chapter binds to
  the throne and the full moon's tohar. The discerner of times wears the
  full moon's stone under the sunrise standard.

## The lapses — the hireling's fork

- **Baasha** — the one Issachar king — takes the throne by murder (1 Kgs
  15:27), executes the prophesied judgment on Jeroboam's house, and walks
  in the same sins: a hireling who did the work and kept the wages of
  iniquity; his own house is swept away for it (16:1-4, 7).
- **The mis-kept Passover, healed:** __“many of Ephraim, and Manasseh,
  Issachar, and Zebulun had not cleansed themselves, yet did they eat the
  passover otherwise than it was written. But Hezekiah prayed… The good
  LORD pardon every one that prepareth his heart to seek God… though he
  be not cleansed… And the LORD hearkened to Hezekiah, and healed the
  people”__ (2 Chr 30:18-20). The times-tribe itself can lose the
  times — and the heart prepared to seek is heard before the calendar is
  perfect. Mercy for the mis-keeper is written on Issachar's own name.
- The fork's negative pole in the Teacher's mouth: __“the hireling
  (misthōtos) fleeth, because he is an hireling, and careth not for the
  sheep”__ (John 10:13); its positive: __“the labourer is worthy of his
  hire”__ (Luke 10:7), the protected wage (Lev 19:13; Deut 24:14-15; Mal
  3:5), the penny paid at evening (Matt 20:8-9), __“that we receive a
  full reward”__ (2 John 8). The wage defines no one; whom you serve for
  it does — the same fork as the donkey's yoke.

## The end — sealed, gated

Issachar is sealed among the 144,000 (Rev 7:7), holds his portion beside
Judah in the restored land (Ezek 48:25-26), and has his named GATE in the
city (48:33) — the city whose every gate is one pearl. The hire-tribe's
wage stands at the end of the ledger: there is a gate with his name on it.

## Counter-texts weighed

- Most of the 40 occurrences are census, muster, boundary, and genealogy
  lines (Num 1; 26; Josh 17; 19; 21; 1 Chr 2; 6; 7; 27) — the symbol never
  dissolves the tribe. The genealogies themselves keep one note: valiant
  men, and MANY (1 Chr 7:1-5 — 87,000), the third-largest tribe by the
  second census (Num 26:25).
- 1 Chr 26:5 is a different Issachar (Obed-edom's seventh son, a porter) —
  excluded, though even the namesake is a doorkeeper blessed in his
  father's house.
- "Owns the field": association-grade, honestly held — born of a
  field-hire at wheat harvest, heir of the sowing-valley, with Song
  7:11-13 replaying the scene willingly (the bride: __“let us go forth
  into the field… the mandrakes give a smell… I have laid up for thee, O
  my beloved”__ — dudaim again, offered not bartered). No verse says
  "Issachar owns the field"; the pattern says the field is where his
  story keeps happening.
- Virgo: tribe-to-constellation assignments vary in the traditions (some
  lists give Issachar other signs); the identification here is internal
  to the books' system (camp-east + the aviv full moon in Virgo, TTT ch.
  7) and should be cited as such, not as consensus.
- The name-etymology (yesh sachar) is Scripture's own (Gen 30:18 gives
  the sachar derivation in the naming sentence; Jer 31:16 carries the
  full idiom tag-for-tag).

## Verdict

Issachar is **the wage-servant of the appointed times**: born of a hire
and named "there-is-a-wage," the strong ass who bows the shoulder because
he SAW the rest and the pleasant land — the servant who discerns the
times, feeds the king's table, rises from between the saddlebags into the
valley when Israel must be saved, and marches at the sunrise under
Judah's standard wearing the sapphire, the throne-stone of the full moon.
His name is the promise paid to the weeping mother — yesh sachar, the
children come back from the enemy's land — and his gate stands in the
city at the end. The author's glossary entry is supported at every
verifiable point; "owns the field" and the Virgo identification are
association- and system-grade respectively, flagged above.
