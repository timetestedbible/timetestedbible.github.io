# Dead works vs works — the study (2026-07-08)

Author's thesis, tested: **dead works = works that lead to death — works
aligned with the leaven of the Pharisees, the traditions of men; "works"
and "good works" = obedience to the law.** Verified against kjv_strongs.txt.

## The corpus

- **"dead works" (nekra erga): exactly 2** — Heb 6:1 ("repentance from dead
  works") and Heb 9:14 ("purge your conscience from dead works **to serve
  the living God**"). The definition is in the contrast: dead works stand
  opposite SERVICE of the LIVING God — they are service-shaped works with no
  life in them, not "efforts at obedience."
- **"good works": 16** — and Scripture defines them as commanded obedience:
  "created in Christ Jesus unto good works, **which God hath before
  ordained** that we should walk in them" (Eph 2:10 — ordained beforehand:
  the law); "let your light so shine… that they may see your good works"
  (Matt 5:16 — light = the commandment, Prov 6:23); Titus's "pattern of
  good works"; Rev 14:13 "their works do follow them" (the oil the virgins
  stored). Works follow because they were His ordinances walked in.
- **"works of the law": 5** (Rom 3:20, 28; Gal 2:16 ×3) — Paul's phrase for
  law-keeping *as ground of justification*; never called "dead works."
  Scripture never uses "dead works" of obedience.

## The identification: whose works are dead?

1. **Works that lead to death**: "there is a way which seemeth right unto a
   man, but the end thereof are the ways of **death**" (Prov 14:12); "the
   wages of sin is death" (Rom 6:23), and sin is lawlessness (1 John 3:4)
   — lawless works EARN death: dead works.
2. **The traditions of men**: "in **vain** they do worship me, teaching for
   doctrines the **commandments of men**" (Matt 15:9, quoting Isa 29:13;
   "commandments of men" ×3: Isa 29:13; Matt 15:9/Mark 7:7) — worship in
   vain is work with no life in it. Jesus names the mechanism: "ye reject
   the commandment of God, that ye may keep your own tradition" (Mark 7:9)
   — dead works REPLACE the commandments.
3. **The leaven of the Pharisees** = their doctrine (Matt 16:12) and
   hypocrisy (Luke 12:1): the doctrine that manufactures dead works.
4. **The proof text the book already carries**: "have we not… done many
   **wonderful works**? … I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work
   **lawlessness**" (Matt 7:22-23) — impressive religious works, done in
   His name, judged DEAD because lawless. Wonderful works ≠ good works.
5. **Isaiah 1:11-15**: sacrifices, incense, sabbaths, spread hands — God's
   own ordinances rendered "abomination" by hands "full of blood": even
   commanded forms die when severed from obedience. "Who hath required
   this at your hand?" — unrequired work is dead work.
6. **James 2:17-26**: "faith without works is **dead**" — the same
   life/death axis run the other way: Abraham's obedience (offering Isaac)
   is the living work; the absence of obedience leaves faith a corpse.
   Dead works and dead faith are the two failures of one equation:
   life = faith working obedience.

## The verdict

Confirmed, and sharper than the common gloss (which reads "dead works" as
"trying to earn salvation by keeping the law"): Scripture never applies
"dead works" to obedience. Dead works are lawless works — men's
commandments kept in place of God's (Mark 7:9), wonderful works done in
lawlessness (Matt 7:23), forms with bloody hands (Isa 1) — works whose
wages is death. Good works are the ordained ones: the commandments walked
in (Eph 2:10). Repentance from dead works (Heb 6:1) is therefore not
repentance from obedience — it is repentance from the counterfeit of it.

**Glossary candidate — Dead works (likely DIVERGENT: commonly taught as
law-keeping-to-earn-salvation; run the delta grading before adding):**
works that lead to death — the traditions of men and lawless wonders kept
in place of the commandments; opposite of serving the living God.
(Heb 9:14; Prov 14:12; Mark 7:7-9; Matt 7:22-23; Eph 2:10)
