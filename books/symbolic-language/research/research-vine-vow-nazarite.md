# Research: The vine vow — "not drink… until I drink it new" (2026-07-23)

Author-commissioned: the fruit-of-the-vine saying in the Hebrew Gospels,
its connection to the Nazarite vow (purpose, and why Jesus invokes it),
and the month of Av. All citations verified against kjv_strongs.txt and
data/hg-chapters/.

## The saying — Hebrew Gospels witness

- **HG Matt 26:29** — "I will not drink from now onward from the fruit
  of THIS vine (peri ha-gefen ha-zot) until that day when I drink it NEW
  (chadash) with you in the kingdom of heaven." HG note: "fruit of the
  vine" is the exact wording of the wine blessing (borei peri ha-gafen,
  m. Ber. 6:1) — at a Pesach table the cup is named by its blessing.
  (Ms A reads "kingdom of MY FATHER," assimilating to Greek.)
- **HG Mark 14:25** — "I shall not drink of the wine of the vine until I
  drink it new in the kingdom of MY FATHER (b'malkhut AVI)" — the Hebrew
  keeps the filial address where Greek Mark says "of God."
- **HG Luke 22:16** — "I will not eat it until the kingdom of YHWH is
  COMPLETED (nishlam)."
- **HG Luke 22:18** — "I will not drink again from WHAT COMES FORTH of
  the vine (me-ha-yotze min-ha-gefen) until the kingdom of YHWH comes."

## The formula is the Nazarite formula

HG Luke's "what comes forth of the vine" is verbatim the Nazarite
prohibition as the angel gives it for Samson: **"She may not eat of any
thing that COMETH (yatza, H3318) OF THE VINE"** (Judg 13:14). Num 6:3-4
fills the same range Jesus' idiom covers: no wine, no vinegar, no juice,
no grapes moist or dried, "nothing that is made of the vine tree, from
the kernels even to the husk." The saying is not a mood; it is a vow in
the vow's own legal wording, with a stated terminus: the kingdom.

## The vow's purpose (Num 6)

- "To SEPARATE themselves UNTO THE LORD" (6:2 — nazar H5144 / nazir
  H5139): voluntary, temporary, priest-grade consecration open to any
  Israelite, man or woman.
- Its three bans copy priestly service: no vine (as priests on duty,
  **Lev 10:9** — "do not drink wine… when ye go into the tabernacle of
  the congregation, lest ye die"), no corpse defilement even for father
  or mother (6:7, as the high priest, Lev 21:11), no razor.
- "The CONSECRATION (nezer, H5145) of his God is UPON HIS HEAD" (6:7) —
  nezer is the word for the CROWN (Ex 29:6 the holy crown; 2 Kgs 11:12
  crowning Joash). The Nazarite wears an invisible crown.
- **Completion protocol (6:13-20):** brought to the sanctuary door;
  burnt, sin, and peace offerings plus unleavened bread; the head shaved
  and the hair burned in the fire UNDER the peace offering; the wave
  offering — "and AFTER THAT the Nazarite may drink wine" (6:20).
  A vine-vow can only end at the sanctuary, through a completed
  sacrifice. Wine resumes when the offering is accepted.

## Why Jesus invokes it

1. **He is entering service.** From that night he goes on duty as priest
   and victim at once; a serving priest drinks no wine (Lev 10:9). The
   vow marks the supper as the beginning of the offering, not a farewell
   toast.
2. **The terminus is built into the vow's law.** The Nazarite drinks
   wine again only AFTER the completion sacrifice at the sanctuary. "I
   will not drink until the kingdom" = "my consecration ends when my
   offering is accepted and the feast is set." The kingdom feast (Isa
   25:6, wines on the lees well refined) is the completion ceremony.
3. **NEW (chadash) is covenant language.** He has just called the cup
   "the new covenant in my blood" (Luke 22:20); "drink it CHADASH" is
   the same word as the brit CHADASHAH of Jer 31:31 (H2319). The next
   cup he shares is the covenant cup renewed — new wine of a new
   covenant at the completion feast.
4. **The cross keeps the vow.** They offer the consecrated one the vine
   three times: wine mingled with myrrh — "he received it not" (Mark
   15:23); vinegar mingled with gall — "when he had TASTED thereof, he
   would not drink" (Matt 27:34; he tests and refuses); the soldiers'
   mocking vinegar (Luke 23:36). Amos 2:11-12 is the indictment they
   reenact: "I raised up… your young men for NAZARITES; but ye gave the
   Nazarites WINE to drink."
5. **"It is finished" is the vow's terminus word.** HG Luke 22:16 sets
   the terminus as "until the kingdom of YHWH is NISHLAM (completed)" —
   and "It is finished" (John 19:30) back-translates to the same root
   (nishlam/shalem). At "I thirst… that the scripture might be
   fulfilled" (Ps 69:21), he receives the vinegar and declares the
   completion in the vow's own word. OPEN QUESTION for the author:
   vinegar is Nazarite-banned (Num 6:3), so either (a) the received
   vinegar marks the terminus itself — the sacrifice complete at that
   instant, the sour dregs of the old order drained while the CHADASH
   cup still waits for the feast — or (b) John's "received" (labōn)
   stops short of drinking. The data supports (a); the "new" wine
   remains future either way.
6. **The crown.** The nezer of his God was upon his head (Num 6:7); men
   answered it with a crown of thorns (Matt 27:29) — the consecration
   crowned in bramble (see research-bramble-thorns.md).

## The month of Av

- **Scripture's own bridge — Zech 7:3 uses the Nazarite verb of the
  fifth-month fast:** "Should I weep in the FIFTH MONTH, SEPARATING
  myself (hinnazer, H5144), as I have done these so many years?" The
  temple burned in the fifth month (2 Kgs 25:8-9; Jer 52:12-13), and the
  mourning fast for it is called, in the text itself, a nazar-vow.
- **God's promised terminus for that fast is a feast:** "the fast of the
  fifth [month]… shall be to the house of Judah JOY and GLADNESS, and
  CHEERFUL FEASTS" (Zech 8:19) — when "I am returned unto Zion" (8:3).
  The structure is identical to Jesus' vow: a nazar-abstention over the
  ruined sanctuary that converts to feasting when the LORD returns and
  the kingdom stands. Jesus' vine-vow is the fifth-month hinnazer done
  perfectly: mourning-abstention until the true temple is rebuilt in
  three days and the fast becomes the wedding wine.
- **The scroll of Av praises the Nazarites:** Lam 4:7 — "her NAZARITES
  were purer than snow" — the consecrated ones remembered precisely in
  the fifth-month lament.
- **Wordplay (second dimension, flagged):** the month's post-exilic name
  Av is spelled aleph-bet — "father" — and HG Mark's vow ends "in the
  kingdom of my AV." Scripture only ever says "the fifth month," so this
  is wordplay on the later name, not derivation. Likewise the rabbinic
  Nine-Days custom (no wine or meat when Av enters) is post-biblical
  practice that happens to mirror the vow — corroboration of instinct,
  not evidence.
- **Tradition, flagged as such:** Ta'anit 29a dates the spies' weeping
  night (Num 14:1) to 9 Av. Scripture does not date it; but the internal
  logic stands scripturally on its own: Israel tasted the cluster of
  Eshcol — the fruit of the vine carried OUT of the land (Num 13:23-24)
  — and refused to enter; weeping was appointed. Jesus inverts it: he
  will NOT taste the fruit of the vine until he enters the kingdom WITH
  his brethren.

## Joseph, the nazir who drinks with his brothers again

Gen 49:26 / Deut 33:16 — the blessings rest "on the crown of the head of
him that was SEPARATE FROM HIS BRETHREN" — Hebrew: NEZIR echav (H5139),
Joseph the Nazarite-of-his-brothers. And the only time Scripture shows
Joseph drinking is the reunion table: when the brothers who sold him are
gathered, tested, and seated before him, "they DRANK, and were MERRY
WITH HIM" (Gen 43:34). The separated one drinks again — with them, at
his table, in his kingdom. Jesus quotes the pattern's terms exactly:
"until that day when I drink it new WITH YOU in my Father's kingdom."
The vow's point is not private asceticism but postponed communion — the
WITH YOU is the payload.

## Nezer joins hair and vine: the Nazarite vine of Lev 25 (2026-07-23)

Torah calls the unpruned vine a NAZIR: "thou shalt not gather the grapes
of THY VINE UNDRESSED (nezirekha, H5139)" (Lev 25:5, and again 25:11 for
the jubilee). Same word as the Nazarite man; same structure: growth left
uncut because the time is holy. Six years "thou shalt PRUNE (zamar) thy
vineyard" (25:3); the seventh, the vine goes nazir. So the vow's two
bans are one symbol twice over — the Nazarite abstains from the vine's
fruit while wearing the vine's uncut state on his head: a human
sabbath-year vine. Samson literalizes it; Delilah's shears are pruning
by a hand not the husbandman's.

The razor-holder taxonomy (research-hair.md) transfers to the vine
because Jesus transfers it: the night of the vine-vow he says "I am the
TRUE VINE, and my Father is the HUSBANDMAN: every branch that beareth
fruit, he purgeth [prunes] it" (John 15:1-2). Only the husbandman's
knife makes cutting fruitful, as only the sanctuary fire may take the
Nazarite's hair. Unpruned OUTSIDE holy time is not consecration but
abandonment (Isa 5:6 — "it shall not be pruned… briers and thorns").

Observation (flagged): the nazir-vine's sabbath fruit is not harvested
into barns but eaten IN COMMON (Lev 25:6-7 — owner, servant, stranger:
"the sabbath of the land shall be meat for you"); the vow likewise ends
in shared drinking — "new WITH YOU in my Father's kingdom."

Guard: netzer, the Branch (Isa 11:1; Nazarene, Matt 2:23), is a
DIFFERENT root (nun-tsade-resh; tsade, not zayin). The nazar/nezer/nazir
web stands without it; do not conflate.

## Verdict

The saying is a Nazarite consecration in the vow's own formula (HG Luke
= Judg 13:14), invoked because the completion law of the vow (sacrifice
at the sanctuary, then wine) IS the shape of his offering and return;
the fifth-month fast supplies the prophetic frame (Zech 7:3 hinnazer →
8:19 feast), and Joseph the nezir-echav supplies the narrative type.
Strong enough to draft; the vinegar/nishlam tension at John 19:30 is
the one open interpretive call, laid out above for the author.
