# Faith / faithfulness — emunah and pistis (2026-07-08)

Deepening the ch06 Faith section. Thesis (already the sym-faith DIVERGENT
entry): faith = FAITHFULNESS — trusting obedience sustained — not assent
and not a substitute for works. Targeted sweep, not a full concordance
pass; verify counts before print claims.

## The Hebrew: emunah (H530)

- **Hab 2:4 "the just shall live by his faith"** — emunah: faithfulness,
  steadfastness. The verse the NT quotes THREE times (Rom 1:17; Gal 3:11;
  Heb 10:38) as the gospel's spine — and its Hebrew word is the
  steadfastness word, not an assent word.
- **Ex 17:12 — the definition in a picture**: Moses' hands "were steady
  (emunah!) until the going down of the sun." Emunah is hands HELD UP —
  sustained, supported obedience under weight, not a feeling. (The one
  verse where the KJV shows the word's physical meaning.)
- **Deut 32:4** — "a God of truth (emunah)": His faithfulness, not His
  opinion of us. **2 Tim 2:13** answers it: "he abideth faithful."
- **Ps 119:86** — "all thy commandments are faithful (emunah)": the
  commandments themselves carry the word.

## The Greek: pistis / pistos

- **Rev 14:12** — "they that keep the commandments of God, and the faith
  OF Jesus": faith held the way He held it — His fidelity, kept.
- **pistos = faithful**: the "faithful and wise servant" (Matt 24:45),
  "faithful and true witness" (Rev 3:14), "be thou faithful unto death"
  (Rev 2:10). Same root as pistis: the noun of the servant-word.
- **Heb 11 — the catalog is verbs of obedience**: by faith Abel OFFERED,
  Noah PREPARED, Abraham WENT OUT and OFFERED, Moses FORSOOK — every
  entry an act; not one an opinion. Faith is named by what it did.
- **Jas 2:17-26** (already in the chapter): faith without works is dead;
  Abraham's faith "wrought with his works, and by works was faith made
  perfect (teleioō — brought to its goal)."

## The four offices — tested (author's thesis, 2026-07-08)

faith = obeying from trust alone; belief = obeying because the facts
compel; knowing = obeying as covenant/marriage obligation and intimacy;
love = covenant allegiance embodied in the keeping. CONFIRMED distinct: John 20:29 grades belief
(seen) vs faith (not seen, yet believed) in one sentence; Hos 2:20 joins
faithfulness and knowing as betrothal->marriage; Jer 22:16 defines knowing
as covenant duties done; 1 John 5:3 defines commandment-keeping as love's
embodied form; Prov 3:5 opposes trust to leaning on understanding
(faith != belief). "Collapse into one" was the wrong figure — one act,
four offices. Ch06 collapse paragraph rewritten accordingly.

**Faithful = allegiance kept consistent (author, 2026-07-08):** faithfulness is allegiance KEPT — Ex 17:12; Matt 24:46; Rev 2:10; 2 Tim 2:13.

## Belief correction (2026-07-12)

The flat equation "belief is obedience" does not survive every use. James 2:19
predicates real propositional belief of demons, Mark 1:27 says unclean spirits
obey Christ's direct commands, and John 12:42-43 says rulers believed on Him yet
would not confess Him because another love governed them. Scripture therefore
distinguishes factual recognition and compelled compliance from covenant
allegiance.

The defensible core is narrower and stronger: saving belief receives God's
testimony as governing truth in a response that submits to it. John 3:36,
Romans 10:16, and Hebrews 3:18-19 establish that persistent refusal to obey is
judged as unbelief; they do not require every lexical use of _believe_ to mean
the deed of obedience itself. James 2:21-22 likewise joins belief and works
without erasing their distinction: the work brings faith to its goal.

The four terms therefore converge in one covenant response but remain distinct:
love is covenant allegiance embodied in keeping, knowing is the covenant relation proved by keeping, faith
is trust held steady through obedience in hope, and belief receives the word as
governing truth and submits to it. "One act, four offices" was still too flat;
"one covenant response, four distinguishable offices" preserves both the union
and Scripture's own distinctions.

## Faith correction (2026-07-12)

The earlier formula "faith = obedience" likewise collapsed distinguishable
members. James 2:22 says faith works together with works and is brought to its
goal by them. Romans 4:4-5 contrasts a wage-claim that puts God in debt with
believing the One who justifies, while Ephesians 2:8-10 excludes works as the
source of salvation and immediately restores them as the created purpose of the
saved life.

The corrected definition is trusting faithfulness: reliance on God's unseen
promise held steady through obedience. Works neither purchase the promise nor
rename faith. They enact, mature, and make faith visible. This preserves the
Hebrew force of _emunah_ (steadiness/fidelity), the action catalog of Hebrews
11, James's distinction-within-union, and Paul's grace-before-works order.

Belief logically precedes faith without becoming a separate substance. Greek
_pisteuō_ (believe) and _pistis_ (faith) are one word-family. Ephesians 1:13
prints hearing before believing, and Romans 10:17 says faith comes by hearing.
The resulting sequence is: word heard -> belief receives it as governing truth
-> hope lays hold of the unseen promise -> faith holds that belief steady ->
works enact and mature it. Bare belief can stop before living faith; living
faith cannot exist without belief, and faith without works is dead.

### Work is not obedience (2026-07-12)

The two words can meet in one deed without becoming synonyms. A work is a deed;
obedience names the deed's submission to a command. Romans 4:4 uses the
laborer's work offered for wages and therefore creating debt. Luke 17:7-10 says
that even after a servant has done everything commanded, he is unprofitable and
has only done his duty. Complete obedience therefore cannot be the wage-work
Paul excludes.

James 2 uses _work_ differently: Abraham's offering makes his faith visible and
brings it to maturity. Romans asks whether labor purchases the promise; James
asks whether claimed faith ever acts. The first excludes a wage claim, while
the second requires the deed that demonstrates faithfulness.

## Love correction (2026-07-12)

The earlier formula "love is the keeping itself" overreached by turning an
inseparable relationship into a flat identity. First Corinthians 13:3 says a
man may distribute all his goods and surrender his body yet lack love. First
John 4:8-10 says God is love and manifests it by sending His Son; God is not
obeying a superior. Scripture therefore will not let every performed deed be
renamed love.

The definitional texts remain stronger than a fruit-only account. First John
5:3 and 2 John 6 say "this is love" and name walking after the commandments;
Romans 13:10 calls love the fulfilling of the law. The corrected definition is
self-giving covenant allegiance embodied, for those under God's authority, in
commandment-keeping. A deed severed from allegiance can be empty sacrifice,
while claimed allegiance that persistently refuses the commands is a false
profession. This preserves both Paul's distinction and John's definition.
