# Research: Shavuot — the plural Sevens as the Jubilee count

Question (author, 2026-07-16): is Shavuot — WEEKS plural, the Feast of
Weeks — a symbol of the Jubilee count? Not the singular week (the
glossary's existing scale-flexible unit) but the plural: the Sevens.
All claims tag-verified against kjv_strongs.txt / wlc.txt.

## The whole corpus (H7620 shabua — 17 verses, exhaustive)

**Singular — one seven:**
- Gen 29:27, 28 — "fulfil her WEEK" — the BRIDAL week, the
  marriage-covenant seven (Jacob completes Leah's shevua to receive
  Rachel).
- Dan 9:27 — "confirm the covenant with many for ONE WEEK… in the
  midst of the WEEK" — the covenant shavua, which Daniel Unsealed
  (ch. 36) reads as the marriage covenant: THE SAME WORD, SINGULAR, AS
  THE BRIDAL WEEK OF Gen 29:27. The two singulars answer each other —
  the word's own flagship use is a marriage seven.

**Dual:** Lev 12:5 — shevu'ayim, "two weeks" (a distinct form; not the
plural).

**Plural — the feast and its count (every free plural outside Daniel):**
- Ex 34:22 — "the feast of WEEKS, of the FIRSTFRUITS of wheat harvest."
- Num 28:26 — "the day of the firstfruits… after your WEEKS be out."
- Deut 16:9 (×2) — "Seven WEEKS shalt thou number… begin to number the
  seven WEEKS."
- Deut 16:10, 16 — "the feast of WEEKS."
- 2 Chr 8:13 — "the feast of WEEKS" (listed with sabbaths and new
  moons — the appointed cycles).
- Jer 5:24 — "he reserveth unto us the appointed WEEKS (shavuot
  chukkot — statute-weeks) of the harvest."
- Ezek 45:21 — anomaly, see counter-texts.

**Plural — Daniel:**
- Dan 9:24, 25 (×2), 26 — shavuim, BARE (no scale named).
- Dan 10:2, 3 — shavuim YAMIM — "sevens OF DAYS" (KJV hides yamim
  under "three FULL weeks / three WHOLE weeks"). When Daniel means
  day-sevens, he marks the scale — one chapter after leaving 9:24's
  sevens bare.

## The pattern

The plural Sevens is Scripture's name for the COUNTING-FEAST: seven
sevens numbered to a fiftieth. The count formula is written twice, at
two scales, in nearly the same words:
- **Lev 23:15-16** — "seven sabbaths shall be complete: even unto the
  morrow after the seventh sabbath shall ye number FIFTY DAYS" — the
  feast (days).
- **Lev 25:8, 10** — "seven sabbaths of YEARS… and ye shall hallow the
  FIFTIETH YEAR" — the jubilee (years).
Shavuot is the jubilee run in days — the Torah's own day-for-a-year
model, rehearsed every spring. The bare plural in prophecy therefore
carries the jubilee-shaped count, and Dan 10:2-3 proves the grammar
from Daniel's own mouth: bare sevens are NOT day-sevens by default;
day-sevens get yamim.

## The fiftieth carries the same cargo at both scales: liberty

- Lev 25:10 — "proclaim LIBERTY (deror) throughout all the land… it
  shall be a jubile."
- Acts 2 — the Spirit poured out ON the fiftieth day (Pentecost =
  Shavuot); 2 Cor 3:17 — "where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is
  LIBERTY."
- Luke 4:18-19 — "to preach deliverance to the captives… to preach the
  acceptable YEAR of the LORD" — Isa 61's jubilee proclamation in
  Messiah's mouth.
- Ex 34:22 adds the firstfruits thread: Shavuot is "the feast of
  weeks, of the FIRSTFRUITS" — the count runs FROM firstfruits (the
  resurrection day in this book's calendar) TO the liberty-day.

## Counter-texts weighed

- **Ezek 45:21** — "ye shall have the passover, a feast of seven days"
  where the Hebrew reads chag SHAVUOT (plural) of unleavened bread —
  the one plural not obviously the counting-feast. Either the plural
  here names "a feast of sevens" (the paired seven-day feasts), or the
  form is textually disputed; flagged honestly as the anomaly. It does
  not break the pattern: it attaches the plural to an appointed
  feast-cycle, never to ordinary calendar weeks.
- **Lev 12:5** — dual, not plural; a different form for "a pair of
  weeks."
- **Dan 10:2-3** — plural without the feast — but scale-marked with
  yamim, which CONFIRMS the rule rather than breaking it.

## Verdict

The plural Sevens (shavuim/shavuot) is not "weeks" in the calendar
sense anywhere in Scripture apart from an explicit scale-marker; it is
the name of the counted cycle — seven sevens crowned by a fiftieth of
liberty — the jubilee's own shape, which the Feast of Weeks rehearses
in days every year. Daniel's seventy Sevens inherit that office, and
his one singular shavua (9:27) inherits the singular's own flagship:
the bridal week of Gen 29:27. Structure grade, with the formula-pair
(Lev 23:15-16 / 25:8-10) as the derivation texts and Dan 10:2-3 as the
in-book grammar proof.

## Addendum: the singular corpus — four occurrences, two scenes (2026-07-16)

Exact count, WLC-verified: the singular shavua occurs FOUR times in all
of Scripture — Gen 29:27, 29:28 (Jacob's bridal week: "fulfil her WEEK…
and he gave him Rachel"), and Dan 9:27 twice (shavua echad; chatzi
ha-shavua). Two scenes: a wedding and this covenant. The bridal-week
custom is independently attested at Samson's wedding ("the seven days
of the feast," Judg 14:12) — written with the plain numeral (shiv'at),
which sharpens the lexical point: the custom did not require this word;
the text CHOSE it in exactly two scenes.

Counter-forms for ordinary week-counts: dual shevu'ayim ("two weeks,"
Lev 12:5 — consonantally identical to the plural שבעים; the dual is the
Masoretes' pointing); plural + scale-marker (shavuim yamim, Dan
10:2-3); feast-plural shavuot (Deut 16:9). Spelling note: Genesis
writes the singular defective (שבע — the bare skeleton of "seven"
itself); Daniel plene (שבוע).

The weld with ch. 36's Marriage Covenant section: Dan 9:27's covenant-
seven wears the wedding-seven's word, and the covenant runs the
wedding's stages already in the chapter (cup cut, Luke 22:20;
abstention, Luke 22:18; departure to prepare, John 14:2-3). "In the
midst of the week" = the bridegroom's week suspended between espousal
and supper; Gen 29:27's terms stand as promise — fulfil the week, and
the bride is given. Copy drafted at the bottom of the Dan 9:26 sample
(awaiting author approval with the table).

## Addendum 2: the skeleton census and the intensive plural (author, 2026-07-16)

- One word, TWO forms (author's frame): singular and plural. The plural
  carries Hebrew's intensive capacity — the plural of majesty (the
  Elohim-form): not many sevens but THE Seven at its fullest, the
  complete cycle. This is the grammatical warrant for hearing the bare
  plural as the jubilee-shape rather than a tally.
- Skeleton census (WLC-verified): consonantal שבעים = the number
  SEVENTY in ~90 verses (H7657); pointed as "weeks" (masc pl) ONLY in
  Dan 9:24-26 + 10:2-3; pointed as the dual "two weeks" once (Lev
  12:5). One skeleton, three pointings; the vowels decide and context
  chooses the vowels. Daniel's context is Jeremiah's seventy years —
  and Dan 9:24 opens with the skeleton twice: shavuim shivim, unpointed
  "seventy seventy" (ch. 36's existing claim, now census-grounded).
- The feast's plural is the FEMININE form (shavuot, skeleton שבעת/שבעות
  — its own letters); Daniel's masculine plural shares seventy's
  skeleton instead. The copy teaches: two forms, the intensive plural,
  the seventy-skeleton, then the four-occurrence singular.

## Addendum 3: author's ruling on scope (2026-07-16)

The singular/wedding-week material is a RED HERRING for the count
argument — it belongs, if anywhere, with Dan 9:27's Marriage Covenant
section (ch. 36), not with the 9:24-26 unit question. The argument to
carry: the Feast of WEEKS assigns the plural Sevens a symbolic meaning
that parallels the Jubilee — the unit of Daniel's count is the
feast-shaped seven-of-sevens (49 crowned by 50), so seventy Sevens =
seventy jubilees (with 70×70 = 4,900 standing beside it from the same
skeleton). Sample copy restructured accordingly: two forms → intensive
plural → the unit question (70×7 days / 70×7 years / 70×70 / 70
jubilees) → the feast defines the unit (Lev 23:15-16 ∥ 25:8-10) →
Daniel's own guards (10:2-3 sevens-of-days; 9:2 seventy years).

## Addendum 4: the three values and the word-order hint (author's synthesis, 2026-07-16)

One skeleton (שבעים), three values: (a) SYMBOLIC — the Feast-of-Weeks
template, 49+1, the jubilee-shape; (b) NUMERIC — seventy (~90
occurrences); (c) plain PLURAL — sevens/weeks. The disambiguating hint
is WORD ORDER, verified across the corpus:
- Numeral BEFORE the skeleton = plain counted weeks: Deut 16:9
  (shiv'ah shavuot, "SEVEN weeks shalt thou number"); Dan 10:2-3
  (sheloshah shavuim yamim, numeral first + scale named).
- Skeleton BEFORE the numeral (inverted) = the Sevens as counted noun:
  Dan 9:25 (shavuim shiv'ah… v'shavuim shishim u'shnayim — "Sevens
  seven… Sevens sixty-and-two"); Dan 9:26 (ha-shavuim shishim
  u'shnayim, with the written article).
- Skeleton DOUBLED = ambiguous by construction: Dan 9:24 (shavuim
  shivim) reads seventy Sevens / Sevens seventy / seventy seventy — it
  can carry one or ALL meanings at once (the book's multi-hearing
  doctrine), and the rest of Scripture arbitrates: Jeremiah's seventy
  years behind the question (Dan 9:2), the feast's jubilee-template
  within the word.
