# Research: How "the sign of Jonah" is actually taught

Substantiation for the prevalence table in ch. 5 (The Sign of Jonah), prompted
by the author's challenge to an unsupported 8% for the scene-by-scene reading
(2026-07-02). Method: survey the primary published teaching of each major
tradition (official catechesis, the dominant popular Q&A sources, classic
commentaries, patristic texts, and the literal-72-hour sects), quote them
verbatim, and estimate share of teaching volume. Estimates remain estimates —
the point is the shape, not the precision.

## Sources and verbatim positions

**1. GotQuestions.org — "What is the sign of Jonah?"** (the highest-traffic
evangelical Q&A source; proxy for popular evangelical teaching)
> The sign of Jonah is "a typological metaphor for His future crucifixion,
> burial, and resurrection."
Timing only, plus the Nineveh repentance/judgment comparison. Explicitly
waves off literal counting: "attempts to place Jesus' death on Wednesday to
accommodate a literal 72-hour period are probably unnecessary." No parallels
beyond the three days (no Judas, lots, fare, sailors, storm, crown).
https://www.gotquestions.org/sign-of-Jonah.html

**2. Catechism of the Catholic Church §994** (official Catholic teaching)
> "He speaks of this unique event as the 'sign of Jonah,' the sign of the
> temple: he announces that he will be put to death but rise thereafter on
> the third day."
Timing/resurrection only.
https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P2H.HTM

**3. Life, Hope & Truth (Church of God / Armstrong tradition)** — proxy for
the literal-72-hours school (also some Baptist/Adventist-adjacent teachers)
> Jesus "would be buried for 'three days and three nights'" — "exactly 72
> hours"; "Christ was entombed on a Wednesday evening and resurrected on a
> Saturday evening."
Timing only, argued to the hour; "focuses almost exclusively on the temporal
alignment."
https://lifehopeandtruth.com/life/plan-of-salvation/holy-days-vs-holidays/sign-of-jonah/

**4. Matthew Henry, Commentary on Matthew 12** (the classic Protestant
commentary, still the default in many pulpits)
> "As Jonas was three days and three nights in the whale's belly, and then
> came out again safe and well, thus Christ shall be so long in the grave,
> and then shall rise again." … "The grave was to Christ as the belly of the
> fish was to Jonah; thither he was thrown, as a Ransom for lives ready to
> be lost in a storm."
Timing + belly=grave + ransom-in-the-storm + the no-corruption hope (Acts
2:26-27). Partial typology: 3–4 parallels, no scene-by-scene.
https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/mhc/Mat/Mat_012.cfm

**5. Augustine, Letter 102 (to Deogratias), §34** — the deepest historical
typology found:
> "As Jonah passed from the ship to the belly of the whale, so Christ passed
> from the cross to the sepulchre, or into the abyss of death. And as Jonah
> suffered this for the sake of those who were endangered by the storm, so
> Christ suffered for the sake of those who are tossed on the waves of this
> world."
And the gentile turn:
> "the preaching of Jonah did not come to them until after the whale had
> vomited him forth, so prophetic teaching … did not actually come to the
> Gentiles until after the resurrection of Christ from the grave."
Four mappings: ship→cross, ransom-for-the-endangered, three days, and the
turn to the nations. THE SEED OF THE CHAPTER'S READING — but still four
scenes, not the full frame-by-frame. NOTE (author, 2026-07-02): Augustine's
ship→cross frame is also misaligned — read scene by scene, the ship carries
the night of the betrayal (the sleep, the waking, the questioning, the
sailors' lots = Gethsemane through the trial), and the cross appears further
down: the suph (reed-wreath) bound about the prophet's head in the deep
(Jonah 2:5) answers the crown of thorns. The chapter states this correction
explicitly.
https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1102102.htm

**6. Catholic Exchange, "9 Ways Jonah Prefigured Jesus"** — the most
typology-forward popular article found (compiling Augustine and Cyril of
Jerusalem). Its nine parallels include the sleeping-in-the-storm scene and
the gentile mission — but NONE of: the fare paid / Judas's silver, the
sailors' lots / the soldiers' lots, the innocent-blood plea / Pilate, the
suph wreath / crown of thorns, the sailors' fear / the centurion.
https://catholicexchange.com/9-ways-jonah-prefigured-jesus/

**7. Negative search** — targeted searches for the distinctive scene-pairs
("paid the fare" + Judas; sailors' lots + soldiers' garments; etc.) return
the pieces separately but no published source assembling them as typology.

## Analysis → estimates

- **Timing-only** (death/burial/resurrection, incl. the Friday-vs-Wednesday
  counting industry): the primary statement of every tradition surveyed —
  evangelical popular, official Catholic, classic Protestant commentary,
  and the whole literal-72-hour school. **≈55%**
- **Call to repentance** (Nineveh repented; Luke's emphasis; common sermon
  angle; also the note "greater than Jonah is here"): **≈22%**
- **Rebuke of sign-seeking, nothing more** (homiletic angle: "an evil and
  adulterous generation"): **≈12%**
- **Partial typology** (2–9 parallels in the patristic/devotional strand:
  Augustine, Cyril, Matthew Henry's ransom-in-the-storm, listicles): a real
  but minority segment: **≈10%**
- **Full scene-by-scene prophecy of the Passion and the gentile turn**: not
  found in any published source surveyed; even the most typology-forward
  pieces lack all five distinctive scene-pairs. Allowing for obscure sermons
  and blogs that escaped the search: **<1%** (the earlier 8% was wrong).

## Conclusion applied to the book

Table updated to five rows with the figures above, and the chapter now notes
that the full pattern is all but untaught today while quoting Augustine's
Letter 102 as its ancient seed — the reading is a recovery, not a novelty.
