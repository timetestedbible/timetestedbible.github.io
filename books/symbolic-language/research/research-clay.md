# Every "clay" verse in Scripture — what the clay is (2026-07-07)

Working reference for a possible Clay chapter or appendix. Coverage: every KJV verse
containing the clay-family words, gathered by Strong's number and verified against
kjv_strongs.txt and wlc.txt — Hebrew *chomer* H2563 (clay, mortar), *tit* H2916 (mire,
clay), *cheres* H2789 (earthenware, potsherd), *yotser* H3335 (potter — the participle of
*yatsar*, "to form"), Aramaic *chasaph* H2635 with *tina* H2917 (Daniel 2), *melet* H4423,
*yaven* H3121 (miry), *refesh* H7516, *levenah* H3843 / *laban* H3835 (brick), *chemar*
H2564 (slime/bitumen); Greek *pēlos* G4081 (clay), *kerameus* G2763 (potter), *keramikos*
G2764, *ostrakinos* G3749 (earthen). Full occurrence lists in the appendix — nothing
dropped silently. The *homer* measure-uses of H2563 are excluded (listed at the end).
**✦ = verse already quoted in a published chapter** (noted which).

Vocabulary note: the symbol matches by structure, not spelling. "Clay," "mire,"
"potsherd," "earthen vessel," "potter," and "brick" are one material at different moments
of one process — dug, trodden, formed, fired, filled, broken. The words divide by moment,
not by meaning.

---

## The finding

**Clay is man as material in the Maker's hand.** Scripture says so outright, repeatedly,
in both Testaments, of the individual and of the nation: "as the clay is in the potter's
hand, **so are ye in mine hand**, O house of Israel" (Jer 18:6); "we are the clay, and
thou our potter" (Isa 64:8); "I also am formed out of the clay" (Job 33:6); and Daniel's
interpreting angel defines the clay of the image as **"the seed of men"** (Dan 2:43).

The one material passes through moments, and each moment is its own register:

1. **Mire of the streets** (*tit*) — humanity as trodden mass, men underfoot, not yet
   (or no longer) in any forming hand.
2. **Clay in the potter's hand** (*chomer b'yad ha-yotser*) — a man or a people being
   formed. **While the clay is soft, a marred vessel can be made again** (Jer 18:4).
3. **The fired vessel** (*kli yotser*, earthen vessel) — the finished man or nation:
   fixed in form, holding whatever is put in it, unto honour or unto dishonour. **Once
   fired, a marred vessel cannot be reworked — only broken** (Jer 19:11, "cannot be made
   whole again").
4. **The potsherd** (*cheres*) — the judged remainder of a broken vessel.
5. **Brick** (*levenah*) — the counterfeit: clay that *men* form and *men* fire, made
   uniform for men's own towers (Babel, Egypt) — man manufacturing man into building
   material, in place of stone.

The Potter is the Maker. Where the clay talks back to the hand that forms it (Isa 29:16;
45:9; Rom 9:20), or men form gods out of the stuff God formed men from, the world is
"turned upside down" — the clay playing potter.

This is the same humanity the Sea chapter shows as a churning mass — considered one lump
at a time. The two symbols meet in a verse the book already carries: the wicked sea
"cast up mire and dirt" (Isa 57:20) — where the tumult of the peoples breaks against the
land, what it throws up is *tit*, trodden humanity.

## Tier 1 — Scripture states the equation itself

The definition verses; everything else hangs on them.

- **Genesis 2:7** — "the LORD God **formed** (*yatsar* — the potter's verb) man of the
  dust of the ground." Man is the Potter's first vessel; the breath is what fills it.
  Every later potter-text is commentary on this verse.
- **Jeremiah 18:6** — "O house of Israel, cannot I do with you as this potter?...
  Behold, **as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand**." The equation
  stated as flatly as language allows.
- **Isaiah 64:8** — "thou art our father; **we are the clay, and thou our potter**; and
  we all are the work of thy hand." Father and potter in one breath — forming *is*
  fathering.
- **Daniel 2:43** ✦ (ch. 35 Mountain carries Dan 2) — "whereas thou sawest iron mixed
  with miry clay, **they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men**: but they shall
  not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay." The interpretation
  names the clay: *the seed of men*. GEM — the angel does the glossary's work.
- **Job 33:6** — "I also am formed out of the clay." The Hebrew is stronger: *me-chomer
  qoratsti* — "**from the clay I was nipped**" (WLC), the potter's pinch that takes one
  vessel's worth off the lump. One lump, portions nipped from it — Paul's "same lump"
  (Rom 9:21) is already in Elihu's mouth. GEM.
- **Job 10:9** — "thou hast made me as the clay; and wilt thou bring me into dust
  again?" Clay unmade returns to dust — the two ends of Gen 2:7 and 3:19.
- **Job 4:19** — "them that dwell in **houses of clay**, whose foundation is in the
  dust." The body is the clay house of the dweller (echoed at 2 Cor 5:1, the "earthly
  house of this tabernacle").
- **Job 13:12** — KJV "your bodies to bodies of clay"; YLT is literal: "for **high
  places of clay** your heights" — men's proud defenses are clay mounds.
- **Lamentations 4:2** — "The precious sons of Zion, comparable to fine gold, how are
  they esteemed as **earthen pitchers, the work of the hands of the potter**!" Sons =
  pitchers, stated.
- **Romans 9:20–21** — "Shall the thing formed say to him that formed it, Why hast thou
  made me thus? Hath not the **potter power over the clay, of the same lump** to make one
  vessel unto honour, and another unto dishonour?" Paul is quoting Isa 29:16 and 45:9 and
  applying them to persons and peoples.
- **Isaiah 29:16** — "Surely your turning of things upside down shall be esteemed as the
  potter's clay: for shall the work say of him that made it, He made me not?" The
  upside-down world defined: **the clay denying its Potter**.
- **Isaiah 45:9** — "Woe unto him that striveth with his Maker! **Let the potsherd
  strive with the potsherds of the earth.** Shall the clay say to him that fashioneth it,
  What makest thou?" Sherd may quarrel with sherd — like with like; the woe is the clay
  quarrelling upward. GEM — and "potsherds of the earth" (*charsei adamah*) names men as
  sherds *of the ground* they were dug from.
- **Jeremiah 19:11** — "Even so will I **break this people and this city, as one
  breaketh a potter's vessel, that cannot be made whole again**." People = vessel,
  stated at the breaking.
- **Psalm 2:8–9 with Revelation 2:26–27** — "I shall give thee **the heathen** for thine
  inheritance... thou shalt dash them in pieces like a **potter's vessel**"; and the Son
  hands the same rod to the overcomer: "power over **the nations**... as the vessels of a
  potter shall they be broken." Scripture interprets Scripture: potter's vessels =
  nations. GEM.
- **Isaiah 30:13–14** — Judah's iniquity falls "as the breaking of the potters' vessel...
  so that there shall not be found in the bursting of it **a sherd to take fire from the
  hearth, or to take water withal out of the pit**." The broken nation keeps no fragment
  able to carry fire or water — a vessel exists to carry; broken, it carries nothing.
- **2 Corinthians 4:7** — "we have this treasure in **earthen vessels**, that the
  excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us."
- **2 Timothy 2:20–21** — "in a great house... vessels of gold and of silver, but also of
  wood and **of earth**; and some to honour, and some to dishonour. If a man therefore
  **purge himself** from these, he shall be a vessel unto honour... meet for the master's
  use." The honour/dishonour of Rom 9:21, with the man himself answering for which.

## Tier 2 — The symbol at work

### The potter's house: soft clay and fired clay (the repentance window)

Jeremiah 18 and 19 are one deliberate pair — the same potter, the same people, before and
after hardening. GEM of the whole study.

- **Jeremiah 18:2–4** — at the potter's house "the vessel that he made of clay was
  **marred in the hand** of the potter: so **he made it again another vessel**." Wet
  clay: marred is not final.
- **Jeremiah 18:6–10** — the application is explicitly conditional: "at what instant"
  the nation turns, the Potter reworks his intent. Soft clay = a people still able to
  repent and be re-formed.
- **Jeremiah 19:1, 10–11** — "Go and get a **potter's earthen bottle**" — fired,
  finished — "and break the bottle... Even so will I break this people... **that cannot
  be made whole again**." Fired clay: the form is fixed; what remains for a marred vessel
  is breaking.
- **Jeremiah 19:2** — the sign is performed "by the entry of the east gate" — KJV
  flattens the name; the Hebrew is *sha'ar ha-charsit* (H2777), **the Gate of Potsherds**,
  over the valley of Hinnom. The bottle is broken at the potsherd gate, where broken
  vessels are thrown. (Hold this gate — Matthew 27 returns to this valley below.)

### Formed from the clay — the hand and the seal

- **Genesis 2:7** — see Tier 1. *Adam* from *adamah*: the man named after the ground he
  was nipped from.
- **Job 38:14** — the earth at daybreak "is turned as **clay to the seal**; and they
  stand as a garment" — form is pressed into clay from outside, as a signet into sealing
  clay; the formless takes the owner's image at his touch, and the wicked are shaken out
  of it (vv. 13, 15).
- **Job 30:19** — "He hath cast me into the mire, and I am become like dust and ashes" —
  the reverse motion: out of the hand, back toward the trodden state and the dust.
- **Isaiah 41:25** — the raised-up conqueror "shall come upon **princes as upon morter**
  (*chomer*), and **as the potter treadeth clay** (*tit*)." Rulers of nations = clay under
  treading feet — both clay-words and the potter in one verse. GEM.

### The mire of the streets — the trodden mass

*Tit* is clay in the street: humanity underfoot, out of every forming hand.

- **Isaiah 10:6** — Assyria sent "to tread them down **like the mire of the streets**" —
  a people given up to treading.
- **Micah 7:10** — "now shall she be **trodden down as the mire of the streets**" — the
  enemy nation in her turn.
- **2 Samuel 22:43 / Psalm 18:42** — "I did stamp them as the mire of the street" —
  David of his enemies.
- **Zechariah 10:5** — the mighty of Judah "tread down their enemies **in the mire of the
  streets** in the battle."
- **Isaiah 57:20** ✦ (quoted in chs. 25 and 36) — "the wicked are like the troubled sea,
  when it cannot rest, whose waters **cast up mire and dirt**" (*refesh va-tit*). Where
  the sea of peoples breaks against land, what it throws up is trodden humanity. The
  sea-symbol and the clay-symbol are the same crowd in two states.
- **Jude 1:13** ✦ (ch. 25 quotes the Hebrew) — ungodly men are "raging waves of the sea,
  foaming out their own shame." The Hebrew NT reads *refesh va-tit* — and *refesh* occurs
  exactly **once** in the whole Hebrew Bible, at Isaiah 57:20. Hebrew Jude is quoting
  Isaiah's unique word-pair verbatim: what the wicked wave casts out is mire. GEM.
- **Zechariah 9:3** — Tyre "heaped up silver as the dust, and fine gold **as the mire of
  the streets**" — the abundance register: mire is what is most plentiful and least
  valued underfoot; cf. **Job 27:16**, raiment prepared "as the clay."
- **Job 41:30** — under leviathan "sharp potsherds" (*cheres* — KJV "sharp stones"
  flattens it); "he spreadeth sharp pointed things **upon the mire**." The dragon beds on
  broken vessels and drags his sledge-marks across the trodden mass — and the next verse
  has him making "the sea boil like a pot" (41:31, already in the Sea study). The tyrant
  at ease on broken men. GEM.
- **Jeremiah 38:6** — Jeremiah let down into the dungeon-pit: "in the dungeon there was
  no water, but mire: so Jeremiah **sunk in the mire**." The prophet swallowed by the
  trodden mass of the city that would not hear — held for the Messiah pattern below.

### Iron and clay — strength mingled with the seed of men

- **Daniel 2:33–35, 41–43, 45** ✦ (ch. 35 Mountain; glossary sym-image) — feet and toes
  "part of **potters' clay**, and part of iron." The angel interprets every term: iron =
  the strength of the kingdom that "breaketh in pieces" (2:40); clay = "the seed of men"
  (2:43). Rulership alloyed with the mass of mankind: "partly strong, and partly
  broken" — **brittle** (2:42), because "iron is not mixed with clay" — the ruling metal
  can crush the clay or stand on it, but cannot cleave to it.
- Against the whole image stands "the **stone cut out without hands**" (2:34, 45) — not
  clay shaped by hands into brick, not metal cast by men: the kingdom formed by no human
  hand, which grinds the man-made colossus to chaff.

### Brick — clay in man's hand

Brick is the counterfeit stone: clay that men dig, men form, men fire — man manufacturing
man into interchangeable units for his own tower.

- **Genesis 11:3** — Babel: "let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had
  **brick for stone**, and slime had they for morter." The first empire substitutes
  man-made stone for stone — the whole Babel project in one clause. GEM.
- **Exodus 1:14** — Egypt makes Israel's lives "bitter with hard bondage, **in morter
  (chomer), and in brick**" — the empire sets the formed-by-God people to forming clay
  for Pharaoh's store-cities; **Exodus 5:7–19** — the tale of bricks, straw withheld: the
  bondage measured in units of fired clay per day.
- **Isaiah 9:10** — "The **bricks** are fallen down, but we will build with hewn
  stones" — Ephraim's pride: when God knocks the man-made down, man vows to rebuild in
  costlier man-made.
- **Isaiah 65:3** — a people "that burneth incense upon **altars of brick**" — worship on
  man-fired clay, against the altar law of unhewn stone (Ex 20:25: tool-touched stone is
  polluted). Man-made worship on man-made material.
- **Ezekiel 4:1** — "take thee a **tile** (*levenah*, a brick), and pourtray upon it the
  city, even Jerusalem" — the city drawn on fired clay for its siege-sign; the city had
  become brickwork.
- **Nahum 3:14** — doomed Nineveh mocked: "go into clay (*tit*), and tread the morter
  (*chomer*), make strong the **brickkiln**" — shape more clay, empire; the fire comes
  anyway (v. 15).
- **Jeremiah 43:9–10** — stones hidden "in the clay in the **brickkiln**, which is at the
  entry of Pharaoh's house," and Nebuchadnezzar's throne set "upon these stones": stone
  planted under a throne *inside* Egypt's own clay-works — stone over clay, again. GEM.

### Earthen vessels under the law

Torah legislates the symbol before the prophets preach it. The rule that divides clay
from metal: **metal can be scoured; fired clay absorbs, so it must be broken.**

- **Leviticus 6:28** — the sin offering: "the **earthen vessel** wherein it is sodden
  **shall be broken**: and if it be sodden in a brasen pot, it shall be both scoured, and
  rinsed." What sin-flesh is boiled in, if clay, is broken.
- **Leviticus 11:33; 15:12** — uncleanness entering an earthen vessel: "it shall be
  broken" — no purging a clay vessel of what has soaked in.
- **Jeremiah 19:11** applies exactly this law to the nation: the fired bottle that took
  the uncleanness in "cannot be made whole again." The prophets' breaking-texts are the
  Leviticus rule enacted on peoples.
- **Numbers 5:17** — the jealousy trial: "holy water in an **earthen vessel**; and of the
  **dust** that is in the floor of the tabernacle" — dust and water in a clay vessel, the
  very recipe of man, with the Name written and blotted into it, given to the accused to
  drink: the faithless body (the clay house) is unmade by it; the faithful woman "shall
  be free, and shall conceive seed" (5:28). CANDIDATE reading, but every element is in
  the text.
- **Leviticus 14:5, 50** — cleansing the leper and the leprous house: "one of the birds
  be killed **in an earthen vessel over running water**" — the death happens *in* a clay
  vessel over living water; the living bird, dipped in that blood, goes free. Held for
  the Messiah pattern below.
- **Jeremiah 32:14** — the deed of the redeemed field: "put them in an **earthen vessel,
  that they may continue many days**." The evidence of purchase — sealed and open — kept
  safe in clay until the redemption is claimed. Set beside **2 Corinthians 4:7**: "we
  have this treasure in earthen vessels." The purchase-deed stored in clay is the
  gospel's own figure. GEM.

### The Messiah in the clay

- **Psalm 40:2** — "He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the **miry
  clay** (*tit ha-yaven*), and **set my feet upon a rock**" — the psalm of "Lo, I come"
  (40:7, quoted of Messiah at Heb 10:7). One man drawn out of the trodden mass and set on
  rock — clay to rock, the material changed by the hand that lifts.
- **Psalm 69:2, 14** — "I **sink in deep mire**, where there is no standing... Deliver me
  out of the mire, and let me not sink" — the great psalm of the cross (69:9, 21 quoted
  in the Gospels). The Messiah goes down into the mire — into trodden humanity — and is
  brought up out of it. **Jeremiah 38:6**, the prophet sunk in the pit-mire of the doomed
  city, walks this pattern first.
- **Psalm 22:15** — "My strength is **dried up like a potsherd**... thou hast brought me
  into the dust of death." In the psalm that opens "My God, my God, why hast thou
  forsaken me," he becomes the sherd — the broken earthen vessel of the sin-offering law
  (Lev 6:28), the death in the clay vessel of the leper's cleansing (Lev 14:5) — and out
  of it the living bird goes free.
- **John 9:6, 11, 14–15** — "he spat on the ground, and **made clay** of the spittle, and
  he anointed the eyes of the blind man with the clay" — the Potter's act of Genesis 2:7
  repeated on a man **blind from birth** (9:1): not healing what broke, but forming what
  was never formed — "that the works of God should be made manifest in him" (9:3). Then
  "wash in the pool of Siloam, **which is by interpretation, Sent**" (9:7): the formed
  clay opens only when washed in the Sent. The Hebrew Gospel reads "clay was formed with
  that spittle" — the forming verb again. GEM.
- **Zechariah 11:13 with Matthew 27:7–10** — "Cast it unto the potter: a goodly price
  that I was prised at of them. And I took the thirty pieces of silver, and **cast them
  to the potter in the house of the LORD**." Matthew: they "bought with them the
  **potter's field, to bury strangers in**." The Maker's price, thirty silver, ends in
  the potter's ground — the field of dug clay and discarded sherds in the valley of
  Hinnom, where Jeremiah broke the bottle at the Potsherd Gate (Jer 19:2) and Tophet
  became "the place to bury" (19:11). The blood-price of the Messiah purchases the
  potter's own ground — and *strangers* are buried in it: the price paid by Israel's
  rulers buys resting ground for the nations. The Hebrew Matthew reads "the field of the
  man, **a potter of clay** (*yotser cheres*)... for the strangers" — and names the
  prophecy's source **Zechariah** (27:9), where the Greek text says Jeremiah. GEM chain.
- **Judges 7:16–20** — Gideon's three hundred: "empty pitchers, and **lamps within the
  pitchers**... they **brake the pitchers** that were in their hands" — and the light
  shone, and the sword of the LORD won without a blow struck. The treasure-in-earthen-
  vessels of 2 Cor 4:7, enacted: the clay must break for the light inside to be seen.
  (Word-flag: *kad*, the common clay jar — not *cheres*; the match is structural.)

### Counterfeits and inversions

- **Proverbs 26:23** — "Burning lips and a wicked heart are like **a potsherd covered
  with silver dross**" — the glazed sherd: broken clay dressed to shine. The counterfeit
  vessel, one verse.
- **Isaiah 29:16** — the clay denying its Maker: "your turning of things upside down."
- **Hebrew Revelation 9:19 (≈ Rev 9:20)** — the unrepentant worship idols "made **of
  clay** and of stone or of wood" — men forming gods from the stuff God formed men from:
  the full inversion of Genesis 2:7.

## Tier 3 — Literal uses that still carry the emblem

- **1 Kings 7:46 / 2 Chronicles 4:17** — Solomon's temple vessels cast "in the **clay
  ground** between Succoth and Zarthan": even the house of God had its brass formed in
  clay molds in the Jordan plain — form given through clay, then the clay broken away.
- **1 Chronicles 4:23** — "These were the **potters**... there they **dwelt with the king
  for his work**." A genealogy line that reads like the whole symbol's motto.
- **Job 2:8** — "he took him a potsherd to scrape himself withal" — Job, the man being
  re-formed, scrapes his boils with broken vessel.
- **Ezekiel 23:34** — Aholibah drains the cup and "shalt break the **sherds** thereof" —
  judgment drunk to the broken clay at the bottom.
- **Ecclesiastes 12:6–7** — death: "or the **pitcher be broken at the fountain**... Then
  shall the **dust return to the earth as it was**: and the spirit shall return unto God
  who gave it." The verse pair interprets itself: broken pitcher = the clay body giving
  back its dust; the breath that filled the vessel returns to the One who breathed it
  (Gen 2:7 run backward). GEM. (Word-flag: *kad*, Gideon's word.)

- **Jeremiah 22:28** — Coniah "a despised broken idol... **a vessel wherein is no

- **Jeremiah 48:11–12, 38** — Moab "settled on his lees... emptied from vessel to

- **2 Corinthians 5:1** — "our **earthly house** of this tabernacle" — Job 4:19's house
  of clay in Greek dress.

## Wordplay and consonantal notes (the second dimension)

- The consonants **חמר** carry one picture across their whole family: *to churn, ferment,
  foam, redden*. The verb: "though the waters thereof **roar** (*yechemaru*)" (Ps 46:3);
  wine that "is red (*chamar*)... he poureth out of the same" (Ps 75:8); "my bowels are
  **troubled** (*chomarmeru*)" (Lam 1:20; 2:11). *Chomer* the clay, *chemer* the
  fermenting wine, *chemar* the bubbling bitumen, *chomer* the heap — all the churning
  mass. Clay is churned earth-and-water; man is *chomer*: nipped from the churning mass.
  **Habakkuk 3:15** joins the two symbols in one word: "thou didst walk through the sea,
  through the **heap** (*chomer*) of great waters" — the sea of peoples as one churned
  clay-mass under his horses. GEM.
- **Judges 15:16** — the text itself puns the family: *ba-chamor chamor chamoratayim* —
  "with the jawbone of an **ass**, **heaps** upon heaps."
- **Exodus 8:14** — the frogs "gathered together upon **heaps** (*chomarim chomarim*)":
  Egypt's plague piled up clay-wise.
- **Exodus 2:3** — the ark of Moses "daubed with **slime** (*chemar*) and with pitch":
  the same *chemar* that mortared Babel (Gen 11:3) and filled the pits of Siddim (Gen
  14:10) — the deliverer floats through the waters sealed in the empire-builders' own
  paste.
- **Habakkuk 2:6** — KJV "ladeth himself with **thick clay**" is *avtit* (H5671),
  "pledges" (so YLT: "heavy pledges") — not an occurrence of *tit*, but the prophet's pun
  on it: the plunderer's debt-load reads as a load of mire. Flag, don't cite as clay.
- **Jeremiah 19:2** — KJV "east gate" is *sha'ar ha-charsit*: the **Potsherd Gate**.
- **Aramaic** (Dan 2): *chasaph* = the fired clay, *tina* = the mire (cognate of *tit*),
  *pechar* = the potter. "Miry clay" (2:41) is fired-clay-of-mire — brittleness made of
  what was trodden.
- The **homer** measure (H2563, same consonants — a donkey-load, a heap) is excluded
  from the study: Lev 27:16; Num 11:32; Isa 5:10; Ezek 45:11–14; Hos 3:2. One CANDIDATE
  note: Hosea buys back his adulterous wife for silver and "an homer of barley, and an
  half homer" (Hos 3:2) — the redemption price weighed partly in *chomer*.

## The Hebrew-NT second witness (bibles/hg.txt, data/hebrew-gospels-interlinear.json)

- **Revelation 2:26–27** — "I will give nations as his inheritance. And he shall lead
  them with a rod of iron, and **like a potter's vessel** (*kli yotser*) you shall
  shatter them" — Psalm 2:8–9's exact Hebrew vocabulary, confirming the potter's-vessel
  = nations equation is native to the promise.
- **Jude 1:13** — the waves "cast outward *refesh va-tit*" — Isaiah 57:20's unique pair,
  verbatim (see Tier 2).
- **Matthew 27:7, 9** — "the field of the man, a **potter of clay** (*yotser cheres*)...
  that the strangers [be buried] there"; the prophecy attributed to **Zechariah** by
  name.
- **John 9:6** — "clay **was formed** with that spittle" — the *yatsar* register on the
  Lord's act.
- **Revelation 9:19** — idols "made of **clay** (*cheres*) and of stone" among the
  unrepentant's works.
- Outside the canon, for reference only: Sirach 33:13 spells the symbol out ("As the clay
  is in the potter's hand... so man is in the hand of him that made him") — a witness to
  how plainly Second-Temple readers took the equation.

## Meaning (glossary draft)

**Clay**: man as material in the Maker's hand — the same humanity the Sea shows as a
churning mass, taken one lump at a time. In the street it is the **mire**, men trodden
underfoot; in the potter's hand it is a person or people **still soft enough to be made
again** (Jer 18:4); fired, it is the finished **vessel** unto honour or dishonour, which
holds what is put into it and, once marred, can only be **broken** (Jer 19:11) — the
**sherd** is the judged remainder. Its counterfeit is **brick**: clay men form and fire
for their own towers — Babel's stone-substitute, Egypt's bondage-work — man manufacturing
man, against the stone cut without hands.

## Where this touches the book

- **Ch. 35 Mountain** ✦ — Dan 2 iron-and-clay feet and the stone cut without hands are
  already quoted; this study supplies the angel's own definition of the clay (2:43, seed
  of men) and the brick/stone polarity behind "without hands."
- **Chs. 25 & 36 (Sun/Moon/Stars; Sea)** ✦ — Isa 57:20 and Hebrew Jude's "mire and mud"
  already appear; the clay study gives that mire its home register.
- **Glossary** — sym-image (metals + clay) exists; a sym-clay entry can be added from the
  draft above if a chapter or appendix is written.
- Candidate placement: an appendix alongside Butter/The Bow pattern — the Jer 18/19
  soft-vs-fired pair, the Lev 6:28 broken-vessel law, and the Messiah-in-the-clay chain
  (Ps 40 → Ps 69 → Ps 22:15 → John 9 → Matt 27 potter's field) carry a full arc.
