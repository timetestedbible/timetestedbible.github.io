# Research: Oil in Psalms and Proverbs

Focused sweep of every occurrence of H8081 (*shemen*) in Psalms and Proverbs,
commissioned after the skeptical Oil experiment. The purpose is to derive lamp
oil from the Wisdom books rather than require a direct dictionary equation.

## Candidate definition

**Lamp oil is Spirit-taught truth and correction received, kept, and worked
into proven obedience. It is the tested, pressed fruit of the Spirit stored
inwardly; light is that obedience made visible as an example to others.**

This definition distinguishes source, inward store, and outward result:

1. The Spirit teaches truth and causes obedience (John 16:13; Ezekiel 36:27).
2. Oil is correction/counsel received and stored by the wise (Psalm 141:5;
   Proverbs 21:20; 27:5-9).
3. The lamp is the commandment (Proverbs 6:23).
4. Light is obedience visible as good works (Matthew 5:16).

## Identity-grade Wisdom chain

### Reproof is oil

Psalm 141:5 is clearest in the ASV: "Let the righteous smite me, it shall be a
kindness; and let him reprove me, it shall be as oil upon the head; let not my
head refuse it." The psalm's context moves through mouth, lips, wicked works,
dainties, reproof, and words. The Greek Old Testament preserves a variant that
contrasts righteous chastening with "the oil of the sinner" upon the head;
either textual form places oil in the field of counsel and correction.

### A friend's counsel is oil

Proverbs 27:5-9 moves in one sequence from open rebuke, to the faithful wounds
of a friend, to oil and perfume rejoicing the heart, to the sweetness of a
friend's counsel. Oil is not vague readiness here; it is faithful counsel that
may wound before it sweetens.

Psalm 55:21 and Proverbs 5:1-3 supply the counterfeit. Treacherous or seductive
words can be smooth like oil while carrying swords or leading to death. A
symbol's moral value follows its bearer: true oil corrects, false oil flatters.

### The wise store oil; the fool consumes it

Proverbs 21:20 names the same triad as Matthew 25: wise, fool, and oil. The
proverb stores desired treasure and oil in the wise man's dwelling while the
fool swallows or spends it. In the Hebrew Matthew, the corresponding terms are
the same Hebrew words: H2450 (*chakam*, wise), H3684 (*kesil*, fool), and H8081
(*shemen*, oil). The parable carries the Wisdom inventory into personal
vessels.

### What the foolish are sent to buy

Proverbs 23:23 commands, "Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and
instruction, and understanding." The word translated *instruction*, H4148
(*musar*), includes chastening, correction, discipline, doctrine, and reproof.
The parable's purchase therefore returns to the same substance Psalm 141 calls
oil: truth and correction received rather than refused.

## Spirit, quantity, and impartation

Acts 8:17-20 keeps impartation and purchase in the same scene. The Samaritans
receive the Spirit through the apostles' laying on of hands; Simon then offers
money for that power and is rebuked because the gift of God cannot be
purchased. The apostles minister God's gift but do not sell or divide a private
reserve.

Matthew 25:9 is explicitly zero-sum: sharing may leave too little oil for both
groups. The Spirit is not depleted when another person receives Him. Christ
receives the Spirit "not by measure" (John 3:34), while gifts and manifestations
are distributed by the Spirit as He wills (1 Corinthians 12:11). Cornelius's
house receives the gift "while Peter yet spake" (Acts 10:44). Spirit can be
given in a moment; counsel accepted, truth kept, and obedience proven cannot be
borrowed from another life at midnight.

## Confirming narrative: the widow of Zarephath

First Kings 17 offers a rich secondary echo rather than the load-bearing
definition. A widow gathers 2 sticks, obeys the prophetic word with her last
meal and oil, and finds that the meal does not waste and the oil does not fail
until rain returns; her son is then raised from death. Within the book's
vocabulary, widow, 2 trees, sustained oil, rain, obedience, and resurrection
all carry weight. The scene confirms oil as inward provision sustained through
faithful obedience, but its many linked symbols make it better suited to a
short confirming paragraph or footnote than to the core derivation.

## Verdict

### The commandment-lamp's inward content

Proverbs 6:23 should be read as one three-part mechanism rather than mined for
only its first 2 equations: "the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light;
and reproofs of instruction are the way of life." Psalm 141:5 then supplies the
missing material term by calling righteous reproof "an excellent oil."
Commandment/lamp, law/light, and reproof/instruction/oil converge before the
parable is consulted.

Scripture describes the commandment as truth (Psalm 119:151), righteousness
(Psalm 119:172), and life everlasting (John 12:50). The decisive distinction is
between possessing that commandment outwardly and receiving its substance
inwardly. First John 2:4 says the man who claims to know God but does not keep
His commandments has no truth "in him." Proverbs 7:1-3 commands the opposite:
lay up the commandments with thee and write them on the heart. Psalm 119:11
hides the word in the heart so the keeper will not sin; Jeremiah 31:33 makes
the law written in the inward parts the renewed covenant's first promise.

This supplies a precise reading of the 10 lamps and 2 stores. All 10 possess the
commandment-lamp. Only the wise possess its truth and correction inwardly,
where it has become a way of life capable of burning as visible obedience.

The strongest reader-facing argument needs only the Wisdom chain:

1. Proverbs 6:23 and Psalm 141:5 join commandment/lamp, law/light, and
   reproof/instruction/oil to the way of life.
2. Proverbs 21:20 already joins wise, fool, and stored oil.
3. Proverbs 23:23 commands the hearer to buy truth, wisdom, instruction, and
   understanding.
4. Psalm 119:151, 1 John 2:4, and Proverbs 7:1-3 distinguish the commandment
   carried outwardly from its truth laid up inwardly.
5. Ezekiel 36:27 and John 16:13 place the Spirit behind the truth and obedience;
   Matthew 5:16 identifies the resulting light as visible works.

No single verse must state "oil is X." The repeated structure supplies the
definition with fewer inferential steps than the anointing-oil consensus.
