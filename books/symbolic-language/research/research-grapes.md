# Grapes — symbol study (2026-07-10)

Hebrew enab (H6025), 17 verses (19 tagged tokens — Num 6:3 and Deut 32:32
carry it twice) — including Hosea 3:1, where KJV "flagons of wine" is
ashishei ANAVIM (H809+H6025), grape-cakes. Cluster: eshkol (H811), 9×.
Sour/unripe grape: boser (H1155), 4× (+ beser H1154, Job 15:33). "Wild
grapes": beushim (H891), only Isa 5:2, 4 — from ba'ash "to stink": stink-fruit.
Gleanings: olelot (H5955, noun), 6×; the "glean" of Lev 19:10 and Jer 6:9 is
the cognate verb H5953 alal. Vintage: batsir (H1210); grape-gatherer: batsar
(H1219). Fallen single grapes: peret (H6528, hapax, Lev 19:10). Tender grape:
semadar (H5563, Song only). Greek: staphyle (G4718), 3× (Matt 7:16; Luke 6:44;
Rev 14:18); botrus "cluster" (G1009), 1× (Rev 14:18). KJV supplies "grapes"
with no H6025 present at Deut 28:30, 39 and Lev 25:11 (literal context only;
the 17-count excludes them). Verified verse-by-verse against kjv_strongs.txt;
both graders re-ran every tally.

## Candidate definition

**Grapes are a nation's works ripened into its people — persons as the
vineyard's yield.** God plants the vine-nation and comes looking for grapes
(justice, righteousness — Isa 5:7's own decode); what He finds, picks, gleans,
or treads are men: Israel found "like grapes in the wilderness" (Hos 9:10),
the good man as the cluster (Mic 7:1-2), the remnant as the gleanings left on
the vine (Isa 17:6; 24:13; 65:8).

**Second layer (author's hypothesis, tested below): the grape's yield is
blood.** Scripture calls grape-juice "the blood of grapes" verbatim (Gen
49:11; Deut 32:14). Pressed, the grape fills a cup — the covenant's cup of
blessing ("this is my blood of the new testament," said over "this fruit of
the vine") or the cup of wrath (grapes of gall press out to the poison of
dragons; the winepress of wrath runs with blood). Which cup the vintage fills
follows Isaiah's fork: grapes, or stink-fruit.

## The verdict fork (the defining scene)

- **Isa 5:2, 4** — the vineyard planted with the choicest vine (soreq), a
  tower in the midst, a winepress (yeqev H3342) hewn in it; the owner "looked
  that it should bring forth grapes (anavim), and it brought forth wild grapes
  (beushim — stink-fruit)." The inspection is binary: grapes or rot.
- **Isa 5:7 decodes the expected grapes as WORKS**: "he looked for judgment
  (mishpat), but behold oppression (mispach); for righteousness (tsedaqah),
  but behold a cry (tse'aqah)." The grapes God wanted were justice and
  righteousness; the stink-fruit was oppression. GRADER WELD: the decode is
  grammatical, not merely thematic — one verb "looked" (qavah, H6960) runs
  through 5:2, 5:4, and 5:7: the looked-for grapes and the looked-for mishpat
  are the same object of the same verb. This anchors grapes inside the
  established sym-fruit sense (a nation's works, by which it is judged) —
  the grape is the vine-nation's specific fruit.
- **Deut 32 holds both poles inside one song.** v14: God fed Israel until
  "thou didst drink the pure blood of the grape (dam-enav)" — the blessing
  pole. vv32-33: "their vine is of the vine of Sodom… their grapes are grapes
  of gall (rosh), their clusters are bitter: their wine is the poison of
  dragons" — the apostate vintage. Same nation, same song: the grape's
  pressed-out yield is either the pure blood God gives or the venom the
  Sodom-vine brews.
- **Matt 7:16 / Luke 6:44** — "Ye shall know THEM by their fruits. Do men
  gather grapes (staphyle) of thorns?" The fruit names the works; the knowing
  lands on the persons. Thorn-men cannot bear grape-works. HG WITNESS
  (data/hg-chapters/Matthew-7.json v16): the Hebrew Matthew renders "fruits"
  as **ma'aseihem, "their DEEDS" (H4639)** — "by their deeds you will
  recognize them… grapes (anavim H6025) from the thorns" — the fruit=works
  equation is inside the Hebrew text of the grape verse itself.

## Grapes are persons (the texts that carry the second step)

- **Hos 9:10 — "I found ISRAEL like grapes in the wilderness; I saw your
  FATHERS as the firstripe (bikkurah) in the fig tree."** The grapes are the
  people themselves at the finding — the delight of an unexpected vintage
  where nothing grows. The verse then runs the fork in one breath: "but they
  went to Baalpeor… their abominations were according as they loved."
- **Mic 7:1-2 — explicit self-decode.** "There is no cluster (eshkol) to eat:
  my soul desired the firstripe fruit (bikkurah)" — and the next verse
  interprets the empty vine: **"The GOOD MAN is perished out of the earth:
  and there is none upright among men."** The missing cluster IS the missing
  righteous person — and 7:2 continues "they all lie in wait for BLOOD": what
  the hunters of the good-man grapes are after is his blood (grader find,
  joining the blood layer below). (Jer 8:13 is the same inspection from God's
  side: "there shall be no grapes on the vine… and the things that I have
  given them shall pass away.")
- **Isa 65:8 — the remnant IN the cluster.** "As the new wine is found in the
  cluster, and one saith, Destroy it not; for a blessing is in it: **so will
  I do for my servants' sakes, that I may not destroy them all**." Stated
  simile: cluster = the nation under judgment; the new-wine blessing inside
  it = the servants, spared. The remnant is the good grape in a bad bunch.
- **The gleanings = the remnant, persons every time.** The law plants the
  figure: leave the fallen grapes (peret) and the gleanings for the poor and
  stranger (Lev 19:10; Deut 24:21). The prophets harvest it: "there shall be
  as the gleaning grapes when the vintage is done… in the midst of the land
  among the people" (Isa 24:13 — supportive: the phrase locates the simile's
  scene among persons; the decode weight rests on Jer 6:9 and Judg 8:2-3; so
  Isa 17:6); "They shall throughly glean **the remnant of Israel** as a vine:
  turn back thine hand as a grapegatherer into the baskets" (Jer 6:9 — total
  judgment re-picks even the gleanings); "would they not leave some gleaning
  grapes?" (Jer 49:9 verbatim; Obad 5 reads "some grapes") — but Esau is
  searched out to nothing: no gleanings = no remnant.
- **Judg 8:2-3 — an in-text decode nobody plants deliberately.** Gideon:
  "Is not the gleaning of the grapes (olelot) of Ephraim better than the
  vintage (batsir) of Abiezer?" — and v3 cashes the figure as PERSONS: "God
  hath delivered into your hands the princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb."
  Ephraim's gleaned grapes are two captured princes.
- The Song's clusters (the bride's breasts, Song 7:7-8; the beloved "as a
  cluster of camphire," Song 1:14) are a separate register — love-song
  delight, not the vineyard-inspection structure. Noted for completeness,
  claimed as nothing (grader correction: not even light support).

## Ripeness — judgment waits for the vintage to be full

- **Rev 14:18 — "gather the clusters (botrus) of the vine of the earth; for
  her grapes (staphyle) are fully ripe (akmazo)."** The vintage is cut when
  ripe — the measure full (the grammar of Gen 15:16, "the iniquity of the
  Amorites is not yet full"). What the press then yields is not juice but
  **blood** (14:20).
- **Isa 18:5** — the other timing: "afore the harvest… the sour grape (boser)
  is ripening in the flower, he shall cut off the sprigs" — judgment can also
  strike a vintage before it ripens. Job 15:33: the wicked "shall shake off
  his unripe grape (beser) as the vine" — fruit lost before maturity.
- **The sour-grape proverb (boser): Ezek 18:2; Jer 31:29-30.** "The fathers
  have eaten a sour grape, and the children's teeth are set on edge" — the
  grape here is a man's own deed with its consequence stored in it, and God
  annuls the proverb's transfer: "every one shall die for his own iniquity:
  every man that eateth the sour grape, HIS teeth shall be set on edge"
  (Jer 31:30); "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezek 18:4). Each mouth
  tastes its own vintage — and Jeremiah says it as the doorstep of the new
  covenant (31:31 follows immediately). Individual accountability is stated
  in grape-grammar.

## The blood of grapes — the two cups (author's layer, tested)

The lexical base is verbatim, twice: **"the blood (dam H1818) of grapes
(anavim H6025)"** (Gen 49:11) and **"the pure blood of the grape (dam-enav)"**
(Deut 32:14). Scripture's own law fixes what blood is: "the blood is the life
(nephesh)" (Deut 12:23; Lev 17:11). So the grape pressed gives up its LIFE —
and the pressed-out life is drunk from a cup. The two cups then divide on
Isaiah's fork:

- **The covenant cup.** Jesus takes the grape's yield and names it: "this is
  my BLOOD of the new testament… I will not drink henceforth of this FRUIT OF
  THE VINE, until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's
  kingdom" (Matt 26:28-29); "the cup of blessing… is it not the communion of
  the blood of Christ?" (1 Cor 10:16). The true Grape trodden: "he hath
  POURED OUT his soul (nephesh) unto death" (Isa 53:12) — blood is nephesh,
  and His, poured out, fills the cup of the new covenant (the covenant
  Jeremiah announced two verses after fixing the sour-grape proverb, 31:31).
  New wine = the Spirit's pole (Acts 2:13 "full of new wine" at the pouring
  of the Spirit; Isa 65:8's new-wine blessing in the cluster) — the wine
  study (draft-wine.adoc) carries that half.
- **The wrath cup.** Deut 32:33 names whose vintage fills it: "their wine is
  the poison of dragons." Jer 25 holds cup and press in one oracle: "Take the
  WINE CUP of this fury at my hand, and cause all the nations… to drink it"
  (25:15) — and the vintage-shout of 25:30, "as they that tread" with the
  heydad-cry, against all the inhabitants of the earth (KJV's "the grapes"
  there is a translator's supplement; the treading reading stands without the
  noun). Isa 63:2-3, 6: garments "like him that treadeth in the winefat…
  their blood shall be sprinkled upon my garments… I will make them DRUNK in
  my fury" — lexeme note: Isa 63:3's "blood" is netsach (H5332, juice/gore),
  not dam; the KJV is quoted accurately and the juice-word used for gore if
  anything tightens the grape/blood exchange, but the verbatim dam-chain does
  not run through Isa 63. Rev 14:19-20: the earth's ripe grapes go into "the
  great winepress of the wrath of God… and BLOOD came out of the winepress."
  Lam 1:15: "the Lord hath trodden the virgin, the daughter of Judah, as in a
  winepress" (gath H1660, the treading-floor — a different press-word than
  Isa 5:2's yeqev; the persons-trodden point rides the image, not a lexeme) —
  trodden grapes are persons, stated flat. Joel 3:13: "the press is full, the
  fats overflow; for their WICKEDNESS is great" — the press's contents named
  as the works, and "fats" there is yeqev (H3342) again, Isaiah's vineyard
  vat (grader find).
- **The cups answer the treading — measure for measure.** "They have shed the
  blood of saints and prophets, and thou hast given them BLOOD TO DRINK; for
  they are worthy" (Rev 16:6). Babylon runs the counterfeit communion:
  "drunken with the blood of the saints" (Rev 17:6) — she presses the wrong
  grapes and drinks the cup she filled (Ps 75:8: the wicked "wring out" the
  dregs and drink them; Obad 16).
- **Gen 49:11 is the hinge text** — Judah's Shiloh "washed his garments in
  wine, and his clothes in the BLOOD OF GRAPES": the image unfolds both ways,
  into Isa 63:3 (their blood on His garments, wrath) and Rev 19:13-15
  ("vesture dipped in blood… he treadeth the winepress of the fierceness and
  wrath"), while the same Person's own poured-out blood is the covenant cup.
  The treader's garments are stained either way; the grape's blood is the
  common term.
- **First pressing in Scripture, association-grade:** the butler's dream —
  "I took the grapes, and pressed them into Pharaoh's cup, and I gave the cup
  into Pharaoh's hand" (Gen 40:11) — interpreted life and restoration
  (40:13), while the twin dream reads death. From the first page the pressed
  grape fills a cup, and the cup is a verdict.

## Hebrew-witness welds (Rev 14 / Isa 5)

data/hg-chapters/Revelation-14.json (versification runs one verse off the
Greek): the vintage angel says "harvest the grapes (innevei, H6025) of the
land, for its fruits (H6529) are ripened," and casts them "into the great
winepress (yeqev, H3342) of the wrath of YHWH" — **the same yeqev Isaiah's
vineyard had hewn in it (Isa 5:2)**, and "the blood (dam, H1818) from the
winepress went up to the bridle of the horses." In the Hebrew witness,
Revelation's wrath-press is Isaiah's vineyard-press by lexeme, and its output
is named dam — the vineyard built its own press for the fruit's blood from
the start, and the winepress study (concurrent, sym-winepress) carries the
press itself: same fork, press = where the blood is exacted.

## Consistency with sym-fruit and the wine study (refine, don't collide)

sym-fruit stands: "A nation's works, by which it is judged" (Matt 7:17;
21:43). Isa 5:7 keeps grapes inside that definition — the looked-for grapes
ARE mishpat and tsedaqah. The grape texts then take the one step fruit-in-
general never takes: the fruit inspected turns out to be people (Hos 9:10;
Mic 7:1-2; Isa 65:8; Isa 24:13; Judg 8:2-3; Lam 1:15; Rev 14 pressed to
blood). The unity: works have no existence apart from their doers — when the
vineyard's fruit is picked, gleaned, spared, or trodden, what is handled is
persons; "ye shall know THEM by their fruits." Grapes = the nation's works
embodied in its people. draft-wine.adoc ("a people's grapes are its works;
trodden in the press, those grapes become the wine… the spirit a man carries
is the pressed-out juice of his deeds") is untouched — this study supplies
the intermediate term the wine chapter assumes: the pressed thing is the
person-as-fruit, and the pressed-out yield is called blood, i.e. life
(nephesh), before it is called wine.

## Counter-texts weighed

- **Literal viticulture abounds and stays literal**: eating in a neighbor's
  vineyard (Deut 23:24), sabbath/jubilee vintage rules (Lev 25:5, 11),
  sabbath commerce in grapes (Neh 13:15), the covenant curses (Deut 28:30,
  39), Eshcol's cluster carried between two on a staff (Num 13:20, 23-24 —
  the land's goodness demonstrated; named for the cluster; no symbolic claim
  made), Amos 9:13's treader overtaking the sower (restoration abundance —
  which also fixes a boundary: treading is not inherently wrath; the press's
  verdict follows the vintage, never the other way).
  The symbol never dissolves the crop or the law — the gleaning command in
  particular stays a literal duty whose mercy-shape the prophets then read
  God's own vintage by.
- **The Nazarite (Num 6:3-4)** abstains from everything of the vine, grape to
  husk — a literal vow, cited as law, not symbol. Echo only, flagged as echo:
  Jesus closes the supper with a vine-abstention of the same shape — "I will
  not drink henceforth of this fruit of the vine, until…" (Matt 26:29).
- **Hos 3:1 lexeme disclosure**: KJV "flagons of wine" hides anavim —
  ashishei anavim, pressed grape-cakes, loved at the idol feasts; Israel
  "look to other gods, and love grape-cakes." Counted in the 17, flagged so
  the tally is honest.
- **Is the wrath-wine really the wicked's own vintage, or just God's
  independent potion?** Jer 25:15 alone would allow a mere handed cup. But
  Deut 32:33 names the poison-wine as THEIR wine from THEIR grapes; Joel 3:13
  fills the press with "their wickedness"; Rev 14 presses the earth's own
  ripe grapes and gets blood; Rev 16:6 states the exchange rate (shed blood,
  drink blood); Ps 75:8's wicked wring out and drink the dregs themselves.
  The cup of wrath is the return of the drinker's own pressing — the
  counter-reading does not survive the sweep.
- **Overclaim check on "grapes = persons" as an identity**: Isa 5:7 decodes
  the grapes as works, not men; the definition therefore leads with works and
  lets the person-texts (Hos 9:10; Mic 7:1-2; Isa 65:8; Judg 8:2-3; Lam 1:15)
  supply the embodiment step, rather than claiming "grape = person"
  everywhere. Range, not one fitted sense.
