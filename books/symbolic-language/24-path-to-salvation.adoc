Ask any Christian what grace is, and the answer comes back in a formula: *unmerited favor* — getting what you don't deserve.
Ask about mercy and the usual answer is: *not getting the punishment you do deserve*.
Ask about the gift of God: *salvation — free; a gift can only be received, never earned*.

None of these is wrong.
Every one of them is half-true.

The formulas tell you what grace and mercy cost you — nothing — and stop before telling you what they *do*.
Scripture goes further, and it starts higher: both words are written into God's own sym:sym-name[Name], proclaimed to Moses in the cleft of the rock — __“merciful and gracious… keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will *by no means clear the guilty*.”__
Forgiveness and the unforgiven stand in the same sentence of the same Name.
The formulas are missing a clause — something decides which side of that sentence a man stands on — and this chapter recovers the three words to find it.

[discrete]
=== Grace: Favor That Moves First

Begin where the formula is right.
Grace moves before any deserving:

[quote.scripture, Romans 5:8]
____
But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, *while we were yet* sym:sym-sin[*sinners*], Christ died for us.
____

The word's first appearance in Scripture is the same shape: __“Noah found *grace* in the eyes of the LORD”__ (Genesis 6:8) — favor toward one man in a world already condemned.
Unmerited is exactly right.

Now watch what the formula leaves out.
When John says __“*grace and truth* came by Jesus Christ”__ (John 1:17), he is quoting the Name-proclamation — __“abundant in *goodness and truth*”__ — the pair God announced at Sinai now walking in person.
Grace never travels alone; its companion is the truth, and __“thy *law* is the sym:sym-truth[truth]”__ (Psalm 119:142).
Paul gives grace its job description:

[quote.scripture, Titus 2:11-12]
____
For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men,
*Teaching us* that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live *soberly, righteously, and godly*, in this present world;
____

Grace arrives free, and then it goes to work — a tutor, not an exemption.
The one verse always quoted against this — __“ye are not under the law, but under *grace*”__ (Romans 6:14) — Paul guards himself, in the next breath: __“What then? shall we sin, because we are not under the law, but under grace? *God forbid*”__ (Romans 6:15); to yield to sin is to be sin's servant, __“or of *obedience* unto sym:sym-righteousness[righteousness]”__ (Romans 6:16).
Not under the law's *condemnation*; never out from under its instruction — grace is the teacher, not the end of school.
Scripture even names the counterfeit: __“ungodly men, turning the grace of our God into *lasciviousness*”__ (Jude 4) — grace re-sold as license.
The test between them is simple: real grace produces sober, righteous, godly living; the counterfeit produces permission.

[discrete]
=== Mercy: The Covenant's Loyalty

The Hebrew beneath most of the Bible's __“mercy”__ is _chesed_ — covenant loyalty, steadfast love — and Moses sets it in its frame:

[quote.scripture, Deuteronomy 7:9]
____
Know therefore that the LORD thy God, he is God, the *faithful* God, which keepeth *covenant and mercy* with them that *love him and keep his commandments* to a thousand generations;
____

__“Covenant and mercy”__ travel as one phrase; mercy lives inside the covenant, kept toward its keepers — __“the mercy of the LORD is from everlasting to everlasting upon them that *fear him*… to such as *keep his covenant*”__ (Psalm 103:17-18).
Hebrew's other mercy-word says how deep it runs: _racham_, compassion, shares its letters with _rechem_, the *womb* — __“Can a woman forget her sucking child?… yea, they may forget, yet will I *not* forget thee”__ (Isaiah 49:15).
Mercy is womb-love: loyal because of what you are to Him, not because of what you did.

How is it obtained?
Scripture answers in mechanics, not mood:

[quote.scripture, Proverbs 28:13]
____
He that *covereth* his sins shall not prosper: but whoso *confesseth and forsaketh* them shall have *mercy*.
____

Confess — and forsake.
Isaiah adds the turning: __“Let the wicked *forsake his way*… and let him *return* unto the LORD, and he will have mercy upon him; and to our God, for he will *abundantly pardon*”__ (Isaiah 55:7).
Ezekiel writes the clause that should astonish more than it does:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 33:15-16]
____
If the wicked *restore the pledge*, give again that he had robbed, walk in the statutes of life, without committing iniquity; he shall surely *live*, he shall not die.
*None of his sins* that he hath committed shall be *mentioned* unto him: he hath done that which is lawful and right; he shall surely live.
____

Turn, do right, restore what can be restored — and the ledger is wiped: none of it so much as *mentioned*.
Repentance in Scripture has four parts, not three: confess the sin; plead guilty — __“against thee, thee only, have I sinned… that thou mightest be *justified* when thou speakest”__ (Psalm 51:4); change the heart to desire what is good; and make restitution where restitution can be made, as Zacchaeus did fourfold (Luke 19:8).

[discrete]
=== The Canceled Debt

Jesus told mercy as accounting:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 18:23-27, 32-35]
____
Therefore is the kingdom of heaven likened unto a certain king, which would *take account* of his servants.
And when he had begun to *reckon*, one was brought unto him, which owed him ten thousand talents.
But forasmuch as he had *not to pay*, his lord commanded him to be sold… The servant therefore fell down… saying, Lord, have patience with me, and I will pay thee all.
Then the lord of that servant was moved with compassion, and loosed him, and *forgave him the debt*…
O thou wicked servant, I forgave thee all that debt, *because thou desiredst me*: Shouldest not thou also have had compassion on thy fellowservant, even as I had pity on thee?
And his lord was wroth, and delivered him to the tormentors, till he should pay all that was due unto him.
*So likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you*, if ye from your hearts forgive not every one his brother their trespasses.
____

Here mercy is shown in full.
The debt is real and unpayable — ten thousand talents, a sum no servant sees in a lifetime.
The cancellation is total, and it is granted *on asking*: __“because thou *desiredst* me.”__
Paul names the instrument: __“*blotting out the handwriting* that was against us… nailing it to his cross”__ (Colossians 2:14) — the debt-note itself, canceled and nailed up where every debtor can read it.

The canceled note has terms on the back.
The pardoned man who will not pardon gets his debt *reinstated* — __“so likewise shall my heavenly Father do also unto you.”__
This is no isolated hard saying; it is the standing rule of the kingdom: __“Blessed are the *merciful*: for they shall *obtain mercy*”__ (Matthew 5:7); __“if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses”__ (Matthew 6:15); __“he shall have sym:sym-judgment[judgment] *without mercy*, that hath shewed *no mercy*”__ (James 2:13).
Mercy in Scripture is not a one-way transaction; it is a circuit — received, then required.
Jesus files it where the formulas never look: among __“the *weightier matters of the law*, judgment, *mercy*, and faith”__ (Matthew 23:23) — mercy is law-content, a thing commanded and *done*: __“do justly, and *love mercy*, and walk humbly with thy God”__ (Micah 6:8).
The Name-proclamation's two poles now close: He keeps mercy for thousands and forgives — the confessing, forsaking, forgiving; He will by no means clear the guilty — the covering, defiant, unmerciful.
The clause the formulas were missing is repentance, and mercy practiced.

[discrete]
=== The Gift Against the Wages

One verse holds the whole economy:

[quote.scripture, Romans 6:23]
____
For the *wages* of sym:sym-sin[sin] is *death*; but the *gift* of God is *eternal life* through Jesus Christ our Lord.
____

The contrast is the verse's own structure: _opsōnia_, a soldier's wages — earned, owed, paid in full — against _charisma_, the grace-gift.
Death is the one thing every sinner has fully earned; eternal life is the one thing no man ever will.
Jesus closes the earning door in a sentence:

[quote.scripture, Luke 17:9-10, NKJV]
____
Does he thank that servant because he did the things that were commanded him?
I think not.
So likewise you, when you have done *all* those things which you are commanded, say, ‘We are *unprofitable* servants.
We have done what was our *duty* to do.’
____

Perfect obedience earns nothing; it was already owed.
As servants, we have nothing our Creator did not give us first; we owe Him our very life, and every sin grows the debt — and the first rule of getting out of a hole is to stop digging.
Obedience cannot pay the old debt; only the blood cancels that.
What obedience does is stop the new one.

Paul refuses works in exactly this ledger's terms, and he defines the working he refuses: __“Now to him that *worketh* is the reward reckoned not of grace, but of *debt*”__ (Romans 4:4) — work done to put God in debt, obedience tendered as a ransom-price.
Hebrew carries the wage inside the word: one noun, _pe'ullah_, is both *work* and *wages*, and Isaiah announces the coming LORD with it — __“behold, his *reward* is with him, and his *work* before him”__ (Isaiah 40:10) — the very line Revelation quotes as the judgment formula: __“behold, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give every man according as his *work* shall be”__ (Revelation 22:12).
Deeds carry their pay — which is exactly why they cannot buy back a forfeited life whose wage is already earned.
What Paul never refuses is the doing of the law: __“the *doers* of the law shall be justified”__ (Romans 2:13); __“do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid: yea, we *establish* the law”__ (Romans 3:31).
The working he refuses is wage-logic; the walk he commands is obedience.

[quote.scripture, Ephesians 2:8-10, ESV]
____
For by grace you have been saved through sym:sym-faith[faith].
And this is not your own doing; it is the *gift* of God, not a result of *works*, so that no one may boast.
For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus *for good works*, which God prepared beforehand, that we should *walk in them*.
____

Both hands in one passage: not *of* works — no man earns it; *for* good works — no man receives it and stands idle.
The gift, like the wedding sym:sym-garment[garment], is granted, then worn.

[discrete]
=== The High Priest and the Two Kinds of Sin

Grace and mercy meet at a specific address:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 4:14-16]
____
Seeing then that we have a great *high priest*… Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession.
For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like we are, yet without sym:sym-sin[sin].
Let us therefore come boldly unto the *throne of grace*, that we may *obtain mercy*, and find grace to help in time of need.
____

The throne of grace is where mercy is obtained — the two words the formulas separate work one office, and the office is the law's own.
It is the law that establishes a High Priest, the sacrifice he presents, and the sins his atonement covers — and the law divides sin in two:

[quote.scripture, Numbers 15:28-30, AMP]
____
The priest shall make atonement before the LORD for the person who commits an error when he sym:sym-sin[sins] *unintentionally*, making atonement for him so that he may be forgiven…
But the person who does anything wrong *willfully and defiantly*… that one is *blaspheming* the LORD, and that person shall be *cut off* from among his people.
____

Unintentional sin has an altar; defiant sin refuses the altar's terms.
Hebrews carries the same division into the new covenant:

[quote.scripture, Hebrews 10:26-29, NKJV]
____
For if we sym:sym-sin[sin] *willfully* after we have received the knowledge of the sym:sym-truth[truth], there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a certain fearful expectation of sym:sym-judgment[judgment]…
Of how much worse punishment, do you suppose, will he be thought worthy who has trampled the Son of God underfoot, counted the *blood of the covenant* by which he was sanctified a common thing, and *insulted the Spirit of grace*?
____

The truth is the law, so willful sin after receiving it is lawbreaking with the eyes open — and the most open-eyed form of all is the claim that there is no law to break, that it was done away, that __“Jesus has paid the price for you not following it.”__
That claim does not plead the blood; it counts the blood a common thing.
Jesus prescribes the escape to a fallen assembly, and it is measured in works resumed: __“Remember therefore from whence thou art fallen, and *repent*, and *do the first works*”__ (Revelation 2:5) — repentance is not a feeling; it is what is resumed.
The healed are dismissed with the same charge — __“go, and sym:sym-sin[sin] *no more*, lest a worse thing come upon you”__ (John 5:14; 8:11) — and since sin is lawlessness, __“sin no more”__ means *go and keep the law*.

[discrete]
=== The War in the Members

If willful sin after knowledge is worse than never knowing, who then can be saved?
Paul answers with his own struggle:

[quote.scripture, Romans 7:19, 22-25, NKJV]
____
For the good that I will to do, I do not do; but the evil I will not to do, that I practice…
For I *delight in the law* of God according to the inward man.
But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sym:sym-sin[sin] which is in my members.
O wretched man that I am!
Who will deliver me from this body of death?
I thank God — through Jesus Christ our Lord!
So then, with the *mind* I myself serve the *law of God*, but with the flesh the law of sin.
____

Paul's own position is clear: he calls the law good and delights in it in the inward man, while the flesh he drags behind him keeps breaking it.
That is not the defiant sinner of Numbers 15; it is the unintentional one, at war, confessing, covered by the High Priest — the publican's whole prayer: __“God be *merciful* to me a sinner”__ (Luke 18:13).
The man who agrees with the law and loses battles against his flesh has an altar.
The man who denies the law has refused it.

[discrete]
=== How Then Shall We Be Saved?

[quote.scripture, Romans 10:9, BSB]
____
That if you confess with your mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and sym:sym-belief[believe] in your sym:sym-heart[heart] that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.
____

Confessing Jesus as *Lord* means He is your Master — and He does nothing but what the Father commanded: __“I have come down from heaven, *not to do My own will*, but to do the will of Him who sent Me”__ (John 6:38).
Call Him Master, and His Father's law becomes your desire; deny the law, and you have denied the Lordship in the confession — the word emptied while the mouth still moves.

The familiar three-step path holds, but each step stands on the law it forgot.
*Admit you are a sinner* — you cannot, without the law that defines sin.
*Believe He paid for your sin on the cross* — you cannot, without the law that demands the sacrifice, establishes the High Priest, and prophesies the Messiah who meets both.
*Confess Him Lord* — you cannot, without submitting to obey Him.

Is perfect obedience possible?
Not in the flesh.
Is perfect obedience required for salvation?
No. But a mind and heart of obedience is.
Many say __“Lord, Lord,”__ while their hearts say *there are no laws, because I am forgiven* — a reckless driver claiming there are no rules of the road because someone has paid his tickets.
The paid ticket is mercy; the gratitude that slows down is grace doing its teaching; the license kept is the gift.
He who *does* the will of the Father enters (Matthew 7:21) — not earning the kingdom, but refusing to insult the Spirit of the grace that brought him to its gate.

The servant who means to keep His commands exactly will want them exactly as they were written — which raises a question this book has been leaning on since the Introduction, where one word was read two ways: what, precisely, did the prophets write?
That question opens the next part of this book.
