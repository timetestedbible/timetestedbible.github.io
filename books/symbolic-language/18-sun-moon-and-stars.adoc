From the first page of Scripture the lights of heaven are given a job, and it is not merely to glow:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 1:16]
____
And God made two great lights; the *greater light to rule the day*, and the *lesser light to rule the night*: he made the *stars* also.
____

Two offices are named at the founding: the lights are set __“to rule”__ (Genesis 1:16-18) and to be __“for signs, and for seasons”__ (Genesis 1:14) — the appointed times. They govern, and they mark the feasts. Take the first office — _to rule_ — and a whole register of prophecy opens.

[discrete]
=== Joseph's Dream

Scripture interprets its own symbol. Joseph dreams of the heavens bowing, and his father reads the dream on the spot:

[quote.scripture, Genesis 37:9-10]
____
…behold, the *sun and the moon and the eleven stars* made obeisance to me… Shall *I and thy mother and thy brethren* indeed come to bow down ourselves to thee…?
____

Jacob does not hesitate: the _sun_ is the father, the _moon_ the mother, the _eleven stars_ the brothers. The lights are a household's governing order — the head, the authority beside him, and the sons who rule beneath. What holds for a house holds for a kingdom: the sun is the supreme ruler, the moon the authority that reflects his light, the stars the princes under them.

[discrete]
=== Stars and Princes

So a star is a ruler — the Hebrew _kokab_ carries the sense. Balaam sets it beside the sceptre: __“there shall come a Star out of Jacob, and a Sceptre shall rise out of Israel”__ (Numbers 24:17). The risen Christ holds seven stars, and they are named outright — __“the seven stars are the angels of the seven churches”__ (Revelation 1:20), the messengers set over them. When a kingdom overreaches it __“cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them”__ (Daniel 8:10) — rulers thrown from their place. And Israel herself wears the whole hierarchy: __“a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars”__ (Revelation 12:1) — the covenant nation and her twelve tribes, the very lights of Joseph's dream.

[discrete]
=== When the Lights Go Dark

Now the prophets' strangest oracles come clear. When Scripture says the sun is darkened and the stars fall, it is not announcing the end of the universe; it is announcing the fall of a government. Hear it spoken over Babylon:

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 13:10]
____
For the *stars of heaven*… shall not give their light: the *sun shall be darkened* in his going forth, and the *moon shall not cause her light to shine*.
____

That is __“the burden of Babylon”__ (Isaiah 13:1) — Babylon's lights, not the world's. The same words fall on Egypt, and the text leaves no doubt whose sky goes black: __“I will cover the sun with a cloud, and the moon shall not give her light… and set darkness upon thy land”__ (Ezekiel 32:7-8). _Thy_ land — Egypt's ruling order put out. So when Jesus says __“the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be shaken”__ (Matthew 24:29), He speaks the prophets' own grammar: the powers are shaken; the rulers fall. It happened in miniature at the cross — __“the sun was darkened, and the veil of the temple was rent in the midst”__ (Luke 23:45): the light failed and the old ruling sanctuary was torn open, its day done. (How these same signs appear in the literal heavens — the darkened sun, the blood moon, the falling stars, and the feasts that fall on the full moon — is the work of _Time Tested Tradition_; here the point is the symbol the signs carry.)

[discrete]
=== The Righteous Shall Shine

And the lights are not only the rulers of the nations that fall; they are the destiny of the righteous. The wise — those who heard and did — are promised the same brightness:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 12:3]
____
And they that be *wise* shall *shine as the brightness of the firmament*; and they that turn many to righteousness *as the stars* for ever and ever.
____

__“Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father”__ (Matthew 13:43); even now they __“shine as lights in the world”__ (Philippians 2:15) — for light is the law lived, made visible, as an earlier chapter showed. The saints become what the symbol always meant: lights set to rule, stars that do not fall. And when the kingdom is full, the mediated lights are needed no longer: __“the city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof”__ (Revelation 21:23).

So the sun, moon, and stars are the governing lights — the rulers of a people, and the markers of its appointed times. When a kingdom is judged, its lights go out; when the kingdom of God comes, its rulers are the righteous, shining as the sun and the stars, under the one Light that never sets.
