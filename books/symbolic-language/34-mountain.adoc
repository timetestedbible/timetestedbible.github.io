The first case study is __“mountain,”__ which represents kingdoms or power structures.
The pyramid-like structure of a mountain mirrors the power structure of delegated authority and is an apt analogy — but subjective aptness is not a basis for defining a symbol.
Thankfully, in this case Scripture comes right out and defines it:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 17:9-10]
____
This calls for a mind with wisdom.
The seven heads are *seven mountains* on which the woman sits.
They are also *seven kings* (kingdoms).
____

Revelation tells us that mountains mean kingdoms, and by analogy hills are cities.
The question is whether Scripture consistently uses mountain with the same symbolic meaning, or whether this is a one-off.
Other times in Scripture we are given a vision and an interpretation, which establishes the definition by analogy:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 2:35 (NKJV)]
____
Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver, and the gold were crushed together, and became like sym:sym-chaff[chaff] from the summer threshing floors; the sym:sym-wind[wind] carried them away so that no trace of them was found.
And the sym:sym-stone[stone] that struck the sym:sym-image[image] became a *great mountain* and *filled the whole earth*.
____

[quote.scripture, Daniel 2:44-45 (NKJV)]
____
And in the days of these kings the God of heaven will set up a *kingdom* which shall never be destroyed; and the kingdom shall not be left to other people; it shall break in pieces and *consume all these kingdoms*, and it shall stand forever.
Inasmuch as you saw that the sym:sym-stone[stone] was cut out of the *mountain* without hands, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver, and the gold — the great God has made known to the king what will come to pass after this.
The dream is certain, and its interpretation is sure.
____

In this case Daniel explains a vision of a statue with layers of gold, silver, bronze, and feet of clay and iron, and explains that this statue represents successive kingdoms.
Then a final kingdom comes and destroys the statue and rules for ever and ever.
The final kingdom of the statue is consumed when God’s kingdom comes as a stone that grows into a mountain over the entire earth.

And that sym:sym-stone[stone] repays a closer look.
It is __“cut out… without hands”__ (Daniel 2:45) — and _to cut_ link:/books/symbolic-language/daniel-unsealed/[is the covenant verb] (Genesis 15:18).footnote:fn33karath[Covenants are cut, not made — _karath_ — as the link:/books/symbolic-language/daniel-unsealed/[Daniel Unsealed] chapter showed.]
A stone cut by no human hand is a covenant cut by God Himself — and Scripture names the sym:sym-rock[Rock]: __“that sym:sym-rock[Rock] was Christ”__ (1 Corinthians 10:4), the covenant in person; __“the sym:sym-stone[stone] which the builders refused”__ is become __“the head stone of the corner”__ (Psalm 118:22).
So the stone and the rock are the covenant, and the mountain it grows into is that covenant become a kingdom over all the earth.

[quote.scripture, Daniel 2:38-39 (NKJV)]
____
You are this head of gold.
After you shall arise another *kingdom* inferior to yours; then another, a third *kingdom* of bronze, which shall *rule over all the earth*.
____

The fact that the mountain is described as growing and covering the entire earth is another clue that this isn’t a physical mountain.
The analogy from Daniel is carried on in Isaiah.

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 2:2-3]
____
It shall come to pass in the latter days that the *mountain* of the house of the Lord shall be established as the highest of the *mountains*, and shall be lifted up above the *hills*; and all the *nations* shall flow to it, and many peoples shall come.
____

Then see the same symbolic meaning applied in Jeremiah, describing Babylon the Great, which rules over the whole earth and destroys nation after nation.
Here Jeremiah tells us this destroying mountain will become a burnt mountain.

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 51:25]
____
“Behold, I am against you, O *destroying mountain*, Who destroys all the earth,” says the Lord.
“And I will stretch out My hand against you, Roll you down from the rocks, And make you a *burnt mountain*.”
____

And now we have Daniel, Revelation, Isaiah, and Jeremiah all agree on the meaning of this symbol of a Mountain as a Kingdom.
With this knowledge we can now gain greater insight into many other scriptures.
Consider the account in Revelation of a flaming mountain thrown into the sea:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 8:8-9]
____
Then the second angel sounded his trumpet, and something like a *huge mountain*, all ablaze, was *thrown into the* sym:sym-sea[sea].
A third of the *sea* turned into blood, a third of the *living creatures* in the sea died….
____

So how do we know that this isn’t a literal mountain in this context?
The answer is in the phrase __“thrown into the sea,”__ the __“sea turning into blood,”__ and the __“creatures in the sea,”__ all in the same sentence.
In a later chapter I will show the evidence that sym:sym-sea[sea] is a symbol that means the masses of people, the population of the earth, and that sym:sym-living[living creatures], sym:sym-beast[beast], and sym:sym-beast[animals] represent gentile nations.

So symbolically Revelation 8:8-9 is saying that when a __“huge mountain”__ — a world empire — is thrown down, a third of the people will die and a third of the nations will be destroyed.
This implies that, on the order of two billion people, will die when this mountain is thrown down.

One final supporting piece of evidence is that a __“stone, rock, mountain, etc.”__ being __“thrown into the sea”__ to __“never rise again”__ is the prophetic symbol tied to the fall of Babylon the Great.
And thus symbols can be comprised of both words and catch-phrases.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 17:20]
____
So Jesus said to them, “Because of your unbelief; for assuredly, I say to you, if you have sym:sym-faith[faith] *as a mustard* sym:sym-seed[seed], you will say to this *mountain*, ‘Move from here to there,’ and it will move; and nothing will be impossible for you.”
____

[quote.scripture, Matthew 21:21]
____

____

[quote.scripture, Mark 11:23]
____
For assuredly, I say to you, whoever says to this *mountain*, ‘Be removed and be *cast into the sea*,’ and does not doubt in his heart, but believes that those things he says will be done, he will have whatever he says.
____

So how do we know that this mountain is not a literal mountain, or merely an impossible obstacle, in this context?
Because the context is a fig tree and a mustard tree — and sym:sym-tree[trees] are another symbol that Scripture defines as kingdoms or nations — and of course __“thrown into the sea”__ is a symbol clearly referring to Babylon the Great, as the Fall of Babylon chapter demonstrated — and as the next chapter will trace from the Song of the Sea onward.

What we learn from this is that the symbolic language of the Bible is interconnected: symbols are defined in terms of symbols and phrases, as well as the verbs, adjectives, and other personifications used with the symbol.
For this reason, we often see the same set of symbols connected over and over.

Now consider this poetic picture from Joel:

[quote.scripture, Joel 2:2]
____
A day of darkness and gloom, A day of sym:sym-cloud[clouds] and of thick [dark] mist, Like the *dawn* spread over the *mountains*;
____

The picture is of __“dawn,”__ __“mist,”__ or __“fog”__ spread over the __“mountains,”__ which is quite common in early morning and aligns with the visual of a __“day of clouds and thick dark mist.”__
The Hebrew word for __“dawn,”__ _shachar_, link:/books/symbolic-language/lucifers-declared-plan/[names the dark before sunrise] — the first light while the sky is still quite dark, before the sun burns away the fog.footnote:fn33shachar[Derived — _shachar_ written on the letters of blackness — in the link:/books/symbolic-language/lucifers-declared-plan/[Lucifer's Declared Plan] chapter.]
Now, with the greater context, we have dark clouds of smoke spread over the nations.
This is just one of dozens of verses that gain new meaning when you apply the symbolic meaning of mountain.

[quote.scripture, Matthew 24:15-16]
____
So when you see the abomination of desolation spoken of by the prophet Daniel, standing in the holy place (let the reader understand), then let those who are in Judea *flee to the mountains*.
____

Here is a verse that contains instructions for the future, and to understand the instructions it is helpful to know that __“mountains”__ means nations.
In other words: get out of Judea.
In 70 AD, those who survived the fall of Jerusalem were scattered to the nations, so this prophecy has been fulfilled in this sense already.

[quote.scripture, Luke 21:20-21]
____
When you see Jerusalem being surrounded by armies, you will know that its desolation is near.
Then let those who are in Judea *flee to the mountains*, let those in the *city* get out, and let those in the country not enter the city.
____

Another verse that is poetic on the surface, but carries a far more practical message once you read it through the lens of the Bible’s Symbolic Language:

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 50:6]
____
My people have been sym:sym-lost[lost sheep]; their *shepherds* have led them astray, in the *mountains* they have turned them away. *From mountain to hill* they have gone; they have forgotten their resting place.
____

Here His people are the sheep; the shepherds are the pastors and teachers who led people astray among the nations they were scattered to.
His people have gone from nation to city and have forgotten their home.

[quote.scripture, Isaiah 40:4]
____
Every sym:sym-valley[valley] shall be exalted, and every *mountain and hill* shall be made low.
____

This verse from Isaiah now has far deeper meaning: the nations and cities will be humbled (brought low).
And those who are humble, obedient even to death, will be exalted and lifted up.
The mountains are high places, proud places, and they contrast with their opposite, the valleys — those enslaved by the mountains.

[quote.scripture, Jeremiah 16:16]
____
Behold, I will send for many *fishers*, said the LORD, and they shall fish them; and after will I send for many *hunters*, and they shall hunt them from *every mountain*, and from *every hill*, and out of the *holes of the rocks*.
____

Here is another case where mountains (nations) are used with other symbols: fishermen and hunters.
Here Jeremiah is prophesying about Jesus calling people to be fishers of men.
Then, those who are not caught as fish will be hunted down from among the nations, in every city, and out of every bunker.
The holes of the rocks are then referenced in Revelation:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 6:14-16]
____
And the heaven departed as a scroll when it is rolled together; and *every mountain and* sym:sym-island[island] were moved out of their places.
And the kings of the earth, and the great men, and the rich men, and the chief captains, and the mighty men, and every slave, and every free man, hid themselves in the dens and in the *rocks of the mountains*; and said to the *mountains and rocks*, Fall on us, and hide us.
____

And now that you know that mountain means a kingdom, we should ask ourselves: what does an sym:sym-island[island] represent?

[quote.scripture, Revelation 16:19-20]
____
And great *Babylon* came in remembrance before God, to give to her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath.
And *every* sym:sym-island[island] *fled away*, and the *mountains were not found*.
____

Are literal mountains going to disappear, or is this clearly talking about the third of the nations thrown into the sea, never to rise again?
When I read this, it struck me as odd that an island could flee, or escape.
It is the personification of island that led me to research the sym:sym-island[island] symbol, which we will cover in a future chapter.

And now I will close this chapter on mountains with one final notable use, when Jesus was tested in the desert:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 4:8]
____
Again, the devil takes him up into an *exceeding high mountain*, and shows him *all the kingdoms of the world*, and the glory of them;
____

Here we see an exceedingly high mountain from which all the other kingdoms of the earth can be seen.
In other words, he is taken to a kingdom ruling over all the other kings of the earth — likely Babylon the Great.

By now you should see the pattern: Scripture reliably and consistently uses the word mountain as a way to say __“nation”__ or __“kingdom”__ in many different contexts across the whole of Scripture.
I have only addressed a few of the notable uses, but once you internalize this symbol you will find it used in countless other places in profound ways.

In the process of exploring the mountain symbol, we came across many other symbols: the sym:sym-sea[sea] (the masses of mankind), the hills (cities), the sym:sym-tree[tree] and the fig tree (kingdoms and Israel), the stone and the rock (the covenant), the sym:sym-beast[beasts] and living creatures (the gentile nations), the sym:sym-island[island], the fishers and the hunters, the valley, and Babylon the Great.
The sea, the trees and their beasts, and the island are taken up in the chapters just ahead, each defined the same way we defined the mountain — by letting Scripture interpret Scripture.
Babylon the Great we have already traced through Part Five, and the glossary carries the rest.
