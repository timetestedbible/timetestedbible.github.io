The last chapter ended with a question about calendars, and it is fair to ask why a book about symbols cares.
Here is why: the office the enemy set his heart to seize — the seat over the appointed times, worked from the far recess of the moon’s darkness — is the agenda Daniel heard named without a figure: he __“shall think to *change times and laws*”__ (Daniel 7:25).
And that office has a symbol of its own in Scripture; the symbol is a *pearl*.
Jesus hung an entire parable on it and left the parable unkeyed: a merchant seeking goodly pearls finds __“*one pearl of great price*,”__ sells all, and buys it (Matthew 13:45-46).

[discrete]
=== The Pearls the Translators Buried

The link:/books/symbolic-language/the-parables-of-the-kingdom/[Parables of the Kingdom] chapter already dug these pearls out from under the King James' __“rubies”__: the Hebrew _peninim_ is, by Strong's own definition, __“probably *a pearl* (as round).”__
Jesus left this parable unkeyed — no interpretation recorded — so the derivation stands where that chapter left it: on *our* method alone, tested like everything else and held fast if it proves good.

Now lay the pearls out and look at them properly, for everything that follows hangs on what a pearl *is*.
sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] is priced above *pearls* (Proverbs 3:15; 8:11; Job 28:18), and the seeker who searches for her __“as for *hid treasures*”__ (Proverbs 2:4) is himself the merchant of the parable.
And Solomon prices her in the merchant's own terms — __“the *merchandise* of it is better than the merchandise of silver”__ (3:14) — a word Scripture stamps *good* in exactly two more places: the woman of this same book, whose __“*merchandise* is *good*”__ (31:18), and the kingdom's __“merchant man, seeking *goodly* pearls”__ (Matthew 13:45).
Three texts, one thread — sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom], the woman, the pearl: the *good merchandise*.
And keep reading the wisdom passage, for it walks straight into this chapter's subject: __“*Length of days* is in her right hand”__ (3:16) — and the moon holds exactly that: the *length* of the month's days, twenty-nine or thirty, is hers to declare.
The root under __“length”__ keeps a second sense in the same letters — _arik_, __“it was not *meet* for us”__ (Ezra 4:14), Strong's __“reaching to a given point; to suit”__ — so her right hand holds not only the count of the days but their *fitting*: the appointed day, the day of meeting, the sym:sym-meat[meat] given __“in *due season*”__ (Matthew 24:45).
And then, __“The LORD by *wisdom* hath founded the earth; by sym:sym-understanding[understanding] hath he *established the heavens*”__ (3:19).
The wisdom priced above pearls is the wisdom that built the sky: the hand that holds the days hung the lights that count them — __“when he prepared the heavens, *I was there*”__ (8:27).
The *virtuous woman* — the one sym:sym-marriage[bride] — is __“far above *pearls*”__ (Proverbs 31:10), drawn up out of the sym:sym-sea[sea] of the nations, and formed the way a pearl is formed: slowly, in hiddenness, around a wound.

And stay a moment with that woman, for Solomon paints her portrait, and the portrait keeps doing strange things for a housewife.
She __“riseth also while it is yet sym:sym-night[night], and giveth sym:sym-meat[meat] to her household”__ (Proverbs 31:15); __“her sym:sym-lamp[candle] goeth *not out by night*”__ (31:18).
She is __“like the *merchants'* ships; she bringeth her food *from afar*”__ (31:14) — provision carried across a great distance, and the merchant-word again.
She __“maketh *fine linen*, and selleth it; and delivereth girdles unto the *merchant*”__ (31:24) — the merchant a third time, and the cloth she weaves is the bright white weave the sym:sym-marriage[bride] wears at the wedding: __“arrayed in *fine linen*, clean and *white*”__ (Revelation 19:8).
__“She *looketh well* to the ways of her household, and eateth not the bread of *idleness*”__ (31:27) — and the verb under __“looketh well”__ is the watchman's, the very word of __“I have made thee a *watchman* unto the house of Israel”__ (Ezekiel 3:17): she keeps the night watch, and no phase of her is idle.
Her household is __“clothed with sym:sym-scarlet[scarlet]”__ (31:21); she does her husband good __“*all the days* of her life”__ (31:12); __“Her *husband* is known *in the gates*, when he sitteth among the *elders* of the land”__ (31:23) — both spouses of this portrait keep appearing at gates.
And at the end her children rise and call her blessed — __“her *husband* also, and he *praiseth* her”__ (31:28).
And the portrait's very last line sets her praise at a threshold: __“let her own works praise her *in the gates*”__ (31:31).
A woman who rises in the dark, whose lamp burns the whole night, who keeps the watchman’s watch, who ferries food from far away and weaves the shining white linen, dressed in scarlet, praised by her husband, her works praising her in the gates, priced above pearls — Solomon is describing more than any one wife, and more waits beneath the vowel points of her portrait; before this chapter closes, both her poem and wisdom's will be read again, whole, on the bare letters.

And mark the pearl's properties as Scripture handles the figure.
It is *one*: the merchant found __“one pearl of great price”__ and stopped collecting, for the kingdom is not a collection but one bride, bought whole.
It is *round and complete*: the roundness sits inside Strong's definition itself.
It is *white* — a bearer of soft, borrowed light out of darkness — and it is born of the *waters*.
Then Revelation prices the city's entrances in it:

[quote.scripture, Revelation 21:21]
____
And the twelve sym:sym-door[gates] were twelve *pearls*; *every several gate was of one pearl*…
____

The Greek doubles the point — _each one_ of the gates, _of one_ pearl — and the Hebrew Revelation doubles it the same way: every *single* gate, of *one* pearl.
Not gates studded with pearls: each gate *is* one pearl — one, complete, whole.
So the pearl, wherever Scripture spends it, is the one complete thing of great price — and it is a *gate*.
Now ask the merchant's question: has the King hung such a thing anywhere in plain sight — one, round, white, complete, and standing at a door?

[discrete]
=== The One Pearl of the Night Sky

Look up.
Of all the moon's phases and all the lights of heaven, exactly one is white, round, and complete: the night sky owns precisely one pearl.
And Scripture had already assigned that light its office: __“He appointed the *moon* for *seasons*”__ — _moedim_, the appointed times (Psalm 104:19); the lights were hung __“for signs, and for seasons”__ from the fourth day (Genesis 1:14).
Even the bride wears its face: __“Who is she that looketh forth as the morning, *fair as the moon*, clear as the sun…?”__ (Song of Solomon 6:10) — the woman priced above pearls, fair as the one pearl of the sky.

And Scripture set that pairing at the pattern's founding.
In Joseph's dream — the dream that taught the Sun, Moon, and Stars chapter to read the lights — the sun is the father and the moon is the *mother* (Genesis 37:9-10); and the sun comes out of his chamber __“as a *bridegroom*”__ (Psalm 19:5).
If the sun comes forth a bridegroom, the light that answers him is a bride — and at the fulness she answers him exactly: she rises as he sets, the two great lights face to face across the whole earth, she reflecting the whole of his light back to him.
Nor does this quarrel with the faithful witness of that chapter, for the moon's office is *reflected fulness*, and Scripture gives that office to two who are one — the Son, who does nothing of Himself, and the sym:sym-marriage[bride], who wears everything her Bridegroom gives her — one flesh in the marriage of the Lamb.

=== The Letters Confirm It

If the pearl has led truly, the letters should be waiting at the same spot — and they are.
Scripture keeps a __“fulness”__ overhead, and the King James buried it exactly as it buried the pearls.
The Hebrew is _keseh_ — Strong's: __“properly, *fulness* or the *full moon*”__ — and most translations render it as what it is:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 81:3, NKJV]
____
Blow the trumpet at the time of the New Moon, at the *full* sym:sym-moon[moon], on our solemn feast day.
____

Here the King James stands nearly alone with its __“in the time appointed”__; the NKJV, the ESV, the NASB, the Jewish Publication Society's version — __“at the full moon for our feast-day”__ — and Smith's Literal Translation all read *full moon*.
And the oldest translation of all leans the same way: the Septuagint, made centuries before Messiah, renders the day __“in the *glorious* day of your feast”__ — the Greek is _eusēmos_, *well-marked, conspicuous*, built on _sēma_, a *sign*, the very root the Greek of Genesis 1:14 hangs the lights on, __“for *signs*.”__
Whatever else those translators heard in _keseh_, they heard the day the sky cannot hide — and of the moon's phases exactly one is conspicuous, while the dark conjunction is the one day the sky shows no sign at all.
And mark what the verse does not say: there is no __“and”__ between the new moon and the full moon — one day, not two.
Some translations insert an __“and”__ to split them apart; the Hebrew, rendered literally, in its own order, runs the other way — _you shall blow with the new-moon trumpet in the full moon for the day of our festival sacrifice_ — the full moon *is* the head of the month, the feast-day of Numbers 10:10 and 28:11.

Solomon uses the same word for the day the traveling husband returns: __“he will come home at the *day appointed*”__ (Proverbs 7:20) — at the _keseh_, the full moon — and there it is spelled כסא, letter for letter the spelling of _kisse'_, __“throne.”__
The last chapter stood on that identity from the enemy's side; here is the LORD's side of it: both words grow from one root, _kasah_, to cover, to fill — the throne is the covered seat, the full moon the filled light — and Job holds the two senses up in a single verse: __“He holdeth back the face of *his throne*, and spreadeth *his cloud* upon it”__ (Job 26:9), the veiled face of His full sym:sym-moon[moon].
And if it is asked which way the covering runs — whether the throne-word is covered in darkness or covered in light — Scripture describes the throne itself.
Daniel saw the throne-room itself, and every detail belongs to this chapter:

[quote.scripture, Daniel 7:9 — rendered from the Aramaic]
____
I beheld till the *thrones* were *set in place*, and the *Ancient of days* did sit, whose garment was *white as snow*, and the hair of his head like the *pure wool*: his throne was like the *fiery flame*, and his wheels as burning fire.
____

The thrones are *set* — placed like lights in their stations.footnote:dan79set[The King James prints __“the thrones were cast down”__; the Aramaic verb is one of placing, and the newer translations read it so — the thrones were set, not overthrown.]
And the title under __“Ancient of days”__ is built on a root of moving onward, going before — read once, He is the Ancient of Days; read again, He is the *Foremost of Days*: the One at the head of the days, seated where the days begin.
His garment white as snow, His hair like the pure wool, His throne flame, His wheels burning fire — and the plural on __“wheels”__ is the ending this book met on Daniel's sevens: an intensive, not a count — *the wheel complete*, the perfect circle of fire.
Whiteness enthroned on light; brightness round about it, __“as the appearance of the sym:sym-bow[bow] that is in the sym:sym-cloud[cloud] in the day of rain”__ (Ezekiel 1:26-28); and its Occupant __“coverest thyself with *light* as with a garment”__ (Psalm 104:2).
If the throne is the _keseh_, Daniel's vision draws what the letters spell: the full moons set in their stations, and the Head of Days taking His seat — white, complete, covered in light — where the month begins.
Then the psalm that gave this book the faithful witness says it in so many words — the same breath that establishes the throne sets it among the lights:

[quote.scripture, Psalm 89:36-37]
____
His seed shall endure for ever, and *his throne as the* sym:sym-sun[sun] before me.
*It shall be established for ever as the* sym:sym-moon[moon], and as a sym:sym-faithful-witness[*faithful witness*] in heaven.
____

The throne as the *sun*; the throne established for ever as the *moon* — _kisse'_ and the moon set side by side not by wordplay but by plain simile, and the throne is a throne of light.
Where darkness stands near it, it stands *outside*, as a veil: __“clouds and darkness are *round about* him”__ (Psalm 97:2) — round about, not within — the cloud of Job 26:9 spread *upon* the face, holding the light back.
The _keseh_ is covered in light; only the counterfeit is covered in dark.

=== The Gate That Opens

The sky is built like a sanctuary: __“In them hath he set a *tabernacle* for the sun, which is as a *bridegroom coming out of his chamber*”__ (Psalm 19:4-5) — the lights come and go through the doors of a tent whose front, like the tabernacle's, faces the sunrise, the sym:sym-east[east].
Now hear Ezekiel on the gates of the house:

[quote.scripture, Ezekiel 46:1]
____
Thus saith the Lord GOD; The sym:sym-door[gate] of the inner court that looketh toward the sym:sym-east[east] shall be *shut the six working days*; but on the *sabbath* it shall be opened, and in the day of the *new* sym:sym-moon[moon] it shall be opened.
____

Three kinds of days — working days, sabbaths, new-moon days (Amos knew the third kind too: __“When will the *new moon* be gone, that we may sell corn?”__ Amos 8:5) — and on the day that begins the month, the east gate stands open.
A gate is opened because something is meant to come through it.
And exactly one phase enters by the east gate at the fall of night: the *full* moon rises in the east at the very hour the sun sets in the west — through the front door of the sky's tabernacle, holding authority the whole night through, the faithful witness at maximum.
The sliver, by contrast, is hunted low in the *west*, at sunset, beside the sun — and the sheepfold has a rule about entrances: __“He that entereth not by the sym:sym-door[door]… but climbeth up some other way, the same is a *thief* and a robber. But he that entereth in by the door is the *shepherd*”__ (John 10:1-2); the prince of Ezekiel's house comes and goes __“by the way of the gate that looketh toward the *east*”__ (Ezekiel 46:12).
The true light enters at the front sym:sym-door[gate]; the counterfeit climbs in from the dark side — which is precisely the address his heart chose in the last chapter.

=== One Pearl, One Gate

Now set the pieces together.
The pearl is the full moon; the city's pearl-gates are His appointed times — for the _moedim_ are, by the word's own meaning, *meetings*: the fixed days on which the King receives.
Twelve gates; twelve months of full moons; the sym:sym-stars[stars] of God gathered into the twelve constellations, the __“Mazzaroth”__ brought forth __“in his season”__ (Job 38:32), through which the full moon makes its yearly round.
And there Solomon's woman collects what her portrait promised: her works __“praise her *in the gates*”__ — praised in the twelve gates her fulness passes through, before her husband's face; and the husband is at home there too, __“known in the gates,”__ seated __“among the *elders* of the land”__ — the sym:sym-sun[sun] on his own circuit through the same twelve.
The merchant who sold all he had for *one pearl of great price* was not buying jewelry.
He was buying his entry at the King's gate — the appointed time of meeting — and Isaiah had already welded the twin parables into one sentence: __“sym:sym-wisdom[wisdom] and knowledge shall be the stability of thy *times*… the sym:sym-fear[fear] of the LORD is his *treasure*”__ (Isaiah 33:6).
The treasure and the times; the pearl and the gate.
__“It is the glory of God to *conceal* a thing: but the honour of kings is to *search out* a matter”__ (Proverbs 25:2) — He hid the pearl where only a merchant who would sell everything looks.

=== The Poems in Plain Language

The first thing the whole texts show that the fragments could not: the acrostic *quotes* the wisdom poem, word for word.
__“Her merchandise is good”__ (31:18) is, in the Hebrew, letter for letter the clause of 3:14 — כִּי־טוֹב סַחְרָהּ — one clause, two poems.
And Proverbs interchanges the subject in its own grammar: the find-verb of 3:13 returns exactly twice in the book — __“whoso findeth *me*”__ (8:35, Wisdom speaking) and __“whoso findeth *a wife*”__ (18:22).
sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] and the sym:sym-marriage[bride] hold one slot in Solomon's own sentences; the two poems are one portrait, priced in sym:sym-pearl[pearls] at both ends.

Now say plainly what the portrait paints.
She rises while it is yet night, and her lamp goes not out in the night — of all the lights a reader has ever seen, exactly one rises as the dark falls and burns until morning: the *full moon*, which climbs the east gate at sunset and holds the sky the whole night through.
She deals out the covenant's sym:sym-meat[meat] and the *appointed portion* — the word is _choq_, the __“ordinances of the sym:sym-moon[moon] and of the sym:sym-stars[stars]”__ by sym:sym-night[night] (Jeremiah 31:35).
Her palms hold *the circuit* — the round of the year through the twelve gates.
She is more *bright* than pearls — and __“bright”__ is the King James' own word for the moon: __“the *moon* walking in *brightness*”__ (Job 31:26), the comparison priced in the moon-verse's own vocabulary.
And the *length of days* rests in her right hand: the month's own length, twenty-nine days or thirty, is declared at her fulness — with __“riches and glory”__ in her left, for Strong's reads _yamin_ as __“the south”__ and the left hand's word as __“properly dark, the north”__: days in her bright hand, glory even in her dark one.

And where the portrait outruns any housewife, it fits the Bridegroom's own record — the moon shows the face of the One whose light she bears, and He fills in every gap.
Her *palm* is spread to the sym:sym-poor[poor] — the _kaph_, the hollow covering palm God laid over Moses while the glory passed by (Exodus 33:22) — and the poor are the King's signature: __“He shall deliver the needy when he crieth; the *poor* also”__ (Psalm 72:12), in the very psalm where they fear Him __“with the sun and *before the face of* the moon”__ through all revolutions of time (72:5, as the letters run — the King James' __“endure”__ is supplied; the Hebrew says *before the face*, and the full moon is the one night the two lights stand face to face).
She considers a sym:sym-field[field] and buys it, and plants a vineyard with the fruit of her palms — the Lord of the parable *planted the vineyard* (Matthew 21:33), which Isaiah names: __“the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of *Israel*”__ (Isaiah 5:7); and He *bought the field* for the treasure hidden in it, and __“the field is the *world*”__ (Matthew 13:44, 38).
And she *strengthens* what she plants — girding her loins with strength, strengthening her arms — where the counterfeit __“didst *weaken* the nations”__ (Isaiah 14:12) and wears out the saints (Daniel 7:25); one plants and strengthens, the other wears down.

Her household fears no *snow* — no whiteness — for they are clothed *in double*, the reading the oldest translations kept: the Septuagint's __“garments of double texture,”__ the Vulgate's __“double garments.”__
Her clothing is *white linen* — _shesh_, by Strong's definition __“bleached stuff, white linen.”__
Her husband — the sym:sym-sun[sun] of this portrait — is known in the gates, seated __“among the *elders*”__: the Sun, Moon, and Stars chapter already seated that bench, the elders among the host of heaven, the court in the twelve figures of the sky.
He __“praiseth her”__ — and the verb is _halal_, whose first sense is *to shine* (the King James' own __“when his candle *shined*,”__ Job 29:3): he *makes her shine*, as the sun makes the moon.
And she *laughs at the last day* — beneath the laugh, in the same letters, stands the _shachaq_, the sky where the __“faithful witness”__ is established (Psalm 89:37); she can laugh at the day because she knows its date — sym:sym-wisdom[Wisdom] laughs at calamity (Proverbs 1:26) for the reason the psalm gives: __“he seeth that his *day is coming*”__ (Psalm 37:13).

And the poem never says *moon*; the Hebrew of the portrait contains no moon-word at all.
It paints a light that rises in the dark, shines the whole night with a shining her husband gives her, deals the appointed portions, holds the circuit — and withholds the name to the last line, where her works __“make her shine *in the gates*.”__
The Song of Songs supplies what the portrait withheld, singing the same two praise-verbs over the same one-above-the-daughters woman: __“The daughters saw her, and *blessed* her… and they *praised* her.
Who is she that looketh forth as the morning, *fair as the moon*, clear as the sun…?”__ (Song of Solomon 6:9-10).
The poem paints the portrait; the Song signs it.

Wisdom's poem reads the same way whole.
She is a sym:sym-tree[tree] of life to them that lay hold on her, and blessed is the one *holding her fast* — _tamak_, the verb of the palms holding the circuit (31:19), and the verb of Moses' hands on the mountain, held up *steady* — the word is _emunah_, the *faithful-witness* word — __“until the going down of the sun,”__ while Joshua *weakened* Amalek with the counterfeit's own rare verb (Exodus 17:12-13; the verb of Isaiah 14:12 — three occurrences in all of Scripture).
The poem ends where the letters keep leading: __“by understanding hath he *established* the heavens”__ — _konen_, the verb of __“the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*”__ (Psalm 8:3) — and its last line sets the dew dropping from the _shachaqim_, the witness's own sky, beside the *broken-open* deeps of Genesis 7:11, the calendar-stamped verse the Noah Uncovered chapter read twice.

=== Every Word Weighed

The rule of the What Is the Point? chapter cuts both ways: the pointing gets a vote, not a veto — and neither does the re-reading, until the letters and the witnesses agree.
So here is every departure, weighed; whatever is not listed is the King James untouched.

*In wisdom's poem (Proverbs 3:13-20):*

- __draweth out__ (3:13) — _puq_, Strong's "to issue, draw out"; the King James' "getteth" flattens a verb of drawing forth.
- __going-round__ (3:14) — _sachar_, "to travel round (as a pedlar); traffic" — the merchant-word of 31:14, 18; rendered by its own root-sense.
- __harvest__ (3:14) — _tevuah_, "income, i.e. produce"; the King James renders it "increase" and "fruits" elsewhere — the field-word, kept agricultural.
- __bright__ (3:15) — _yaqar_: the King James' own rendering at Job 31:26 — "the moon walking in *brightness*" — the one place it describes a light.
- __pearls__ (3:15; 31:10) — _peninim_, Strong's "probably a pearl (as round)"; the chapter's founding recovery.
- __splendour / wholeness__ (3:17) — _noam_, "agreeableness, i.e. delight, suitableness, splendor"; _shalom_, "safe, i.e. well, happy... completeness" — peace as the whole and unbroken.
- __holdeth her fast__ (3:18) — _tamak_, "to sustain; to obtain, keep fast" — the verb of 31:19.
- __established__ (3:19) — _konen_ (H3559), the verb of Psalm 8:3, "the moon and the stars, which thou hast *ordained*."
- __broken open / skies__ (3:20) — _baqa_, the verb of Genesis 7:11 ("were all the fountains of the great deep *broken up*"); _shachaqim_, the sky-word of Psalm 89:37.

*In the bride's portrait (Proverbs 31:10-31):*

- __never diminish__ (31:11) — _chaser_: Strong's King James column reads "decrease, abated, fail" — the gain that does not wane.
- __palms__ (31:13, 16, 19, 20) — _kaph_, "the hollow hand or palm," kept visible because the poem itself sets _kaph_ beside _yad_ inside single verses (vv19-20): the palm and the hand are distinguished by the poet, not by us.
- __the one who travels round__ (31:14) — _sachar_ again, the same going-round as 3:14.
- __the covenant's meat / the appointed portion__ (31:15) — _teref_ is the noun of Psalm 111:5, "he hath given *meat* unto them that fear him: he will ever be mindful of his *covenant*"; _choq_ is "an enactment; an appointment (of time)" — the King James' own "set time" (Job 14:13), and the word of Jeremiah 31:35's moon-and-stars ordinances.
- __tasteth that it is good / in the night__ (31:18) — the Hebrew order restored: טָעֲמָה כִּי־טוֹב — "she tasteth that *it is good*," the _ki tov_ spoken over the light (Genesis 1:4); "in the night" is the written text (the kethib).
- __the circuit__ (31:19) — _pelekh_, "from an unused root meaning to be round; a circuit... also a spindle (as whirled)"; the King James itself renders it as a district ("part," Nehemiah 3). Both spin: the housewife's whorl and the sky's round.
- __in double__ (31:21) — the letters שנים read as _shnayim_, "double": so the Septuagint ("garments of double texture"), the Vulgate, and Smith's Literal Translation; the pointing reads _shanim_, "scarlet." The elder witnesses carry the double; the same letters also spell "years" — a revolution of time.
- __white linen__ (31:22) — _shesh_, "bleached stuff, i.e. white linen" — the whiteness sits inside the plain lexeme; the King James' "silk" is the departure, not ours.
- __laugheth at the last day__ (31:25) — _sachaq_ + _le-yom acharon_: so Young's Literal ("she rejoiceth at a latter day"), the Douay-Rheims ("she shall laugh in the latter day"), and Smith's Literal; the identical phrase is "the *last day*" at Isaiah 30:8. And unpointed, שחק is also _shachaq_ — the witness's sky — a second reading standing in the letters.
- __keepeth the watch / walkings__ (31:27) — _tsofiyah_, the watchman's verb ("I have made thee a *watchman*," Ezekiel 3:17); _halichot_, "a walking, a procession" — the family of Job 31:26's moon "*walking* in brightness."
- __maketh her shine / she it is that shall shine / make her shine in the gates__ (31:28, 30, 31) — _halal_, whose base sense is "to *shine*" before it is "to praise" (the King James' own "when his candle *shined*," Job 29:3; "the moon walking in brightness" verse sits in the same field): to praise her is to make her shine, and both senses are true at once.
- __gone up above them all__ (31:29) — _alah_, "thou hast gone up": so Young's and Smith's, literally.
- __vapour__ (31:30) — _hevel_, "breath, vapour" — Ecclesiastes' word, rendered literally.

Weigh them all; the reading asks no favors.
Every changed word is an attested sense of the very letters the scribes counted, most of them the King James' own renderings from other verses — and the elder witnesses, the Greek and the Latin, already read the doubled garments a thousand years before the points were fixed.
The pointing keeps the housewife; the letters keep the housewife *and* the light she was always painted from.
Both are true — that is how this language testifies.

=== Pearls Before Swine

Jesus spent the pearl-word one other time, and it is a warning:

[quote.scripture, Matthew 7:6]
____
Give not that which is *holy* unto the sym:sym-dog[dogs], neither cast ye your *pearls* before *swine*, lest they *trample them under their feet*, and turn again and rend you.
____

The dogs and the swine are one family — unclean sym:sym-beast[beasts], the nations outside the covenant table.
And what swine do to pearls is exactly what Scripture says the nations do to holy things: they tread them underfoot — __“it is given unto the *Gentiles*: and the holy city shall they *tread under foot*”__ (Revelation 11:2); the sanctuary and the host __“*trodden under foot*”__ (Daniel 8:13); __“Jerusalem shall be *trodden down* of the Gentiles, until the *times* of the Gentiles be fulfilled”__ (Luke 21:24).
Mark the last word of that sentence: what the trampling runs until is a matter of *times*.
If the pearl is the appointed time of meeting, the warning reads whole: hand the holy times to the nations and they will not keep them — they will trample them, and turn and rend the hand that gave them.
Daniel named the plan — to change the times and the laws; this verse is the Lord's advance notice of how the theft would look on the ground — the pearl underfoot.

=== The Two Moons

Read this chapter and the last as the pair they are.
The enemy raises the moon covered in *darkness*; the LORD's sign is the same seat covered in *light* — the Father's whole face borne through the dark.
One moon reflects everything; the other reflects nothing — for a moon has no light of its own: it shows what its source gives it, and the source this one serves produces only dark.
One enters by the door; the other climbs up some other way.
And the sky itself stages the ambition, for the two moons stand in two different places: the full moon stands *opposite* the sun, face to face across the whole earth, and can never come between; only the moon covered in darkness can stand *over* the sun — slide before his face and put out the day at noon — and blot the sym:sym-stars[stars] from the same sky.
That is Daniel's warning drawn in one picture: the dark moon set above the Sun and the stars of God, ruling the appointed times from in front of the light — __“Woe unto them… that put *darkness for light*, and *light for darkness*”__ (Isaiah 5:20).
And the sym:sym-stars[stars] — the righteous, who keep time — follow one moon or the other.
That is why the parable's merchant sells *all*: the pearl is not an ornament on the kingdom; it is the gate into it, and there has only ever been one seller who could ask that price and one buyer who paid it gladly.

Grade this chapter the way the Parables of the Kingdom chapter graded everything, for the method matters more than any single finding.
Every link stands in the text: the pearl-word under the King James' __“rubies”__; the city's gates each of *one pearl*; the moon set over the _moedim_ from the fourth day; the fulness-word under __“throne”__ and __“time appointed”__ confirming where the pearl had already led; the east gate opened on the new-moon day; and the two poems rendered whole on the bare letters, every departure weighed word by word.
The chain that joins the links is a reading — this book's reading, offered to be tested, not swallowed.
And the reckoning that follows from it — where the month truly begins, how the sabbaths fall, what history did to the calendar and what the sky and the records of the nations still testify — is a book of its own: the complete case is the work of _Time Tested Tradition_.
This chapter needs only the symbol.
The kingdom has a gate; the gate is one complete pearl; and the merchant who understands what is being sold does not haggle.
