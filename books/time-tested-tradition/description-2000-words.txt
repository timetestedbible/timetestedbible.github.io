Time Tested Tradition: The Renewed Biblical Calendar (Second Edition) by Daniel Larimer is written for a simple, urgent problem: how can ordinary people keep the times God appointed when the maps we inherited may not be the map Scripture and the heavens require?

Most of us never ask. We inherit a day for rest, a method for months, and a story about the year of the cross, then assume the matter is settled. Teachers and pastors often cannot even consider another answer without risking congregation, platform, or livelihood. So the first case we heard keeps winning—until someone examines it.

This book begins with questions hard to unsee once asked. If the seventh day is a single global weekday from creation until now, how did separated peoples share one day-identity on a round earth before modern longitude and a man-made date line? If the lights were given for signs, appointed times, days, and years, why does obedience so often depend on experts, algorithms, or traditions Scripture never names as the standard? If good intentions still leave us in ignorance, what does it cost to refuse knowledge once the harder case is on the table?

Time Tested Tradition does not ask you to adopt a slogan on page one. It asks you to rebuild the calendar the way a careful case is built: fix principles of evidence first; define day, month, and year from Scripture and observable light; only then test competing week models against dated history that does not smuggle in the weekday answer in advance. The longest fights in Christian chronology—especially the year and week of the cross—often assume the Sabbath model first and shop for a year second. This book reverses that order so the terrain can judge the maps. An Evidence Outline, Glossary, and Scripture Index support fair dispute. Pastors risk platforms by asking; this book is for those who ask anyway. Freely reproducible complete and unmodified; other rights reserved. Start with the problem; let the chapters earn the answers. Then obey.
