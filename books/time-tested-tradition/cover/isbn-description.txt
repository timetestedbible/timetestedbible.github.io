Every believer inherits a calendar. Which day is the Sabbath, when the
month begins, which day opens the year — almost no one checks the
arithmetic behind the answers, because almost no one imagines it can be
checked.

Time Tested Tradition checks it. Taking Scripture and the primary
historical record together — Josephus, Philo, the Gospels, eclipses
documented in stone — it asks one question: if we can fix the years,
months, and weekdays of known events, which calendar matches the
testimony? The results are startling. On the most direct reading of
nearly every primary source, the crucifixion fell in AD 32 — a year in
which the inherited reckonings put the cross on a Monday, an
impossibility for rising on the third day. Either Scripture errs, or
the calendar handed down to us is not the calendar kept at the temple.

From that dilemma the book rebuilds the reckoning from the ground up:
the day beginning at first light; the month beginning at the full moon,
the faithful witness in the sky; the year anchored by the equinox; the
Sabbath counted from the renewed moon — the 8th, 15th, 22nd, and
29th — exactly where the manna, the Jordan crossing, and the
resurrection accounts place it. Daniel's seventy sevens, the jubilee
clock running from 1406 BC, and the Sign of Jonah are tested against
the same sky, and every computed date can be rerun by any reader with
free software and a set of coordinates.

This second edition adds the Sabbath chapter — showing from the law's
own grammar that the word names the whole week — the complete Sign of
Jonah, and a reorganized evidence outline.

Tradition is a map, and the map is not the terrain. This book walks the
terrain, and invites you to check every step.
