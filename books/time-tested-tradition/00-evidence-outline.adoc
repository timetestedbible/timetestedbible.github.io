[.atlas-guide]
The root question is which calendar reproduces the dated Passion sequence without changing its rules to obtain the desired weekday.
The controlling safeguard is to fix the crucifixion year from chronology that does not depend on a Sabbath model, and only then compare what each calendar predicts for that year.
The detailed branches still follow the order of development: define the day, month, year, and week; establish AD 32 independently; then apply the calendar test.
No conclusion needs to be accepted without following the evidence into the cited chapter.
Each main bullet names a question or body of evidence and ends with the page where the full discussion begins.
The indented lines show what the evidence is said to establish and which later conclusions depend on it.
Some branches extend to a fourth level when a statement itself rests on an independently dated source.
Read from the first branch downward to follow the complete argument, or use any page number to examine only the step you question.

=== Testing the Calendar After the Year Is Fixed

[.evidence-outline]
--
* Use fixed historical terrain to test competing calendar maps
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# A calendar cannot validate itself by choosing the crucifixion year that gives its preferred weekday.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# The year must first be derived without assuming the weekday or Sabbath model being tested.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Only then can the calendar models make distinct, falsifiable predictions for the same event.
* Fix the crucifixion year from the controlling chronology
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-tiberius-ministry[Luke places John's ministry in Tiberius's fifteenth year]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The birth and ministry sequence places Jesus before Passover AD 29.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-capture-reign[Josephus's Roman anchors place Herod's death in 1 BC]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧩# Herod's reign places the start of Temple construction about 17 BC.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-temple-forty-six[John's forty-six-year statement places the first named Passover in AD 30]
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-year-case[John's three Passovers place the crucifixion in AD 32]
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Sabbatical cycles, prophecy, and astronomical signs support the result but do not control this chain.
* Define the calendar models without using the AD 32 result
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/where-does-the-day-start/#atlas-day-location[The day must be identifiable at every inhabited location]
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Local observation lets the heavenly sign itself carry the date around the earth.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-day-start/#atlas-day-first-light[The calendar day begins at first light]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Creation, sacrifice, Passover, and resurrection narratives repeatedly use morning as the operative boundary.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-month-start/#atlas-month-start[The month begins at first light after the full moon]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The appointed lesser light rules the night with the stars, and Psalm 81:3 identifies the renewed moon with fullness.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-year-start/#atlas-year-start[The year begins with the first renewed moon on or after the spring equinox]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The first month is Abib, and its barley must be ready for First Fruits on the 16th.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-lunar-sabbath[The lunar model places four complete weeks after Renewed Moon Day]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Six working days followed by rest place the Sabbaths on the 8th, 15th, 22nd, and 29th.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-first-fruits[First Fruits acts as a calendar checksum]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# First Fruits is fixed on the 16th and is the day after a Sabbath.
* Test the proposed calendars against AD 32
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/passion-week/[The Gospel sequence requires Passover on the sixth day, the 15th as Sabbath, and resurrection on First Fruits, the 16th]
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-signs[Dark-moon and crescent calendars do not produce that sequence in AD 32]
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-lunar-sabbath[The lunar Sabbath produces the sequence by construction]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧩# The 14th is the sixth working day, the 15th is Sabbath, and the 16th begins the next week.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The chronology fixes the terrain at AD 32 before any Sabbath model is allowed to interpret it; the day, month, year, and week branches then define the maps to be tested against that fixed event.*

=== Establishing Where and When a Day Begins

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/where-does-the-day-start/#atlas-day-location[Why the calendar must answer where a day begins]
** A continuous weekly count needs a boundary shared by people who travel in opposite directions.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Magellan's surviving voyage record documents a one-day disagreement after circumnavigation.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Counting personal sunsets cannot tell returning travelers which local date is authoritative.
** No fixed biblical meridian is stated.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Jerusalem, the modern International Date Line, and other fixed lines are proposed reference points.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Each proposal adds a geographical rule that Scripture does not expressly give.
** A fixed reference point was not generally usable in the ancient world.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# Reliable longitude at sea was not practically solved until the modern era.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# A remote observer would need longitude, time difference, and lunar visibility at the reference point.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# That method makes obedience depend on elite calculation or rapid communication.
** A moving celestial boundary follows the observable sign.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Genesis assigns the lights to signs, appointed times, days, and years.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Local observation allows each community to identify the same kind of event without a man-made meridian.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The lunar sign's changing position naturally explains why the effective date boundary moves.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-day-start/#atlas-day-first-light[Why the day begins at first light rather than sunset]
** Creation presents light before darkness and names the light “day.”
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Genesis repeats “evening and morning” as the completed cycle.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Morning closes the night and begins the next active daylight period.
** Biblical narratives use morning as the practical boundary.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The Passover and departure sequence distinguishes the night, the following morning, and the next day's journey.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The women reach the tomb while it is still dark on the first day of the week.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Sacrificial leftovers remain until morning, marking the next permitted period.
** The vocabulary and repeated imagery point toward renewal at dawn.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Mercies are new in the morning, joy comes in the morning, and the sun begins its circuit from its chamber.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# _Boker_ conveys breaking forth into clarity, while _erev_ conveys evening mixture.
** Traditional evening-start proof texts do not require a universal sunset boundary.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Atonement's evening-to-evening command can be read as a special extension for that observance.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Nehemiah's gate closing can be read as practical enforcement rather than a definition of the day.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# “Unclean until evening” can mark sacrificial cleansing rather than a calendar change.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The proposed day is locally observable and begins when light first breaks, without requiring a fixed international meridian.*

=== Establishing When a Month Begins

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-month-start/#atlas-month-start[The creation mandate identifies the kind of sign to seek]
** The moon is appointed as a great light governing the night with the stars.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Genesis 1:14–16 assigns the lights to signs and appointed times.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Psalm 136:8–9 pairs the moon and stars as rulers of the night.
** The full moon uniquely matches that physical description.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# It rises near sunset, remains visible through the night, and appears with the stars.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# A crescent is brief and partial; a dark conjunction supplies no visible light.
* Psalm 81:3 directly relates the renewed moon to fullness
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The Hebrew line places _ḥodesh_ and _keseh_ in synonymous poetic parallelism.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Without inserting “and,” the two expressions describe one trumpet-blowing feast day.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# The competing reading treats them as two different dates or restricts the feast to a midmonth pilgrimage.
* The language of renewal and covering supports a fully illuminated moon
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# _Ḥadash_ is read as restoration to a complete state rather than the first trace of reappearance.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# _Keseh_ is read as the moon covered with light rather than hidden in darkness.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The Semitic cognates and the throne/faithful-witness imagery agree with fullness.
* Biblical narratives and physical signs test the interpretation
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Ezekiel's east gate opens on Renewed Moon Day.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# A full moon rises in the east as night begins; a young crescent is first sighted in the west.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# David can say in advance, “Tomorrow is the renewed moon.”
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# Approaching fullness is visibly predictable; first-crescent visibility is weather-sensitive.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Abraham's covenant night and the Exodus “selfsame day” place darkness near the 15th.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# A full-moon start naturally makes the middle of the month dark.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The darkness at the crucifixion fits the same monthly phase, although the reported duration remains supernatural.
* Received calendar practice does not define the biblical term by itself
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Dark-moon and crescent beginnings were widespread among surrounding nations.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Calling conjunction the scientific “new moon” assumes the very calendar beginning being tested.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Later Jewish practice is evidence of that practice, not a direct definition supplied by Scripture.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The cumulative case identifies the renewed moon with fullness and begins the month at first light after the full-moon night.*

=== Establishing When a Year Begins

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-year-start/#atlas-year-start[Scripture places the first month in spring]
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Exodus calls the Passover month the beginning of months and names it Abib.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Abib describes barley in the ear and capable of ripening for harvest.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# The year must begin early enough for barley to mature, but not so late that the harvest is already lost.
* sym:sym-firstfruits[First Fruits] supplies the agricultural deadline
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The barley wave sheaf is offered on the 16th of the first month.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The chosen first month therefore gives the crop about half a lunar month to reach harvest readiness.
** The name has a broader generic use elsewhere.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Scripture also names first fruits of wheat, wine, and oil.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Those later harvest offerings do not move the capitalized First Fruits observance from the first-month 16th.
* The spring equinox provides the solar boundary
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The lights mark years, while _tekufah_ names the annual turning.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# Barley growth follows the solar season, so an equinox rule connects the lunar month to spring agriculture.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The equinox can be observed globally through the sun's rising and setting positions and the balance of day and night.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# The first renewed moon on or after the equinox yields one repeatable rule without a central authority.
* Competing year-start rules are tested against stability and harvest
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Direct barley inspection is geographically local and varies by field and weather.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# A rule tied to Spica can align in some eras but drifts against the seasons through precession.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# The calculated Jewish calendar can begin the first month before the equinox.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧩# That early start is required by common AD 33 crucifixion reconstructions.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# It makes barley readiness by the 16th less plausible under the agricultural constraint.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# When equinox, Abib barley, and the Virgo sign agree, all proposed witnesses identify the same month.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# The present case acknowledges ambiguity in a year when those signs would diverge.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The working rule begins the year with the first renewed moon on or after the spring equinox, while Abib barley and First Fruits verify its seasonal result.*

=== Establishing the Weekly Pattern

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-lunar-sabbath[The Sabbath is a holy time, not merely an individual's seventh counted day]
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day before commanding Israel to remember it.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Scripture calls the Sabbath both a sign and an appointed time.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧩# An appointed time must be recoverable from the heavenly signs already assigned to calendar time.
* Scripture distinguishes three classes of calendar days
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Ezekiel says the east gate is shut on six working days but open on Sabbaths and Renewed Moon days.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Renewed Moon Day cannot simultaneously be one of the six ordinary working days without supplying an unstated priority rule.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Treating Renewed Moon Day as outside the six-plus-one count removes that conflict.
* The lunar month contains four complete Sabbaton units
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Counting six working days after Renewed Moon Day places rest on the 8th.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Repeating the unit places Sabbaths on the 15th, 22nd, and 29th.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The manna narrative independently places rest on the 15th and 22nd of the second month.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-first-fruits[First Fruits and Shavuot test the week]
** First Fruits has three simultaneous constraints.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The wave sheaf is fixed on the 16th of the first month.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# It occurs on the day after a Sabbath.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# It begins the count of seven complete Sabbatons.
** Shavuot supplies the landing constraint.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# It is the day after the seventh Sabbaton.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# A continuous week cannot keep a fixed 16th as the first day of the week in six years out of seven.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Excluding Renewed Moon days lets both ends retain their stated weekly position.
** The word “complete” performs a necessary function on a lunar count.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# On an uninterrupted count, every seven-day block is automatically complete.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Where month-boundary days stand outside weeks, the command prevents counting a broken Sabbaton.
* Additional historical narratives supply test cases
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Exodus manna places Sabbaths on fixed lunar dates in 1446 BC.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The Jordan crossing and eating of the land place First Fruits on the 16th in 1406 BC.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The lunar pattern fits both narratives without changing the weekly rule between events.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The textual pattern yields four six-plus-one weeks after Renewed Moon Day, but the dated Passion provides the decisive historical test.*

=== Evidence That Herod Died in 1 BC

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-eclipse[Which lunar eclipse preceded Herod's death?]
** The total eclipse of January 1 BC leaves more time for all the events Josephus narrates.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Josephus places a lunar eclipse before Herod's final illness, death, funeral, and the following Passover.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# The January 9–10 eclipse of 1 BC leaves 114 days before Passover.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# The partial eclipse of 4 BC leaves a much shorter interval for the same events.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-capture-reign[Josephus's intervals date Herod's capture and death]
** The stated intervals place Herod's capture of Jerusalem in 36 BC and his death in 1 BC.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# Pompey's Jerusalem capture is independently fixed to 63 BC.
**** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Josephus names Cicero and Antonius as the consuls.
**** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# Roman consular chronology places both consuls in 63 BC.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Josephus says Herod captured it on the same day after twenty-seven years.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Josephus's effective reign total from 36 BC reaches 1 BC.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The proposed sabbatical-year cycle also fits the 36 BC capture.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-age[Josephus reports Herod's age at two different events]
** Herod being fifteen at his first post and nearly seventy at death fits the 1 BC reconstruction better.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Josephus supplies both age statements.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# Independent Roman chronology places Antipater's government settlement in 55 BC.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🔗# Antipater's 55 BC settlement provides the likely setting for Herod's first Galilee appointment.
* Luke's chronology provides an independent check on Herod's death
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-tiberius-ministry[Luke works backward from Tiberius and Jesus's age]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Luke dates John's ministry by Tiberius and says Jesus began at about thirty.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The biblical age sequence places Jesus's birth in spring 2 BC.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-capture-reign[Josephus works forward from Roman-dated events and Herod's intervals]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# That separate historical sequence places Herod's death in 1 BC.
** The two chains converge without sharing their controlling premises.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# A spring 2 BC birth followed by Herod's 1 BC death fits both reconstructions.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Their agreement is redundant corroboration, not a second count of the same evidence.
* Evidence that supports 1 BC but cannot prove it alone
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-loyalty-oath[Augustus's 2 BC loyalty oath may explain Luke's enrollment]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The events are plausibly connected, but no surviving source directly identifies them as the same enrollment.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-high-priests[Josephus's 107-year high-priest total fits 1 BC more closely]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The 107-year total fits 1 BC more closely but does not decide between the two dates by itself.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herod-the-great/#atlas-herod-philip[Philip's death date varies among surviving witnesses]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# Taken together, the Latin manuscript tradition and Philip's Year 37 coin favor 1 BC, though neither alone establishes Herod's death date.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herods-appointment-and-battle-of-actium/#atlas-herod-appointment[Josephus may date Herod's appointment story from its opening event]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🔗# Dating from the story's opening event permits the later Roman appointment date but remains an interpretation of the narrative.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/herods-appointment-and-battle-of-actium/#atlas-herod-actium[Josephus's interval from Actium fits the reconstructed reign]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The interval fits the 1 BC reconstruction more closely than the competing chronology.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*Josephus leads to 1 BC, Luke independently reaches the same result, and the combined model explains the observations with fewer adjustments.*

--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-tiberius-ministry[Luke dates the beginning of John's public ministry]
** Tiberius's fifteenth year limits John's beginning to late AD 28 or AD 29.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# Tiberius's ordinary fifteenth regnal year spans late AD 28–29.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Luke says God's word came to John during that year.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Jesus being “about thirty,” the priestly age threshold, and the reconstructed birth date are read together.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The combined evidence places John in fall AD 28 and Jesus before Passover AD 29.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-temple-forty-six[John 2's Temple statement dates the first Passover]
** The reconstructed start of Herod's Temple work makes John 2 the Passover of AD 30.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🔗# Josephus's eighteenth-year statement and the preparation period place the start of Temple work about 17 BC.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# John 2 reports that the Temple work has continued for forty-six years.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Counting forty-six years from spring 17 BC reaches Passover AD 30.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-year-case[John names three Passovers in the ministry of Jesus]
** John 2 is AD 30; John 6 is at least AD 31, and John 11 at least AD 32.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# AD 30 is therefore the first named feast, not the crucifixion feast.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Reading the named Passovers as consecutive makes AD 32 the exact result.
* Why the other proposed crucifixion years do not fit this sequence
** AD 28 and AD 29 occur before the first Passover fixed by John 2.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# AD 30 is the first named Passover, while AD 31 is the second at the earliest.
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# AD 32 is the earliest year that can contain the third named Passover.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# AD 33 requires either an additional ministry year or a different date for the start of Temple work.
* Other evidence that agrees with AD 32
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-year-of-release[Jesus's proclamation of the Year of Release]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The reconstructed sabbatical-year cycle agrees with a proclamation in AD 29.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-daniel-490[The inclusive count of Daniel's 490 years]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The inclusive 490th year begins in spring AD 32.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-signs[Historical reports and astronomical signs near the crucifixion]
*** [.atlas-icon]#✔# They agree with the proposed period but support rather than independently establish AD 32.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*AD 32 is the earliest result; the consecutive-feast reading makes it exact.*

[discrete]
=== Applying the Calendar Test to AD 32

[.evidence-outline]
--
* Begin with the date and narrative before selecting the weekly model
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-year-case[The chronology fixes the crucifixion Passover in AD 32]
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧩# This result depends on the Tiberius, Herod, Temple, and three-Passover branches above.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/passion-week/[The Gospel accounts fix the order of the Passion events]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Jesus dies as Passover on the 14th and is buried before the Sabbath.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The 15th is the Sabbath and high feast day.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The tomb is already empty on the first day of the week, the third day in the inclusive count.
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-first-fruits[First Fruits fixes the date of resurrection]
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Jesus is called the first fruits of those raised.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The barley wave sheaf is always the 16th of the first month.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The 14th, 15th, and 16th are the repeatedly stated three days.
* Apply the previously established month and year rules to AD 32
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-month-start/#atlas-month-start[The full-moon month start fixes the numbered lunar dates]
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-does-the-year-start/#atlas-year-start[The equinox rule selects the first month]
** link:/books/time-tested-tradition/32-ad-resurrection/#atlas-cross-signs[Astronomical positions can be retrocalculated independently]
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# The calculated sun and moon positions do not depend on later Jewish calendar tradition.
* Compare the continuous-week alternatives
** A dark-conjunction start does not yield a Friday Passover in AD 32.
*** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The two possible months place Passover on Sunday or Monday.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Moving the year boundary selects a different month but still misses the required sequence.
** A first-visible-crescent start also fails unless another calendar rule is changed.
*** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# Proposed Friday results require an early first month that conflicts with the Abib/equinox constraint.
*** [.atlas-icon]#💡# The resulting reconstruction combines month and year rules not used consistently in the other test cases.
* Compare the lunar Sabbath model
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The 14th is always the sixth working day of the second week.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The 15th is always the weekly Sabbath and the first day of Unleavened Bread.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# The 16th is always the first day of the next week and First Fruits.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The same arrangement satisfies the burial, high Sabbath, resurrection, and fixed-date evidence in AD 32.
* Check whether the solution also resolves the longer count
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Leviticus counts seven complete Sabbatons from First Fruits.
** [.atlas-icon]#🧮# Excluding Renewed Moon days preserves the fixed 16th and the first-day landing.
** [.atlas-icon]#✔# The model resolves the First Fruits paradox rather than making one endpoint move from year to year.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*AD 32 is not chosen because it fits a lunar Sabbath. It is reached through chronology first; once fixed, the Passion sequence fits the lunar Sabbath and conflicts with the tested continuous-week calendars.*

[discrete]
=== Why Surviving Tradition Does Not Settle the Calendar

[.evidence-outline]
--
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/inherited-lies/#atlas-calendar-plurality[More than one Jewish calendar was practiced]
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Second Temple evidence directly attests multiple calendars.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Surviving writings are not a neutral sample of earlier practice.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Later uniformity cannot prove uniformity before the Temple fell.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/the-day-of-saturn/#atlas-sectarian-power[Jewish groups held different kinds of religious power]
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Pharisees influenced the people; Sadducees held elite priestly offices.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# Public accommodation need not reveal each group's interpretation.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚓# The Temple establishment that might preserve a priestly rival fell in AD 70.
* link:/books/time-tested-tradition/when-is-the-sabbath/#atlas-philo-sabbath[Philo is the earliest witness cited for a continuous Sabbath]
** His evidence leaves the month boundary unstated.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# He describes the full moon at the end of the second week.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# He puts Sabbaths after six days but gives no starting point.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# He fixes the sheaf after the first Paschal day: the 16th.
*** [.atlas-icon]#📜# He gives seven sevens plus one but no weekday for Shavuot.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# His language permits a continuous count in his community.
** [.atlas-icon]#⚖# It does not establish uniform treatment of Renewed Moon days.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# With Leviticus, the fixed 16th reproduces the continuous-week paradox.
* The later witnesses have the same limits
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# Josephus's histories postdate the Temple, though he witnessed its fall.
** [.atlas-icon]#📜# The rabbinic compilations are later still.
** [.atlas-icon]#💡# They prove described practices, not the absence of rivals.
--

[.atlas-conclusion]
*The outline does not ask later tradition to prove or disprove the calendar. It tests the calendar's premises against Scripture, observable astronomy, independently dated history, and the fixed AD 32 Passion sequence.*
