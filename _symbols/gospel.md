# Gospel of the Kingdom

## Approved Definition

**The gospel of the coming kingdom, in which the law goes forth from Zion — proclaimed beforehand to Abraham and at Mount Sinai, and renewed under Jesus' rule.**
> *"And this **gospel of the kingdom** shall be preached in all the world for a **witness** unto all nations; and then shall **the end** come."* — Mat 24:14

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The gospel of the coming kingdom, in which the law goes forth from Zion — proclaimed beforehand to Abraham and at Mount Sinai, and renewed under Jesus' rule.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **gospel** begins: “Glad tidings; especially, the good news concerning Christ, the Kingdom of God, and salvation.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

The phrase most readers glide past in Mat 24:14 is not "gospel" or "kingdom" — it is "for a witness" (G3142 *marturion*). This word does not mean "so they can hear about it." It means **legal evidence submitted before a court**.

The same word appears in Mat 8:4, where Jesus tells a healed leper to show himself to the priest "for a *marturion* unto them" — as courtroom-grade evidence that the healing is real. It appears in Mat 10:18, where disciples are hauled before governors and kings "for a *marturion* against them." It appears in the tabernacle itself: the ark of the covenant is housed in the "tabernacle of the *marturion*" (Acts 7:44, Rev 15:5) — the testimony IS the covenant terms.

This changes everything about Mat 24:14. The gospel of the kingdom is not merely announced to the nations — it is **entered into the record** as covenant evidence. And only when that legal testimony is complete — when every nation has received the evidence — does the $the-end arrive. The clock does not run out on the gospel. The gospel finishes the clock.

**Symbolizes:** The herald-announcement that God's covenant reign has arrived, delivered as legal testimony

**Opposite:** $deceive — false prophets who misrepresent God's commands, leading people away from the covenant

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:14, Mat 4:23, Mk 1:14–15, Heb 4:2, Gal 3:8, Rev 11:7

**The surprise:** This gospel was not new at Pentecost. It was preached to Abraham (Gal 3:8) and preached at Sinai (Heb 4:2). The same verb — G2097 *euangelizo* — describes what happened when Israel was told "ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests." The content has always been the same: God's kingdom has arrived, and you can enter it.

**Connected:** $the-end, $name, $faith, $deceive, $birth-pains, $wickedness

## Gospel of the Kingdom Across Scripture

### The Same Gospel, from Abraham to the Age's End

The modern assumption is that "the gospel" began with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus. But Scripture uses the word *euangelizo* (G2097 — to announce good news) for events centuries before the cross:

> *"And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the heathen through faith, **preached before the gospel** unto Abraham, saying, In thee shall all nations be blessed."* — Gal 3:8

The Greek word here is G4283 *proeuangelizo* — literally "pre-gospeled." Abraham received the gospel. Its content: "in thee shall all nations be blessed." The scope: *all nations* — the same scope as Mat 24:14.

> *"For unto us was the **gospel preached**, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with **faith**."* — Heb 4:2

"Unto them" is the Exodus generation. The same verb (*euangelizo*) used for apostolic preaching describes what happened at Sinai. And what was the content at Sinai?

> *"Ye shall be unto me a **kingdom** of priests, and an holy nation."* — Exod 19:6

A kingdom announcement. The declaration that God's covenant reign has arrived and that Israel can enter it. Hebrews 4:2 adds a devastating detail: the gospel did not profit them because it was "not being mixed with $faith" — and Heb 3:18–19 equates that unbelief with *disobedience*. The gospel demanded trusting obedience then, just as it does now.

Jesus preached the gospel of the kingdom *before* his death, burial, and resurrection (Mat 4:23, Mat 9:35, Mk 1:14–15). The cross is the mechanism by which the kingdom is secured — "It is finished" (Jn 19:30, G5055 *teleo*) is the prototype of testimony-completion. But the gospel itself is not *about* the mechanism. The gospel IS the kingdom announcement: God reigns, repent, believe, obey.

> *"The time is fulfilled, and the **kingdom** of God is at hand: repent ye, and believe the **gospel**."* — Mk 1:15

Four elements: (1) the appointed time (*kairos*) has arrived, (2) God's kingdom is near, (3) repent, (4) believe. This is the gospel of the kingdom in its most compressed form. It is not an invitation to a transaction. It is a herald's proclamation that the King has arrived.

### The Herald-Cry: Not a Conversation but a Proclamation

The verb for "preached" in Mat 24:14 is G2784 *kerusso* — to herald as a public crier, to proclaim with the authority of the one who sent you. A *kerux* (herald) did not negotiate. He did not invite dialogue. He stood in the public square and announced the king's decree. The content was not his; the authority was not his; the delivery was his assignment.

> *"And Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and **preaching** the **gospel of the kingdom**, and healing all manner of sickness."* — Mat 4:23

> *"And Jesus went about all the cities and villages, teaching in their synagogues, and **preaching** the **gospel of the kingdom**, and healing every sickness."* — Mat 9:35

These two verses form bookends around the Sermon on the Mount and the healing narratives of Matthew 5–9. They establish a triple ministry: *didasko* (teach — convey content) + *kerusso* (herald — proclaim authority) + *therapeuo* (heal — demonstrate power). All three together constitute the kingdom arriving. Remove any one and the gospel is incomplete: teaching without heralding lacks authority; heralding without teaching lacks content; both without healing lack demonstration.

The final verse of Acts preserves the same structure:

> *"**Preaching** the **kingdom** of God, and **teaching** those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him."* — Acts 28:31

Paul in Rome — the capital of the *oikoumene* — preaching the kingdom and teaching about Jesus. The arc of Acts runs from Jerusalem to Rome, from the Jewish center to the Gentile imperial seat. The gospel of the kingdom reaches the world's capital. The herald-announcement advances.

### Testimony That Costs Your Life

The word *marturion* (G3142) in Mat 24:14 descends from G3144 *martus* — witness. English borrowed this word and turned it into "martyr." The semantic evolution is not accidental. It is theology.

> *"And ye shall be brought before governors and kings for my sake, for a **testimony** against them and the Gentiles."* — Mat 10:18

The context of testimony is *persecution*. Governors and kings do not invite heralds for friendly conversation. The witness stand before hostile authorities is the delivery mechanism. Mk 13:9 and Lk 21:13 confirm the same pattern: "it shall turn to you for a *marturion*." The trial IS the testimony. The persecution IS the delivery.

Revelation traces the cost to its conclusion:

> *"And when they shall have **finished** their **testimony**, the beast that ascendeth out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, and shall overcome them, and kill them."* — Rev 11:7

> *"And I saw under the altar the souls of them that were slain for the word of God, and for the **testimony** which they held."* — Rev 6:9

> *"And they overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their **testimony**; and they loved not their lives unto the death."* — Rev 12:11

> *"And I saw the souls of them that were **beheaded** for the **witness** of Jesus, and for the word of God… and they lived and **reigned** with Christ."* — Rev 20:4

The chain is explicit: *marturion* (evidence given, Mat 24:14) → *marturia* (the testimony held, Rev 6:9) → *martus* (the one who testifies, Rev 1:5) → martyr (the one killed for testifying, Rev 20:4). And the end of the chain is reign: "they reigned with Christ." The cost IS the crown. The death IS the delivery. The witness IS the martyr.

This inverts the common reading of Mat 24:14. The gospel is not racing against time. The testimony must be *completed* — and completion happens through the very persecution that appears to threaten it.

### Finished — Then the End

The structural connection between "finished testimony" and "the end" runs on a single Greek root: G5055 *teleo* (to finish, complete) derives from G5056 *telos* (end, goal, purpose). They are not just related — they are the same word in verb and noun form.

Four passages use this structure:

**Mat 24:14** — The gospel of the kingdom is preached for a *marturion* to all nations → "then shall the *telos* come."

**Rev 11:7** — "When they shall have *teleo'd* their *marturia*" → the beast kills them → they rise after 3.5 days → the seventh trumpet sounds → "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord" (Rev 11:15).

**Rev 10:7** — "In the days of the voice of the seventh angel… the mystery of God should be *teleo'd*, as he declared to (*euangelizo'd*) his servants the prophets." The same verb *euangelizo* that describes the gospel in Mat 4:23 appears here for what was declared to the prophets. And the mystery is *teleo'd* — finished, completed.

**Jn 19:30** — "*Tetelestai*." It is finished. Jesus completes his testimony on the cross. Death follows. Then resurrection. Then all authority in heaven and earth.

The pattern across all four: testimony completed (*teleo*) → death/judgment → resurrection/vindication → authority/kingdom. Jesus is the prototype. The witnesses in Rev 11 repeat the pattern. Mat 24:14 describes the global scale. The finishing of the witness IS the mechanism that triggers the $the-end — not "the end" as mere cessation, but *telos* as the **goal arriving**, the covenant purpose reaching its consummation.

### The Beast Cannot Act Until the Testimony Finishes

This may be the most overlooked detail in Rev 11:7. The beast does not kill the witnesses at any point of his choosing. He kills them **when they have finished their testimony**:

> *"And **when** they shall have finished (G5055 *teleo*) their testimony, the beast… shall overcome them."* — Rev 11:7

The witness-period is *protected*. The appointed testimony must be completed before the beast is permitted to act. Only the *teleo* — the finishing — opens the door. This mirrors the pattern in Jesus' own arrest: he was not taken until he declared his work complete. The disciples were scattered (Mat 26:31 — "all ye shall be $[skandalizo]'d"), the testimony was finished, and *then* the persecution was released.

The implication for Mat 24:14 is staggering. The gospel of the kingdom cannot be stopped before its testimony is complete. The beast, the false prophets, the $wickedness that multiplies — none of these prevent the *marturion* from reaching every nation. They are the context in which it is delivered. They are the courtroom.

### The Mat 24:9–14 Sequence

Mat 24:14 does not stand alone. It is the conclusion of a unified sequence that begins at verse 9:

> *"Then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my **$[name]**'s sake."* — Mat 24:9

> *"And then shall many be offended ($[skandalizo]), and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another."* — Mat 24:10

> *"And many false prophets shall rise, and shall **$[deceive]** many."* — Mat 24:11

> *"And because **$[wickedness]** shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold."* — Mat 24:12

> *"But he that shall endure unto **$[the-end]**, the same shall be saved."* — Mat 24:13

> *"And this **gospel of the kingdom** shall be preached in all the world for a **witness** unto all nations; and then shall **$[the-end]** come."* — Mat 24:14

Six stages: (1) persecution for the $name — for the covenant relationship; (2) falling away, betrayal; (3) false prophets who $deceive — who misrepresent God's commands and lead people away from covenant; (4) $wickedness (G458 *anomia* — lawlessness, covenant transgression) multiplies, love grows cold; (5) the one who endures in $faith — trusting obedience — to the $the-end is saved; (6) the gospel testimony is completed → *then* the $the-end comes.

The pressure that causes the rootless to fall away is the *same* pressure that refines the faithful into witnesses. The $skandalizo and the *marturion* are produced by the same persecution. The false prophets who $deceive are the counterpart to the true herald who proclaims. $wickedness — lawlessness, covenant transgression — is the environment in which the gospel-testimony achieves its legal force. You do not need testimony in a courtroom where no crime is contested. The gospel of the kingdom is preached *precisely because* lawlessness abounds.

Daniel confirms the same double pattern:

> *"And they that understand among the people shall instruct many: yet they shall fall by the sword, and by flame, by captivity, and by spoil, many days."* — Dan 11:33

> *"And some of them of understanding shall fall, to try them, and to purge, and to make them white, even to the time of the end."* — Dan 11:35

The wise instruct many AND fall by the sword. The teaching and the suffering are not alternative outcomes — they are the same vocation. Stumbling purifies them *to the time of the end*.

### The Everlasting Gospel

> *"And I saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the **everlasting gospel** to preach unto them that dwell on the earth, and to every nation, and kindred, and tongue, and people."* — Rev 14:6

The scope — "every nation, kindred, tongue, people" — matches Mat 24:14 exactly. But the modifier is striking: *everlasting* (G166 *aionios*). Not new. Not updated. The same gospel that Abraham received, that Israel received at Sinai, that Jesus preached in Galilee, that Paul carried to Rome. Its content, declared in the next verse: "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come" (Rev 14:7). The kingdom has arrived. The judge is on the bench. Repent.

That this angel-proclamation occurs in Revelation's sequence immediately before the harvest and the winepress (Rev 14:14–20) underscores the pattern: testimony → then judgment. The *marturion* precedes the *telos*.

## Patterns

1. **The gospel of the kingdom is a trigger-condition, not a prediction.** Mat 24:14 does not say "the gospel will be preached and then coincidentally the end comes." It says "this gospel shall be preached for a *marturion* to all nations; and **then** shall the *telos* come." The *telos* is contingent on the *marturion*'s completion. The testimony finishes the clock.

2. **The *teleo* → *telos* chain links testimony-completion to age-completion.** G5055 *teleo* (finish) → G5056 *telos* (end/goal). Rev 11:7 "finished (*teleo*) their testimony" → Rev 10:7 "mystery of God finished (*teleo*)" → Mat 24:14 "then shall the *telos* come." Jn 19:30 "*tetelestai*" is the prototype: testimony finished → death → resurrection → authority.

3. **The herald-mode (*kerusso*) is not optional.** The gospel of the kingdom is *heralded* — publicly proclaimed with royal authority, not privately shared. G2784 *kerusso* appears in every "gospel of the kingdom" passage. The herald does not own the message; he delivers it under commission.

4. **The testimony is legal evidence, not mere information.** G3142 *marturion* links to the Decalogue ark (Acts 7:44, Rev 15:5) — the tabernacle of the *marturion*. The gospel-witness IS covenant-witness. It places nations under the same legal accountability that Israel bore at Sinai.

5. **Persecution is the delivery mechanism.** Mat 10:18 — brought before governors and kings "for a *marturion*." Mk 13:9 — councils and synagogues "for a *marturion*." The courtroom appearance IS the testimony event. The hostile venue amplifies the legal weight.

6. **The same gospel was preached across all covenants.** Abraham (Gal 3:8), Sinai (Heb 4:2), Jesus (Mat 4:23), apostles (Acts 28:31), angels (Rev 14:6). One gospel, one kingdom, one announcement — spanning the entire biblical narrative.

7. **The beast is restrained until the testimony finishes.** Rev 11:7 — the beast acts only *after* the witnesses complete their task. The witness-period is divinely protected. The gospel cannot be silenced before its appointed scope is reached.

8. **The witness and the martyr are the same person.** G3144 *martus* → English "martyr." Rev 12:11 — they overcome by (1) the Lamb's blood, (2) the word of their *marturia*, (3) loving not their lives unto death. Rev 20:4 — beheaded for *marturia* → reign with Christ. The cost of testimony is death; the reward of testimony is the kingdom.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$the-end** — The $the-end is not termination but covenant fulfillment — the goal (*telos*) toward which God's covenant process drives. Mat 24:14 makes the gospel-testimony the trigger-condition: the *telos* cannot arrive until the *marturion* is complete. The finishing of witness (*teleo*) and the arriving of the goal (*telos*) share the same root because they are the same event viewed from two sides.

- **$name** — The $name represents covenant relationship. Believers are persecuted "for my name's sake" (Mat 24:9) — for the covenant relationship itself. Acts 8:12 pairs the kingdom and the $name of Jesus as inseparable: "when they believed Philip preaching the things concerning the kingdom of God, and the name of Jesus Christ." The gospel of the kingdom is preached in the $name — under the authority of the covenant.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience, not mere mental assent. Heb 4:2 says the gospel was preached at Sinai but "did not profit them, not being mixed with $faith" — and Heb 3:18–19 equates that unbelief with disobedience. The gospel demands the same response it has always demanded: hear, believe, obey. Mat 24:13 — "he that shall endure to the end" = $faith as persevering obedience under pressure.

- **$deceive** — $deceive means to cause covenant-breaking by misrepresenting God's commands — leading away from the way. In Mat 24:11, false prophets $deceive many, immediately following the *skandalizo* of verse 10 and immediately preceding the $wickedness of verse 12. $deceive is the anti-gospel: where the gospel heralds God's reign and commands repentance, the false prophets misrepresent what God requires and redirect allegiance.

- **$birth-pains** — $birth-pains represent covenant judgment producing a $renewed-covenant order — painful transition preceding deliverance. Mat 24:8 identifies wars, famines, and earthquakes as "the beginning of $birth-pains." These are not the $the-end; they are the labor that precedes it. The gospel of the kingdom is preached *during* the birth pains (Mat 24:14 follows 24:8) — the testimony is delivered in the midst of the painful transition, not after it.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness is lawlessness (G458 *anomia*) — covenant transgression. In Mat 24:12, multiplying $wickedness causes love to grow cold — but this is the immediate context for the gospel-testimony of verse 14. The legal testimony achieves its sharpest force in a lawless environment. The courtroom exists *because* there is a crime.

## For Further Study

- **$the-end** — What does *telos* actually mean, and why does it matter that it means "goal" rather than "cessation"? The gospel of the kingdom only makes sense when you understand what it triggers.

- **$name** — The gospel is preached "for my name's sake." If the $name represents covenant relationship, what does it mean to suffer for it — and to preach in it?

- **$faith** — The Sinai generation heard the gospel but it was "not mixed with $faith." What is the $faith that the gospel demands, and why does Hebrews equate its absence with disobedience?

- **$deceive** — The anti-gospel. False prophets rise in the same sequence as the gospel-testimony (Mat 24:11, 14). How does deception work as covenant-misdirection rather than mere lying?

- **$birth-pains** — The gospel is preached during the $birth-pains, not after them. What is the relationship between the painful transition of the old order and the herald-cry of the new?

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness multiplies (Mat 24:12) immediately before the gospel-testimony is completed (Mat 24:14). Why does the legal testimony require a lawless context?

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **gospel** begins:

> “See God, and Spell, v.] 1. Glad tidings; especially, the good news concerning Christ, the Kingdom of God, and salvation. And Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, and preaching the gospel of the kingdom. Matt. iv. 23. The steadfast belief of the promises of the gospel. Bentley. Note: It is probable that gospel is from. OE. godspel, God story, the narrative concerning God; but it was early confused with god spell, good story, good tidings, and was so used by the translators of the Authorized version of Scripture. This use has been retained in most cases in the Revised Version. Thus the literal sense [of gospel] is the ”narrative of God,” i. e., the life of Christ. Skeat.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **the good news that Christ died for our sins, was buried, and rose again.**

- The contrast with the explicitly noted common view could indicate that the book intends to replace, not merely refine, the Christ-event identification; on that reading the entry would be divergent. The printed wording alone, however, does not explicitly deny that Christ’s death and resurrection enact the renewed covenant.

- One could argue REFINED: the consensus itself lists 'the gospel of the kingdom' as a common alternative reading, and the book's phrase leads with exactly that; Jer 31:33's new covenant is also arguably contained within mainstream gospel theology (Christ's blood inaugurating the new covenant), so the book might be seen as a covenantal sharpening of an admitted alternative rather than a replacement. Additionally, Heb 4:2 genuinely shows biblical usage of 'gospel' preached before Christ's death, lending some textual footing to a definition not anchored in the cross-resurrection events.

- The Apostle Paul explicitly defines the Gospel as the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ in 1 Corinthians 15:1-4. While the Old Testament texts provided discuss the law, Zion, and the new covenant, applying the term 'Gospel' exclusively to these concepts ignores its explicit New Testament definition.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The gospel of the coming kingdom, in which the law goes forth from Zion — proclaimed beforehand to Abraham and at Mount Sinai, and renewed under Jesus' rule.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *gospel*, *gospel of the kingdom*, *good tidings*. The English-form sweep found **109 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
