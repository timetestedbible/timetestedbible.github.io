# Hell

**Three Hebrew concepts that English collapses into one word — and the collapse has created a doctrine Scripture does not teach.**

> *"For the **wages** of sin is **death**, but the gift of God is **eternal life** in Christ Jesus our Lord."* — Rom 6:23

---

## The Key Insight

The English word "hell" obscures three distinct Hebrew/Greek terms that Scripture never conflates:

| Term | Hebrew/Greek | What It Is |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| **$sheol** / Hades | H7585 / G86 | The demanding place — mortality's claim on all flesh. Both righteous and wicked go here. Temporary. |
| **Gehinnom** / Gehenna | H1516 / G1067 | Divine judgment. Originally the Valley of Hinnom where Israel burned children to Molech; by the Second Temple period, the Hebrew word for post-death punishment of the wicked — what English calls "hell." |
| **Fire of Sheol** | H784 + H7585 | God's consuming nature within the realm of the dead. The Hebrew gospel's term for what Greek calls "the lake of fire." |

The KJV translates H7585 *sheol* as "grave" 31 times and "hell" 31 times — making one word look like two. Meanwhile, it translates both G86 *hades* (the unseen) and G1067 *gehenna* (Valley of Hinnom) as "hell" — making two words look like one. The result: readers see "hell" everywhere and assume it always means the same thing. It doesn't.

**Symbolizes:** The fate of the uncovered — what happens when the $fire of God's presence meets flesh without covering

**Opposite:** Eternal life / the resurrection / the Garden of Eden

**The surprise:** The biblical contrast is not heaven vs. hell (two eternal conscious states). It is life vs. death (existence vs. non-existence). The wages of sin is *death* — not eternal torment.

**Connected:** $sheol, $fire, $pit-abyss, $darkness, $serpent, $water, $naked

## God IS the Unquenchable Fire

The starting point is not "where do the wicked go?" It is "who is God?"

> *"For the LORD your God is a **consuming fire**."* — Deut 4:24

> *"For our God is a **consuming fire**."* — Heb 12:29

God is not a God who USES fire. He IS $fire. The $fire is not a punishment tool separate from Him — it is His nature. And His nature is eternal, unquenchable, without sabbath.

The Hebrew gospel of Mark 9:44 says the fire "does not rest" (H5117 *nuach* — the sabbath-rest word, Noah's name). The fire has no sabbath because God does not rest from being what He is. "Unquenchable" does not mean the fuel burns forever. It means the FIRE cannot be extinguished — because the fire is God, and God cannot be put out.

The sun illustrates this: the sun does not need your body to keep burning. Throw something into the sun — the sun continues; the object is consumed instantly. The sun is "unquenchable." The fuel is not.

> *"For the LORD God is a **sun** and shield."* — Ps 84:11

The same $fire that consumes also sustains. At the right distance, with the right covering (atmosphere, magnetosphere), the sun gives life. Without covering, it incinerates. The difference is not the fire — it is the COVERING.

## Fire as Un-Creation

Before God differentiated creation, everything was H8415 *tehom* — the deep, the formless void, undifferentiated chaos (Gen 1:2). Creation was the act of DIFFERENTIATION: separating light from $darkness, $water above from water below, land from sea, kind from kind. God spoke order into chaos.

$fire reverses this. It undifferentiates. It breaks complex structures to basic components — ash, gas, energy. It returns the formed to the formless. The second death is un-creation: the return of the differentiated to the undifferentiated, the created to the uncreated.

> *"They shall be **as though they had not been**."* — Obad 1:16

The spirit returns to God who gave it (Eccl 12:7). The body returns to dust. Everything returns to its origin — becomes undifferentiated. Resurrection is re-creation: God differentiates again, forming new body, breathing spirit back in, as He did with Adam from the dust.

## Three Responses to the Same Fire

Scripture shows three outcomes when creatures encounter God's $fire:

### 1. Consumed — No Covering

> *"The day which is coming shall **burn them up**... they shall be **ashes** under the soles of your feet."* — Mal 4:1, 3

Stubble meets $fire. Nothing remains but ashes. The combustion is complete. See the full analysis in the [Malachi 4:1 verse study](/research/verses/mal-4-1/).

### 2. Sustained — God's Chosen Vessel

> *"The bush **burned** with fire, and the bush was **not consumed**."* — Ex 3:2

Same $fire. Same God. But the bush survives — not because the fire is weaker, but because God sustains what He chooses. The burning bush is God's presence without destruction.

### 3. Liberated — Covered by the Fourth Man

> *"He answered and said, 'Look! I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire; and they are **not hurt**."* — Dan 3:25

Nebuchadnezzar heated the furnace SEVEN times (Dan 3:19) — the perfected, complete $fire. In the seven-fold fire:
- The **soldiers** (uncovered) died from the heat before entering (Dan 3:22)
- The **ropes** (bondage) burned off — consumed
- The **three** (covered by the fourth man) walked freely, not a hair singed, no smell of smoke (Dan 3:27)

The perfect fire LIBERATES the covered. It consumes bondage but leaves the person untouched. This is the refiner's fire: "He shall sit as a refiner and purifier of silver" (Mal 3:3). Dross consumed. Silver purified. Same fire. Different outcomes. Based entirely on covering.

## What "Torment" Actually Means

### The Greek: Touchstone Testing

G931 *basanos* — literally a touchstone: a black stone used to test whether gold is genuine by rubbing the metal against it. The test damages the object — that is the connection to "torment." But the root concept is TESTING for authenticity, not punishment for its own sake.

The word appears only 3 times in the NT — Matt 4:24 (diseases), Luke 16:23 (the rich man), Luke 16:28 (this place of testing). In Luke 16, the rich man is in *basanos* in Hades — being tested, not tortured. See [Luke 16:23 verse study](/research/verses/luk-16-23/).

### The Hebrew Gospel: Judicial Penalty, Not Torture

The Hebrew gospel of Revelation replaces Greek "torment" words with Hebrew words that carry fundamentally different weight:

| Passage | Greek | Hebrew | Meaning |
|---------|-------|--------|---------|
| Rev 14:10 | G928 *basanizo* (tormented) | H6064 *anash* | To fine, penalize judicially — a court sentence |
| Rev 14:11 | G929 *basanismos* (torment) | [same root — punishment] | Smoke of their PUNISHMENT |
| Rev 20:10 | G928 *basanizo* (tormented) | H6031 *anah* | To humble, bring low, afflict — Israel in Egypt, fasting |
| Rev 14:11 | "no rest" | H4496 *menuchah* negated | Denied sabbath-rest — exclusion from the inheritance |

None of these are words for torture. They describe judicial penalty (*anash*), humbling (*anah*), and exclusion from rest (*menuchah*). See the full analysis in the [Rev 14:10](/research/verses/rev-14-10/) and [Rev 20:10](/research/verses/rev-20-10/) verse studies.

### Pruning, Not Torture

The word for "punishment" in Matt 25:46 ("eternal punishment") is G2851 *kolasis* — from G2849 *kolazo*, meaning "to lop or prune, as trees and wings." Aristotle distinguished *kolasis* (corrective/removal punishment) from G5098 *timoria* (retributive punishment). Jesus chose *kolasis*. The branch is pruned off — cut away. The $fire finishes it (John 15:6). See [Matt 25:41 verse study](/research/verses/mat-25-41/).

## The Soul That Wanders Away

> *"What does it profit a man if he gains all the world and **loses** (H6 **abad**) his **soul** (H5315 **nephesh**)?"* — Mark 8:36 (Hebrew gospel)

H6 *abad* means "to wander away, lose oneself; by implication to perish." The soul doesn't persist in torment — it wanders away, dissipates, perishes. If the nephesh has been *abad*-ed, what conscious entity remains to be tormented?

> *"The wicked shall perish... into **smoke** shall they **consume away**."* — Ps 37:20

> *"Yet a little while, and the wicked **shall not be**."* — Ps 37:10

Smoke rises and dissipates. The wicked "shall not be." This is cessation — the opposite of eternal conscious existence.

## The Hebrew Gospel Revelation Map

The Hebrew text of Revelation uses distinct terms where English flattens everything to "hell" or "lake of fire":

| Verse | Greek | Hebrew Gospel | Significance |
|-------|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Rev 17:8, 11 | destruction | **Sheol** (H7585) | The beast goes to sheol |
| Rev 19:20 | lake of fire | **Sheol that burns** with sulfur | "Lake of fire" = fire-side of sheol |
| Rev 20:10 | lake of fire | **Fire of Sheol** (H7585) | Devil/beast/false prophet — afflicted (*anah*), not tortured |
| Rev 20:13 | Death and Hades | Death and **Sheol** | Standard usage — sheol gives up the dead |
| Rev 20:14 | Death and Hades into lake of fire | Death and **Gehinnom** into the fire | GEHINNOM here — not sheol. Sheol is being destroyed |
| Rev 21:8 | Lake that burns | **Fire of Sheol** (H7585) — wages from the fire | "Second death" — wages paid |

The critical distinction: Rev 20:14 switches from Sheol to **Gehinnom** — the Valley of Hinnom. Why? Because $sheol (the demanding place) is itself being destroyed. Death and the grave-concept are cast into the $fire. The demanding place runs out of things to demand. Death dies.

And for humans: the text says "second **death**" (H4194 *maveth*) — not "second torment." See [Rev 20:14 verse study](/research/verses/rev-20-14/).

## The Second Death — *Acher Maveth* (Another Kind of Death)

### The Hebrew Revelation's Two Words

The Hebrew Revelation uses two different words for "second" across its four occurrences of "second death":

| Verse | Hebrew | Word | Meaning |
|-------|--------|------|---------|
| Rev 2:10 | *acher maveth* | H312 *acher* | **another, different** — a death of a different kind |
| Rev 20:6 | *acher maveth* | H312 *acher* | **another, different** |
| Rev 20:14 | *acher maveth* | H312 *acher* | **another, different** |
| Rev 21:8 | *sheni maveth* | H8145 *sheni* | **second** (ordinal) — the final summary |

H312 *acher* does not merely mean "second in sequence." It means **another kind** — a qualitatively different one. Abraham took *acher* wife (Gen 25:1) — a different wife. Caleb had an *acher* spirit (Num 14:24) — a qualitatively different spirit. So *acher maveth* is not "death #2." It is a death of a different kind — unlike ordinary death.

Only in the final summary (Rev 21:8) does the text switch to the ordinal H8145 *sheni*. By then the concept is established. The Greek collapses both into G1208 *deuteros* (second), losing the "different kind" nuance that *acher* carries.

### The Targums Saw the Same Signal in the OT

The phrase "second death" does not appear in the Hebrew Bible. But the Aramaic Targums — Jewish translations of the OT that predate or are contemporary with the NT — use it six times. What prompted them? The OT Hebrew uses H4191 *muth* (to die) in contexts where ordinary death clearly doesn't fit:

| Passage | Hebrew Marker | Why Ordinary Death Doesn't Fit | Targum Rendering |
|---------|--------------|-------------------------------|------------------|
| Deut 33:6 | "Live (H2421) and not die (H4191)" | Exemption from ordinary death is impossible — the prayer must be for something beyond it | "not die the second death which the wicked die in the world to come" |
| Isa 22:14 | "Not atoned (H3722 *kaphar*) till you die" | Ordinary death doesn't atone — this death IS the reckoning | "till you die the second death" |
| Isa 65:6 | "Recompense (H7999 *shalam*) into their bosom" | Account settled bodily — finality of repayment | "deliver their bodies to the second death" |
| Isa 65:15 | "The Lord GOD shall slay (H4191 hiphil)" | Divine execution — God causes this death, not natural mortality | "second death" |
| Jer 51:39 | "Perpetual sleep (H5769 *olam*), not wake (H6974 *quts*)" | Sleep with NO waking — resurrection explicitly denied | "second death" |

The Targumists didn't invent the concept. They named what the Hebrew was already signaling: a category of death that is permanent, final, beyond atonement, without waking. The Hebrew Revelation's *acher maveth* carries the same signal — a different death, unlike ordinary mortality.

And in every Targum occurrence, "second death" means **permanent death** — the death from which there is no further resurrection. Not eternal conscious torment. Not ongoing suffering. The death you don't come back from.

## Carcasses, Not Souls

The source text for "eternal torment" imagery is Isaiah 66:24, which Jesus quotes in Mark 9:43-48:

> *"They shall go forth, and look upon the **carcasses** of the men that have transgressed... for their worm shall not die, neither shall their fire be quenched."* — Isa 66:24

H6297 *peger* — carcasses, corpses, dead bodies. The living LOOK AT the dead. The worm and fire consume dead flesh. That is what worms and fire do to corpses. The dead are not experiencing anything — they are the object of abhorrence (H1860 *dera'on*), not the subject of conscious torment.

The Hebrew gospel of Mark transforms the image further: the "worm" becomes venomous creatures, "hell" becomes Gei-Hinnom (the valley), and the contrast is Garden of Eden vs. Valley of Hinnom — not "heaven vs. hell." See [Isaiah 66:24](/research/verses/isa-66-24/) and [Mark 9:43](/research/verses/mark-9-43/) verse studies.

## Smoke: Evidence of Completed Destruction

> *"The **smoke** of their punishment shall go upward from **age** to **age**."* — Rev 14:11 (Hebrew gospel)

H6227 *ashan* — smoke, the product of combustion. Smoke is what remains AFTER something burns. It rises, dissipates, and is gone. It is the evidence that $fire did its work — not proof of ongoing burning.

Compare: "the smoke of [Babylon] rose up from generation to generation" (Rev 19:3, Hebrew). Babylon is destroyed — not still burning. Edom's smoke "goes up for ever" (Isa 34:10) — Edom is not still smoking. The language describes permanent desolation, the lasting evidence of judgment. The result endures. The burning does not.

## Life vs. Death — The Real Contrast

The biblical opposition is not heaven vs. hell (two eternal conscious states). It is life vs. death (existence vs. non-existence):

> *"The wages of sin is **death**, but the gift of God is **eternal life**."* — Rom 6:23

| Sin's wage | God's gift |
|-----------|-----------|
| Death (G2288 *thanatos*) | Eternal life (G166 *aionios* + G2222 *zoe*) |

If eternal life means conscious, ongoing existence, then death means its opposite: non-existence, cessation. Not two parallel eternal states. One side gets to exist. The other side doesn't. See [Romans 6:23 verse study](/research/verses/rom-6-23/).

This picture is closer to what the atheist expects (cessation) than to what traditional Christianity teaches (eternal conscious torment). The difference: the atheist thinks cessation is universal; Scripture says the righteous are resurrected to eternal life. The uncovered are "as though they had not been" (Obad 1:16).

## Patterns

1. **Three terms, not one.** $sheol (the demanding place), Gehinnom (Valley of Hinnom), and the fire of sheol are distinct. English "hell" collapses them into a single concept Scripture never presents.

2. **God IS the fire.** The unquenchable fire is not a punishment chamber — it is God's nature (Deut 4:24, Heb 12:29). It cannot be quenched because God cannot be extinguished. The fuel is consumed; the fire continues.

3. **Same fire, different outcomes.** Ashes (Mal 4:1), burning bush (Ex 3:2), three in the furnace (Dan 3:25). What differs is the covering, not the fire.

4. **"Torment" words are testing/pruning/humbling words.** G931 *basanos* (touchstone), G2851 *kolasis* (pruning), H6031 *anah* (humbling), H6064 *anash* (fining). None mean torture.

5. **The Hebrew gospel distinguishes what English conflates.** Different Hebrew words for different fates: the devil is afflicted (*anah*) from age to age; humans receive the second death (*maveth*). The text itself makes the distinction.

6. **Isaiah 66:24 describes carcasses.** H6297 *peger* — dead bodies viewed by the living. The source text for Mark 9's "worm and fire" is about decomposition of corpses, not torment of conscious souls.

7. **The soul wanders away.** H6 *abad* — to lose oneself, to perish. The nephesh dissipates. What has dissipated cannot be tormented.

8. **Smoke = aftermath, not ongoing burning.** Babylon's smoke "rises from generation to generation" — but Babylon is destroyed, not still burning. Smoke is evidence the fire finished.

9. **The contrast is life vs. death.** Rom 6:23 frames everything: wages = death, gift = life. Existence vs. non-existence. Not bliss vs. torture.

## Connections

- **$sheol** — The demanding place. Both righteous and wicked descend there. It is temporary — gives up its dead (Rev 20:13) and is itself destroyed (Rev 20:14). This study covers what happens AFTER sheol: the second death.

- **$fire** — God's consuming nature. The fire study addresses fire as covenant enforcement; this study addresses fire as the mechanism of un-creation and the touchstone that tests what is genuine.

- **$pit-abyss** — The *tehom* (deep) of Gen 1:2 connects to the abyss/bottomless pit. The undifferentiated chaos before creation is what the uncovered return to.

- **$darkness** — Sheol's environment. "Outer darkness" in the parables (Matt 22:13, 25:30) is exclusion from the light of God's kingdom — not a separate torture chamber.

- **$serpent** — The Hebrew gospel replaces "worm" with venomous creatures in Mark 9:44-48. The serpent that does not die inhabits the place of judgment.

- **$naked** — Without covering. The naked are exposed to the fire. The covered (blood on the doorpost, the fourth man in the furnace, the garments of righteousness) survive it.
