# Brass

**Hard, corrupt authority — the serpent's metal.**

> *"Thus saith the Lord GOD; Because thy **filthiness** was poured out, and thy nakedness discovered through thy whoredoms with thy lovers..."* — Ezek 16:36

The word translated "filthiness" here is H5178 *nechosheth* — the same word translated "brass" everywhere else. The translators couldn't bring themselves to write "thy brass was poured out," so they rendered the meaning the context demanded: **filthiness**.

---

## The Key Insight

The Hebrew word for brass — H5178 *nechosheth* — shares its root with H5175 *nachash*, the **serpent**. Both derive from H5172 *nachash*: to hiss, to whisper a spell, to prognosticate. This is not coincidence. Brass literally carries the serpent's name.

When Moses made a "serpent of brass" (Num 21:9), the Hebrew reads *nechosheth nachash* — a wordplay so tight it's nearly a single compound. When Hezekiah destroyed it centuries later because Israel was worshiping it, he called it **Nehushtan** — "the brass thing" (2Ki 18:4). The name stuck because everyone heard the pun.

This etymological bond is the thread that ties every use of brass together: brass is the **serpent's metal** — representing the hard, corrupt authority that the nachash exercises through nations, kingdoms, and systems.

**Symbolizes:** Hard, corrupt authority rooted in the serpent

**Opposite:** $gold — divine glory and nature (Isa 60:17: "for brass I will bring gold")

**Connected:** $iron, $gold, $fire, serpent, $mountains, $feet

## Brass Across Scripture

### The Metal Speaks: Filthiness, Fetters, and Corrupters

The KJV translates H5178 *nechosheth* not only as "brass" and "copper" but also as **fetters**, **chains**, and — in Ezek 16:36 — **filthiness**. These aren't translation errors; they are the word's semantic range. The same metal that builds altars also names the impurity that defiles.

Three prophets use brass to describe Israel's corruption:

> *"They are all grievous revolters, walking with slanders: they are **brass** and iron; they are all corrupters."* — Jer 6:28

> *"Son of man, the house of Israel is to me become dross: all they are **brass**, and tin, and iron, and lead, in the midst of the furnace; they are even the dross of silver."* — Ezek 22:18

> *"Because thy **nechosheth** was poured out, and thy nakedness discovered through thy whoredoms..."* — Ezek 16:36

The pattern is consistent. When Scripture calls people "brass," it means they are hard, corrupt, and debased — dross where silver was expected.

### The Brow of Brass — Obstinacy

> *"Because I knew that thou art obstinate, and thy neck is an iron sinew, and thy brow **brass**."* — Isa 48:4

H5153 *nachuwsh* is defined by Strong's as "coppery, i.e. figuratively **hard**." A brow of brass is a face that will not yield — hardened against correction, immovable in rebellion. The same hardness that makes brass useful for gates and fetters makes it the perfect image for a people who refuse to turn.

### Fetters and Gates — The Serpent's Binding Power

When Samson was captured, the Philistines "bound him with fetters of brass" (Judg 16:21). When Zedekiah fell to Babylon, they "bound him with fetters of brass, and carried him to Babylon" (2Ki 25:7). Nebuchadnezzar's stump is bound with "a band of iron and brass" (Dan 4:15).

Brass binds. It imprisons. The same word that means "fetters" and "chains" (H5178) means "brass."

> *"For he hath broken the **gates of brass**, and cut the bars of iron in sunder."* — Ps 107:16

> *"I will break in pieces the **gates of brass**, and cut in sunder the bars of iron."* — Isa 45:2

The gates of brass are barriers of serpent-rooted authority — the power structures that imprison nations and souls. Only God breaks them.

### Heaven of Brass — The Curse

> *"And thy heaven that is over thy head shall be **brass**, and the earth that is under thee shall be iron."* — Deut 28:23

> *"I will make your heaven as iron, and your earth as **brass**."* — Lev 26:19

When the covenant is broken, heaven becomes brass — impenetrable, unyielding, closed. No rain passes through. No prayer reaches. The sky itself takes on the character of the serpent's metal: hard, corrupt, blocking the relationship between God and His people.

### Daniel's Kingdoms — The Degradation Sequence

> *"This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of **brass**."* — Dan 2:32

> *"And another third kingdom of **brass**, which shall bear rule over all the earth."* — Dan 2:39

Daniel's image descends from gold to silver to brass to iron. Each metal is harder but less precious — increasing brute strength, decreasing divine glory. The brass kingdom "bears rule over all the earth" — broad dominion through human power rather than divine mandate.

The Rehoboam narrative enacts this degradation in miniature: Solomon's gold shields are replaced by Rehoboam's brass shields (1Ki 14:27). Same form. Debased substance. The split kingdom takes the serpent's metal in place of divine glory.

Isaiah promises the reversal: *"For brass I will bring gold, and for iron I will bring silver"* (Isa 60:17). The eschatological upgrade replaces the serpent's authority with divine nature.

### The Brass Serpent — Sin Cast in Its Own Metal

> *"And Moses made a **serpent of brass**, and put it upon a pole, and it came to pass, that if a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass, he lived."* — Num 21:9

The *nechosheth nachash* — the serpent cast in the serpent's metal. The wordplay is the point. God instructed Moses to make sin visible in its own substance, lift it up, and have the afflicted look at it. Looking at the judged serpent brought healing.

Jesus identified Himself with this image: *"As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, even so must the Son of man be lifted up"* (Jn 3:14). The one who was "made sin for us" (2Cor 5:21) was lifted on the pole — the serpent's corruption externalized and judged.

But the brass serpent became an idol. By Hezekiah's day, Israel was burning incense to it (2Ki 18:4). What was designed to point to God's healing became an object of serpent-worship. The instrument of liberation became another fetter.

### Christ's Feet of Brass — Treading the Serpent

> *"And his feet like unto **fine brass**, as if they burned in a furnace."* — Rev 1:15

> *"These things saith the Son of God, who hath his eyes like unto a flame of fire, and his feet are like **fine brass**."* — Rev 2:18

G5474 *chalkolibanon* — burnished brass, brilliant with fire. Christ's feet are the serpent's own metal, passed through the furnace, now purified and blazing. These are the feet of Gen 3:15: *"He shall bruise thy head."*

The same brass that forges fetters and gates and thrones is under His feet. He has taken the serpent's authority and walks on it. The divine beings of Ezek 1:7 and Dan 10:6 share this appearance — feet and limbs "like the colour of burnished brass." They carry the brightness of authority that has passed through fire.

### The Tabernacle — Where the Serpent's Corruption Meets the Fire

In the tabernacle, brass occupies the **outer court** — the brasen altar where offerings burn, the brasen laver where priests wash, the sockets and pins that anchor the structure to the ground. Gold is hidden in the holy of holies. Silver connects the posts. Brass faces the fire and the public.

The brasen altar is where sin meets judgment. The serpent's corruption (what the offering carries) encounters God's fire on the serpent's metal. The altar is not corrupted by what it bears — it endures the burning. The laver of brass holds water for purification — cleansing that happens at the boundary between common and holy.

Brass in the tabernacle is not a celebration of the serpent. It is the serpent's domain being **repurposed by God** — the corrupt metal pressed into holy service, forced to bear the fire that judges the very corruption it represents.

### Mountains of Brass — Zechariah's Vision

> *"And I turned, and lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and, behold, there came four chariots out from between two **mountains**; and the mountains were mountains of **brass**."* — Zech 6:1

Mountains represent nations. Brass characterizes them: hard, corrupt, serpent-ruled. The four spirits of heaven emerge from the gap **between** these two nations of serpent-authority — having stood before the Lord of all the earth (v.5).

The spirits go forth to the north and south. Those going north "quieted my spirit in the north country" (v.8) — the direction of Babylon. God's purposes are executed through and between the serpent's kingdoms. He sends His winds through the corridor that the corrupt nations themselves form, and those winds accomplish His judgment where it matters most.

## Patterns

1. **The serpent's root.** *Nechosheth* and *nachash* share the primitive root H5172. Brass is etymologically the serpent's metal. This connection is not accidental — it surfaces in the brass serpent, in the filthiness translation, and in the entire pattern of what brass represents.

2. **One word, three faces.** H5178 is translated "brass," "fetters," and "filthiness" in the KJV. These aren't separate concepts — they're three dimensions of the same reality: the serpent's authority is hard (brass), it binds (fetters), and it corrupts (filthiness).

3. **The degradation sequence.** Gold → silver → brass → iron (Dan 2). Gold shields → brass shields (Rehoboam). Brass → gold (Isa 60:17 reversal). Brass consistently occupies the position of debased substitute — what you get when divine glory departs and serpent-authority fills the void.

4. **The metal endures the fire.** The brasen altar, Christ's brass feet burned in a furnace, brass molten from stone (Job 28:2), the brass pot heated to purge filthiness (Ezek 24:11). Brass and fire are inseparable. The serpent's metal either undergoes or administers the furnace.

5. **God repurposes it.** The tabernacle's brass, the brass serpent, Christ's brass feet — God takes the serpent's own material and turns it against the serpent. The metal of fetters becomes the metal of the altar. The metal of corruption becomes the platform for purification. The serpent's authority ends up under the Messiah's feet.

## Connections

- **$gold** — The explicit opposite in the degradation hierarchy. Gold is divine nature; brass is the serpent's debased substitute. Isa 60:17 promises the upgrade: "for brass I will bring gold."

- **$iron** — Brass and iron almost always appear together: fetters of brass (Judg 16:21, 2Ki 25:7), gates of brass and bars of iron (Ps 107:16, Isa 45:2), heaven of brass and earth of iron (Deut 28:23), band of iron and brass (Dan 4:15). Iron breaks; brass binds. Together they represent the full spectrum of the serpent's constraining power.

- **$fire** — Brass is smelted from ore by fire (Job 28:2), bears the fire on the altar, is heated to purge filthiness (Ezek 24:11), and characterizes Christ's feet "burned in a furnace" (Rev 1:15). Fire and brass are inseparable — the serpent's metal and the element that judges it.

- **$mountains** — Mountains represent nations. Mountains of brass (Zech 6:1) = nations characterized by the serpent's hard, corrupt authority — the power structures between which God sends His spirits.

- **$shadow** — The tabernacle's outer court of brass is where judgment meets the public. The tabernacle itself is "a shadow of heavenly things" (Heb 8:5). The brass outer court is the shadow's boundary — where the serpent's corruption first encounters the covenant protection.

## For Further Study

- **$gold** — The metal brass is replaced by in the restoration (Isa 60:17). What does gold represent, and what does the brass-to-gold upgrade mean for the nations?
- **$shadow** — The outer court of brass is the boundary of the tabernacle-shadow. How does the serpent's metal serve as the threshold of covenant protection?
- **$iron** — Brass's constant companion. Iron breaks and crushes (Dan 2:40); brass binds and hardens. Together they form the serpent's full toolkit — and together they are broken by the stone cut without hands.
