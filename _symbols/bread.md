# Bread

## Approved Definition

**The word of God. Unleavened bread is pure doctrine; [leavened bread](/research/symbols/leaven/) contains corrupting doctrine.**
> *"And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with **manna**, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by **bread** only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live."* — Deut 8:3

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The word of God. Unleavened bread is pure doctrine; [leavened bread](/research/symbols/leaven/) contains corrupting doctrine.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **bread** begins: “To spread. [Obs.] Ray.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

God let Israel go hungry — then fed them with something they had never seen. Not so they would learn about bread. So they would learn about *words*.

The logic of Deuteronomy 8:3 is frequently reversed. People read it as: "bread is important, but God's word is *more* important." But Moses says the hunger and the manna were both deliberate. God withheld ordinary food and replaced it with a substance Israel "knewest not" — a substance that arrived with *rules*. Gather this much. Not that much. None on the sixth day's portion spoils, but the extra does. Rest on the seventh day.

The manna was not merely provision. It was Torah with texture. Every morning Israel woke up to bread that had *instructions attached to it*. Exodus 16:4 makes this explicit: God says He will rain bread from heaven "that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law (H8451 *torah*), or no."

The bread *was* the test of obedience. The bread *carried* the commandments. The bread *taught* the Word.

This is why Jesus, when Satan offers Him stones-to-bread, does not say "I don't need bread." He says: man lives by every word from God's mouth (Mat 4:4). The bread that truly sustains is the Word itself. Physical bread keeps the body going. The Word from God's mouth keeps the *man* alive.

Every appearance of bread in Scripture — manna in the wilderness, shewbread in the tabernacle, the bread Jesus breaks at the Last Supper, the bread of life discourse in John 6 — resonates with this single equation: **bread = the Word of God, the terms of His covenant, received and consumed.**

**Symbolizes:** The Word of God — His covenant terms, received as life-sustaining nourishment

**Opposite:** Famine — specifically "not a famine of bread… but of hearing the words of the LORD" (Amos 8:11)

**Defining verses:** Deut 8:3, Mat 4:4, Exod 16:4, Jn 6:35, Jn 6:63, Amos 8:11

**The surprise:** When Jesus says "the bread that I will give is my flesh" (Jn 6:51), He is not introducing a new idea — He is completing a chain that began with "the Word was made flesh" (Jn 1:14). If the Word became flesh, and bread = the Word, then His flesh *is* the bread. John 6:63 confirms: "the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life." The eating is the hearing.

**Connected:** $wine, $rock, $faith, $fruit, $the-way, $marriage, $truth, $mark, $wickedness, $fornication

## Bread Across Scripture

### Manna — Torah You Could Hold in Your Hand

> *"Then said the LORD unto Moses, Behold, I will rain **bread** from heaven for you; and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them, whether they will walk in my law, or no."* — Exod 16:4

The stated purpose of manna is not nutrition. It is *testing*. The word "prove" (H5254 *nasah*) means to test or try. And the thing being tested is whether Israel will walk in God's *torah*.

Consider what the manna required:

- Gather a set amount per person — no more, no less (Exod 16:16–18). Those who gathered excess found it bred worms. Obedience to the prescribed measure was built into the bread itself.
- On the sixth day, gather double (Exod 16:22–26). The bread that normally spoiled overnight would keep for the Sabbath — but only on that day. The Sabbath rest was encoded in the bread's behavior.
- Do not go out on the seventh day (Exod 16:27–29). Some went anyway. "How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws?" God asked.

The manna was edible Torah. Its gathering rules were commandment-keeping in miniature. To eat it properly was to obey. To disobey was to lose it.

Moses' retrospective in Deuteronomy 8:3 makes the interpretive key explicit: God used the manna experience — hunger, then strange bread, then rules — to teach Israel that true life comes not from bread but from "every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD." The bread from heaven and the word from God's mouth are mapped onto each other. They are the same sustenance under two forms.

### The Word Made Flesh Made Bread

> *"I am the living **bread** which came down from heaven: if any man eat of this **bread**, he shall live for ever: and the **bread** that I will give is my flesh, which I will give for the life of the world."* — Jn 6:51

The bread of life discourse in John 6 is often read as a standalone teaching. But it sits inside a chain that John has been building since his opening sentence:

- Jn 1:1 — "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."
- Jn 1:14 — "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us."
- Jn 6:35 — "I am the **bread** of life: he that cometh to me shall never hunger."
- Jn 6:51 — "The **bread** that I will give is my flesh."
- Jn 6:63 — "It is the spirit that quickeneth; the flesh profiteth nothing: the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life."

The chain: Word → flesh → bread → words that are spirit and life. John 6:63 is the decoding verse. The crowd is confused because they are thinking about literal flesh (Jn 6:52). Jesus redirects: "the flesh profiteth nothing." The eating He describes is the receiving of His *words*. The bread is the Word. His flesh is the Word made visible. To eat His flesh is to consume — to take in, to live by — what He speaks.

This is not a departure from Deuteronomy 8:3. It is its fulfillment. Moses said man lives by the word from God's mouth. Jesus says: I am that Word, and My words are the bread.

> *"Labour not for the meat which perisheth, but for that meat which endureth unto everlasting life, which the Son of man shall give unto you."* — Jn 6:27

Physical bread perishes. The Word endures forever (Isa 40:8, 1 Pe 1:25). The bread worth laboring for is the bread that does not spoil — the eternal covenant terms of God.

### Shewbread — The Word Always Before His Face

> *"And thou shalt set upon the table **shewbread** before me alway."* — Exod 25:30

The Hebrew for shewbread is לֶחֶם הַפָּנִים (*lechem ha-panim*) — literally "bread of the face" or "bread of the presence." Twelve loaves, one for each tribe, placed on a gold table in the Holy Place, perpetually before God's face.

Two details stand out:

First, the bread was renewed every Sabbath (1 Chr 9:32, Lev 24:8). The day of rest — the day of assembly and hearing — was when fresh bread appeared. Fresh Word, every Sabbath, continually.

Second, the bread was "before me alway." Not consumed and gone, but *perpetually present*. God's Word does not expire. It stands before Him as a permanent fixture of the covenant relationship. If bread = Word, then the shewbread is the visible declaration that God's covenant terms are always available, always standing, always on the table.

The priests who ate the old loaves when the new were placed (1 Sam 21:6, Mat 12:4) were consuming what had been in God's presence — receiving the Word that had stood before Him.

### Breaking Bread — Cutting Covenant

> *"This cup is the new covenant in my $[blood], which is shed for you."* — Lk 22:20

> *"And they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in **breaking of bread**, and in prayers."* — Acts 2:42

The Hebrew idiom for making a covenant is כָּרַת בְּרִית (*karat berit*) — to *cut* a covenant. The word for cut is the same used for dividing sacrificial animals (Gen 15:10). Covenants are not signed. They are cut.

If bread = the Word / covenant terms, then *breaking* bread is *entering* the covenant. You receive the terms. You consume them. They become part of you.

The Last Supper enacts this with precision. Jesus takes bread, breaks it, says "this is my body" — the Word made flesh, now broken and distributed. He takes the cup, says "this is the $renewed-covenant in my blood" — $wine, the covenant blood that ratifies the terms. The bread carries the terms; the $wine seals them. Together, they constitute the covenant meal.

This pattern is not new at the Last Supper. It appears at Sinai:

- Exod 24:7 — Moses reads the covenant words to the people (= the bread, the terms)
- Exod 24:8 — Moses sprinkles blood on the people: "Behold the blood of the covenant" (= the $wine, the ratification)
- Exod 24:11 — The elders go up and "did eat and drink" in God's presence (= the covenant meal)

The sequence: hear the terms → blood ratifies → eat and drink together. Acts 2:42 preserves exactly this structure: "the apostles' doctrine" (the terms / the teaching) *and* "breaking of bread" (the covenant meal). The early community did not add a ritual. They continued the pattern: receive the Word, ratify the covenant, eat together in His presence.

### Famine — When the Bread Is Taken Away

> *"Behold, the days come, saith the Lord GOD, that I will send a famine in the land, not a famine of **bread**, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD."* — Amos 8:11

Amos names the opposite of bread directly. The worst famine is not empty stomachs. It is closed heavens — no Word from God. People will "wander from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east, they shall run to and fro to seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it" (Amos 8:12).

This is the negative confirmation. If bread = the Word of God, then famine = the absence of His Word. The physical image clarifies the spiritual reality: a people cut off from God's instructions are starving, no matter how full their tables.

### Unleavened Bread — The Word Without Addition

> *"Then Jesus said unto them, Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees."* — Mat 16:6

> *"Then understood they how that he bade them not beware of the leaven of **bread**, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees."* — Mat 16:12

The disciples think Jesus is talking about literal bread. He corrects them. Leaven = doctrine. The text says this plainly: "Then understood they" — Matthew tells us the disciples decoded the symbol.

If leaven = false doctrine, then unleavened bread = the Word of God *without human addition*. The feast of Unleavened Bread (Exod 12:15–20), eaten for seven days after Passover, becomes a week of consuming the pure, unaltered Word — the covenant terms with nothing mixed in.

Paul confirms the equation in a different register: "Purge out therefore the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, as ye are unleavened. For even Christ our passover is sacrificed for us: therefore let us keep the feast, not with old leaven, neither with the leaven of malice and $[wickedness]; but with the unleavened **bread** of sincerity and $[truth]" (1 Cor 5:7–8).

Unleavened bread of $truth — the Word of God kept pure, without the distortion of human $wickedness or false teaching. The bread itself is good. The leaven is what corrupts it.

## The Full Chain

A single thread runs from Exodus to John:

1. **Physical bread** sustains the body — but God says this is not enough (Deut 8:3)
2. **Manna** arrives from heaven as a Torah-test — bread with commandments embedded in its gathering rules (Exod 16:4)
3. **The Word from God's mouth** is what truly sustains man (Deut 8:3, Mat 4:4)
4. **Shewbread** stands perpetually before God — His Word, always present, renewed every Sabbath (Exod 25:30)
5. **Jesus** is the Word made flesh (Jn 1:14), the living bread come down from heaven (Jn 6:51)
6. **His words** are spirit and life (Jn 6:63) — the eating is the hearing, the bread is the teaching
7. **Breaking bread** = receiving covenant terms and entering covenant (Lk 22:19–20, Acts 2:42)
8. **Famine of bread** = famine of hearing God's Word (Amos 8:11)

At every stage, bread is the same thing: the Word of God — the covenant terms that give life when received and leave starvation when absent.

## Patterns

1. **Bread comes down from heaven.** Manna rained from the sky (Exod 16:4). Jesus is "the living bread which came down from heaven" (Jn 6:51). God's Word is not produced by man — it descends from above.

2. **Bread must be received daily.** Manna could not be hoarded (Exod 16:19–20). The shewbread was renewed every Sabbath. "Give us this day our daily bread" (Mat 6:11). The Word requires ongoing reception, not a one-time acquisition.

3. **Bread pairs with $wine as terms pair with ratification.** Bread = covenant terms; $wine = covenant blood. Together they form the complete covenant meal. Sinai: words read (Exod 24:7), blood sprinkled (Exod 24:8), meal shared (Exod 24:11). Last Supper: bread broken, cup given (Lk 22:19–20).

4. **Leaven corrupts bread as false doctrine corrupts the Word.** Mat 16:12 decodes it explicitly. Unleavened bread = the pure Word of God. The feast of Unleavened Bread = sustained consumption of the Word without human distortion.

5. **Bread that perishes points to bread that endures.** Physical bread spoils (manna with worms, Exod 16:20). Labour not for the perishing bread but for the enduring (Jn 6:27). The Word of the Lord endures forever (1 Pe 1:25).

6. **The one who provides bread is the one who speaks.** God rained manna and spoke the Torah. Jesus is both the bread of life and the Word incarnate. The provider of bread is the speaker of the Word.

7. **Eating = receiving / obeying.** Eating manna came with rules of obedience (Exod 16:4). Eating Jesus' flesh = receiving His words (Jn 6:63). Breaking bread in Acts followed the apostles' *doctrine* (Acts 2:42). To eat is to take the terms inside yourself and live by them.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$wine** — Bread and wine form the two halves of the covenant meal. Bread = the terms; wine = the blood that ratifies them. At Sinai, Moses reads the words (Exod 24:7), then sprinkles the blood (Exod 24:8). At the Last Supper, Jesus breaks bread, then says "this cup is the $renewed-covenant in my blood" (Lk 22:20). One without the other is incomplete.

- **$rock** — The $rock is the covenant itself — unchanging, immovable. Bread is the content of that covenant — the terms spoken and received. Israel ate manna in the wilderness and drank from the $rock (1 Cor 10:3–4). The Word (bread) and the covenant (rock) traveled together.

- **$truth** — "Thy word is truth" (Jn 17:17). "Thy law is the truth" (Ps 119:142). If bread = the Word, and $truth = God's commandments, then bread and $truth converge: what you eat, what you obey, and what sanctifies you are the same substance.

- **$faith** — God's Word (bread) arrives; $faith (obedient response) receives it. The manna tested whether Israel would "walk in my law" (Exod 16:4) — that is, whether they would respond to the bread with $faith. Bread without $faith is manna left ungathered.

- **$marriage** — Breaking bread = covenant ceremony. The $marriage between God and His people is ratified through the covenant meal: receiving the terms (bread), sealed by blood ($wine), eaten together in His presence (Exod 24:11). The Last Supper is a $marriage feast in miniature.

- **$the-way** — Manna was a walking-lesson in Torah. The bread tested whether they would walk in God's $the-way (Exod 16:4). Bread and $the-way are practically synonymous: the Word received (bread) and the Word walked (the way).

- **$fruit** — $fruit = works/deeds, the output of a life. Bread = the Word received as input. The one who consumes the bread (hears and obeys the Word) produces $fruit (visible works of obedience). No bread, no fruit.

- **$mark** — The shewbread was renewed every Sabbath (1 Chr 9:32). The Sabbath is called a $mark / sign (Exod 31:13). The observable practice of Sabbath and the perpetual bread before God's face are linked — the $mark of covenant observance includes regular reception of the Word.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness = lawlessness, covenant transgression. Paul contrasts "the leaven of malice and wickedness" with "the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth" (1 Cor 5:8). $wickedness is the leaven that corrupts the bread — false doctrine mixed into the pure Word.

- **$fornication** — $fornication = idolatry, turning to other masters. To eat bread at another's table — to receive another authority's terms — is $fornication. The pure bread of God's Word versus the corrupted bread of false teaching: "Stolen waters are sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant" (Prov 9:17) — the harlot's invitation.

## For Further Study

- **$wine** — The other half of the covenant meal. If bread = the terms, wine = the blood that seals them. How does the dual nature of wine (joy in covenant, wrath outside it) illuminate what happens when the terms are accepted versus rejected?

- **$rock** — The covenant itself — the unchanging foundation. How do the bread (terms) and the rock (covenant structure) relate? Israel ate bread and drank from the rock simultaneously (1 Cor 10:3–4).

- **$truth** — "Thy word is truth." If bread = the Word and truth = the commandments, how do these two symbols converge — and what does it mean to "eat" truth?

- **$marriage** — The covenant meal is a wedding feast. How does breaking bread together map onto the $marriage between God and His people?

- **$mark** — The Sabbath is a sign, and the shewbread was renewed every Sabbath. How does the regular rhythm of receiving the Word connect to the visible practices that identify covenant people?

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **bread** begins:

> “Bread, v. t. Etym: [AS. brædan to make broad, to spread. See Broad, a.] Defn: To spread. [Obs.] Ray.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The printed verses explicitly contrast ordinary bread with the word proceeding from God rather than directly identifying bread as that word. They support an analogy of nourishment and dependence, but only indirectly support the glossary’s categorical equation.

- One could argue REFINED: the consensus's broadest category is 'God-given spiritual nourishment,' and the book merely narrows within that category to the word of God — a sense every response lists as common, and one the notes in Response A even say is often folded into the christological reading (Christ as the Word). Additionally, the cited verses themselves set bread in contrast to the word ('not by bread alone'), so the equation bread=word is derivative; a defender would say the analogy of nourishment underwrites it, keeping the book within the consensus's own recognized range.

- The provided citations (Deuteronomy 8:3 and Matthew 4:4) contrast physical bread with the Word of God rather than explicitly identifying bread as a symbol for the Word. Furthermore, isolating this single meaning ignores the far more dominant New Testament identification of bread with Christ's body and the Eucharist.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The word of God. Unleavened bread is pure doctrine; [leavened bread](/research/symbols/leaven/) contains corrupting doctrine.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *bread*, *loaf*, *loaves*, *manna*, *shewbread*. The English-form sweep found **383 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
