# Water

**The life that flows from God's presence — distinguished from the $sea by its source and movement, and from human religion by its continuous, self-renewing supply.**

> *"For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the **fountain** of **living waters**, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no **water**."* — Jer 2:13

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture uses the same word — H4325 *mayim* — for two very different things: the chaotic $sea of nations (Rev 17:15) and the life-giving water that flows from God's throne (Rev 22:1). How do you know which is which?

The physical world provides the answer. Water exists in two states:

1. **Living water** (*mayim chayim*) — flowing from a source. Springs, rivers, fountains. Continuously renewed. Fresh. Moving.
2. **Gathered water** — collected in seas, cisterns, pools. Stagnant. No source — only what was put in. Can go bitter, salty, dead.

The Hebrew phrase H2416 *chay* + H4325 *mayim* — literally "living waters" — appears in both the Torah's purification laws and the prophets' descriptions of God. The KJV translates the same phrase as "running water" in Leviticus and "living waters" in Jeremiah — but the Hebrew is identical. The water that cleanses a leper and the water that flows from God are the same kind: *mayim chayim* — water that is *alive*, sourced, moving.

The $sea study established that gathered waters represent nations and peoples in their chaotic, ungoverned state (Rev 17:15). This study asks: what does the *other* water represent — the water that springs, flows, heals, and gives life?

**Symbolizes:** God's life flowing to humanity — His Spirit, His Word, His presence — proceeding from the divine source and bringing life wherever it goes

**Opposite:** Cisterns (man-made, broken, containment of dead water) / bitter water / the $sea (gathered, chaotic, restless)

**The surprise:** The living water from God's temple flows *into* the $sea and heals it (Eze 47:8–9). The symbolic system is not two separate categories — it is one drama. God's life enters the mass of nations and transforms them.

**Connected:** $sea, $rock, $fire, $wine, $blood, $tree, $pit-abyss

## Water Across Scripture

### The Fountain and the Cistern

Jeremiah 2:13 is the single verse that unlocks the entire symbol. It names two evils — not one:

1. They have forsaken ME — **the fountain** (H4726 *maqor*) of living waters
2. They have hewed out **cisterns** (H877 *bor*) — broken cisterns that hold no water

H4726 *maqor* means "something dug, a source — of water, even when naturally flowing; figuratively, of happiness, wisdom, progeny." It is the *source itself* — the place where water originates.

H877 *bor* means "a cistern" — a pit that holds collected water. Remarkably, it shares the same root (H874 *ba'ar*) as H875 *be'er* — "a well." A well and a cistern are both dug pits. The difference is functional: a well reaches a living spring beneath the ground; a cistern stores rainwater that was gathered from above. One accesses a source. The other contains a supply.

But H877 *bor* has a darker relative. H953 *bowr* — defined by Strong's as "a pit hole, especially one used as **a cistern or a prison**" — is translated by the KJV as "pit," "cistern," "dungeon," and "well." And in the Psalms and Prophets, this same *bowr* is used in synonymous parallelism with H7585 *sheol* — the grave:

> *"Thou hast brought up my soul from the **grave**; thou hast kept me alive, that I should not go down to the **pit**."* — Ps 30:3

> *"Thou shalt be brought down to **hell**, to the sides of the **pit**."* — Isa 14:15

> *"The **grave** cannot praise thee… they that go down into the **pit** cannot hope for thy truth."* — Isa 38:18

The cistern IS the grave in Hebrew poetic structure. The same *bowr* that holds dead water is the *bowr* that holds the dead. Joseph was cast into an empty *bowr* with "no water" (Gen 37:24) — a death-container. Jeremiah was thrown into a *bowr* with "no water, but mire" (Jer 38:6) — and sank. Psalm 40:2 reverses the image: *"He brought me up out of an horrible **pit**, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a **rock**."* Out of the cistern-grave and onto the $rock — from death to covenant.

So when Jeremiah 2:13 says the people "hewed out cisterns," the Hebrew ear hears: they dug their own graves. To forsake the fountain of living water and choose a *bor* is to choose a death-container over the source of life. The broken cistern that holds no water is an open tomb.

This distinction maps precisely: God is the living spring — continuous, self-renewing, inexhaustible. Human religious systems are cisterns — containers built to hold what was collected. And the cisterns are *broken* — they cannot even preserve what they captured. They are graves.

Jeremiah 17:13 repeats the image: *"They have forsaken the LORD, the **fountain** of **living waters**."*

And Psalm 36:9 collapses fountain and life into a single phrase: *"For with thee is the **fountain** of **life**."* H4726 *maqor* + H2416 *chayim*. The same *maqor* that produces living water in Jeremiah produces life itself in the Psalms. The fountain of water and the fountain of life are one source.

### Only Living Water Purifies

The Torah's purification laws contain a quiet but absolute requirement: serious defilement demands *mayim chayim* — living, running water. No substitute.

> *"As for the living bird, he shall take it, and the cedar wood, and the scarlet, and the hyssop, and shall dip them and the living bird in the blood of the bird that was killed over the **running water**."* — Lev 14:6

> *"And for an unclean person they shall take of the ashes of the burnt heifer of purification for sin, and **running water** shall be put thereto in a vessel."* — Num 19:17

> *"He shall… bathe his flesh in **running water**, and shall be clean."* — Lev 15:13

The Hebrew in all three passages is identical: H2416 *chay* + H4325 *mayim*. The same "living water" that God is the fountain of.

The conditions that require it are the most severe forms of defilement: leprosy (Lev 14), contact with death (Num 19), and persistent bodily discharge (Lev 15). Ordinary washing uses ordinary water. But when death or deep corruption is involved, only living water — water connected to a source — will do.

The purification power is not in the volume. It is in the *source-connection*. Water that flows from a spring carries something that collected water does not: the continuous renewal of its origin.

Leviticus 11:36 confirms the principle from the other direction: *"A **fountain** or pit, wherein there is plenty of water, shall be **clean**."* The fountain does not become unclean by contact — it is continuously renewed. The dead thing that touches it is absorbed; the source overwhelms the contamination.

### Water from the Rock

> *"Behold, I will stand before thee there upon the **rock** in Horeb; and thou shalt smite the **rock**, and there shall come **water** out of it, that the people may drink."* — Exod 17:6

The wilderness narrative is structured around water crises. The people thirst, and God provides — always from an unexpected source: the $rock.

At Horeb, Moses strikes the rock and water flows. At Kadesh, God tells Moses to *speak* to the rock (Num 20:8). In both cases, the $rock — which the $rock study identifies as covenant — produces the water that sustains Israel. The covenant is the source; the water is what flows from it.

Psalm 114:8 captures both: *"Which turned the **rock** into a standing **water**, the flint into a **fountain** of **waters**."* The rock *becomes* a fountain. The covenant itself is the spring from which living water flows.

This pattern culminates in two visions:

> *"There is a **river**, the streams whereof shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the most High."* — Ps 46:4

> *"And he shewed me a pure **river** of **water** of **life**, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb."* — Rev 22:1

The river of life proceeds from God's throne — the ultimate "rock," the seat of covenant authority. From Horeb to Revelation, the pattern is consistent: the living water comes from the $rock.

### Water and Spirit

Isaiah draws the parallel explicitly:

> *"I will pour **water** upon him that is thirsty, and floods upon the dry ground: I will pour my **spirit** upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring."* — Isa 44:3

Two lines, synonymous parallelism:

| Line A | Line B |
|--------|--------|
| water upon the thirsty | spirit upon thy seed |
| floods upon dry ground | blessing upon offspring |

Water and Spirit are parallel expressions of the same gift: God's life poured out on those who lack it.

John confirms: *"He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of **living water**. But this spake he of the **Spirit**."* — Jn 7:38–39

And Ezekiel 36:25–27 gives the sequence:

1. *"I will sprinkle **clean water** upon you, and ye shall be clean"* (v.25)
2. *"A new heart also will I give you, and a **new spirit** will I put within you"* (v.26)
3. *"I will put **my Spirit** within you"* (v.27)

Clean water first. Then new spirit. Then God's Spirit. The water-washing prepares the ground; the Spirit-indwelling fills it. They are not identical, but they are inseparable — two movements of the same flow from the same fountain.

### Water and Word

A second parallel emerges: water and the spoken/written Word.

> *"That he might sanctify and cleanse it with the washing of **water** by the **word**."* — Eph 5:26

> *"The words of a man's mouth are as deep **waters**, and the **wellspring** of wisdom as a flowing brook."* — Prov 18:4

> *"For as the rain cometh down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but **watereth** the earth, and maketh it bring forth and bud… So shall my **word** be that goeth forth out of my mouth."* — Isa 55:10–11

The pattern: God's Word descends like water from heaven, enters the earth, and produces life. The same downward trajectory as rain — heaven's substance entering the earthly domain and causing growth. Isaiah 55:1 opens with the invitation: *"Ho, every one that **thirsteth**, come ye to the **waters**."* Thirst for water is thirst for God's Word.

Both Spirit and Word are expressions of the living water. They are the means by which God's life flows from the throne to humanity — the Spirit as internal fountain (Jn 4:14: "a well of water springing up into everlasting life"), the Word as external washing (Eph 5:26).

### The Bitter Made Sweet

> *"And when they came to Marah, they could not drink of the **waters** of Marah, for they were **bitter**… And the LORD shewed him a **tree**, which when he had cast into the **waters**, the **waters** were made sweet."* — Exod 15:23–25

The first water crisis after the Red Sea. The water exists but is undrinkable — bitter. God's remedy: a $tree cast into the bitter water transforms it. Immediately after, God establishes a statute and ordinance and declares: *"I am the LORD that **healeth** thee"* (Exod 15:26).

The tree transforms death-water into life-water. The passage connects healing, Torah (statute and ordinance), and the transformation of what is bitter into what sustains. The $tree — planted by the water — is the agent of healing.

This foreshadows Ezekiel 47:12, where the trees by the temple river produce fruit for food and leaves *"for medicine"* — and their power comes from the water: *"their waters they issued out of the sanctuary."*

### Living Water Heals the Sea

The most remarkable water passage in the prophets is Ezekiel 47:1–12. Water issues from under the temple threshold and flows eastward:

> *"He measured a thousand cubits, and he brought me through the **waters**; the waters were to the ankles… to the knees… to the loins… **waters to swim in**, a river that could not be passed over."* — Eze 47:3–5

The river starts as a trickle and becomes un-crossable. It deepens as it flows from the source — the sanctuary. And then:

> *"These **waters** issue out toward the east country, and go down into the desert, and go into the **sea**: which being brought forth into the **sea**, the **waters** shall be **healed**."* — Eze 47:8

> *"Every thing that liveth, which moveth, whithersoever the rivers shall come, shall **live**… every thing shall live whither the river cometh."* — Eze 47:9

The living water enters the $sea and *heals* it. This is the bridge between the two water symbols. The $sea is the chaotic mass of nations. The living water is God's life flowing from the sanctuary. When the living water reaches the sea, the sea is transformed — it teems with life. The fish become abundant, "as the fish of the great sea, exceeding many" (Eze 47:10).

But not everything is healed: *"The miry places thereof and the marishes thereof shall not be healed; they shall be given to salt"* (Eze 47:11). The marshes — mixed, neither sea nor land, neither flowing nor gathered — remain salt. The living water heals what receives it. What resists remains desolate.

Revelation completes the image: *"He shewed me a pure **river** of **water of life**, clear as crystal, proceeding out of the throne of God and of the Lamb"* (Rev 22:1). *"And the Spirit and the bride say, Come… And whosoever will, let him take the **water of life** freely"* (Rev 22:17). In the final vision, the river from the throne is offered to all who thirst. The temple river of Ezekiel has become the throne river of Revelation — and the invitation is universal.

## How to Tell the Difference

When Scripture says "water," the context always signals which kind:

| Marker | Sense | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| "living," "running," "springing" | God's life | Jer 2:13, Jn 4:10 |
| "from the rock," "from the throne," "from the sanctuary" | God's provision | Exod 17:6, Rev 22:1, Eze 47:1 |
| "clean," "pure" | Purification | Eze 36:25, Heb 10:22 |
| "many waters," "great waters," "troubled" | Nations / peoples | Rev 17:15, Isa 17:12, Ps 29:3 |
| "sea" (H3220 / G2281) | Gathered mass of nations | Rev 13:1, Rev 21:1 |
| "cistern," "broken" | Man-made religion | Jer 2:13 |
| "bitter" | Cursed / undrinkable | Exod 15:23, Num 5:24 |
| "flood," "overflowing" | Judgment / invasion | Isa 28:2, Dan 9:26 |

The H2416 *chay* qualifier is the most reliable: whenever *mayim* is modified by *chay* ("living/running"), the water is God's life. The $sea (H3220 *yam*) always means the gathered mass.

## Patterns

1. **Source is everything.** The defining difference between living water and dead water is not composition — it is origin. A fountain has a source. A cistern has a supply. God is the fountain (Jer 2:13). Human systems are cisterns.

2. **Living water = Spirit and Word.** Both are called water. Isaiah 44:3 equates water with Spirit in synonymous parallelism. John 7:39 confirms. Ephesians 5:26 equates water with Word. They are two expressions of the same living flow from the same source.

3. **Only sourced water purifies.** The Torah requires *mayim chayim* for serious defilement (Lev 14, 15; Num 19). The purification power is in the source-connection, not the volume.

4. **Well vs. cistern = accessing the source vs. storing a supply.** H875 *be'er* (well) and H877 *bor* (cistern) share the same root. The well reaches the living spring below. The cistern holds collected water from above. Same pit, opposite function.

5. **The rock produces the water.** From Horeb (Exod 17:6) to Revelation (Rev 22:1), the living water flows from the $rock — the covenant source. The throne of God is the ultimate rock from which the river of life proceeds.

6. **Living water heals the sea.** Eze 47:8–9. The sanctuary river enters the chaotic mass and transforms it. God's life does not merely oppose the sea — it enters and heals it.

7. **The tree transforms bitter to sweet.** Exod 15:25. The $tree cast into death-water produces life-water. The trees by Ezekiel's river produce medicine (Eze 47:12). The tree of life stands by the river of life (Rev 22:2).

## Connections

- **$sea** — The gathered mass of nations, chaotic and ungoverned (Rev 17:15). The sea is the *opposite* of living water: gathered vs. sourced, restless vs. flowing, stagnant vs. alive. But they are not merely opposites — living water enters the sea and heals it (Eze 47:8–9). The river from the sanctuary transforms what the sea represents.

- **$rock** — The $rock produces the water. At Horeb (Exod 17:6) and Kadesh (Num 20:8), the rock is struck or spoken to and water flows. Psalm 114:8 — the rock *becomes* a fountain. If rock = covenant, then living water = what covenant produces: God's life flowing to His people.

- **$fire** — The other purifying agent. Both $fire and water cleanse, but by different means: fire consumes what violates covenant; water washes and restores. Num 31:23 — "every thing that may abide the fire, ye shall make it go through the fire… and all that abideth not the fire ye shall make go through the water."

- **$wine** — $wine is pressed from grapes; water flows from a source. At Cana, water *becomes* wine (Jn 2:1–11). The connection: water is the raw material of God's provision; wine is covenant blood. Water sustains life; wine seals covenant.

- **$blood** — Water and blood flow together from Christ's side (Jn 19:34). 1 John 5:6–8 names three witnesses: Spirit, water, and blood. Water purifies; blood atones. They are distinct but inseparable in the covenant drama.

- **$tree** — The tree transforms bitter water to sweet (Exod 15:25). Trees by the river produce medicine (Eze 47:12). The tree of life stands by the river of life (Rev 22:2). The $tree is the living organism that connects to the water source and produces fruit.

- **$pit-abyss** — The cistern (H877/H953 *bor/bowr*) and the grave (H7585 *sheol*) are the same word-field. Ps 30:3 places "grave" and "pit" in synonymous parallelism. Joseph's waterless pit (Gen 37:24) and Jeremiah's miry dungeon (Jer 38:6) are H953 *bowr* — the cistern-as-grave. To choose cisterns over the fountain (Jer 2:13) is to choose graves over life.

## For Further Study

- **$sea** — The chaotic mass of nations that living water heals (Eze 47:8–9). If the sea is what humanity looks like without covenant, and living water is God's life flowing from the sanctuary, what does it mean that the river enters the sea rather than destroying it?

- **$rock** — The covenant source from which living water flows (Exod 17:6, Ps 114:8). If rock = covenant, and water proceeds from the rock, what does it mean that Moses was told to *speak* to the rock the second time (Num 20:8) rather than strike it?

- **$fire** — The other purifying element. Fire and water both cleanse, but fire consumes while water washes. Num 31:23 separates what goes through fire from what goes through water. How do these two purifications relate to judgment and restoration?

- **$tree** — The agent that transforms bitter water into sweet (Exod 15:25), the organism that draws from the water source and produces fruit and medicine (Eze 47:12, Rev 22:2). How does the tree's relationship to water illuminate its symbolic meaning?

- **$pit-abyss** — H953 *bowr* (pit/cistern) parallels H7585 *sheol* (grave) throughout the Psalms and Prophets (Ps 30:3, 88:4–6, Isa 14:15, 38:18). The cistern and the grave are the same *bowr*. Joseph's empty pit with no water (Gen 37:24) and Jeremiah's dungeon with no water but mire (Jer 38:6) are both H953 — death-containers from which God draws His servants out.
