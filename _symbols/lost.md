### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **lost** begins: “Parted with unwillingly or unintentionally; not to be found; missing; as, a lost book or sheep.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Matthew 15:24 strongly identifies the immediate referent as the house of Israel, but that verse alone does not establish that all uses of “lost sheep” have this exclusive referent, nor does it explicitly state the fuller scattered-and-regathered formulation.

- Matthew 15:24 explicitly says Jesus was sent 'unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel,' so one could argue the book's identification is not a narrowing but the plain textual meaning of that verse, making it EQUIVALENT for the Matthean usage; however, the consensus term covers the broader biblical image (Luke 15, Isa 53:6, Ps 119:176), and the book's single-verse basis does not establish the 'regathered' scattered-Israel emphasis, nor does it address the dominant sinner/repentance usage.

- The parables in Luke 15 and Matthew 18 are traditionally and contextually read as applying to individual sinners and vulnerable disciples generally, not solely as allegories for the ethnic or historical house of Israel.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
