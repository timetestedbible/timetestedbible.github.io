## The Short Case

Reverent awe expressed as keeping the commandments and departing from evil.

> *“Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man.”* — Ecclesiastes 12:13

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The sole printed citation, Ecclesiastes 12:13, strongly supports fear expressed through commandment-keeping, but it does not by itself establish the gloss 'reverent awe' or the additional element of departing from evil.

- One could argue the book's definition, by casting fear wholly as behavioral expression (commandment-keeping) rather than including any element of genuine dread or the wisdom-foundation motif, slightly narrows the semantic range some consensus responses preserve, which might suggest REFINED. But the consensus responses themselves treat the covenant-obedience expression as part of the primary meaning, so this is emphasis, not boundary change. Also, the single printed citation (Eccl 12:13) supports 'keeping the commandments' but not explicitly 'departing from evil,' which is why support is only partial.

- None.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
