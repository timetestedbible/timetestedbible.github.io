# Wind

## Summary

**Spirit — ruach.**
> *"The **wind** bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit."* — Jn 3:8

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**Spirit — ruach.**

### Literal Biblical Definition

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **wind** begins: “To turn completely, or with repeated turns; especially, to turn about something fixed; to cause to form convolutions about anything; to coil; to twine; to twist; to wreathe; as, to wind thread on a spool or into a ball.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

Hebrew has one word where English needs three. H7307 *ruach* means wind, breath, and spirit — not because the Hebrews couldn't distinguish between them, but because Scripture treats them as the same reality operating at different scales. Wind is what Spirit looks like when it touches the physical world. Breath is what Spirit feels like when it enters a body.

This is not a metaphor. It is a single word describing a single invisible force that blows through the world — you hear it, you see its effects, but you cannot see *it*. Jesus said this explicitly: "The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit" (Jn 3:8). He was not drawing an analogy between two separate things. He was pointing at *one thing* — *ruach* — and saying: this is how Spirit works.

The angel in Zechariah confirms the identification. Four chariots with colored horses go out from between two mountains of brass. Zechariah asks what they are. The angel answers: "These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth" (Zech 6:5). The word translated "spirits" is H7307 *ruach*. The four winds *are* the four spirits — God's executing agents dispatched from the throne room.

Once this identity holds, the wind passages across Scripture resolve into a single pattern: invisible power sent from God's presence, carrying out specific assignments — scattering, gathering, stirring, restraining, giving life, bringing death.

**Symbolizes:** God's invisible executing power — Spirit-forces dispatched from His throne

**Opposite:** Stillness — when the winds are held back, God's executing power is restrained (Rev 7:1)

**Defining verses:** Jn 3:8, Zech 6:5, Ezek 37:9, Dan 7:2–3, Rev 7:1, Ps 104:4

**The surprise:** The four winds are not impersonal weather. They are *angelic beings* — spirits who stand before the Lord and go forth on mission. Psalm 104:4 makes this explicit: "Who maketh his angels spirits (*ruach*); his ministers a flaming fire."

**Connected:** $sea, $four-winds, $eagle, $animal, $tree, $grass, $wings

## Wind Across Scripture

### Angels Are Winds — Winds Are Angels

> *"Who maketh his **angels** spirits; his ministers a flaming fire."* — Ps 104:4

The word translated "spirits" is H7307 *ruach* — the same word translated "wind" everywhere else. God *makes* His angels into winds. They are not merely compared to winds; they are described as operating *as* winds. Hebrews 1:7 quotes this psalm directly into the New Testament: "Who maketh his angels spirits, and his ministers a flame of fire."

Zechariah 6:5 confirms the same equation from the other direction. The four chariots are "the four spirits (*ruach*) of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth." These spirits stand in the divine council — they have position, assignment, direction. They are dispatched, not random. They are the $eagle-messengers of God's throne, the executing agents who carry His purposes to the four corners of the earth.

The number four matters. Four winds, four directions, four corners — the number signifies comprehensive, universal scope. When God sends the four winds, He is acting across the *whole* earth. Nothing is outside their reach.

### The Four Assignments

The four winds appear across Scripture in four distinct but related functions. Each function is a mode of the same power:

#### 1. Scattering — Covenant Judgment

> *"And upon Elam will I bring the four **winds** from the four quarters of heaven, and will scatter them toward all those **winds**; and there shall be no nation whither the outcasts of Elam shall not come."* — Jer 49:36

> *"I have spread you abroad as the four **winds** of the heaven, saith the LORD."* — Zech 2:6

The four winds as instruments of scattering — dispersal to the four corners. This is covenant curse language. To be "spread abroad as the four winds" is to be dissolved as a people, cast in every direction, with no center remaining. The invisible power that once gathered Israel now drives them apart.

#### 2. Gathering — Covenant Restoration

> *"And he shall send his $[eagle]s with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four **winds**, from one end of heaven to the other."* — Mat 24:31

The same winds that scatter also define the directions *from which* the gathering occurs. The $eagle-messengers (angels) are sent to the four winds to collect the elect. The scattering and the gathering use the same geography — the four quarters of heaven. What was dispersed to the winds is recovered from the winds.

#### 3. Stirring Nations

> *"Daniel spake and said, I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four **winds** of the heaven strove upon the great $[sea]. And four great $[animal]s came up from the $[sea], diverse one from another."* — Dan 7:2–3

Here the four winds act upon the $sea — the chaotic mass of nations and peoples. The winds *strive* upon the waters, and from the agitation, four beasts rise. The $animal-kingdoms (Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome) do not emerge by their own power. They are stirred up — called forth by the invisible executing spirits working on the surface of the nations.

The image is precise: Spirit-forces agitate the peoples, and from the turbulence, empires emerge. The kingdoms serve God's prophetic purposes not because they choose to, but because the winds drove them up out of the deep.

#### 4. Restraining — Judgment Delayed

> *"And after these things I saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four **winds** of the earth, that the **wind** should not blow on the $[earth], nor on the $[sea], nor on any $[tree]."* — Rev 7:1

Four angels *hold* the four winds. The same executing forces that scatter, gather, and stir are now restrained — held at the corners until the 144,000 are sealed. When the wind is held back, judgment does not fall. The $sea (nations), the $tree (people groups), the $grass (mortal people) — all are spared until the sealing is complete.

This is the opposite of the wind. The wind's counterpart is not another symbol — it is *stillness*. When God's executing power is withheld, there is calm. The four angels at the four corners can hold back the storm because they are the same order of being as the storm itself: spirits restraining spirits, angels holding winds.

### The East Wind — The LORD's Specific Instrument

While the four winds represent comprehensive power across all the earth, the *east wind* appears repeatedly as a specific instrument of covenant judgment:

> *"And the seven thin ears and blasted with the east **wind** shall be seven years of famine."* — Gen 41:27

> *"Ephraim feedeth on **wind**, and followeth after the east **wind**: he daily increaseth lies and desolation."* — Hos 12:1

> *"Though he be fruitful among his brethren, an east **wind** shall come, the **wind** of the LORD shall come up from the wilderness, and his spring shall become dry, and his fountain shall be dried up."* — Hos 13:15

> *"Their faces shall sup up as the east **wind**."* — Hab 1:9

The east wind comes from the desert — from the wilderness. It is hot, drying, destructive. Hosea calls it explicitly "the wind of the LORD" (Hos 13:15). It blasts grain, dries springs, drives desolation. And Ephraim *feeds on it* — pursues the very force that will destroy him.

The contrast with the four winds is instructive. The four winds operate universally; the east wind operates specifically, targeting covenant-breakers with drying judgment. Both are *ruach*. Both are the LORD's. But the east wind carries the character of the wilderness — the place of testing, stripping, and exposure.

### Come From the Four Winds, O Breath

> *"Then said he unto me, Prophesy unto the **wind**, prophesy, son of man, and say to the **wind**, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Come from the four **winds**, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live."* — Ezek 37:9

This is perhaps the most extraordinary wind passage in Scripture. The valley of dry bones — scattered, disconnected, lifeless. Ezekiel prophesies, and the bones reassemble: bone to bone, sinew to sinew, flesh to flesh. But they do not live. Something is missing.

"Prophesy unto the wind."

The word in every instance here is H7307 *ruach*. Ezekiel is told to call the *ruach* from the four *ruach* to breathe (*ruach*) upon the slain. Wind, spirit, and breath converge in a single verse. The same invisible executing power that scatters nations and stirs kingdoms now *enters the dead* and they stand up — "an exceeding great army" (Ezek 37:10).

The four winds carry life as readily as death. The same force that scattered Israel to the four corners now comes *from* those four corners to resurrect them. The scattering and the regathering are performed by the same agent. God's executing spirits do not have a single mode — they carry whatever purpose is dispatched from the throne.

## Patterns

1. **Wind and Spirit are the same word.** H7307 *ruach* is translated "wind" when the context emphasizes physical force and "spirit" when the context emphasizes invisible agency — but the Hebrew makes no such distinction. Every wind passage is also a spirit passage.

2. **The four winds are throne-room agents.** Zechariah 6:5 identifies them as spirits that *stand before the Lord* and *go forth* on assignment. Psalm 104:4 identifies angels as winds. These are not impersonal forces — they are dispatched beings.

3. **The same wind scatters and gathers.** Jeremiah 49:36 and Zechariah 2:6 use the four winds for judgment-scattering. Matthew 24:31 and Ezekiel 37:9 use them for restoration-gathering. The wind's purpose depends on the sender's assignment, not on its own nature.

4. **Wind stirs the sea; beasts rise.** Daniel 7:2–3 shows spirit-forces acting on the nations ($sea) to produce kingdoms ($animal). The empires of history are not self-generated — they are stirred up by invisible power.

5. **Wind restrained = judgment delayed.** Revelation 7:1 shows that when the winds are held, nothing on $earth, $sea, or $tree is harmed. Stillness is the opposite of wind — the withholding of God's executing power.

6. **East wind = specific covenant curse.** The general four winds cover the whole earth; the east wind — from the wilderness, drying and destructive — targets the covenant-breaker specifically (Gen 41:27, Hos 12:1, Hos 13:15).

7. **Wind gives life to the dead.** Ezekiel 37:9 — *ruach* from the four *ruach* breathes upon the slain and they stand up alive. The executing power of God is also the life-giving power of God. Scattering and resurrection come from the same source.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$sea** — Wind acts *upon* the sea. Daniel 7:2–3 shows four winds striving on the $sea (nations / chaotic peoples), stirring up the kingdoms that rise from it. Revelation 7:1 shows wind restrained so it does not blow on the $sea. The wind is the force; the sea is the field.

- **$four-winds** — The "four winds" are the specifically numbered expression of this symbol — God's executing spirits dispatched from the throne to the four quarters of the earth. Zechariah 6:5 defines them as spirits standing before the Lord. They represent the universal scope of God's invisible agency.

- **$eagle** — Psalm 104:4 identifies angels as winds/spirits. The $eagle represents the messenger/angel — the sent agent operating under covenant authority. The wind *is* the eagle at work: the invisible force that carries the messenger's assignment.

- **$animal** — Beasts rise from the $sea when the winds stir it (Dan 7:2–3). The $animal (people / tribe / sub-state) does not generate itself — it is called forth by the wind's action on the nations. Spirit-power produces kingdoms.

- **$tree** — Revelation 7:1 holds the wind back so it does not blow on any $tree (nation / people group). The $tree is what the wind would strike if released. Trees bend, break, or stand under the wind — nations respond to the Spirit-forces God sends.

- **$grass** — Isaiah 40:7: "The grass withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit (*ruach*) of the LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people is grass." The wind acts upon $grass (mortal people) and they wither. The same Spirit that gives life (Ezek 37:9) also removes it. The $grass passage explicitly equates people with grass *and* identifies the wind as the agent of their passing.

- **$wings** — H3671 *kanaph* (wings / covenant-covering) connects to wind through flight. The $eagle has wings; the wind *is* the spirit. Both are agents of covenant purpose. The four corners (H3671 *kanaph*) of the earth in Isaiah 11:12 are the same domain where the four winds operate.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **wind** begins:

> “Wind, v. t. [imp. & p. p. Wound (wound) (rarely Winded); p. pr. & vb. n. Winding.] Etym: [OE. winden, AS. windan; akin to OS. windan, D. & G. winden, OHG. wintan, Icel. & Sw. vinda, Dan. vinde, Goth. windan (in comp.). Cf. Wander, Wend.] 1. To turn completely, or with repeated turns; especially, to turn about something fixed; to cause to form convolutions about anything; to coil; to twine; to twist; to wreathe; as, to wind thread on a spool or into a ball. Whether to wind The woodbine round this arbor. Milton.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The entry might be read simply as the standard lexical equivalence of ruach with wind/spirit, making it a MATCH. Nevertheless, Zechariah 6:5’s plural heavenly spirits indicates a broader application than the consensus’s primary identification with the Holy Spirit.

- One could argue the book's sole citation, Zech 6:5, points to the 'four spirits/winds of the heavens' — which several consensus responses classify as a distinct sense (angelic agents or cosmic directions), not the Holy Spirit — so the book might be identifying wind with created spirits rather than God's Spirit, which would push toward REFINED or even a different referent. However, the bare gloss 'Spirit — ruach' most naturally states the general lexical-symbolic equation the consensus itself treats as primary, and a single illustrative verse is thin evidence for a deliberate reclassification.

- Zechariah 6:5 is treated by the consensus mainly under the secondary 'four winds' sense (directions, agents or angelic spirits) rather than as a primary proof-text for the Holy Spirit, so the book's sole printed citation may appear under-powered for the full consensus claim.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**Spirit — ruach.**

This is the glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.
