# Wickedness

**Covenant transgression — lawlessness that rejects God's standard, burns like fire, and consumes itself.**

> *"If there be found among you… man or woman, that hath wrought **wickedness** in the sight of the LORD thy God, in transgressing his covenant"* — Deut 17:2

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not leave "wickedness" as a vague moral category. It defines the word — twice, in two languages.

In Deuteronomy, wickedness is given an explicit definition: **transgressing his covenant**. A man or woman who crosses the line of God's covenant has "wrought wickedness." Not a feeling. Not a disposition. An act — the act of breaking covenant.

In the Greek New Testament, the word behind "iniquity" and "wicked" confirms the same idea from the other direction. Paul writes of the "mystery of iniquity" (G458 *anomia* — literally *a-nomos*, without law) and "that Wicked" (G459 *anomos* — the lawless one). The Greek dismantles any ambiguity: wickedness = **lawlessness**. The absence of, or rebellion against, *nomos* — God's law, His standard, His covenant terms.

These are not two different meanings. They are the same meaning stated from two angles. To transgress the covenant *is* to be lawless. To be lawless *is* to transgress the covenant. The Hebrew gives the relational dimension (breaking faith with a covenant partner); the Greek gives the structural dimension (operating outside the law). Together they produce one unified picture: **wickedness is the rejection of God's standard**.

This matters because it means wickedness is not measured against cultural norms, personal conscience, or comparative morality. It is measured against one fixed reference: God's covenant and law — what the pipeline identifies as $truth (commandment / God's standard). $truth is the standard; wickedness is the transgression of that standard. They are exact opposites in motion.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant transgression — lawlessness, the rejection of God's standard

**Opposite:** Righteousness — covenant keeping, walking according to God's law

**Defining verses:** Deut 17:2, 2 Thess 2:7–8, Isa 9:18–20, Isa 57:20–21

**The surprise:** Wickedness is not just a legal category — it is described as a *fire* that devours its own fuel. The wicked become the combustion material for the very destruction their lawlessness ignites. The system consumes itself.

**Connected:** $truth, $thorns, $sea, $fruit, $babylon

## Wickedness Across Scripture

### The Two Definitions

The Old Testament definition is embedded in a legal case:

> *"If there be found among you, within any of thy gates which the LORD thy God giveth thee, man or woman, that hath wrought **wickedness** in the sight of the LORD thy God, **in transgressing his covenant**"* — Deut 17:2

The syntax is precise. "Wrought wickedness" is the charge. "In transgressing his covenant" is the definition. The two phrases are not listing separate offenses — the second clause explains the first. Wickedness *is* covenant transgression.

The New Testament offers the same definition in Greek architecture:

> *"For the mystery of **iniquity** (G458 *anomia*) doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way. And then shall that **Wicked** (G459 *anomos*) be revealed"* — 2 Thess 2:7–8

G458 *anomia* breaks apart into *a-* (without) + *nomos* (law). It is not "sin" generically — it is the specific condition of being *without law*, operating outside God's established order. And the person who embodies this principle is called *anomos* — the lawless one. The "mystery of iniquity" is the mystery of lawlessness already at work, concealed, waiting to be revealed in its fullest expression.

Notice the progression Paul traces: lawlessness is already present as a hidden principle ("doth already work"), restrained for a time, and will ultimately manifest in a person — "that Wicked," the lawless one. The principle and the person share the same name because they share the same nature: rejection of God's law.

This dual definition — Hebrew covenant transgression, Greek lawlessness — establishes that wickedness in Scripture is never merely "bad behavior." It is a specific posture toward God's standard: refusal.

### The Fire That Eats Itself

Once the definition is established, Isaiah reveals what wickedness *does*:

> *"For **wickedness** burneth as the fire: it shall devour the $[thorns] and briers, and shall kindle in the thickets of the forest, and they shall mount up like the lifting up of smoke."* — Isa 9:18

> *"Through the wrath of the LORD of hosts is the land darkened, and the people shall be as the fuel of the fire: no man shall spare his brother. And he shall snatch on the right hand, and be hungry; and he shall eat on the left hand, and they shall not be satisfied: they shall eat every man the flesh of his own arm."* — Isa 9:19–20

Three verbs define wickedness's behavior: it **burns**, it **devours**, it **kindles**. Wickedness operates like a fire — but the passage immediately reveals what that fire consumes. First the $thorns and briers — the worldly, choking people characterized by "cares and riches and pleasures of this life" (Lk 8:14). Wickedness ignites the very population that lawlessness produces.

Then the circle tightens: "the people shall be as the fuel of the fire." The lawless society becomes its own combustion material. "No man shall spare his brother" — covenant bonds dissolve. "They shall eat every man the flesh of his own arm" — the system consumes its own strength.

This is not poetic excess. It is the logic of lawlessness carried to its conclusion. When the covenant standard is rejected, there is no external framework to restrain appetite. Each person becomes fuel for the fire of the next. The $thorns — worldly people whose cares and riches choke the word — are the first to burn, but eventually everyone in the system is consumed.

The same self-consuming pattern appears in $babylon's judgment. Ezekiel describes Tyre's destruction as "fire from the midst of thee" (Ezek 28:18) — the fire originates *within* the system. Isaiah's $thorns and briers are burned "in one day" (Isa 10:17), which echoes Revelation's verdict on $babylon: "her plagues come in one day" (Rev 18:8). The smoke of Isa 9:18 ("mount up like the lifting up of smoke") anticipates the kings watching $babylon's smoke rise (Rev 18:9). Wickedness generates the fuel, strikes the match, and immolates itself.

### The Restless Sea

> *"But the **wicked** are like the troubled $[sea], when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. There is no peace, saith my God, to the **wicked**."* — Isa 57:20–21

The $sea represents nations and chaotic peoples — the unsettled, unsanctified mass of humanity (Rev 17:15 makes this explicit: "waters… are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues"). Isaiah's comparison is not casual. The wicked are *like the $sea* — they share its essential quality: **restlessness**.

"When it cannot rest" — this is the nature of lawlessness. The covenant provides structure, order, rest. Rejecting the covenant removes the boundary. What remains is turbulence — ceaseless agitation, casting up "mire and dirt" from its own depths.

The verdict follows: "There is no peace… to the wicked." Not as punishment imposed from outside, but as a description of the condition itself. Lawlessness and peace are structurally incompatible. Peace (*shalom* — wholeness, completeness) requires the ordering framework that the covenant provides. Remove the law, and the result is the $sea: powerful, churning, and unable to find rest.

This connects directly to the creation pattern. In Gen 1:9–10, God separates the waters from the dry land — chaos from order. The $sea is gathered and bounded; land appears. The appearance of dry land *from* the sea is the act of bringing order out of chaos. The wicked, Isaiah says, are those who have returned to the pre-separation state — chaotic, unbounded, restless.

### The Lawless One and the Mystery Already at Work

Paul's language in 2 Thess 2:7–8 suggests that wickedness is not merely individual acts of covenant-breaking but a systemic principle — a "mystery" (G3466 *mysterion*) that operates behind visible events. This mystery of lawlessness has three stages:

1. **Already working** — "the mystery of iniquity doth already work." Lawlessness as a hidden principle, embedded in the present order.
2. **Restrained** — "he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." Something holds the full expression of lawlessness in check.
3. **Revealed** — "then shall that Wicked be revealed." The principle finally manifests in a person, "the lawless one."

The trajectory from hidden principle to visible embodiment mirrors $babylon's arc: a system that begins in concealment (Zech 5:5–11 — wickedness sealed in an ephah, transported to Shinar) and ends in open, catastrophic manifestation (Rev 17–18). In Zechariah's vision, wickedness is personified as a woman placed inside a measuring vessel and carried to Shinar — $babylon's ancient homeland. Wickedness, generated by the system, is returned to its origin for judgment. The cycle completes.

## Patterns

1. **Wickedness is explicitly defined, not inferred.** Deut 17:2 states it outright: covenant transgression. G458 *anomia* states it outright: lawlessness. Scripture does not leave this symbol to guesswork.

2. **Wickedness is the opposite of $truth.** $truth = God's commandment, His standard (Jn 17:17, Ps 119:142). Wickedness = rejection of that standard. They are precise mirror images: one walks *in* the law; the other walks *without* it.

3. **Wickedness is self-consuming.** Isa 9:18–20 — the fire of lawlessness devours the $thorns it produces, then the people become the fuel. The system eats itself. This is the internal logic of every $babylon.

4. **The wicked share the nature of the $sea.** Isa 57:20 — restless, turbulent, casting up what is unclean from within. The comparison suggests that the wicked are not simply *in* the chaotic nations — they *are* the chaotic nations in their essential character.

5. **Wickedness produces no peace.** "No peace to the wicked" (Isa 57:21) is not a curse spoken over them — it is a diagnosis. Lawlessness and *shalom* are structurally incompatible.

6. **Wickedness operates as a hidden principle before it manifests.** 2 Thess 2:7 — the mystery of lawlessness works in concealment before "that Wicked" is revealed. The principle precedes the person.

7. **Wickedness returns to $babylon.** Zech 5:5–11 — wickedness is carried in an ephah to Shinar, its birthplace. The worldly kingdom produces the lawlessness that becomes the instrument of its own judgment.

## Connections

- **$truth** — The exact opposite direction. $truth is God's commandment, His standard (Ps 119:142: "thy law is the truth"). Wickedness is the transgression of that standard. To "do the truth" (Jn 3:21) is covenant obedience; to work wickedness (Deut 17:2) is covenant transgression. Where $truth sets free (Jn 8:32), wickedness produces bondage.

- **$thorns** — The fuel wickedness consumes. $thorns represent worldly, choking people — those characterized by cares, riches, and pleasures (Lk 8:14). Isa 9:18 says wickedness "burneth as the fire" and "shall devour the briers and thorns." Lawlessness produces a worldly population, then ignites them as its own fuel.

- **$sea** — The condition wickedness creates. The $sea represents nations and chaotic peoples — restless, turbulent, unbounded. "The wicked are like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest" (Isa 57:20). Lawlessness returns people to the state of the uncreated deep — chaos without the ordering power of covenant.

- **$fruit** — What wickedness prevents. $fruit represents works and deeds (Gal 5:19–22). Wickedness — lawlessness — is the opposite of the covenant obedience that produces $fruit. The fire of wickedness (Isa 9:18) devours $thorns that already "bring no fruit to perfection" (Lk 8:14). Where the covenant yields $fruit, lawlessness yields ashes.

- **$babylon** — The system wickedness builds. $babylon is man's substitute system for God's covenant rule. Wickedness is its operating principle — the lawlessness that powers the worldly kingdom. Zech 5:5–11 literalizes the connection: wickedness personified is transported to Shinar ($babylon's homeland). The worldly kingdom runs on covenant transgression, and when the transgression matures, the kingdom burns from within (Ezek 28:18).

## For Further Study

- **$truth** — The standard wickedness rejects. If wickedness is covenant transgression, $truth is the covenant itself — God's word, law, and commandment. How does Scripture define the standard that lawlessness opposes?

- **$sea** — The wicked share the sea's nature. If the $sea represents chaotic, unsanctified peoples, what does it mean that creation's resolution is "no more sea" (Rev 21:1)? How does the sea's removal connect to the end of lawlessness?

- **$babylon** — The system wickedness builds. Wickedness is $babylon's operating principle — but $babylon also returns wickedness to itself for judgment. How does the worldly kingdom's self-destruction unfold across Scripture?

- **$thorns** — The fuel wickedness burns. If $thorns represent worldly, choking people, and wickedness devours them as fire, what does this reveal about the relationship between materialism and lawlessness?
