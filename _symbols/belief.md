### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **belief** begins: “Assent to a proposition or affirmation, or the acceptance of a fact, opinion, or assertion as real or true, without immediate personal knowledge; reliance upon word or testimony; partial or full assurance without positive knowledge or absolute certainty; persuasion; conviction; confidence; as, belief of a witness; the belief of our senses.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The book defines belief as receiving God’s testimony as governing truth and submitting to it in obedience. Bare factual assent is inadequate, while persistent disobedience reveals unbelief.

> *“And they were all amazed, insomuch that they questioned among themselves, saying, What thing is this? what new doctrine is this? for with authority commandeth he even the unclean spirits, and they do obey him.”* — Mark 1:27

> *“He that believeth on the Son hath everlasting life: and he that believeth not the Son shall not see life; but the wrath of God abideth on him.”* — John 3:36

> *“Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue:”* — John 12:42

> *“For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God.”* — John 12:43

> *“But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Esaias saith, Lord, who hath believed our report?”* — Romans 10:16

> *“And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not?”* — Hebrews 3:18

> *“So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief.”* — Hebrews 3:19

> *“Thou believest that there is one God; thou doest well: the devils also believe, and tremble.”* — James 2:19

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **personal trust in Christ, not mere assent — the instrument that receives salvation.**

- The entry may blur the distinction between faith itself and the works or obedience that result from and demonstrate faith. Several cited texts show that genuine belief and obedience are inseparable, but they do not necessarily prove that obedience belongs to the definition of belief rather than being its necessary fruit.

- One could argue the book quietly displaces rather than refines the consensus: mainstream teaching treats obedience as the fruit or evidence of belief, whereas the book makes submission constitutive of belief itself ('saving belief obeys the word believed'), which edges toward the faith-and-works readings that strict 'faith alone' traditions reject — so a DIVERGENT classification is arguable. Additionally, Mark 1:27 (unclean spirits obeying Christ's authority) is an oblique support that does not directly concern human saving belief.

- The book may over-weight obedience/works language at the expense of the consensus primary of fiduciary trust, risking a works-oriented displacement of grace-received faith even while citing the same assent-vs-true-faith texts.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
