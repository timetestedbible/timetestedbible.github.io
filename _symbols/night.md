# Night

## Approved Definition

**The absence of [Day](/research/symbols/day/) and [Light](/research/symbols/light/) — visible obedience — marked by sleep or death and by sinful deeds concealed in [darkness](/research/symbols/darkness/). At the largest scale, it is the period from the fall of man until the Kingdom of God begins on earth.**
> *"The **night** is far spent, the $[day] is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of $[darkness], and let us put on the armour of $[light]."* — Rom 13:12

At the largest scale, it is the period from the fall of man until the Kingdom of God begins on earth.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **night** begins: “That part of the natural day when the sun is beneath the horizon, or the time from sunset to sunrise; esp., the time between dusk and dawn, when there is no light of the sun, but only moonlight, starlight, or artificial light.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

God named two things on the first day of creation. He called $light "Day" and He called $darkness "Night" (Gen 1:5). That foundational equation — $darkness *is* night — governs every symbolic use that follows.

But night is not simply a synonym for $darkness. $darkness is a *state* — hidden, concealed disobedience. Night is the *season* when that state prevails. $darkness describes what is happening; night describes *when* it happens. The distinction matters because seasons change. Night is far spent. $day is at hand.

Paul makes the four-term structure explicit:

> *"Ye are all the children of $[light], and the children of the **$[day]**: we are not of the **night**, nor of $[darkness]."* — 1 Thess 5:5

$light and $day on one side. Night and $darkness on the other. $light (visible obedience) is to $day (exposure and accountability) as $darkness (concealed disobedience) is to night (the season when concealment works). Night is the time-frame in which hiddenness operates — the window when thieves move, when $sleep holds people unconscious, when no one sees what is really happening.

And that window is closing.

**Symbolizes:** The season of hiddenness and unawareness — the temporal frame when concealment prevails and spiritual alertness is absent

**Opposite:** $day — exposure, the time of accountability, when everything hidden is brought into the open

**Defining verses:** Gen 1:5, Jn 9:4, 1 Thess 5:5–7, Rom 13:12

**The surprise:** Night is not primarily about evil *activity* but about *unawareness*. The people caught by the thief in the night are not the thieves — they are the ones who didn't see it coming. Night's danger is not that bad things happen in it, but that you cannot see them happening.

**Connected:** $darkness, $day, $light, $sleep

## Night Across Scripture

### The Naming — Darkness Gets a Season

> *"And God called the $[light] Day, and the $[darkness] he called **Night**."* — Gen 1:5

This is the definitional verse. Whatever $darkness *is*, night is its temporal expression. The pipeline research on $darkness converges on "hidden, concealed disobedience" — conduct that avoids scrutiny, deeds done where no one can see. Night, then, is the season when that concealment has free reign. Not forever — the verse continues: "And the evening and the morning were the first day." The cycle turns. Night gives way to morning.

The naming also establishes a quiet pattern: God names both $light and $darkness. He gives them their domains. Night is not outside God's ordering — it is a bounded season within it. It has an end built into its beginning.

### The Window That Closes

> *"I must work the works of him that sent me, while it is $[day]: the **night** cometh, when no man can work."* — Jn 9:4

Jesus speaks this just before healing a man born blind. The immediate reference is to His own ministry — there is a bounded season for the work of visible, public healing and teaching. But the principle extends further: night represents the closing of opportunity. When the season of hiddenness fully descends, the time for productive, visible work has passed.

The next chapter reinforces the point:

> *"If a man walk in the $[day], he stumbleth not, because he seeth the $[light] of this world. But if a man walk in the **night**, he stumbleth, because there is no $[light] in him."* — Jn 11:9–10

Walking in night means walking without $light — without visible example, without awareness. The result is stumbling. Not because night placed obstacles in the path, but because the walker *cannot see* what was already there. Night does not create dangers; it removes the ability to perceive them.

This is the core function of night as a symbol: it is not the source of evil but the *condition of unawareness* in which evil operates undetected.

### The Thief Comes at Night

> *"For yourselves know perfectly that the $[day] of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the **night**."* — 1 Thess 5:2

Why does a thief come at night? Not because night makes the thief evil — the thief was already a thief. Night provides *cover*. The household is asleep. No one is watching. The thief's advantage is not his own strength but the household's unawareness.

Paul immediately draws the application:

> *"But ye, brethren, are not in $[darkness], that that $[day] should overtake you as a thief. Ye are all the children of $[light], and the children of the $[day]: we are not of the **night**, nor of $[darkness]."* — 1 Thess 5:4–5

The thief-in-the-night pattern is not about the timing of Christ's return — it is about the *spiritual condition* of those it catches. Those who belong to night are unaware. Those who belong to $day are not overtaken, not because they know the date, but because they are *awake*. They are watching. They see.

Peter confirms the pattern:

> *"But the $[day] of the Lord will come as a thief in the **night**; in the which the heavens shall pass away with a great noise, and the elements shall melt with fervent heat, the earth also and the works that are therein shall be burned up."* — 2 Pet 3:10

$day arrives — exposure, accountability, judgment. Everything hidden is revealed. The works done under cover of night are burned up in the $light of $day. Night's concealment was always temporary; $day was always coming.

### Sleep and Drunkenness Belong to Night

> *"Therefore let us not $[sleep], as do others; but let us watch and be sober. For they that $[sleep] $[sleep] in the **night**; and they that be drunken are drunken in the **night**."* — 1 Thess 5:6–7

Paul clusters three conditions together as characteristic of night: $sleep, drunkenness, and unawareness. Each is a different facet of the same state:

- **$sleep** — spiritual unconsciousness, death-like unresponsiveness to reality. $sleep in Scripture represents death and spiritual unawareness — the condition of being unresponsive to what God is doing.
- **Drunkenness** — impaired judgment, the inability to perceive clearly even while technically conscious.
- **Unawareness** — the overarching condition. The sleeper does not know the thief is coming. The drunk cannot assess the danger accurately.

All three "belong to" night. They are night's native inhabitants. This is what makes night a *season* rather than merely a backdrop: night is the period when these conditions dominate. To be "of the night" is to be characterized by unconsciousness, impaired perception, and blindness to what is actually happening.

The antidote is not information but *alertness*: "Let us watch and be sober" (1 Thess 5:6). The opposite of night is not knowledge — it is the wakeful, clear-eyed posture of those who belong to $day.

### Night Is Far Spent

> *"The **night** is far spent, the $[day] is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of $[darkness], and let us put on the armour of $[light]."* — Rom 13:12

This verse gathers the full four-term structure into a single exhortation. Night is paired with $darkness (the state of concealed disobedience). $day is paired with $light (visible obedience, example). And the movement is directional: night → $day. $darkness → $light. Cast off → put on.

The phrase "far spent" suggests that the season of hiddenness is not eternal. It is winding down. The exposure is imminent. The practical command follows: stop living as though the cover of night will last, because $day is about to break.

Rom 13:13 continues: *"Let us walk honestly, as in the $[day]; not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying."* The behaviors listed are all things done under cover — excess, secret immorality, hidden hostility. They are night-behaviors. The command is to walk *as in the $day* — as if you are already in full exposure, because you soon will be.

### David's Night — What Is Done in Secret

> *"For thou didst it secretly: but I will do this thing before all $[israel], and before the sun."* — 2 Sam 12:12

Nathan's rebuke of David does not use the word "night," but it enacts the night-to-$day pattern precisely. David's sin with Bathsheba was done in concealment — under cover, in hiddenness. God's response is to bring it into full exposure: before all Israel, before the sun. What was hidden in the season of night will be made visible in the blaze of $day.

This confirms night's symbolic core: not the act of sinning, but the *concealment* under which it operates. David's judgment is not merely for what he did — it is specifically that he did it *secretly*, and God will answer *publicly*.

## Patterns

1. **Night is to $darkness as $day is to $light.** The four-term parallel in 1 Thess 5:5 is the structural key. $darkness and $light are *states* (hidden vs. visible). Night and $day are *seasons* (the time-frame in which those states prevail). Night is the season when hiddenness dominates.

2. **Night is defined by unawareness, not by evil.** The thief-in-the-night pattern does not describe the thief's wickedness — it describes the household's unconsciousness. Night's danger is that those in it *do not see what is coming*.

3. **Night closes opportunity.** "The night cometh, when no man can work" (Jn 9:4). The season of hiddenness is also the season when productive, visible labor becomes impossible. Night is not merely a cover for sin but a condition that shuts down good work.

4. **$sleep and drunkenness are night's native conditions.** They that $sleep, $sleep in the night (1 Thess 5:7). $sleep — spiritual death and unawareness — belongs to the season of night. So does impaired judgment. The cluster is consistent: night = the time when people are unconscious to spiritual reality.

5. **Night is bounded.** "The night is far spent" (Rom 13:12). "The evening and the morning" (Gen 1:5). Night is never presented as permanent. $day is always coming. The season of hiddenness has an expiration date.

6. **What night conceals, $day exposes.** 2 Sam 12:12, 1 Cor 4:5, 2 Pet 3:10. The transition from night to $day is the transition from concealment to accountability. Nothing hidden in night stays hidden once $day breaks.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$darkness** — Night's definitional twin. Gen 1:5 equates them: God called $darkness "Night." $darkness is the *state* of concealed disobedience; night is the *season* when that concealment prevails. Where $darkness describes what is hidden, night describes the temporal window of hiddenness.

- **$day** — Night's defining opposite. $day represents exposure and the time of accountability — the season when everything hidden is brought to $light. The movement from night to $day (Rom 13:12) is the movement from concealment to full visibility. The "Day of the Lord" is the ultimate end of night: every work exposed, every secret burned up.

- **$light** — The state that corresponds to $day as $darkness corresponds to night. $light is visible obedience — example that shines outward. Night is the absence of that visibility. To walk in night is to walk without $light "in him" (Jn 11:10). The armor of $light replaces the works of $darkness when night ends (Rom 13:12).

- **$sleep** — Night's characteristic condition. $sleep represents death and spiritual unawareness — the unconsciousness of those who do not perceive what God is doing. "They that sleep sleep in the night" (1 Thess 5:7). $sleep belongs to night the way wakefulness belongs to $day. The call to "awake out of $sleep" (Rom 13:11) is the call to leave the season of night behind.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The printed passages strongly support the darkness/day contrast and the association with sleep and drunkenness, but they do not by themselves establish the claims about hiding, deeds done secretly, or opportunity closing. Genesis 1:5 merely names literal night, and additional texts would be needed for those extensions.

- One could argue the entry should be REFINED because it elevates 'when opportunity closes' (a secondary John 9:4 motif) into the core definition and silently excludes the positive/neutral uses of night that the consensus acknowledges, effectively presenting a narrower, wholly negative symbol. However, glossary compression that emphasizes the dominant sense without denying alternatives does not constitute a material boundary change, and Response B's own primary meaning already includes the closed-opportunity element.

- The book foregrounds 'opportunity closes' and behavioral particulars (hiding/sleep/drunkenness), which could be read as tilting toward the 'end of opportunity' alternative rather than pure ontological darkness, but this remains inside the consensus primary and its common supporting texts.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

**The absence of [Day](/research/symbols/day/) and [Light](/research/symbols/light/) — visible obedience — marked by sleep or death and by sinful deeds concealed in [darkness](/research/symbols/darkness/).
