# Fig Tree

**The visible indicator of covenant fruitfulness — its leafing signals the season is recognizable, its $fruit reveals covenant standing, and its failure triggers judgment.**

> *"Now learn a parable of the **fig tree**; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh: So likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors."* — Mat 24:32–33

---

## The Key Insight

Every tree bears fruit. But the fig tree is the one Scripture consistently uses as the *test case* — the tree you look at to know where things stand.

When the prophets want to describe covenant blessing fulfilled, they say "every man under his $vine and under his **fig tree**" (Mic 4:4, 1 Ki 4:25, Zech 3:10). When God wants to sort the people by covenant loyalty, He shows Jeremiah two baskets of **figs** — good and bad — and says: *these are the people* (Jer 24:1–10). When Jesus wants to pronounce judgment on the temple system, He curses a fig tree with leaves but no $fruit (Mat 21:19). And when He wants to teach His disciples to read the signs of the times, He points at the fig tree's budding leaves: *you know what this means* (Mat 24:32).

The fig tree is not the covenant itself. It is the **indicator** of covenant health — the visible gauge on the wall that tells you whether what should be happening *is* happening. Leaves signal the season. $fruit confirms the reality. And when a fig tree is all leaves and no fruit, you are looking at a covenant community that has the appearance of life but none of its substance.

**Symbolizes:** The visible indicator of covenant fruitfulness — its condition reveals covenant standing, its leafing marks the season, its barrenness demands judgment

**Opposite:** The $vine shares the fig tree's covenant-peace pairing, but each tests a different dimension: the barren vine produces no $wine (covenant blood); the barren fig produces no $fruit (covenant works). Together they constitute the full diagnostic.

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:32–33, Mat 21:19, Jer 24:1–10, Hos 9:10, Mic 4:4, Lk 13:6–9

**The surprise:** The first fig appearance in Scripture is not fruit — it is **leaves**. Adam and Eve sew fig leaves to cover their nakedness (Gen 3:7). The very first use establishes the pattern that runs through the entire Bible: fig leaves *without* fig fruit = human effort to cover guilt without covenant substance. When Jesus finds leaves but no fruit on the fig tree outside Jerusalem (Mk 11:13), He is reenacting Genesis 3.

**Connected:** $vine, $fruit, $harvest, $peace-shalom, $shadow, $wind, $sun-moon-stars, $curse, $faith, $mountain

## Fig Tree Across Scripture

### Leaves Without Fruit — The Pattern That Begins in Eden

> *"And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed **fig** leaves together, and made themselves aprons."* — Gen 3:7

The fig tree's first appearance in Scripture involves no fruit at all — only leaves. Adam and Eve reach for fig leaves to cover what has been exposed. The covering fails. God replaces it with animal skins (Gen 3:21), which require blood — a death, a sacrifice.

This is not incidental. It establishes the fig tree's symbolic logic at the very beginning: **fig leaves = visible religious covering that lacks the substance to actually cover.** The appearance of the thing, without the reality of the thing.

This pattern resurfaces with devastating force when Jesus approaches a fig tree outside Jerusalem:

> *"And seeing a **fig tree** afar off having leaves, he came, if haply he might find any thing thereon: and when he came to it, he found nothing but leaves; for the time of **figs** was not yet."* — Mk 11:13

> *"And Jesus answered and said unto it, No man eat **fruit** of thee hereafter for ever. And his disciples heard it."* — Mk 11:14

Mark adds the strange detail: "the time of figs was not yet" (G2540 *kairos*). Jesus expects $fruit *out of season* from this particular tree. Why? Because this tree, covered in leaves — broadcasting that it is alive and productive — stands just outside the temple. The temple had leaves: rituals, sacrifices, religious activity. But when the owner comes looking for $fruit — for the covenant works that all this religious apparatus should be producing — He finds nothing.

The next thing Jesus does is enter the temple and overturn the money-changers' tables (Mk 11:15–17). The cursed fig tree *is* the temple system: leaves without $fruit, covering without substance, appearance without covenant faithfulness. The next morning, the disciples find the tree "dried up from the roots" (Mk 11:20). Not merely damaged — dead from the foundation.

### Good Figs and Bad Figs — People Sorted by Covenant Response

> *"The LORD shewed me, and, behold, two baskets of **figs** were set before the temple of the LORD... One basket had very good **figs**, even like the **figs** that are first ripe: and the other basket had very naughty **figs**, which could not be eaten, they were so bad."* — Jer 24:1–2

God Himself interprets the vision:

> *"Like these good **figs**, so will I acknowledge them that are carried away captive of Judah... For I will set mine eyes upon them for good, and I will bring them again to this land... And I will give them an heart to know me."* — Jer 24:5–7

> *"And as the evil **figs**, which cannot be eaten, they are so evil... So will I give Zedekiah the king of Judah, and his princes, and the residue of Jerusalem... to be a reproach and a proverb, a taunt and a **$[curse]**."* — Jer 24:8–9

The figs *are* the people. Not metaphorically — this is God's own equation. And the sorting principle is startling: the *good* figs are the ones who accepted judgment (exile to Babylon). The *bad* figs are the ones who refused it — who stayed in the land or fled to Egypt, clinging to the appearance of covenant standing while rejecting God's actual word.

Good $fruit = accepting God's discipline. Bad $fruit = resisting it. The fig tree reveals which kind you are.

### The Firstripe Fig — What Israel Was Meant to Be

> *"I found $[israel] like grapes in the wilderness; I saw your fathers as the firstripe in the **fig tree** at her first time: but they went to Baalpeor, and separated themselves unto that shame."* — Hos 9:10

The firstripe fig (H1063 *bikkurah*) is the first fruit of the season — eagerly anticipated, precious, sweet. God saw Israel's earliest generation as *that* — the first ripe $fruit on the tree, the thing the gardener has been watching for.

But "they went to Baalpeor." The precious first $fruit turned immediately to idolatry. The fig tree promised everything and delivered corruption. This is the same pattern: initial promise of covenant fruitfulness, followed by failure to produce what was promised.

Nahum uses the same image for Nineveh's fortifications: "Thy strong holds shall be like **fig trees** with the firstripe figs: if they be shaken, they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater" (Nah 3:12). What looks impressive collapses at the first pressure — the outward appearance of strength without internal substance.

### Under the Vine and Under the Fig Tree — Covenant Wholeness

> *"But they shall sit every man under his $[vine] and under his **fig tree**; and none shall make them afraid: for the mouth of the LORD of hosts hath spoken it."* — Mic 4:4

This is the covenant peace formula — the picture of $peace-shalom fulfilled. It appears at the height of Solomon's reign:

> *"And Judah and $[israel] dwelt safely, every man under his $[vine] and under his **fig tree**, from Dan even to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon."* — 1 Ki 4:25

And again in the Assyrian taunts (2 Ki 18:31, Isa 36:16), and in Zechariah's eschatological vision:

> *"In that day, saith the LORD of hosts, shall ye call every man his neighbour under the $[vine] and under the **fig tree**."* — Zech 3:10

The $vine and the fig tree together constitute the full covenant indicator. The $vine produces $wine (covenant blood); the fig tree produces $fruit (covenant works). To sit *under* them is to dwell in their $shadow — the protection and provision of a functioning covenant community. "None shall make them afraid" — this is the opposite of the $curse, the fullness of covenant-wholeness ($peace-shalom) where nothing is lacking, nothing is broken.

When this formula is operating, the covenant is healthy. When the fig tree fails — when there are no figs on the fig tree (Jer 8:13), when the fig tree does not blossom (Hab 3:17) — the covenant community has collapsed.

### The Fig Tree and the Shadow

The vine-and-fig formula says "under his vine and under his fig tree" — and the word "under" is not incidental. Every tree casts a $shadow. To sit *under* the fig tree is to position yourself in its shade — in its covenant covering.

This is why Nathanael's location matters:

> *"Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and saith of him, Behold an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile! Nathanael saith unto him, Whence knowest thou me? Jesus answered and said unto him, Before that Philip called thee, when thou wast under the **fig tree**, I saw thee."* — Jn 1:47–48

Nathanael was "under the fig tree" — positioned in the covenant community's $shadow, likely studying Torah, dwelling honestly in the covenant's covering. Jesus identifies him as a "true Israelite" — the kind of person the fig tree is supposed to produce. And Jesus saw him *before Philip called him* — the owner of the vineyard knows the $fruit before anyone else does.

Nathanael's response is immediate and total: "Rabbi, thou art the Son of God; thou art the King of Israel" (Jn 1:49). The one honestly dwelling in the fig tree's $shadow recognizes the one whose body *casts* that shadow (Col 2:17).

But not every $shadow is legitimate. The bramble of Jotham's parable offers shade to the trees (Judg 9:15) but produces $fire instead of shelter — a false $shadow, a destructive counterfeit. When the cursed fig tree withers from its roots (Mk 11:20), the $shadow vanishes with it. The temple system's covering — the appearance of covenant protection — dries up because it was leaves without $fruit, a shade without substance. Like the $shadow of Egypt that Judah trusted instead of the LORD's own shade (Isa 30:2–3), it was a false covering that could not protect.

### The Barren Fig Tree — Three Years of Patience

> *"A certain man had a **fig tree** planted in his vineyard; and he came and sought **fruit** thereon, and found none. Then said he unto the dresser of his vineyard, Behold, these three years I come seeking **fruit** on this **fig tree**, and find none: cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground?"* — Lk 13:6–7

> *"And he answering said unto him, Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it: And if it bear **fruit**, well: and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down."* — Lk 13:8–9

Three years of fruitlessness. The owner's patience has run its course. The dresser asks for *one more year* of intensive care — digging, dunging, giving every possible advantage. If $fruit comes, the tree lives. If not, judgment falls.

The parable maps directly onto Jesus' three-year ministry to Israel. One more year of grace — but grace with a deadline. The fig tree is not condemned for producing *bad* fruit but for producing *no* fruit at all. It "cumbers the ground" — it occupies space in the vineyard (covenant community) without contributing anything. A covenant indicator that indicates nothing is worse than useless; it actively misleads.

### Figs Cast by Wind — Authorities Shaken Loose

> *"And the stars of heaven fell unto the earth, even as a **fig tree** casteth her untimely **figs**, when she is shaken of a mighty $[wind]."* — Rev 6:13

> *"And all the host of heaven shall be dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled together as a scroll: and all their host shall fall down, as the leaf falleth off from the vine, and as a falling **fig** from the **fig tree**."* — Isa 34:4

The word "untimely figs" in Revelation is G3653 *olynthos* — a late, winter fig that never ripened. It clings to the branch through the cold season but never matures. When the great $wind comes, it is shaken loose and falls.

Here the $sun-moon-stars — the governing authority hierarchy — and the fig tree merge into a single image. The $stars represent subordinate authorities; the unripe figs represent covenant participants who never produced $fruit. They fall together because they are the same thing: people in positions of covenant authority who never bore what they were supposed to bear, shaken loose by the $wind of divine judgment (invisible spiritual power executing God's purposes).

The $wind that shakes the fig tree is the same $wind that stirs the $sea in Dan 7:2 and is held back by four angels in Rev 7:1 — the invisible power of God's executing forces. When that power is released, everything unripe is torn away.

### When the Fig Tree Does Not Blossom

> *"Although the **fig tree** shall not blossom, neither shall **fruit** be in the $[vine]s; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation."* — Hab 3:17–18

Habakkuk describes total covenant indicator failure: no figs, no grapes, no olives, no grain, no livestock. Every visible sign of covenant health has disappeared. And yet — "I will rejoice in the LORD."

This is the ultimate test. When the fig tree gives no reading — when the visible indicator goes dark — does $faith persist? Habakkuk's answer is the counterpart to the cursed fig tree: the covenant partner who trusts even when there is nothing to see. The fig tree measures covenant fruitfulness; Habakkuk measures covenant $faith when fruitfulness is absent.

### The Season Indicator — Learn This Parable

> *"Now learn a parable of the **fig tree**; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh."* — Mat 24:32

> *"And he spake to them a parable; Behold the **fig tree**, and all the trees; When they now shoot forth, ye see and know of your own selves that summer is now nigh at hand."* — Lk 21:29–30

Luke's addition — "and all the trees" — clarifies that the fig tree's function here is not as a coded symbol for one nation. It is doing what it always does: indicating the season. The fig tree's budding leaves tell you that summer is near, just as the signs Jesus has described tell you that His coming is near.

This is the fig tree's most fundamental role: **it makes the season recognizable.** Compare Jer 8:7, where the turtledove knows her appointed times but God's people do not. The fig tree and the turtledove serve the same diagnostic function — they mark the $harvest calendar. The fig tree's spring budding (Song 2:13: "The **fig tree** putteth forth her green figs") signals that the season has turned. In the Song, this coincides with the bridegroom's call: "Arise, my love, my fair one, and come away."

The fig tree announces. If you cannot read the fig tree, you cannot read the times.

## The Cursed Fig Tree and the Mountain Cast Into the Sea

The connection between the cursed fig tree and the teaching on $faith deserves closer attention:

> *"And Jesus answered and said unto them, Verily I say unto you, If ye have $[faith], and doubt not, ye shall not only do what is done to the **fig tree**, but also if ye shall say unto this $[mountain], Be thou removed, and be thou cast into the sea; it shall be done."* — Mat 21:21

The $mountain is a kingdom — a ruling authority (Dan 2:35, Rev 17:9). "This mountain" in context is the temple mount, the seat of Jerusalem's religious authority. The cursed fig tree *is* the temple; the $mountain *is* the temple. Both images describe the same reality: a covenant institution that has failed to produce $fruit will be removed by the $faith of those who speak God's word.

The fig tree withers. The $mountain is cast into the sea (the nations). The religious authority that produced leaves but no $fruit is uprooted and thrown into the chaos of the Gentile world. This is not a general lesson about prayer power — it is a specific pronouncement of covenant judgment executed through trusting obedience ($faith).

## Patterns

1. **Leaves without fruit = appearance without substance.** Gen 3:7 (fig leaves cover nakedness), Mat 21:19 / Mk 11:13 (leaves but no fruit → $curse). The consistent pattern: fig leaves represent the *visible display* of covenant participation without the *actual works* it requires.

2. **Figs are people.** Jer 24:1–10 makes this explicit: good figs = the faithful exiles, bad figs = the resistant remnant. The fig does not merely *represent* covenant standing abstractly — the figs *are* the individuals sorted by their response to God's word.

3. **Fig + vine = complete covenant diagnostic.** Mic 4:4, 1 Ki 4:25, 2 Ki 18:31, Zech 3:10. The $vine produces $wine (covenant blood), the fig produces $fruit (covenant works). Together under their $shadow = $peace-shalom, covenant-wholeness where nothing is lacking.

4. **Fig tree as season indicator.** Mat 24:32, Song 2:13, Jer 8:13. The fig tree's condition tells you *where you are* in God's calendar. Budding = summer near. Barren = judgment pending. Fruitful = covenant fulfilled.

5. **Barrenness before judgment.** Lk 13:6–9 (three years, no fruit → one more chance), Mat 21:19 (no fruit → withered), Jer 8:13 (no figs on fig tree), Joel 1:7 (fig tree barked). Fruitlessness is never merely unfortunate — it is a prelude to the $curse.

6. **Firstripe figs fall easily.** Hos 9:10 (Israel's first generation went to Baalpeor), Nah 3:12 (strongholds collapse at first shaking), Rev 6:13 (unripe figs shaken by $wind). What appears ripe but never matured is the first to fall.

7. **Under the fig tree = positioned in covenant shadow.** Jn 1:48 (Nathanael seen and known), Mic 4:4 (none shall make afraid), Zech 3:10 (eschatological calling). The fig tree's $shadow is legitimate only when the tree is producing $fruit.

## Connections

- **$vine** — The fig tree's constant companion in the covenant peace formula. The $vine produces $wine (covenant blood); the fig tree produces $fruit (covenant works). Their joint failure marks total covenant collapse (Jer 5:17, Jer 8:13, Joel 1:7,12, Hab 3:17). Their joint flourishing marks covenant-wholeness (Mic 4:4, 1 Ki 4:25, Zech 3:10).

- **$fruit** — $fruit = works/deeds. The fig tree's entire diagnostic function depends on whether it produces $fruit. Leaves without $fruit = religious appearance without covenant works (Gen 3:7, Mat 21:19). Good $fruit = obedient response to God (Jer 24:5). No $fruit = ground for the $curse (Lk 13:6–9).

- **$harvest** — The $harvest is the appointed separation when accumulated works reach fullness. The fig tree signals when $harvest is near (Mat 24:32). Unripe figs falling in Rev 6:13 is a $harvest image — the separation of the unfruitful from their positions when the appointed time comes. Joel 3:13's sickle-and-harvest sequence follows Joel 1:7,12's devastated fig trees.

- **$peace-shalom** — Covenant-wholeness: nothing lacking, nothing broken. "Under his vine and under his fig tree, none shall make afraid" (Mic 4:4) = the visible condition of $peace-shalom. When the fig tree fails, $peace-shalom is absent. The Assyrian counterfeit — promising vine and fig (2 Ki 18:31) — is false $peace-shalom offered by the wrong master.

- **$shadow** — The fig tree casts a $shadow: covenant protection for those who dwell under it. But $shadow is only legitimate when the body casting it is real. A barren fig tree's $shadow is false covering — leaves without substance, like the bramble's shade (Judg 9:15) or Egypt's $shadow (Isa 30:2–3). When the cursed fig tree withers (Mk 11:20), the $shadow vanishes.

- **$wind** — Invisible spiritual power that shakes the unfruitful loose. Rev 6:13: figs cast by a mighty $wind. The same $wind gathers or scatters at God's command (Jer 49:36, Mat 24:31). When God sends His executing $wind, everything unripe is torn from the branch.

- **$sun-moon-stars** — The governing authority hierarchy. In Rev 6:12–13, the darkening of $sun-moon-stars and the falling of figs occur in the same judgment sequence. Stars falling like figs (Isa 34:4, Rev 6:13) merges two symbolic registers: authorities who never matured in covenant fruitfulness are shaken from their positions.

- **$curse** — The covenant penalty clause. The cursed fig tree (Mat 21:19) is the $curse activated: the covenant indicator that showed leaves but no $fruit triggers the self-executing consequence of covenant violation. Bad figs become "a $curse" among the nations (Jer 24:9, 29:18). The fig tree's barrenness is what makes the $curse visible.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience. The cursed fig tree leads directly to the teaching on $faith (Mat 21:21): "if ye have faith... say unto this mountain, be removed." $faith is what speaks judgment on the fruitless system and removes the barren authority. Hab 3:17–18 tests $faith in the other direction: when the fig tree gives no sign, $faith persists.

- **$mountain** — Kingdom / ruling authority. "This mountain" (Mat 21:21) in context = the temple mount, the religious authority that the cursed fig tree represents. The fruitless institution is both the withered fig tree and the $mountain cast into the sea — the same judgment described in two images.
