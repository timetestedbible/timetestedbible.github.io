# The End

**Covenant fulfillment — the goal toward which God's covenant process drives, arriving at its appointed consummation.**

> *"And this gospel of the $[kingdom] shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall **the end** come."* — Mat 24:14

---

## The Key Insight

English collapses everything into one word: "end." But Scripture uses at least seven distinct words for different kinds of ending — and the one Jesus uses in His most famous discourse about the future does not mean "termination."

G5056 *telos* means the **goal aimed at**. The finish line. The purpose fulfilled. Paul uses it plainly:

> *"For Christ is **the end** (G5056 *telos*) of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth."* — Rom 10:4

Christ is not the abolition of the Torah. He is its *purpose arriving*. The Torah was aiming at something — and that something showed up.

> *"Now **the end** (G5056 *telos*) of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned."* — 1Ti 1:5

The *telos* of the commandment is love. Not because the commandment stops — but because love is what the commandment was *for*.

When the disciples ask Jesus on the Mount of Olives, "What shall be the sign of thy coming, and of **the end** of the world?" (Mat 24:3), they use a different word: G4930 *synteleia* — the joint-completion, the consummation of the age-process. Jesus answers in Mat 24:6 with *telos*: "the **end** is not yet." He is not correcting their vocabulary. He is answering their question with precision: *the purpose has not yet arrived*. Wars and rumors of wars are not the goal. $famine and $earthquake are not the destination. They are $birth-pains — the labor, not the child.

The *telos* comes in Mat 24:14 — when the gospel of the $kingdom has been preached as a witness to all nations. The testimony completed. Then the purpose arrives.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant fulfillment — the aimed-at goal of God's covenant process reaching its consummation

**Opposite:** BEGINNING (H7225 *reshith* / G746 *arche*) — the origin point from which the purpose departs. The beginning initiates; the end consummates.

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:6, Mat 24:13–14, Rom 10:4, 1Co 10:11, Dan 12:13, Rev 21:6

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word for "end" (H7093 *qets*) comes from H7112 *qatsats* — to **cut off**. The same cutting action as covenant-cutting (H3772 *karath*). The "end" in Hebrew is not a fade-out. It is a **cut** — the decisive boundary where covenant terms are executed. Meanwhile the Greek *telos* shares its root with *teleios* (perfect, mature) — suggesting that the end is not death but *full growth*.

**Connected:** $babylon, $kingdom, $famine, $pestilence, $earthquake, $birth-pains, $faith, $the-way, $wickedness, $deceive

## The End Across Scripture

### Seven Words for "End" — and Why It Matters

Scripture does not have a single concept called "the end." It has at least seven, each encoding a different relationship to the thing that concludes:

| Word | Meaning | Emphasis |
|------|---------|----------|
| G5056 *telos* | Goal, purpose, completion | The aimed-at thing **arrives** |
| G4930 *synteleia* | Joint-completion, consummation | A process **matures together** |
| G2078 *eschatos* | Last, uttermost, extreme | Temporal **position** (final in a sequence) |
| G4009 *peras* | Extremity, boundary | Geographic or conceptual **limit** |
| H7093 *qets* | Cut, boundary, termination | The **cutting point** — covenant enacted |
| H319 *achariyth* | Latter end, aftermath, posterity | What comes **after** — the end has a future |
| H8503 *takliyth* | Completeness, perfection | The **completion-point** of a thing |

When Jesus says "the *telos* is not yet" (Mat 24:6), He means "the purpose hasn't arrived yet" — not "the tribulation hasn't started yet." When Daniel is told he will stand in his lot "at the *qets*" (Dan 12:13), the emphasis is on the *cutting point* — the covenant boundary where destinies are sealed. When Jeremiah promises "to give you an *achariyth* and a hope" (Jer 29:11), the word itself insists that the ending *has a future*.

Collapsing these into a single "the end = tribulation/destruction" loses each word's testimony. The consistent thread is not cessation but **arrival** — covenant terms reaching their appointed execution point.

### "The End Is Not Yet" — The Olivet Discourse

The Olivet Discourse (Mat 24, Mk 13, Lk 21) is where most readers encounter "the end." But Jesus spends more time saying what the end is *not* than what it is:

> *"And ye shall hear of $[wars and rumors of wars]: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but **the end** is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

> *"For $[nation] shall rise against $[nation], and $[kingdom] against $[kingdom]: and there shall be $[famine]s, and $[pestilence]s, and $[earthquake]s, in divers places. All these are the beginning of $[birth pains]."* — Mat 24:7–8

Note the structure. Jesus lists $nation-against-nation — covenant peoples fracturing under divine discipline. $famine — the Word of God being withdrawn (Amos 8:11). $pestilence — covenant death-consequence for refusing that Word. $earthquake — God shaking every established order to expose what is unfaithful (Heb 12:27). Then He says: these are the *beginning* of $birth-pains.

Birth pains are not the baby. They are the labor that precedes deliverance. To mistake the contractions for the child is to mistake the process for the goal. The *telos* is not the pain — it is what the pain produces.

What stands between the $birth-pains and the *telos*?

> *"And then shall many be offended, and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another. And many false prophets shall rise, and shall $[deceive] many. And because $[wickedness] shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold."* — Mat 24:10–12

$deceive — leading covenant people away from $the-way by misrepresenting God's commands. $wickedness — lawlessness, covenant transgression (the Greek *anomia* in Mat 24:12 is literally "without Torah"). Love grows cold — and *telos* of the commandment *is* love (1Ti 1:5). The goal recedes when its substance — trusting obedience expressed as love — is abandoned.

Then comes the pivot:

> *"But he that shall endure unto **the end** (G5056 *telos*), the same shall be saved. And this gospel of the $[kingdom] shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall **the end** (G5056 *telos*) come."* — Mat 24:13–14

Two conditions. First: $faith that endures — trusting obedience that persists through the entire labor. Second: the gospel of the $kingdom preached as a *witness* (G3142 *marturion*) to all nations. The testimony must be completed. Then — and only then — the *telos* comes. The purpose arrives.

### The Finished Testimony Triggers the Goal

A pattern emerges across the New Testament linking *teleo* (G5055, to finish/complete) and *telos* (G5056, goal/end). They share the same root, and they appear in the same sequence: **testimony finished → telos arrives**.

**Mat 24:14** — The gospel preached for a *marturion* → "then shall the *telos* come."

**Rev 11:7** — "When they shall have *finished* (G5055 *telesōsin*) their *marturian* (testimony)" → the beast kills the witnesses → the seventh trumpet sounds.

**Rev 10:7** — "The mystery of God should be *finished* (G5055 *telesthē*), as he hath declared to his servants the prophets."

**1Co 15:24** — "Then cometh the *telos*, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God."

The pattern is consistent: the completion (*teleo*) of the witness triggers the arrival of the goal (*telos*). The seventh trumpet in Revelation is the marker:

> *"The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever."* — Rev 11:15

This is the same event as 1Co 15:24 — the kingdom delivered. And the same as Dan 7:14 — "there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom." The *telos* is the $kingdom fully transferred — $babylon's claim to sovereignty ended, God's covenant rule established without rival.

The testimony is not merely information about the kingdom. It *is* the covenant process reaching completion. When the witness is finished, the purpose has arrived.

### The Cut — Hebrew *Qets* and Covenant Execution

The Hebrew H7093 *qets* comes from H7112 *qatsats* — to cut off. This is not incidental. The "end" in Hebrew carries the imagery of a blade reaching a boundary line. And the primary act of covenant-making in the Old Testament is *cutting*: H3772 *karath* — "to cut a covenant."

The *qets* is the point where covenant terms are executed — for blessing or for curse. Daniel uses it repeatedly:

> *"And he said, Behold, I will make thee know what shall be in the last end (H7093 *qets*) of the indignation: for at the time appointed **the end** (H7093 *qets*) shall be."* — Dan 8:19

> *"And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off (H3772 *karath*)... and **the end** (H7093 *qets*) thereof shall be with a flood, and unto **the end** (H7093 *qets*) of the war desolations are determined."* — Dan 9:26

Notice Dan 9:26: Messiah is *cut off* (karath — the covenant-cutting verb), and *the end* (qets — from the cutting root) follows. The cutting of Messiah and the cutting-point of the age are linked by the same semantic field. The covenant is cut in the death of Messiah, and the consequences of that cutting unfold until the *qets* — the boundary where all covenant terms are fully executed.

> *"But go thou thy way till **the end** (H7093 *qets*) be: for thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at **the end** (H7093 *qets*) of the days."* — Dan 12:13

Daniel is told to go, rest, and *stand in his lot* at the *qets*. The word "lot" (H1486 *goral*) is the covenant inheritance allotment. At the cutting-point, Daniel receives his portion. The *qets* is not mere termination — it is the moment covenant allotments are distributed.

The LXX translates *qets* as *synteleia* (joint-completion) in Dan 9:26 — and this is the exact word the disciples use in Mat 24:3 when they ask about "the *synteleia* of the age." They are thinking in Daniel's categories. Jesus answers with *telos* — the goal. The Hebrew emphasizes the cutting boundary; the Greek emphasizes the purpose arriving. Both describe the same appointed moment from different angles.

### Christ Is the *Telos* of the Torah

> *"For Christ is **the end** (G5056 *telos*) of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth."* — Rom 10:4

This verse has been read as "Christ terminated the law." But *telos* does not mean termination. It means goal. Christ is what the Torah was aiming at. Every sacrifice pointed to His sacrifice. Every feast (H4150 *mo'ed*) pointed to His appointed fulfillment. Every commandment's purpose — love (1Ti 1:5) — found its embodiment in Him.

This reframes what "the end" means across all of Scripture. If Christ is the *telos* of the Torah, and the *telos* of the age is the $kingdom fully established under Christ, then *the end* is not a cosmic shutdown. It is the arrival of everything the covenant was always driving toward.

> *"I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and **the end** (G5056 *telos*)."* — Rev 21:6

The same person is both the *arche* (beginning, origin) and the *telos* (end, goal). The origin and the destination are the same. He who initiated the covenant purpose is Himself the fulfillment of it.

### "The Ends of the Ages Have Reached Us"

> *"Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom **the ends** (G5056 *telos*, plural) of the world are come."* — 1Co 10:11

Paul says the Exodus story was written for those upon whom "the ends of the ages" have arrived. The plural is striking — not one end but multiple covenant-age purposes converging. The Exodus, the wilderness, the entry into the land — all those patterns find their *telos* in the present covenant community. The types have reached their goal.

> *"But **the end** (G5056 *telos*) of all things is at hand: be ye therefore sober, and watch unto prayer."* — 1Pe 4:7

Peter says the *telos* of all things is at hand — and his response is not panic but sobriety, prayer, and love (1Pe 4:8). If the end were mere catastrophe, sobriety would be strange counsel. But if the end is the covenant purpose arriving, then the appropriate response is to be alert, faithful, and walking in the love that *is* the commandment's goal.

### The End of the Wicked — Covenant Terms Executed

In wisdom and prophetic contexts, "the end" of the wicked is not random punishment but the *outcome* of their chosen path:

> *"There is a $[way] which seemeth right unto a man, but **the end** (H319 *achariyth*) thereof are the ways of death."* — Prov 14:12

$the-way — God's commandments, the Torah-path. A way that *seems* right but leads to death is a counterfeit $way — the path of $wickedness that mimics righteousness. The *achariyth* (what comes after, the aftermath) of that way is death. Note that *achariyth* implies a *future beyond the end* — even death is not the final word, but an outcome with consequences.

> *"When I thought to know this, it was too painful for me; Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their **end** (H319 *achariyth*)."* — Ps 73:17

The psalmist cannot understand the prosperity of the wicked until he enters the sanctuary — the covenant meeting place — and sees their *achariyth*. The sanctuary provides the covenant lens through which outcomes become visible. The end is understood only from within the covenant.

> *"But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose **end** is to be burned."* — Heb 6:8

The land that receives rain (covenant blessing) but produces $thorns (covenant cursing) has an *end* — burning. This is not arbitrary. It is covenant agriculture: land given every resource that produces the wrong crop reaches its appointed outcome. $babylon produces $thorns and then burns with its own fuel (Isa 9:18).

### The End of Babylon — Covenant Purpose Against the Counterfeit

> *"O thou that dwellest upon many waters, abundant in treasures, thine **end** (H7093 *qets*) is come, and the measure of thy covetousness."* — Jer 51:13

$babylon — man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — has a *qets*. The cutting-point arrives. The "measure" (H1267 *bitstsah*) of her covetousness is full. The covenant terms against the counterfeit kingdom are executed. $babylon's end is not random destruction but the precise fulfillment of covenant judgment.

> *"And **the end** (H7093 *qets*) is come upon my people of Israel; I will not again pass by them any more."* — Amos 8:2

Here the *qets* falls on $israel. God shows Amos a basket of summer fruit (*qayits* — a wordplay on *qets*) and declares the end has come upon His own people. What follows is a $famine — "not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD" (Amos 8:11). The covenant Word is withdrawn. The *qets* upon Israel is the same pattern as upon $babylon: covenant terms executed. The difference is that Israel's *achariyth* includes restoration (Amos 9:11–15). $babylon's does not.

## Patterns

1. ***Telos* is purpose, not cessation.** Every New Testament use of G5056 *telos* for "the end" carries the sense of goal-arrival. Christ is the *telos* of Torah (Rom 10:4). Love is the *telos* of the commandment (1Ti 1:5). The gospel preached → then the *telos* comes (Mat 24:14). Substituting "purpose" for "end" clarifies every passage.

2. **The testimony triggers the end.** Mat 24:14, Rev 10:7, Rev 11:7, 1Co 15:24 — the pattern is consistent: the witness is completed (*teleo*) → the goal (*telos*) arrives → the $kingdom is transferred. The end is not the interruption of the witness but its *completion*.

3. **Hebrew "end" = covenant cut.** H7093 *qets* from H7112 *qatsats* (to cut). The *qets* is the boundary where covenant terms — blessings and curses — are executed. It shares the semantic domain of H3772 *karath* (to cut a covenant).

4. **Birth pains are not the end.** Jesus explicitly places $nation-against-nation, $famine, $pestilence, and $earthquake before the *telos* and labels them the *beginning* of $birth-pains (Mat 24:8). The labor is the process; the *telos* is the delivery.

5. **$faith is the bridge.** "He that shall endure unto the *telos*, the same shall be saved" (Mat 24:13). $faith — trusting obedience that persists — is what carries the covenant partner from the $birth-pains to the goal. $wickedness (lawlessness) causes love to grow cold, threatening the very thing the *telos* is made of.

6. **$deceive is the chief threat.** Jesus begins the Olivet Discourse with "Take heed that no man $[deceive] you" (Mat 24:4) — before wars, before famine, before anything else. The primary danger is being led away from $the-way before the *telos* arrives. $deceive attempts to redirect covenant allegiance so that the faithful abandon the race before the finish line.

7. **Every ending has an aftermath.** H319 *achariyth* (latter end, posterity) insists that the end is not a wall but a door. Jer 29:11 promises an *achariyth* and a hope. Dan 12:13 promises Daniel will stand in his lot at the *qets*. Even the end of the wicked (Ps 73:17) is an *outcome* — a destination, not a void.

8. **Beginning and End are the same Person.** Rev 21:6 — "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end." The *arche* (origin, first principle) and the *telos* (goal, purpose) converge in Christ. The covenant originates in Him and consummates in Him.

## Connections

- **$babylon** — Man's substitute kingdom has an appointed *qets* — a cutting-point where covenant judgment arrives (Jer 51:13). $babylon's end is the execution of covenant terms against the counterfeit system, and its destruction clears the way for the *telos*: the $kingdom transferred to its rightful ruler (Rev 11:15, Dan 7:14).

- **$birth-pains** — The labor that precedes the *telos*, not the *telos* itself. Covenant judgment producing new covenant order. Mat 24:8 places $nation-against-nation, $famine, $pestilence, and $earthquake as the *beginning* of birth pains — the painful transition, not the goal. Confusing the contractions with the child is the interpretive error Jesus explicitly corrects.

- **$famine** — The withdrawal of God's Word. Amos 8:2 declares the *qets* upon Israel; Amos 8:11 defines that *qets* as a famine "of hearing the words of the LORD." The end and the famine are linked: covenant purpose demands the Word, and when the Word is withdrawn, the covenant reaches its cutting-point.

- **$pestilence** — Covenant death-consequence for refusing the Word. Part of the judgment triad (sword, $famine, $pestilence) that characterizes the period *before* the *telos*. The Hebrew *deber* (pestilence) shares its consonants with *dabar* (word) — the lethal consequence mirrors the rejected communication.

- **$earthquake** — God shaking every established order to remove what is unfaithful (Heb 12:26–27). Listed among the $birth-pains in Mat 24:7. What cannot be shaken *remains* — the unshakeable $kingdom is what the *telos* reveals when everything else is removed.

- **$nation-against-nation** — Covenant peoples fracturing under divine discipline. A judgment-sign, not the end itself (Mat 24:6–7). Echoes 2Chr 15:6 where God Himself sets nation against nation — internal dissolution as covenant curse. The unity that the *telos* restores is precisely what the fracturing destroys.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience that endures to the *telos*. "He that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Mat 24:13). $faith is the means by which the covenant partner participates in the goal's arrival, persisting through the $birth-pains without being turned aside by $deceive.

- **$the-way** — God's commandments, the Torah-path whose *telos* is love (1Ti 1:5) and whose *telos* is Christ (Rom 10:4). The counterfeit way "seemeth right" but its *achariyth* is death (Prov 14:12). The end reveals which path was the true $way.

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness / covenant transgression. In Mat 24:12, $wickedness (*anomia* — literally "without Torah") abounds and love grows cold. Since the *telos* of the commandment is love, $wickedness directly opposes the arrival of the goal by destroying love — the very substance the *telos* is made of.

- **$deceive** — The chief threat to reaching the *telos*. Jesus opens the Olivet Discourse with the warning against deception (Mat 24:4–5) before any other sign. $deceive leads covenant people off $the-way so they abandon the race before the purpose arrives. False prophets (Mat 24:11, 24) intensify as the *telos* approaches.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience that endures to the *telos*. If $wickedness (lawlessness) causes love to grow cold, and the *telos* of the commandment *is* love, what does endurance actually look like?
- **$kingdom** — The thing transferred at the *telos*. "Then cometh the end, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God" (1Co 15:24). What is the kingdom, and what does its transfer accomplish?
