# Highway

**The covenant path — raised above common ground, cleared of obstacles, walked only by the faithful.**

> *"And an **highway** shall be there, and a $[way], and it shall be called The $[way] of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it; but it shall be for those: the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein."* — Isa 35:8

---

## The Key Insight

There are two Hebrew words that English readers collapse into one concept. H4546 *mesillah* is the **highway** — a raised, built-up road. H1870 *derek* is the **$way** — conduct, manner of life. One is *where* you walk. The other is *how* you walk.

Isaiah 35:8 puts them together: "an highway shall be there, *and a way*." The verse doesn't say "a highway that is a way." It names both — the covenant path AND the conduct on it — then unifies them as "the way of holiness."

This distinction runs through every highway passage in Scripture and solves a persistent interpretive problem: when prophets cry that the highway is desolate, they do not mean the Romans neglected road maintenance. They mean the covenant path has been abandoned. And when God commands "prepare the highway," He is not calling for construction crews — He is calling for repentance.

The root of *mesillah* (H4546) is *salal* (H5549) — "to mound up, cast up, exalt." The highway is *elevated*. It is raised above the terrain, cleared of stones, built up above the common ground. This is not just a trail worn by foot traffic. It is a deliberately constructed, lifted road — visible from a distance, distinct from the surrounding landscape. The traveler on the highway is walking above the level of the wilderness around it.

**Symbolizes:** The covenant path — the raised, cleared road of faithfulness to God

**Opposite:** Byways — the crooked, low paths of unfaithfulness (Judg 5:6)

**Defining verses:** Isa 35:8, Isa 40:3, Isa 33:8, Prov 16:17

**The surprise:** Isaiah 33:8 makes the equation explicit — "the **highways** lie waste, the wayfaring man ceaseth: he hath broken the **covenant**." Highways desolate = covenant broken. The text itself equates them.

**Connected:** $way, $rock, $truth, $wilderness, $marriage, $fornication, $virgin, $harlot, $sea

## Highway Across Scripture

### When the Covenant Breaks, the Highway Disappears

> *"The **highways** lie waste, the wayfaring man ceaseth: he hath broken the covenant, he hath despised the cities, he regardeth no man."* — Isa 33:8

This is the verse that locks the meaning in place. Isaiah draws a direct, explicit parallel: the highways lying waste *is* the covenant being broken. These are not two separate calamities listed in sequence — they are synonymous descriptions of the same condition. The wayfaring man ceases *because* the covenant is broken. No covenant, no highway. No highway, no travelers.

The pattern repeats in the earliest highway reference in Scripture:

> *"In the days of Shamgar the son of Anath, in the days of Jael, the **highways** were unoccupied, and the travellers walked through byways."* — Judg 5:6

Deborah's song recalls a time of covenant abandonment. Israel had turned to other gods (Judg 2:12–13). The result: the highways — the raised paths of covenant faithfulness — lay empty. Travelers took the *byways* instead: crooked, low, hidden paths. The word translated "byways" (H6128 *aqalqal*) comes from a root meaning "crooked, twisted" — the opposite of the cleared, straight, elevated highway.

The contrast is stark: the highway is raised, visible, straight. The byway is low, hidden, crooked. When the covenant is kept, the faithful walk the highway in the open. When the covenant is broken, people slink along twisted paths, afraid to walk openly in the $way of the LORD.

### Prepare the Highway — The Call to Repentance

> *"The voice of him that crieth in the $[wilderness], Prepare ye the $[way] of the LORD, make straight in the desert a **highway** for our God."* — Isa 40:3

> *"Every valley shall be exalted, and every mountain and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain."* — Isa 40:4

Matthew identifies this as John the Baptist's mission (Mat 3:1–3). John did not build roads in the Judean $wilderness. He called Israel to repentance — to clear the obstacles that had accumulated on the covenant path. The valleys exalted and mountains brought low are not topography. They are the moral landscape: pride leveled, humility raised, crooked conduct straightened.

The highway must be *prepared*. The root *salal* (H5549) means to cast up, to build up, to clear. The covenant path doesn't simply exist — it must be actively maintained. Stones must be removed (Isa 62:10 — "cast up the highway; gather out the stones"). The faithful must clear the way by removing the obstacles of sin and rebellion.

This is why the highway is prepared *in the $wilderness*. The $wilderness is the place of testing, the terrain between Egypt and the Promised Land, between bondage and covenant rest. The highway runs through it — but it must be cleared by those who walk it. John the Baptist appears in the wilderness calling for exactly this: "Repent ye: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand" (Mat 3:2).

The highway is not the destination. It is the *path to* the destination. And the preparation of that path is repentance itself.

### The Highway of the Upright

> *"The **highway** of the upright is to depart from evil: he that keepeth his $[way] preserveth his soul."* — Prov 16:17

Here the highway and the $way appear in the same verse, distinguished from one another. The highway of the upright (the covenant path they walk) is defined by its *trajectory* — away from evil. And the one who keeps his $way (his conduct, his manner of life) preserves his soul.

The highway tells you *where* you are walking: on the raised path of faithfulness, heading away from evil. The $way tells you *how* you are walking: keeping your conduct in line with $truth — God's commandments, His standard (Ps 119:142, Jn 17:17). Both are needed. A person can know the right path and walk it badly. A person can walk well on the wrong road. Proverbs 16:17 insists that the highway (covenant path) and the $way (faithful conduct) must be unified — just as Isaiah 35:8 joins them as "the way of holiness."

### The Way of Holiness — Highway and Way United

> *"And an **highway** shall be there, and a $[way], and it shall be called The $[way] of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it; but it shall be for those: the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein."* — Isa 35:8

> *"No lion shall be there, nor any ravenous beast shall go up thereon, it shall not be found there; but the redeemed shall walk there."* — Isa 35:9

The highway of Isaiah 35 is no ordinary road. It is characterized by absolute separation. The unclean cannot pass over it. No predator walks it. Only the redeemed travel there.

This is the fullest picture of the highway as covenant path: it is exclusive, holy, and safe. The word "holiness" (H6944 *qodesh*) means *set apart* — separated from the common, dedicated to God. The highway itself is set apart, just as the covenant people are set apart. The path and the people share the same quality.

And notice the promise embedded in it: "the wayfaring men, though fools, shall not err therein." The highway is so clear, so well-marked, so elevated above the surrounding terrain, that even the simple will not lose their way. This is grace built into the path itself. The covenant road is not a hidden trail requiring extraordinary wisdom to follow. It is raised up, visible, unmistakable — a path anyone can walk who is willing to walk it.

### The Highway of Exodus and Return

> *"And there shall be an **highway** for the remnant of his people, which shall be left, from Assyria; like as it was to Israel in the day that he came up out of the land of Egypt."* — Isa 11:16

The highway of return from exile is compared to the original Exodus. Just as God made a path through the $sea for Israel — a dry road through the waters of the nations — so He will make a highway for the remnant returning from Assyria.

The $sea represents nations and chaotic peoples (Rev 17:15, Isa 17:12). The Exodus highway ran through the $sea on dry ground — the covenant path cutting through the midst of the nations. Israel walked between walls of water, surrounded by the $sea but not swallowed by it. The highway is the path of covenant deliverance that runs *through* but remains distinct *from* the chaotic mass of the nations.

This is the pattern that echoes forward: every return, every restoration, every deliverance follows the highway — the raised covenant path that God clears for His people to come home.

### The Highways of the Wedding Feast

> *"Then saith he to his servants, The $[marriage] is ready, but they which were bidden were not worthy. Go ye therefore into the **highways**, and as many as ye shall find, bid to the $[marriage]."* — Mat 22:8–9

In Jesus' parable of the wedding feast (Mat 22:1–14), the king's original invitees refuse the $marriage invitation. Some ignore it. Some murder the servants. The king then sends his servants "into the highways" to gather anyone they find.

The highways here are significant. These are not the byways, not the hedges, not the dark corners. The servants are sent to the *raised path* — the covenant road — to find those already walking it. The people on the highways are those who, though not part of the religious establishment that refused the invitation, are nevertheless on the covenant path.

The parable resonates with the prophetic pattern: the original covenant people reject the invitation, so it extends to others who are already walking faithfully. The $marriage — the covenant picture itself — requires guests. When those who claimed guardianship of the covenant refuse it, the invitation goes to whoever is found on the highway.

But even among these gathered guests, one enters without a wedding garment (Mat 22:11–13). Being *on* the highway is necessary but not sufficient. The $way — one's conduct, the garment one wears — must also be right. Highway (where you walk) and $way (how you walk) must both align. The covenant path and covenant conduct are inseparable.

## Patterns

1. **Highway = covenant path; Way = conduct on it.** Every key passage distinguishes or unites these two: Isa 35:8 (highway + way = way of holiness), Prov 16:17 (highway of the upright / keepeth his way), Isa 40:3 (prepare the way / make straight a highway). The symbolic framework requires both.

2. **Desolate highway = broken covenant.** Isa 33:8 states it explicitly. Judg 5:6 illustrates it. When the people forsake the covenant, the highway lies waste and travelers take the byways.

3. **The highway is raised.** H5549 *salal* = to cast up, to exalt. The highway is *above* the surrounding terrain — elevated, visible, distinct from common ground. This mirrors the holiness (set-apartness) of the covenant itself.

4. **The highway must be prepared.** Isa 40:3–4, Isa 62:10. The covenant path requires active clearing — removal of obstacles through repentance. John the Baptist's ministry is highway-preparation in the $wilderness.

5. **The highway runs through the wilderness and the sea.** Isa 11:16 links the highway to the Exodus crossing. The covenant path cuts through the $wilderness (testing ground) and through the $sea (nations), providing a clear road of deliverance in the midst of chaos.

6. **The highway is exclusive.** Isa 35:8–9 — only the redeemed walk there. The unclean, the predators, are absent. The covenant path is separated from the common way by its nature.

7. **The byway is the highway's opposite.** Judg 5:6 — when highways are abandoned, travelers walk crooked, twisted, hidden paths. The byway (H6128 *aqalqal* — twisted) contrasts with the highway (raised, straight, cleared). Covenant faithfulness vs. covenant abandonment.

## Connections

- **$way** — Conduct, manner of life. The highway is *where* you walk; the $way is *how* you walk. Isaiah 35:8 unites them as "the way of holiness." A highway without right conduct is like the wedding guest without a garment (Mat 22:11–13). Both must align.

- **$rock** — Covenant, the unchanging foundation. The highway suggests the covenant *path* — the raised road one travels. The $rock suggests the covenant *foundation* — the unchanging ground beneath. The highway is built *upon* the $rock. A path raised on shifting $sand would collapse.

- **$truth** — God's commandments, His standard. $truth defines what right conduct looks like *on* the highway. The highway is the covenant path; $truth is the standard that governs travel along it. "Thy law is the truth" (Ps 119:142) — the law marks the highway's boundaries.

- **$wilderness** — The place of testing where the highway must be prepared. Isaiah 40:3 places the highway *in* the wilderness. The covenant path runs through the testing ground. John the Baptist appears in the $wilderness to clear it through repentance.

- **$marriage** — The covenant picture. The wedding feast parable (Mat 22:1–14) sends servants to the highways to gather guests for the $marriage. The highway leads *to* the $marriage. Those on the covenant path are invited into covenant union.

- **$fornication** — Idolatry, serving other gods. If the highway is the covenant path, $fornication is what draws people *off* it. Ephraim's "silly dove" going to Egypt and Assyria (Hos 7:11) is a picture of leaving the highway for the byway — abandoning covenant loyalty for foreign allegiance.

- **$virgin** — Faithful, exclusively devoted. The $virgin walks the highway; the $harlot has left it. The virgin daughter of Zion (Isa 37:22, Jer 31:4) represents Israel on the covenant path. When she becomes a $harlot (Isa 1:21), the highways lie waste.

- **$harlot** — Idolater, covenant breaker. The opposite of the $virgin and the traveler on the highway. The faithful city "become an harlot" (Isa 1:21) is the same city whose highways lie desolate (Isa 33:8). Covenant breaking and highway abandonment are the same event.

- **$sea** — Nations, chaotic peoples. The highway of Isa 11:16 runs through the midst of the $sea — the Exodus path cutting through the nations on dry ground. The covenant path is distinct from the $sea; it passes through the chaos of the nations without being absorbed by it.

## For Further Study

- **$way** — If the highway is the covenant *path*, the $way is the covenant *conduct*. How does Scripture distinguish and unite them — and what happens when one is present without the other?
- **$rock** — The covenant foundation. The highway is the road; the $rock is the ground beneath. How does the unchanging $rock support the covenant path?
- **$wilderness** — The testing ground where the highway must be prepared. Why does the covenant path always run through the $wilderness before reaching the promise?
- **$marriage** — The covenant picture to which the highway leads. The wedding feast parable sends servants to the highways. How does the $marriage metaphor illuminate who walks the highway and who refuses?
- **$truth** — God's standard and commandments. If $truth defines the boundaries of the highway, what does it mean to walk the highway while ignoring the standard?
