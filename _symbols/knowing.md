# Knowing, to know

## Summary

**[Covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) relationship recognized and proven by [faithful obedience](/research/symbols/faith/), extending from marital intimacy to acknowledgment by one's covenant Lord. Knowing is not mere mental assent or awareness.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Love, Knowing, Faith & Belief](/books/symbolic-language/knowing-faith-love-and-belief/).

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**[Covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) relationship recognized and proven by [faithful obedience](/research/symbols/faith/), extending from marital intimacy to acknowledgment by one's covenant Lord. Knowing is not mere mental assent or awareness.**

### Literal Biblical Definition

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **knowing** begins: “Skilful; well informed; intelligent; as, a knowing man; a knowing dog.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

Covenant relationship proven by obedience — the intimate “knew” of marriage; a stranger is one not known.

> *“And Adam knew Eve his wife; and she conceived, and bare Cain, and said, I have gotten a man from the LORD.”* — Genesis 4:1

> *“My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge: because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee, that thou shalt be no priest to me: seeing thou hast forgotten the law of thy God, I will also forget thy children.”* — Hosea 4:6

> *“And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity.”* — Matthew 7:23

> *“And hereby we do know that we know him, if we keep his commandments.”* — 1 John 2:3

> *“He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a liar, and the truth is not in him.”* — 1 John 2:4

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **knowing** begins:

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The wording “proven by obedience” could be read more categorically than the consensus, making obedience a strict defining test rather than a characteristic expression or evidence of relationship. It could also be overgeneralized to exclude ordinary cognitive uses of “know.” In context, however, 1 John 2:3–4 explicitly presents obedience as the test of knowing God, and the concise entry appears directed to the term’s symbolic-theological sense rather than every lexical occurrence.

- One could argue REFINED on the ground that the book elevates obedience from an evidential expression to a defining condition ('proven by obedience'), potentially narrowing 'knowing' to obedient covenant members and excluding, e.g., merely affective or elective senses. But 1 John 2:3-4 itself frames obedience as the test of knowing, and the consensus already incorporates this, so the narrowing is not material.

- None. The agreement is comprehensive and explicit.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
