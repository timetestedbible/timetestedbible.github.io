These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The curse-ground’s crop—those consumed by the care of this world and the deceitfulness of riches: fruitless, choking the word, offering a false shadow, and ending in fire.

> *“Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field;”* — Genesis 3:18

> *“He also that received seed among the thorns is he that heareth the word; and the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the word, and he becometh unfruitful.”* — Matthew 13:22

> *“And the bramble said unto the trees, If in truth ye anoint me king over you, then come and put your trust in my shadow: and if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon.”* — Judges 9:15

> *“But the sons of Belial shall be all of them as thorns thrust away, because they cannot be taken with hands:”* — 2 Samuel 23:6

> *“But the man that shall touch them must be fenced with iron and the staff of a spear; and they shall be utterly burned with fire in the same place.”* — 2 Samuel 23:7

> *“But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned.”* — Hebrews 6:8

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **bramble** begins:

> “Bram”ble, n. Etym: [OE. brembil, AS.brbramal), fr. the same root as E. broom, As. br. See Broom.] 1. (Bot.) Defn: Any plant of the genus Rubus, including the raspberry and blackberry. Hence: Any rough, prickly shrub. The thorny brambles, and embracing bushes. Shak.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- A MATCH classification is plausible because every component of the book’s interpretation appears explicitly in the consensus primary meaning or listed alternatives. Nevertheless, the book presents these components as a unified and comparatively narrow identification, excluding several other applications admitted by the consensus, so REFINED better captures its extension.

- In Matthew 13:22 the thorns are explicitly identified with 'the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches'—the cares themselves—while the unfruitful person is the one who receives seed among thorns; the book reclassifies the thorns as the persons consumed by those cares, which slightly strains that proof-text even though Judges 9:15 and 2 Samuel 23:6-7 do support a personal referent. One could also argue the book's exclusion of the crown of thorns and Paul's thorn omits central consensus material, pushing toward a stronger disagreement than REFINED captures.

- By combining Matthew 13's 'cares of this world' with Judges 9's 'false shadow' and Hebrews 6's 'fire,' the book treats distinct parables as parts of a single, rigid allegorical system, potentially over-harmonizing texts that merely share a common agricultural backdrop.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
