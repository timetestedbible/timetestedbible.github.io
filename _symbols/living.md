# Living creature

## Approved Definition

**A nation or organized people — the living counterpart of a [beast](/research/symbols/beast/) or animal.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Bow](/books/symbolic-language/the-bow/).

## Definition Layers

### Bible literal sense

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **living** begins: “Being alive; having life; as, a living creature.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The book defines 'living creature' as a nation or people, drawing a parallel between the beasts of Daniel's sea and the creatures in Revelation's sea.

> *“And four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another.”* — Daniel 7:3

> *“These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth.”* — Daniel 7:17

> *“And the third part of the creatures which were in the sea, and had life, died; and the third part of the ships were destroyed.”* — Revelation 8:9

> *“And the second angel poured out his vial upon the sea; and it became as the blood of a dead man: and every living soul died in the sea.”* — Revelation 16:3

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **living** begins:

> “Liv”ing, a. Etym: [From Live, v. i.] 1. Being alive; having life; as, a living creature.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Daniel 7 explicitly interprets its beasts as kings, but Revelation 8:9 and 16:3 do not interpret sea creatures or living souls as peoples. The analogy from Daniel’s beasts to Revelation’s marine life does not by itself establish that both symbols have the same political or national referent.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: for the same term in Revelation, the book substitutes 'peoples' where consensus (via the lexical alternative) reads literal sea creatures dying in judgment, so both cannot be true of Rev 8:9/16:3 in the same sense. However, since the consensus's central identification concerns the throne creatures of Rev 4-5 — which the book never denies or replaces — and the nation-reading appears nowhere in the consensus space, absence rather than contradiction better characterizes the relationship. Also, the cited verses support an analogy (Dan 7 explicitly interprets beasts as kings) but never explicitly interpret sea creatures as peoples, so citation support is only partial.

- The text in Revelation 8:9 and 16:3 likely refers to literal marine life affected by the plagues, whereas the 'beasts' (thēria) in Daniel 7 are explicitly defined by the text as symbolic kings/kingdoms. Equating the generic Greek terms for living things in Revelation's sea to the specific symbolic beasts of Daniel is an exegetical leap.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
