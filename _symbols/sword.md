# Sword

The fuller book trail continues in [Garments & Armor](/books/symbolic-language/garments/),
where the Sword of the Spirit appears as one piece of God's covenant armor.

## Approved Definition

**The Word of God wielded by the Spirit to discern and execute [judgment](/research/symbols/judgment/); also war and the governing force that carries judgment out. It proceeds from Christ's mouth, takes [peace](/research/symbols/peace/) from the earth, and guards the [way](/research/symbols/way/).**
> *"For my **sword** shall be bathed in $[heaven]: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment."* — Isa 34:5

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The Word of God wielded by the Spirit to discern and execute [judgment](/research/symbols/judgment/); also war and the governing force that carries judgment out. It proceeds from Christ's mouth, takes [peace](/research/symbols/peace/) from the earth, and guards the [way](/research/symbols/way/).**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **sword** begins: “An offensive weapon, having a long and usually sharp”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

The Hebrew word for sword — H2719 *chereb* — does not come from a root meaning "to cut" or "to pierce." It comes from H2717 *charab*: **to parch, to dry up, to lay waste, to make desolate**.

Every derivative of this root tells the same story:

- H2719 *chereb* = sword (the instrument)
- H2721 *choreb* = drought (listed as a "collateral form of H2719" — the sword IS drought)
- H2722 *Choreb* = Horeb — **the covenant mountain**, the place where Moses received the law
- H2723 *chorbah* = ruin, desolation, waste place

The sword's *name* means desolation-maker. It does not merely cut — it **wastes**. It produces drought. It leaves ruin behind. And yet this same root names the mountain where God gave Israel His covenant. The instrument that makes desolation is etymologically identical to the place where covenant was established.

This paradox — the same root naming both the weapon that destroys and the mountain where God meets His people — turns out to be the key to every sword in Scripture. The sword *makes* covenant (Josh 5:2–3, circumcision knives = H2719) and *breaks* covenant (Lev 26:25, "a sword that shall avenge the quarrel of my covenant"). It is the instrument of the covenant standard itself: the edge that divides faithful from unfaithful, that wastes what violates, and that — when placed in the mouth of the Word of God — becomes the spoken covenant decree that judges the nations.

**Symbolizes:** The desolation-maker — the covenant instrument that wastes what opposes God's standard, whether wielded physically or spoken as His Word

**Opposite:** $plowshare — cultivation where there was destruction (Isa 2:4, Mic 4:3: swords beaten into plowshares = desolation-making reversed into fruitfulness; Joel 3:10 reverses the direction)

**Defining verses:** Gen 3:24, Lev 26:25, Deut 32:41–42, Isa 34:5–6, Zech 13:7, Heb 4:12, Rev 19:15

**The surprise:** Sword (H2719 *chereb*), drought (H2721 *choreb*), and the covenant mountain Horeb (H2722 *Choreb*) are all the same root — and H2719 also translates as "knife" when used for circumcision, making the sword the instrument that *initiates* covenant, not just the one that enforces its curses.

**Connected:** $fire, $famine, $pestilence, $blood, $babylon, $earthquake, $heaven, $thorns, $the-way, $tree, $water

## Sword Across Scripture

### The Flaming Gate — Eden's Sword

> *"So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming **sword** which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life."* — Gen 3:24

The first sword in Scripture is not wielded by a human. It is a H2719 *chereb* wrapped in flame (H3858 *lahat* — to blaze and enwrap) that H2015 *haphak* in every direction. Three words in this verse carry more weight than the English lets on.

**H2015 *haphak*** does not mean "spinning" or "rotating." Strong's: "to turn about or over; to **change**, overturn, return, **be converted**." This is the transformation verb — used for Sodom's overthrow (Gen 19:21, 25), water turned to blood (Exod 7:17), and conversion itself. The sword does not merely move — it *transforms what it contacts*. It converts in every direction.

**H8104 *shamar*** — "to keep" — does not mean "to block." Strong's: "to **hedge about** (as with thorns), **guard**, protect, **preserve**." This is the same word given to Adam in Gen 2:15: God placed him in the garden "to dress it and to **keep** (*shamar*) it." Adam's original mandate was to *shamar* the garden. Now the cherubim and flaming sword *shamar* $the-way of the $tree of life. The way is **preserved**, not destroyed.

And the object of the guarding is *lishmor et-derek etz hachayyim* — "to preserve **the way of** the tree of life." $the-way (*derek*) — covenant-conduct, Torah-faithfulness — still exists. The $tree of life — the living nation/people — still stands. The sword preserves the path to them.

This reframes the entire image. The flaming *chereb* at Eden's gate is not a wall. It is a **gate that transforms**. The same $fire that killed the soldiers at Nebuchadnezzar's furnace and left the covenant-keepers unharmed (Dan 3:22–25). The same fire-law (*eshdath*, H799 — fire + law, from the same *esh* root) given at Horeb (H2722 — from the same root as *chereb*). The sword tests what passes through. Gold survives. Chaff is consumed. $the-way is preserved — for whoever can pass through the fire.

The fusion of sword and $fire recurs throughout Scripture: Joshua burns cities after putting them to the sword (Josh 11:11), Isaiah's final judgment comes "by $[fire] AND by his **sword**" (Isa 66:16), and Christ's eyes are like flame while the sword proceeds from His mouth (Rev 1:14–16, 19:12–15). The flaming sword of Gen 3:24 is the prototype — and Revelation 22:1–2 shows the destination it guards: the $tree of life standing by the river of $water of life, with leaves "for the healing of the nations." The way was never destroyed. It was guarded by fire, preserved through judgment, and opened again in the renewed creation.

### The Circumcision Knife — Covenant Entry

> *"At that time the LORD said unto Joshua, Make thee sharp **knives**, and circumcise again the children of Israel the second time."* — Josh 5:2

The word translated "knives" here is H2719 *chereb* — the same word translated "sword" in every battle passage. The desolation-maker is also the covenant-entry instrument. Joshua uses *swords* to cut away the flesh of uncircumcision before Israel can enter the Promised Land.

This is the paradox the root H2717 encodes. The sword parches and wastes — but the same cutting action that destroys an enemy *initiates* a covenant partner. What the sword removes determines whether it is executing judgment or opening the way to belonging. Circumcision cuts away what is outside the covenant; the sword of battle cuts down who opposes the covenant. Same instrument, same root, same action — the difference lies in *what is being cut*.

The altar law makes the same point from the opposite direction:

> *"And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone: for if thou lift up thy **tool** upon it, thou hast polluted it."* — Exod 20:25

"Tool" here is again H2719. The desolation-maker must not touch the altar. The place of worship must not bear the mark of the cutting instrument. The sword makes covenant (circumcision) and breaks covenant (judgment) — but the place where God receives offering must remain uncut, undesolated, whole.

### The Sword That Avenges the Covenant

> *"And I will bring a **sword** upon you, that shall avenge the quarrel of my covenant."* — Lev 26:25

> *"I will send a **sword** after you."* — Lev 26:33

Leviticus 26 is the covenant blessings-and-curses chapter, and the sword appears explicitly as the enforcer of covenant violation. The phrase is remarkable: the sword does not merely punish sin — it *avenges the quarrel of the covenant*. The covenant has a quarrel, a legal dispute, and the sword is the instrument that settles it.

What follows the sword in Lev 26 is desolation in every register:

> *"And your land shall be desolate, and your cities waste."* — Lev 26:33

> *"Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths, as long as it lieth desolate."* — Lev 26:34

The sword (H2719 *chereb*) produces desolation (H2723 *chorbah*) — the root fulfilling itself. The land that refused sabbath rest is forced into sabbath by the sword, which leaves it waste. The desolation-maker imposes from the outside what should have been offered from within.

### "MY Sword" — Divine Ownership

> *"If I whet my glittering **sword**, and mine hand take hold on judgment; I will render vengeance to mine enemies, and will reward them that hate me. I will make mine arrows drunk with $[blood], and my **sword** shall devour flesh."* — Deut 32:41–42

Throughout Scripture, the sword belongs to God before it belongs to anyone else. He whets it (Deut 32:41). He sheathes and unsheathes it (Ezek 21:3–5). He sends it (Jer 25:16). He even gives it into specific hands:

> *"A **sword**, a **sword** is sharpened, and also furbished: It is sharpened to make a sore slaughter; it is furbished that it may glitter… the **sword** is given into the hand to be used: it is sharpened and furbished to give it into the hand of the slayer."* — Ezek 21:9–11

In Ezekiel's extraordinary sword-song (Ezek 21:1–32), the sword belongs to God but is handed to $babylon's king as the executor of covenant judgment against Jerusalem. The sword is called a "trial" (v. 13) — a testing instrument. $babylon does not own the sword; God lends it. The worldly kingdom becomes the rod of covenant enforcement, not knowing it serves a purpose beyond its own ambition (compare Isa 10:5–7 — Assyria as God's staff that "meaneth not so").

David understood this:

> *"The LORD saveth not with **sword** and spear: for the battle is the LORD'S."* — 1 Sam 17:47

And Joshua encountered it firsthand:

> *"And it came to pass, when Joshua was by Jericho, that he lifted up his eyes and looked, and, behold, there stood a man over against him with his **sword** drawn in his hand."* — Josh 5:13

This figure identifies himself as "captain of the host of the LORD" — and his drawn sword precedes the fall of Jericho without a single Israelite blade being drawn against the walls. The sword that destroyed Jericho was already in divine hands before human armies moved.

### The Triad: Sword, Famine, and Pestilence

> *"When they fast, I will not hear their cry; and when they offer burnt offering and an oblation, I will not accept them: but I will consume them by the **sword**, and by the $[famine], and by the $[pestilence]."* — Jer 14:12

More than thirty times in Jeremiah alone, and roughly ten more in Ezekiel, the sword appears in a fixed triad with $famine and $pestilence. This is not coincidence — it is a covenant-curse formula:

- **Sword** = direct violence, the desolation-maker in active operation
- **$famine** = the absence of God's Word, covenant instruction withdrawn (Amos 8:11 defines famine as "not a famine of bread... but of hearing the words of the LORD")
- **$pestilence** = the covenant death-consequence, God's direct lethal judgment (H1698 *deber* — which shares its consonants with H1697 *dabar*, "word" — pestilence as the dark mirror of the Word)

Together they form a cascade: the covenant standard is enforced (sword), the covenant Word is withdrawn ($famine), and death follows ($pestilence). This triad recurs throughout the prophets as the signature pattern of covenant dissolution:

> *"So it shall be, that the nation and kingdom which will not serve the same Nebuchadnezzar the king of $[babylon], and that will not put their neck under the yoke of the king of $[babylon], that nation will I punish, saith the LORD, with the **sword**, and with the $[famine], and with the $[pestilence], until I have consumed them by his hand."* — Jer 27:8

In Ezekiel, the triad expands into a tetrad — sword, $famine, evil beasts, and $pestilence (Ezek 14:21) — which maps directly onto the four horsemen of Revelation 6:1–8. The sword given to the second horseman (Rev 6:4, G3162 *machaira megalē* — "great sword") takes peace from the earth. The triad and tetrad are the desolation-maker working in concert with its companion judgments: Word withdrawn, death following, the land left waste.

### Bathed in Heaven — The Sword Descends

> *"For my **sword** shall be bathed in $[heaven]: behold, it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my curse, to judgment."* — Isa 34:5

The trajectory here is stunning: the sword is H7301 *ravah* — saturated, soaked, drenched — in $heaven (H8064 *shamayim*). It is prepared in the jurisdictional domain where God's authority operates without opposition, then *descends* upon the people of H2764 *cherem* (devoted to destruction). What follows is total desolation: streams become pitch, dust becomes brimstone, $fire that is never quenched, smoke ascending perpetually (Isa 34:9–10).

The heavens themselves respond to the sword:

> *"And all the host of $[heaven] shall be dissolved, and the $[heaven]s shall be rolled together as a scroll."* — Isa 34:4

This is the sword working in $heaven before it descends to earth — the shaking ($earthquake) of established authorities (Heb 12:26–27) that precedes the desolation of the earth. The sword bathed in heaven suggests that judgment begins from God's throne and works downward, not upward. Man's systems ($babylon) do not provoke an external attack — they provoke the descent of an instrument already prepared above.

### Awake, O Sword — The Shepherd Struck

> *"Awake, O **sword**, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, saith the LORD of hosts: smite the shepherd, and the $[sheep] shall be scattered."* — Zech 13:7

This is perhaps the most startling deployment of the sword in all of Scripture. God turns His own desolation-maker against His own shepherd — "the man that is MY fellow" (H5997 *amith* — close companion, associate, neighbor). The sword that avengers the quarrel of the covenant (Lev 26:25) is now aimed at God's covenant partner.

Jesus quotes this verse the night of His arrest:

> *"Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the $[sheep] of the flock shall be scattered abroad."* — Mat 26:31

The covenant sword strikes Christ. The desolation-maker finds its ultimate target not in the covenant-breaker but in the covenant-keeper — the one who bears the desolation on behalf of others. If the sword = the covenant standard enforced, then Zech 13:7 reveals what happens when that standard is absorbed by the faithful on behalf of the unfaithful. The shepherd takes the desolation; the sheep scatter but survive.

### The Word IS the Sword

> *"For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged **sword**, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."* — Heb 4:12

> *"And take the helmet of salvation, and the **sword** of the Spirit, which is the word of God."* — Eph 6:17

> *"And out of his mouth goeth a sharp **sword**, that with it he should smite the nations."* — Rev 19:15

The progression across Scripture moves from God *wielding* a sword (Deut 32:41) to God's Son *being identified with* a sword proceeding from His mouth (Rev 1:16, 2:12, 19:15). The Word of God IS the desolation-maker. The spoken covenant standard is the instrument that cuts, divides, and wastes whatever violates it.

Hebrews 4:12 describes the Word-sword as a divider — separating soul from spirit, joints from marrow, discerning thoughts and intents. This is the circumcision-knife function (Josh 5:2) applied internally. The same cutting that initiated covenant externally (flesh) now operates internally (heart). The Word enters and separates what belongs from what does not.

Luke 2:35 captures the personal dimension:

> *"Yea, a **sword** shall pierce through thy own soul also, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed."* — Lk 2:35

The word here is G4501 *rhomphaia* — the large sword of divine judgment used in Revelation for Christ's mouth-sword. A *rhomphaia* pierces Mary's soul — and the result is not destruction but *revelation*. The thoughts of many hearts are exposed. The desolation-maker, applied to the faithful, becomes the revealer of hidden things.

In Revelation, the identification is complete. The rider on the white horse is named "The Word of God" (Rev 19:13), and the sharp sword proceeds from His mouth (Rev 19:15, 21). He does not strike with His hands — He speaks, and the sword does its work. The nations are smitten by the spoken covenant decree. The same Word that created (Gen 1) now judges. The desolation-maker and the Word are one.

### Not Peace but a Sword

> *"Think not that I am come to send peace on earth: I came not to send peace, but a **sword**."* — Mat 10:34

Jesus uses G3162 *machaira* — the personal sword, the blade that divides at close range. What follows is a description of household division: father against son, daughter against mother. The sword-as-divider separates even families.

This coheres with the desolation-maker meaning. Christ's coming does not immediately produce the plowshare-era (Isa 2:4). Before swords become plowshares, the sword must do its covenantal work: dividing those who enter from those who refuse, separating the circumcised heart from the uncircumcised, exposing — as Lk 2:35 says — the thoughts of many hearts. The sword Christ brings is the same Word-sword of Heb 4:12 that separates soul from spirit.

The disciples misunderstand this at Gethsemane:

> *"Then said Jesus unto him, Put up again thy **sword** into his place: for all they that take the **sword** shall perish with the **sword**."* — Mat 26:52

Peter takes up a physical *machaira*. Jesus rebukes him — the principle of the sword turns on its wielder. In Rev 13:10, the same principle appears: "He that killeth with the **sword** must be killed with the **sword**." The desolation-maker is not a human instrument to deploy at will. It belongs to God (Deut 32:41). Those who seize it without divine commission find it turned against themselves.

### Swords into Plowshares — The Reversal

> *"And he shall judge among the nations, and shall rebuke many people: and they shall beat their **swords** into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: nation shall not lift up **sword** against nation, neither shall they learn war any more."* — Isa 2:4

> *"Beat your plowshares into **swords**, and your pruninghooks into spears."* — Joel 3:10

The desolation-maker has an opposite: the plowshare. The same metal, reforged. Where the sword wastes and parches (H2717), the plowshare breaks ground for cultivation. The sword-to-plowshare transformation marks the era when God's judgment work is completed and fruitfulness replaces desolation.

Joel 3:10 reverses the direction: plowshares back into swords. This is not contradiction — it is sequence. Before the final peace comes the final judgment. The nations are called to rearm for the battle in the valley of decision (Joel 3:14), after which the LORD is the hope of His people (Joel 3:16). Swords to plowshares is the destination; plowshares to swords is the last station before arrival.

The $thorns — worldly people characterized by cares, riches, and pleasures (Mat 13:22) — are what the sword desolates before the plowshare can work. The ground choked with $thorns must be cleared (Isa 10:17 — $thorns and briers burned "in one day"; compare Rev 18:8 — $babylon judged "in one day"). The sword clears; the plowshare cultivates. Desolation precedes fruitfulness.

## Patterns

1. **Root = desolation.** Every H2717 derivative (sword, drought, Horeb, ruin) points to the same action: parching, wasting, making desolate. The sword doesn't just wound — it leaves wasteland behind. The 372 OT occurrences of H2719 carry this root-meaning throughout.

2. **The sword both makes and breaks covenant.** Circumcision knives (Josh 5:2, H2719) initiate the covenant. The sword avenges the covenant's quarrel (Lev 26:25). The same root names the covenant mountain Horeb (H2722). Making and breaking are the same action applied to different targets.

3. **The triad is a covenant-dissolution formula.** Sword + $famine + $pestilence = violence + Word-withdrawal + death-consequence. This fixed pattern appears ~30× in Jeremiah, ~10× in Ezekiel, and maps onto the horsemen of Revelation 6.

4. **The sword fuses with $fire.** Gen 3:24 (flaming sword), Josh 11:11, Judg 1:8, Isa 34:5–10, Isa 66:16, Rev 19:12–15. $fire = God's holy standard in active contact; sword = the instrument that produces desolation. Together they consume and waste.

5. **God owns the sword.** He whets it (Deut 32:41), sheathes and unsheathes it (Ezek 21:3–5), lends it to $babylon (Ezek 21:9–11), sends it (Jer 25:16), and turns it on His own Son (Zech 13:7). Humans who take it without commission perish by it (Mat 26:52, Rev 13:10).

6. **The sword becomes the Word.** The progression: God *wields* the sword (OT) → the sword proceeds from Christ's *mouth* (Rev 1:16, 19:15) → the Word of God IS sharper than any sword (Heb 4:12) → the sword of the Spirit IS the Word (Eph 6:17). The spoken covenant standard is the ultimate desolation-maker.

7. **Two Greek swords distinguish two domains.** G4501 *rhomphaia* = the divine judgment sword (Christ's mouth-sword, Mary's soul-piercer, Death's weapon on the pale horse). G3162 *machaira* = the sword in human and spiritual contexts (state authority in Rom 13:4, the Word in Eph 6:17 and Heb 4:12, the red horse's instrument in Rev 6:4). The same symbolic meaning — desolation-maker — operates in both registers.

8. **Swords ↔ plowshares marks the era change.** Isa 2:4/Mic 4:3 (swords → plowshares = desolation → cultivation = judgment era ends). Joel 3:10 (plowshares → swords = one final judgment before permanent peace).

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$fire** — The sword's constant companion: both appear at Eden's gate (Gen 3:24), both execute final judgment (Isa 66:16), both characterize Christ's return (Rev 19:12–15). $fire = God's holy standard in active contact, consuming what violates covenant and refining what keeps it. The sword desolates; the fire consumes. Together they are the full expression of the covenant standard applied.

- **$famine** — The absence of God's Word. In the triad (sword + $famine + $pestilence), the sword enforces and the famine withdraws — violence upon the body, silence upon the soul. Amos 8:11 defines $famine as "not a famine of bread, but of hearing the words of the LORD." When the sword comes, the Word leaves.

- **$pestilence** — Covenant death-consequence. The triad's final term: after the sword strikes and the Word withdraws, $pestilence — God's direct lethal judgment — follows. H1698 *deber* mirrors H1697 *dabar* (word); pestilence is the anti-Word, death where the Word is absent.

- **$blood** — The life of the flesh made visible. The sword is the instrument that releases $blood — which then either atones (when offered) or accuses (when taken). Deut 32:42: "my sword shall devour flesh… drunk with $[blood]." The sword draws out the life; the blood testifies of it.

- **$babylon** — Man's substitute system for God's covenant rule. God lends His sword to $babylon to execute covenant judgment (Ezek 21:9–11), and then turns it on $babylon itself (Jer 50:35–37: "A **sword** is upon the Chaldeans"). $babylon wields the sword but does not own it — and is consumed by the same instrument it borrowed.

- **$earthquake** — Violent upheaval, God shaking every established order. In Mat 24:7, the Olivet Discourse substitutes $earthquake for the sword-slot in the Jeremiah triad (sword → $earthquake + $famine + $pestilence), suggesting an escalation from direct violence to systemic destabilization. Both express the same principle: the shakeable ($earthquake) and the desolate (sword) are what God removes (Heb 12:27).

- **$heaven** — The jurisdictional domain where God's will operates. The sword is "bathed in $[heaven]" before descending (Isa 34:5). Judgment originates above and comes down. The sword proceeds from the mouth of the one who rides out of an opened $heaven (Rev 19:11–15).

- **$thorns** — Worldly people characterized by cares, riches, and pleasures. The sword desolates what $thorns represent. Isa 9:18 — wickedness burns as $fire, devours $thorns and briers. The sword clears the ground that the plowshare will cultivate; the $thorns are what must be removed before fruitfulness.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **sword** begins:

> “Sword, n. Etym: [OE. swerd, AS. sweord; akin to OFries. swerd, swird, D. zwaard, OS. swerd, OHG. swert, G. schwert, Icel. sver, Sw. svärd, Dan. sværd; of uncertain origin.] 1. An offensive weapon, having a long and usually sharp”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Revelation 6:4 strongly supports war and killing, and Genesis 3:24 supports guarding or exclusion, but neither excerpt explicitly identifies the sword as a “word of judgment.” Explicit word-and-sword texts such as Ephesians 6:17, Hebrews 4:12, and Revelation 19:15 would be needed to support that part of the gloss strongly.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: the consensus's central identification — the Word of God as the Spirit's sword that believers wield (Eph 6:17) — is effectively displaced by 'war,' and neither printed citation (Rev 6:4; Gen 3:24) mentions a 'word' at all, suggesting the book has replaced the referent rather than refined its boundary. The 'word of judgment' phrase may be doing less bridging work than a REFINED verdict assumes.

- Explicit equations of the sword with the word of God (Eph 6:17; Heb 4:12) and the consistent reading of the sword from Christ’s mouth make Word of God the primary sense; the book’s citations support only secondary war/judgment imagery and omit those texts.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The Word of God wielded by the Spirit to discern and execute [judgment](/research/symbols/judgment/); also war and the governing force that carries judgment out. It proceeds from Christ's mouth, takes [peace](/research/symbols/peace/) from the earth, and guards the [way](/research/symbols/way/).**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *sword*, *swords*, *dagger*, *knife*, *knives*, *tool*, *flaming sword*, *two-edged sword*. The English-form sweep found **420 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
