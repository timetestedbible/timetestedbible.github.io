# Naked

**Without covenant covering — exposed before God and the nations because the garment of covenant relationship has been stripped away or never obtained.**

> *"I spread my skirt over thee, and **covered thy nakedness**: yea, I sware unto thee, and entered into a $[covenant] with thee, saith the Lord GOD, and thou becamest mine."* — Ezek 16:8

---

## The Key Insight

Nakedness is the absence of covering. But in Scripture, the covering is always the covenant.

Ezek 16:8 states this with three phrases in synonymous parallelism: *spread my skirt* + *covered thy nakedness* + *entered into a covenant*. These are not three separate actions — they are three descriptions of the same event. To cover nakedness IS to enter covenant. To be naked IS to be without it.

This single equation unlocks every other use. Adam and Eve were naked without shame — and then suddenly ashamed. What changed? Not their bodies. Their standing. The prophets walked naked as signs — not of poverty, but of covenant stripped away. Revelation warns against walking naked — not literal nudity, but conducting your life outside the covenant's protection. The Levitical laws about "uncovering nakedness" are about violating covenant boundaries, not merely about physical exposure.

**Symbolizes:** Without covenant covering — the state of being exposed, unprotected, and visible in one's breach

**Opposite:** Clothed / covered — brought under covenant protection. God covers nakedness (Gen 3:21, Ezek 16:8); the faithful cover the naked (Isa 58:7, Ezek 18:7)

**Defining verse:** Ezek 16:8 — cover nakedness = enter covenant

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word for "naked" in Gen 3:7 (H5903 *ʻêyrôm*) shares its root (H6191 *ʻâram*) with the word "subtil" describing the serpent in Gen 3:1 (H6174 *ʻârûm*). Same consonant cluster ע-ר-ם. The serpent's craftiness and the resulting nakedness are linguistically bound together — the one who strips you of covering is called by the same root as the condition he leaves you in.

**Connected:** $harlot, $shadow, $fig-tree, $sackcloth, $shame, $fire, $wine, $darkness, $wings

## Naked Across Scripture

### The Garden — When Nakedness Became a Problem

The first occurrence sets the pattern for everything that follows.

> *"And they were both **naked**, the man and his wife, and were not ashamed."* — Gen 2:25

> *"And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were **naked**; and they sewed $[fig] leaves together, and made themselves aprons."* — Gen 3:7

> *"Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and **clothed** them."* — Gen 3:21

Three stages: (1) naked without shame — in unbroken communion, no covering needed; (2) naked with shame — disobedience creates awareness of exposure, and humans attempt to cover themselves with fig leaves; (3) God provides covering through skins — requiring the death of an animal, the first shedding of blood.

The sequence is worth tracing carefully. Adam and Eve do not become physically different after eating. What changes is their *standing*. Before the breach, there is nothing to hide and nothing to fear. After the breach, nakedness becomes unbearable — not because nudity is inherently shameful, but because the relationship that made exposure safe has been violated.

Their self-made covering ($fig-tree leaves) proves inadequate. God replaces it with a covering that requires death. The pattern endures across Scripture: human-manufactured coverings fail; God provides the covenant covering, and it always involves sacrifice.

### Covering Nakedness IS Entering Covenant

Ezek 16:8 is the verse that makes the symbolic meaning explicit. God describes finding Israel as an exposed, abandoned infant (Ezek 16:5-7), growing into young womanhood — "*whereas thou wast **naked** and bare*" (Ezek 16:7). Then:

> *"I spread my $[skirt] over thee, and **covered thy nakedness**: yea, I sware unto thee, and entered into a **covenant** with thee."* — Ezek 16:8

The same action appears as a request in Ruth:

> *"Spread therefore thy **skirt** over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."* — Ruth 3:9

Ruth asks Boaz to spread his covering over her — a covenant (marriage) proposal. And the violation of this principle is cursed:

> *"Cursed be he that lieth with his father's wife; because he **uncovereth his father's skirt**."* — Deut 27:20

The skirt is the covenant garment. To spread it over someone is to bring them under covenant. To uncover it is to violate the covenant boundary. To be without it is to be naked.

### Uncover Nakedness — Break the Covenant

Lev 18 is the most concentrated passage on nakedness in the entire Bible — the phrase "uncover nakedness" (H1540 + H6172 *ʻervâh*) appears 24 times in two chapters. Every prohibited relationship is described as "uncovering nakedness."

The pattern reveals something important: "nakedness" here is not merely about sexual exposure. It is about violating the protective structure of covenant family relationships. "The nakedness of thy father's wife... it IS thy father's nakedness" (Lev 18:8). The wife's nakedness belongs to the husband — it is his covenant domain. To uncover it is to breach his covenant.

This framework reaches back to Noah:

> *"And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the **nakedness** of his father, and told his two brethren without. And Shem and Japheth took a garment, and laid it upon both their shoulders, and went backward, and **covered the nakedness** of their father."* — Gen 9:22-23

Ham's offense is seeing and exposing his father's nakedness — a covenant violation. Shem and Japheth cover it — covenant honor. The curse and the blessing that follow are covenantal consequences.

### Walking Naked — Living Outside the Covenant

When nakedness is paired with "walking" (Hebrew H1980 *halak*, Greek G4043 *peripateō*), the meaning sharpens. "Walking" in Hebrew idiom is not merely locomotion — it is the conduct of life ("walk in my statutes," "walk before me"). To *walk* naked is to *live* without covenant covering.

Isaiah's prophetic sign is the paradigm:

> *"Go and loose the $[sackcloth] from off thy loins, and put off thy shoe from thy foot. And he did so, **walking naked and barefoot**."* — Isa 20:2

> *"Like as my servant Isaiah hath **walked naked** and barefoot three years for a **sign** and wonder upon Egypt and upon Ethiopia; so shall the king of Assyria lead away the Egyptians prisoners, and the Ethiopians captives, young and old, **naked** and barefoot... to the **shame** of Egypt."* — Isa 20:3-4

Isaiah walks naked for three years — not as personal humiliation, but as a living prophecy. Egypt and Ethiopia will be led away stripped of their national covering, their sovereignty, their protection. The nakedness signifies a nation without defense because it trusted in the wrong covering.

Micah does the same:

> *"Therefore I will wail and howl, I will go stripped and **naked**."* — Mic 1:8

And Revelation brings the image into its final form:

> *"Blessed is he that watcheth, and **keepeth his garments**, lest he **walk naked**, and they see his **shame**."* — Rev 16:15

"Keepeth his garments" is the opposite of "walk naked." The garments must be actively maintained — this is not a one-time clothing but an ongoing posture. Walking naked is the failure to maintain covenant faithfulness. Compare with the parable of the wedding garment (Mat 22:11-13): the guest who appears without the proper garment is cast out.

### Cast Off Restraint = Made Naked

A remarkable Hebrew connection hides in plain sight. The word H6544 *pâraʻ* means "to loosen, expose, make naked." It appears in Ex 32:25 for the golden calf incident:

> *"And when Moses saw that the people were **naked**; (for Aaron had **made them naked** unto their shame among their enemies.)"* — Ex 32:25

The same word appears in Prov 29:18:

> *"Where there is no vision, the people **perish** (H6544)."*

Most translations obscure the connection. The Hebrew is identical: the people become *naked* / *unrestrained* / *cast off covering*. When there is no prophetic vision, the people strip off their covenant covering — they become naked. The golden calf episode is the enacted version: Aaron "made them naked" by removing the covenant restraint, and their enemies saw their shame.

Compare Prov 13:18: "Poverty and **shame** shall be to him that **refuseth** (H6544) instruction." To refuse Torah instruction is to become naked — to strip yourself of covenant covering.

### Judgment Strips the Garment

The prophetic books form a consistent pattern: when God judges a covenant-breaking nation, the judgment is described as stripping her naked.

> *"Thy **nakedness** shall be uncovered, yea, thy shame shall be seen."* — Isa 47:3 (Babylon)

> *"Lest I strip her **naked**, and set her as in the day that she was born."* — Hos 2:3 (Israel)

> *"I will discover thy skirts upon thy face, and I will shew the nations thy **nakedness**, and the kingdoms thy shame."* — Nah 3:5 (Nineveh)

> *"They shall... leave thee **naked** and bare: and the **nakedness** of thy whoredoms shall be discovered."* — Ezek 23:29 (Jerusalem)

Every one of these nations had a form of covering — national sovereignty, alliance, religious institution. When the covering is stripped, nakedness is exposed. In Ezekiel's extended allegory (ch. 16 and 23), the progression is explicit: God covered Jerusalem's nakedness (entered covenant), Jerusalem played the $harlot (broke covenant), and judgment returns her to nakedness.

Hos 2:9 makes the economics clear: "*I will recover my wool and my flax given to cover her nakedness.*" The covering materials — the covenant provisions — were God's to begin with. He gave them. When the covenant is broken, He takes them back.

### Laodicea — Rich but Naked

> *"Because thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and **naked**."* — Rev 3:17

Laodicea's condition mirrors Gen 3 in reverse. Adam and Eve *knew* they were naked. Laodicea does *not know*. This is the more dangerous state — unaware of exposure, unaware that the covenant garments are missing.

The remedy follows Ezek 16:8's pattern — the covering must come from God, not self:

> *"I counsel thee to buy of me $[gold] tried in the $[fire], that thou mayest be rich; and **white raiment**, that thou mayest be **clothed**, and that the shame of thy **nakedness** do not appear."* — Rev 3:18

White raiment from Christ replaces what human prosperity could not provide. Just as God replaced fig leaves with skins in the Garden, so Christ offers covenant garments to replace Laodicea's self-assessed adequacy.

### The $harlot Made Naked

The convergence of nakedness and harlotry reaches its climax in Rev 17:16:

> *"And the ten horns which thou sawest upon the beast, these shall hate the $[whore], and shall make her desolate and **naked**, and shall eat her flesh, and burn her with $[fire]."* — Rev 17:16

The $harlot — the covenant breaker — is made naked by the very powers she relied on. The covering she sought from alliances with the $beast is stripped away. The pattern from Ezek 16 and 23 repeats: the covenant was broken, the false coverings are removed, and nakedness is exposed.

## Patterns

1. **Covering = covenant, nakedness = without covenant** — this equation holds across every context: Garden, Leviticus, prophets, and Revelation.

2. **Human coverings fail; God's covering requires sacrifice** — fig leaves → replaced by skins (Gen 3:7,21). Self-assessed wealth → replaced by white raiment from Christ (Rev 3:17-18).

3. **Walking naked = conducting life outside the covenant** — the prophetic sign (Isa 20:2-4, Mic 1:8) and the eschatological warning (Rev 16:15) both describe a way of life, not a moment.

4. **Judgment is described as stripping** — Isa 47:3, Hos 2:3, Nah 3:5, Ezek 16:39, Ezek 23:29. The covering was on loan; when the covenant is broken, it is removed.

5. **Same root connects nakedness, subtlety, and unrestrained lawlessness** — H6191 links the serpent's craftiness (Gen 3:1) to the resulting nakedness (Gen 3:7). H6544 links being "made naked" (Ex 32:25) to "casting off restraint" (Prov 29:18).

6. **The skirt is the covenant** — spreading a skirt covers nakedness and enters covenant (Ezek 16:8, Ruth 3:9). Uncovering a skirt violates the covenant (Deut 27:20). Nations grasp the skirt to seek covenant (Zech 8:23).

7. **Covering the naked is the covenant command** — Isa 58:7, Ezek 18:7, Ezek 18:16, Mat 25:36-44. Extending covering to the exposed is extending covenant care.

## Connections

- **$harlot** — The harlot is the covenant breaker; her judgment is to be made naked (Ezek 16:39, Rev 17:16). The two symbols are intertwined: harlotry breaks the covenant, nakedness is the resulting condition.

- **$shadow** — The shadow is the covenant protection cast by a body standing between you and judgment ($shadow = covenant covering). Nakedness is the opposite — outside the shadow, fully exposed.

- **$fig-tree** — Adam and Eve sewed fig leaves to cover their nakedness (Gen 3:7) — the first failed attempt at human self-covering. When the fig tree is barren and cursed (Mat 21:19), the covering disappears.

- **$sackcloth** — Isaiah removes sackcloth to walk naked (Isa 20:2). Sackcloth is itself a garment of judgment; removing even *that* leaves the prophetic sign of total exposure — covenant stripped entirely.

- **$wings** — God's wings are covenant shelter (Ps 91:4, Ruth 2:12). The skirt (H3671 *kanaph* — also "wing") that covers nakedness in Ezek 16:8 uses the same word. Wings cover; nakedness is the absence of that covering.

- **$fire** — The $harlot is made naked and burned with fire (Rev 17:16). Nakedness exposes; fire consumes. The two are the sequential stages of judgment.

- **$wine** — "Woe unto him that giveth his neighbour drink... that thou mayest look on their **nakedness**!" (Hab 2:15). Wine intoxicates and strips away covering — the one who causes others to stumble into covenant exposure.

- **$darkness** — Nakedness is visibility of shame; $darkness is concealment. Job 26:6 — "Hell is naked before him, and destruction hath no covering." Even darkness cannot hide nakedness from God's sight.

## For Further Study

- **$harlot** — The covenant breaker whose judgment ends in nakedness. Traces the full arc of Ezek 16: covered → unfaithful → stripped. Understanding harlotry as covenant-breaking (not mere sexual immorality) transforms what nakedness as judgment means.

- **$shadow** — The positive counterpart: covenant protection cast by a body between you and destruction. Where nakedness is the absence of covering, shadow is the presence of it. Ps 91:1 paired with Rev 16:15 creates a complete picture.

- **$fig-tree** — From the first failed covering (Gen 3:7) to the cursed tree (Mat 21:19), the fig tree traces the story of human-made coverings that cannot replace what God provides.

- **$sackcloth** — The garment that *replaces* glory after judgment. When the covering is stripped (naked), what remains is either sackcloth (dark testimony of loss) or nothing at all. Isaiah removes sackcloth to walk naked (Isa 20:2) — even the garment of mourning is taken away.
