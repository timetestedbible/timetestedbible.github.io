# Sign

## Summary

**A prophecy given in advance — often a prophet's lived story — whose fulfillment testifies; not a wonder performed on demand.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Signs and Similitudes](/books/symbolic-language/signs-and-similitudes/).

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**A prophecy given in advance — often a prophet's lived story — whose fulfillment testifies; not a wonder performed on demand.**

### Literal Biblical Definition

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **sign** begins: “That by which anything is made known or represented; that which furnishes evidence; a mark; a token; an indication; a proof.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

A prophecy given in advance—often a prophet’s lived story—whose fulfillment testifies; not a wonder performed on demand.

> *“Therefore the Lord himself shall give you a sign; Behold, a virgin shall conceive, and bear a son, and shall call his name Immanuel.”* — Isaiah 7:14

> *“Thus Ezekiel is unto you a sign: according to all that he hath done shall ye do: and when this cometh, ye shall know that I am the Lord GOD.”* — Ezekiel 24:24

> *“For as Jonas was a sign unto the Ninevites, so shall also the Son of man be to this generation.”* — Luke 11:30

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **sign** begins:

> “by the ancients as indicating the will of some deity; a prodigy; an omen. (b) An event considered by the Jews as indicating the divine will, or as manifesting an interposition of the divine power for some special end; a miracle; a wonder. Through mighty signs and wonders, by the power of the Spirit of God. Rom. xv. 19. It shall come to pass, if they will not believe thee, neither hearken to the voice of the first sign, that they will believe the voice of the latter sign. Ex. iv. 8. (c) Something serving to indicate the existence, or preserve the memory, of a thing; a token; a memorial; a monument. What time the fire devoured two hundred and fifty men, and they became a sign. Num. xxvi. 10.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Evidence by Sense

The entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Core sense:** A prophecy given in advance — often a prophet's lived story — whose fulfillment testifies; not a wonder performed on demand.
2. **Defining witnesses:** Isa 7:14; Ezek 24:24; Luke 11:30
3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **a miraculous act pointing to God's power and Christ's identity.**

- Isaiah 7:14, Ezekiel 24:24, and Luke 11:30 support prophecy, persons, and lived events functioning as signs, but they do not establish that all signs must be advance prophecies fulfilled later or that miracles cannot be signs. The exclusivist boundary is especially difficult to derive from these citations alone given explicit biblical descriptions of miracles as signs elsewhere.

- One could argue for DIVERGENT: the book's common-view note explicitly names the consensus reading ('a miraculous act pointing to God's power and Christ's identity') and its definition seems to reject it ('not a wonder performed on demand'), which reads as a replacement of the consensus's central identification, not a mere boundary adjustment — since the Johannine miracle-signs are the consensus's paradigm case and appear excluded. Conversely, a MATCH argument could note that the book merely foregrounds a sense every consensus response already lists. The REFINED verdict sits between these: the qualifier 'on demand' suggests the book rejects sign-seeking wonders rather than all miraculous signs, preserving the shared category while contesting its center of gravity.

- Consensus already lists prophetic sign-acts and the sign of Jonah among common alternatives, so the book’s focus could be read as prioritization of one recognized sense rather than outright replacement of the polyvalent core.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Relationship to Other Symbols

No reciprocal opposite is assigned. Related images may share a scene or a consequence without becoming aliases.
