# Tyre

**$babylon's commercial face — the trade-network that corrupts covering into traffick, worship into commodity, and persons into merchandise.**

> *"By the multitude of thy **traffick** they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned: therefore I will cast thee as profane out of the mountain of God: and I will destroy thee, O covering cherub, from the midst of the $[fire] stones."* — Ezek 28:16

---

## The Key Insight

The king of Tyre was a covering cherub in Eden. His anointed function was to *protect* (H5526 *sakak* — to cover, to hedge, to shield). The same Hebrew root gives us *sukkah* — the booth of covenant shelter. This figure was built for worship: nine precious stones, pipes and tabrets prepared in him the day he was created (Ezek 28:13). He was placed upon the holy $mountain of God (Ezek 28:14).

Then a single word describes what went wrong:

> *"By the multitude of thy **traffick** (H7404 *rekullah*) they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned."* — Ezek 28:16

H7404 *rekullah* appears exactly four times in the entire Old Testament — all four in the Tyre oracles (Ezek 26:12, 28:5, 28:16, 28:18). It is *the* sin-word for Tyre. And it carries a devastating etymology: H7402 *rakal* means to travel for trading, but the same root produces H7400 *rakiyl* — *talebearer*. The merchant and the gossip share a verb. Trading and tale-bearing are the same motion: carrying something that belongs in one place and selling it somewhere else for personal gain.

The covering cherub became a trader. The protector became a profiteer. Worship became commerce. And this corruption didn't require an external invader — it generated its own destruction:

> *"I will bring forth a **$[fire]** from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee."* — Ezek 28:18

The $fire comes from *within*. Tyre's iniquity is its own fuel. This is not Sodom, struck from above by heaven-sent $fire. This is a system whose commerce-as-worship breeds violence that ignites from the inside.

Every detail of Tyre's oracles in Ezekiel 26–28 reappears, verse by verse, in Revelation 18. Tyre is not merely *like* $babylon's commercial system — Tyre *is* the template. To understand Revelation 18, you must first understand Ezekiel 27.

**Symbolizes:** The commercial system of $babylon — trade that commodifies everything, from temple materials to human souls

**Opposite:** Jerusalem — the worship/offering economy where goods serve the covenant rather than replacing it

**Defining verses:** Ezek 28:16, Ezek 27:3, Isa 23:8–17, Rev 18:11–19

**The surprise:** The Hebrew name Tyre (H6865 *Tsôr*) means "rock" — from H6696 *tsûr*, to confine or besiege. God is the $rock (H6697 *tsûr*); Tyre is the counterfeit rock rising from the $sea. A fortress built on the nations rather than on the covenant.

**Connected:** $babylon, $sea, $fire, $mountain, $earthquake, $harlot, $fornication, $thorns, $island, $sackcloth, $lead

## Tyre Across Scripture

### The Covering Cherub Who Became a Trader

Ezekiel 28:12–19 pulls back a curtain that no other passage does. Behind the human prince of Tyre (Ezek 28:1–10) stands a figure whose origin predates nations:

> *"Thou sealest up the sum, full of wisdom, and perfect in beauty. Thou hast been in **Eden** the garden of God; every precious stone was thy covering… the workmanship of thy tabrets and of thy pipes was prepared in thee in the day that thou wast created."* — Ezek 28:12–13

> *"Thou art the anointed cherub that **covereth** (H5526 *sakak*); and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy **$[mountain]** of God; thou hast walked up and down in the midst of the stones of $[fire]."* — Ezek 28:14

Nine stones. Musical instruments built into his very being. Set on the holy $mountain — the seat of God's ruling authority. His function: *sakak* — to cover, to protect, to hedge about. This is worship infrastructure. This is a guardian of holy space.

The fall is not dramatic rebellion. It is gradual corruption through a specific mechanism:

> *"By the multitude of thy merchandise (H7404 *rekullah*) they have filled the midst of thee with **violence**, and thou hast sinned."* — Ezek 28:16

> *"Thine heart was lifted up because of thy **beauty**, thou hast corrupted thy **wisdom** by reason of thy **brightness**."* — Ezek 28:17

The chain is precise: WISDOM → COMMERCE → RICHES → PRIDE (see also Ezek 28:4–5: "with thy wisdom and with thine understanding thou hast gotten thee riches… and thine heart is lifted up because of thy riches"). The same gifts that enabled covering/worship were redirected toward acquisition. Wisdom that should have served the sanctuary served the market instead.

The prince of Tyre declares the endpoint of this progression:

> *"I am a God, I sit in the seat of God, in the midst of the **$[sea]**s"* — Ezek 28:2

Compare with $babylon: "I am, and none else beside me" (Isa 47:8). Compare with Revelation's great city: "I sit a queen, and am no widow" (Rev 18:7). The self-deifying formula is identical across all three. Tyre, Babylon, and the eschatological commercial system share one voice.

### The Ship Made of All Nations

Ezekiel 27 contains the most extraordinary metaphor in the prophetic literature. Tyre is described as a $ship — a composite vessel built from every nation's resources:

> *"O thou that art situate at the entry of the **$[sea]**, which art a merchant of the people for many $[island]s… Thy builders have perfected thy beauty."* — Ezek 27:3–4

The ship's components read like a geopolitical map:

- Fir trees of Senir for planks (v5)
- Cedars of Lebanon for masts (v5)
- Oaks of Bashan for oars (v6)
- Ivory-inlaid boxwood from Chittim for benches (v6)
- Fine linen with embroidered work from Egypt for sails (v7)
- Blue and purple from Elishah for covering (v7)
- Zidon and Arvad for mariners (v8)
- Gebal's ancients for calkers (v9)
- Persia, Lud, and Phut for soldiers (v10)

Tyre builds nothing from its own soil. It draws from every nation, connects them, and extracts profit from the connections. It is the $sea's marketplace — positioned at the entry of the chaotic nations, trading among the $islands (the set-apart peoples), pulling everyone into its network.

Then comes the merchandise catalog (Ezek 27:12–25): silver, iron, tin, $lead from Tarshish. Slaves and bronze vessels from Javan, Tubal, Meshech. Horses and mules from Togarmah. Ivory and ebony from Dedan. Emeralds, purple, coral, agate from Syria. Wheat, honey, oil, balm from Judah and Israel (v17 — even the covenant people trade in Tyre's system). Spices, precious stones, gold from Sheba and Raamah. And finally, the last item on the list:

> *"Javan, Tubal, and Meshech… they traded the **persons of men**."* — Ezek 27:13

Human beings as commodity. Persons of men (*nephesh adam*) at the end of the catalog, after the spices and stones — as if the progression from luxury goods to human flesh were merely the next line item. This detail reappears at the climax of Revelation's commercial lament:

> *"And cinnamon, and odours, and ointments, and frankincense, and wine, and oil, and fine flour, and wheat, and beasts, and sheep, and horses, and chariots, and **slaves, and souls of men**."* — Rev 18:13

The same escalation. The same final item. The system that commodifies everything eventually commodifies everyone.

Every major element of Revelation's commercial judgment is already present in Ezekiel. The reader who studies Tyre holds the blueprint for the fall of $babylon.

### "Sing as a Harlot" — Commerce and Idolatry

Isaiah 23 adds a dimension that Ezekiel implies but does not name directly:

> *"Who hath taken this counsel against **Tyre**, the crowning city, whose **merchants** are princes, whose **traffickers** (H3667 *Kenaan*) are the honourable of the earth?"* — Isa 23:8

The word translated "traffickers" is H3667 *Kenaan* — *Canaan*. The same word names both the merchant-class and the land God commanded Israel to conquer. The conquest of Canaan was, at the symbolic level, the displacement of a merchant-system by a worship-system. Tyre is Canaan's commercial heir.

Isaiah then describes Tyre as a forgotten $harlot:

> *"Take an harp, go about the city, thou **harlot** that hast been forgotten; make sweet melody, sing many songs, that thou mayest be remembered."* — Isa 23:15

> *"And she shall turn to her hire, and shall commit **$[fornication]** with all the kingdoms of the world upon the face of the earth."* — Isa 23:17

Trade with all the kingdoms = $fornication. Commerce that replaces covenant loyalty with commercial alliance is idolatry — serving multiple masters, giving yourself to whichever buyer pays the highest price. The $harlot makes no distinction between partners. She goes with whoever enriches her. This is precisely $fornication: covenant-breaking through divided allegiance, except the "other gods" here are trade partners.

The seventy years of Tyre's desolation (Isa 23:15) match Babylon's seventy years (Jer 25:12). The two systems share a clock.

### The Anomaly — Commerce Redeemed

And then Isaiah says something that no other judgment oracle says:

> *"And her **merchandise** and her **hire** shall be **holiness to the LORD**: it shall not be treasured nor laid up; for her merchandise shall be for them that dwell before the LORD, to eat sufficiently, and for durable clothing."* — Isa 23:18

Commerce itself is not condemned. What is condemned is commerce-as-idol — the system that replaces worship with trade. When merchandise becomes "holiness to the LORD," when profit serves those who dwell before God rather than being "treasured" and "laid up," the corruption is reversed.

This is the only Tyre text that hints at redemption. And it is confirmed by two unexpected witnesses:

> *"The daughter of **Tyre** shall be there with a gift"* — Ps 45:12 (at the royal wedding)

> *"I will make mention of Rahab and Babylon to them that know me: behold Philistia, and **Tyre**, with Ethiopia; this man was born there."* — Ps 87:4 (Tyre counted among those born in Zion)

And by Jesus himself:

> *"It shall be more tolerable for **Tyre** and Sidon at the day of judgment, than for you."* — Mat 11:22

Tyre, if she had seen the miracles, would have repented in $sackcloth and ashes. The commercial system is *capable* of turning. The covering cherub's fall was not irreversible in the human instance — only in the cosmic one.

### The Alliance That Turned

The story begins with legitimate cooperation. Hiram king of Tyre sends cedar, carpenters, and masons to David (2 Sam 5:11). His craftsman — also named Hiram — fills Solomon's temple with bronze work wrought in wisdom (1 Ki 7:13–14), a Tyrian parallel to Bezalel whom the Spirit of God filled for the tabernacle (Exod 31:3). Every luxury material in Revelation 18's judgment catalog was originally deployed *for the temple*.

But the alliance pivots:

> *"And Solomon gave Hiram twenty cities in the land of Galilee."* — 1 Ki 9:11

Covenant territory traded for building materials. The economy begins driving the covenant rather than serving it. By Nehemiah's time, the corruption is complete:

> *"There dwelt men of **Tyre** also therein, which brought fish, and all manner of ware, and sold on the **sabbath** unto the children of Judah, and in **Jerusalem**."* — Neh 13:16

Tyre's merchants trade *in* Jerusalem *on* the Sabbath. Commerce has invaded worship-time and worship-space. The covering cherub is in the temple — not protecting it, but selling in it. Jesus overturning the moneychangers' tables (Mat 21:12) is the final iteration of this same pattern.

### Self-Consuming Fire

> *"Thou hast defiled thy sanctuaries by the multitude of thine iniquities, by the iniquity of thy **traffick** (H7404); therefore will I bring forth a **$[fire]** from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to **ashes** upon the earth."* — Ezek 28:18

The $fire that consumes Tyre originates *within*. This is the self-consuming pattern that defines $babylon's commercial face. The iniquity of traffick — the corruption of covering into commodity — becomes its own fuel. The system does not need an invader to destroy it. Its own violence, generated by its own merchandise (Ezek 28:16), ignites from the center outward.

Zechariah confirms with double judgment:

> *"And the Lord will cast her out, and he will smite her power in the **$[sea]**; and she shall be devoured with **$[fire]**."* — Zech 9:4

Smitten in the $sea (chaotic nations) and devoured by $fire (God's consuming standard) — both judgment images converging on a single city.

Amos adds the covenant-breaking dimension:

> *"For three transgressions of **Tyre**, and for four, I will not turn away the punishment thereof; because they delivered up the whole captivity to Edom, and remembered not the **brotherly covenant**."* — Amos 1:9

The specific sin: slave trade that violated a *brotherly covenant*. Commerce overriding covenant relationship. Persons of men delivered for profit. The traffick pattern again — but now with "brotherly covenant" named as what was broken.

## Patterns

1. **The covering-to-traffick corruption.** H5526 *sakak* (cover, protect) → H7404 *rekullah* (traffick). The anointed function was worship and guardianship. Commerce redirected that function toward profit. The same hands that covered the holy place now sell what was in it.

2. **The composite parasite.** Tyre produces nothing from its own land. It draws from every nation (Ezek 27:4–11), connects them commercially, and extracts value from the connections. Acts 12:20 confirms the pattern: Tyre was "nourished by the king's country" — it cannot feed itself. The commercial network depends on the productive world but contributes only the network itself.

3. **The self-deifying formula.** "I am a God, I sit in the seat of God" (Ezek 28:2) = "I am, and none else beside me" ($babylon, Isa 47:8) = "I sit a queen, and am no widow" (Rev 18:7). The commercial system claims divine status — total self-sufficiency, ultimate authority, immunity from judgment.

4. **The commodity escalation.** Every Tyre catalog ends with human beings: "persons of men" (Ezek 27:13), "slaves and souls of men" (Rev 18:13). The system that commodifies goods eventually commodifies persons. Human trafficking is not an aberration of the system — it is the system's logical terminus.

5. **The self-consuming $fire.** Ezek 28:18 — $fire from within. Not heaven-sent judgment as at Sodom, but the system's own iniquity becoming fuel. The $wickedness generated by the commercial system returns upon it as its own destruction (compare Zech 5:5–11, where $wickedness is carried *back* to Shinar/$babylon, sealed under $lead).

6. **The terminal declaration.** "Thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more" (Ezek 27:36). "Thou shalt be no more; though thou be sought for, yet shalt thou never be found again" (Ezek 26:21). Commerce-as-idol has no afterlife. This echoes directly in Rev 18:21: "shall be found no more at all."

7. **Commerce sanctified is the exception.** Isa 23:18 — merchandise becomes holiness to the LORD. Ps 45:12 — Tyre brings a gift to the wedding. Ps 87:4 — Tyre counted among Zion's citizens. Mat 11:21–22 — Tyre would have repented. The system is redeemable when it serves covenant rather than replacing it. The Syro-Phoenician woman found faith at Tyre's borders (Mat 15:21, Mk 7:24).

## Connections

- **$babylon** — Tyre is $babylon's commercial face. Where $babylon is the full substitute system for God's covenant rule, Tyre is specifically its trade-network — the merchants, the ships, the luxury catalogs. Ezekiel's Tyre oracles provide the template that Revelation 18 deploys at global scale. They share the self-deifying formula, the self-consuming $fire, and the "never any more" verdict.

- **$sea** — Tyre sits "at the entry of the sea" (Ezek 27:3) — positioned at the gateway of the chaotic nations. The prince claims to sit "in the seat of God, in the midst of the seas" (Ezek 28:2). Tyre is the $sea's marketplace: rising from the nations, drawing from the nations, smitten in the $sea (Zech 9:4), and cast into the $sea (Ezek 27:34, Rev 18:21).

- **$fire** — God's covenant standard in active contact. For Tyre, the $fire comes from within (Ezek 28:18) — the iniquity of traffick is its own fuel. The same standard that refines $gold also consumes the system built on commerce-as-idol. Zech 9:4 adds external $fire. The dual-origin (internal + divine) marks Tyre's judgment as both self-inflicted and divinely sanctioned.

- **$mountain** — The covering cherub was set "upon the holy mountain of God" (Ezek 28:14). Tyre's fall is a fall from the seat of divine authority — cast as profane *from* the $mountain. The commercial system occupied a $mountain-position (ruling authority) that belonged to worship.

- **$earthquake** — At Tyre's fall, the $islands shake (Ezek 26:15, 18), the $sea's coasts tremble, the mariners cry out (Ezek 27:28–29). H7493 *ra'ash* describes the upheaval (Ezek 27:28). The commercial network's collapse shakes every set-apart people ($island) and kingdom ($mountain) connected to it — systemic destabilization.

- **$harlot** — "Sing as a harlot" (Isa 23:15–16). Tyre's trade with all kingdoms = $fornication (Isa 23:17). The $harlot gives herself to whatever master pays; the commercial system allies with whatever kingdom enriches. Divided loyalty for profit is the economic form of covenant-breaking.

- **$fornication** — Commerce with all kingdoms "upon the face of the earth" (Isa 23:17) is explicitly named $fornication. This is not metaphor for sexual sin but for the idolatry of serving multiple masters through commercial alliance — the act of breaking covenant loyalty for trade advantage.

- **$thorns** — Ezek 28:24 prophesies no more "pricking brier" or "grieving thorn" to the house of Israel from surrounding peoples. $thorns = worldly people whose cares and riches choke covenant faithfulness. Tyre's merchants — whose wealth and trade choke out worship — are the commercial expression of the $thorns in Jesus' parable.

- **$island** — The $islands (set-apart peoples) are shaken at Tyre's fall (Ezek 26:15, 18, 27:35). Tyre trades with "many isles" (Ezek 27:3) — the commercial system draws even sanctified peoples into its network. When it collapses, they tremble.

- **$sackcloth** — The mariners and princes wear $sackcloth at Tyre's fall (Ezek 27:31), and Jesus says Tyre *would have* repented in $sackcloth and ashes (Mat 11:21). $sackcloth = visible testimony of judgment — the commercial system's collapse produces the dark garment that replaces glory.

- **$lead** — Tarshish trades $lead among base metals in Tyre's marketplace (Ezek 27:12). $lead = the suppression-weight, the densest earthly commodity, bottom of the metal hierarchy. Its presence in the catalog represents the most base and earthly of Tyre's traffic — and connects to Zech 5:7–8, where $lead seals $wickedness for transport to $babylon.
