# Tree

**A nation or people group — a collective body rooted in its origin, sheltering those within its borders, and known by the $fruit it produces.**

> *"For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant."* — Isa 5:7

---

## The Key Insight

When Nebuchadnezzar dreams of a great tree, Daniel does not guess at the meaning. He states it:

> *"The **tree** that thou sawest, which grew, and was strong, whose height reached unto the heaven, and the sight thereof to all the earth… **It is thou, O king**, that art grown and become strong."* — Dan 4:20–22

The tree is the king and his kingdom — Babylon as a collective national entity. The beasts sheltering under it are the peoples and tribes dwelling within the empire's borders. The birds in its branches are the nations finding shade in its reach. When the tree is hewn down, the kingdom falls.

This is not a one-time metaphor. It is how Scripture consistently uses trees. Ezekiel calls Assyria a cedar (Ezek 31:3). Hosea calls Israel a vine (Hos 10:1). Paul calls the covenant community an olive tree (Rom 11:17–24). Isaiah calls the house of Israel a vineyard (Isa 5:7). The type of tree varies — the symbolic function does not. A tree is a nation.

What makes this symbol especially rich is its *anatomy*. A tree is not a simple image. It has roots, a trunk, branches, leaves, and $fruit — and Scripture assigns meaning to each component. The roots are the patriarchal foundation. The branches are the members. The $fruit is the nation's works. The $shadow is its shelter. And a tree can be *cut down* — which is what happens to nations under judgment.

**Symbolizes:** A nation or people group — the collective body in its organic, living structure

**Opposite:** A cut-down tree or dry tree — a judged nation, stripped of life and shelter

**Defining verses:** Dan 4:20–22, Isa 5:7, Ezek 31:3–18, Rom 11:17–24, Hos 10:1

**The surprise:** The same image that represents Babylon's empire (Dan 4) also represents God's covenant people (Rom 11). Tree is not coded to good or evil — it is the *form* a collective people takes. What distinguishes one tree from another is its $fruit.

**Connected:** $mountain, $sea, $animal, $fruit, $thorns, $babylon, $grass

## Tree Across Scripture

### The Anatomy of a Nation

Romans 11 gives the most detailed anatomy of the tree-symbol anywhere in Scripture. Paul describes the covenant community as a single olive tree — and then names its parts:

> *"For if the firstfruit be holy, the lump is also holy: and if the root be holy, so are the branches."* — Rom 11:16

> *"And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive **tree**, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive **tree**; Boast not against the branches."* — Rom 11:17–18

The **root** is the patriarchal origin — the Abrahamic covenant that makes the whole structure holy. The **branches** are the members, the people who belong to the nation. Natural branches (native Israel) can be *broken off* through unbelief. Wild branches (Gentiles) can be *grafted in* through faith. The tree itself — the covenant community as a collective structure — persists through the exchange.

> *"Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in. Well; because of unbelief they were broken off, and thou standest by faith. Be not highminded, but fear: For if God spared not the natural branches, take heed lest he also spare not thee."* — Rom 11:19–21

The warning only works if the tree is the nation and the branches are its people. Individual members can be removed; new ones can be added. The organic structure survives the surgery. Paul even names the mechanism of restoration:

> *"And they also, if they abide not still in unbelief, shall be graffed in: for God is able to graff them in again."* — Rom 11:23

A branch broken off can be re-attached. A member removed from the nation can be restored. The tree — the covenant people group — is living, dynamic, capable of reorganization while retaining its identity through the root.

### The Great Tree — Empire on Display

Daniel 4 and Ezekiel 31 both use the image of a great tree to depict a dominant empire, and both emphasize the same details: overwhelming height, far-reaching shelter, and the subordinate peoples dwelling within.

> *"The **tree** grew, and was strong, and the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth: The leaves thereof were fair, and the **$[fruit]** thereof much, and in it was meat for all: the **$[animal]**s of the field had shadow under it, and the fowls of the heaven dwelt in the boughs thereof, and all flesh was fed of it."* — Dan 4:11–12

The tree's height = the empire's reach. Its $fruit = the empire's productivity and works. The $animal dwelling under it = the peoples and tribes sheltering within its domain. "All flesh was fed of it" — the empire sustains its subject nations. The tree is the entire national structure as a living, sheltering organism.

Ezekiel draws the same picture with Assyria:

> *"Behold, the Assyrian was a cedar in Lebanon with fair branches, and with a shadowing shroud, and of an high stature… All the fowls of heaven made their nests in his boughs, and under his branches did all the **$[animal]**s of the field bring forth their young, and under his **$[shadow]** dwelt all great nations."* — Ezek 31:3, 6

Assyria the cedar. Egypt under Pharaoh is addressed in the same chapter: "To whom art thou thus like in glory and in greatness among the **trees** of Eden?" (Ezek 31:18). The "trees of Eden" are the nations of the world — and Pharaoh's empire is compared to them as one tree among many, competing for height and glory. Ezekiel treats the international order as a *forest* of nations.

The critical detail: both Babylon's tree and Assyria's cedar are *cut down*.

> *"Hew the **tree** down, and destroy it; yet leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth."* — Dan 4:23

A cut-down tree is a judged nation. The stump suggests the possibility of regrowth — Nebuchadnezzar's kingdom is restored after his period of humiliation. But the act of felling is unmistakable: when God judges a nation, He cuts the tree.

### Known by Its Fruit

> *"Even so every good **tree** bringeth forth good **$[fruit]**; but a corrupt **tree** bringeth forth evil **$[fruit]**. A good **tree** cannot bring forth evil **$[fruit]**, neither can a corrupt **tree** bring forth good **$[fruit]**. Every **tree** that bringeth not forth good **$[fruit]** is hewn down, and cast into the $[fire]."* — Mat 7:17–19

If the tree is a nation or people group, and $fruit is its works and deeds, then Jesus' teaching becomes a statement about how nations are evaluated: by what they produce. A righteous nation produces righteous works. A corrupt nation produces corrupt works. And the tree that bears no good $fruit is *hewn down* — the same language Daniel uses for Babylon's judgment.

The vine imagery in John 15 deepens this:

> *"I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much **$[fruit]**: for without me ye can do nothing."* — Jn 15:5

Jesus is the vine — the living national structure — and the disciples are the branches (the members). The language of abiding, bearing $fruit, and being cast out for fruitlessness mirrors the olive tree of Romans 11. Branches that do not bear $fruit are "taken away" (Jn 15:2), just as natural branches are "broken off" (Rom 11:17).

Hosea adds the negative case:

> *"Israel is an empty vine, he bringeth forth **$[fruit]** unto himself."* — Hos 10:1

Israel as a vine (= tree = nation) that bears $fruit — but produces it *for itself*. The works are self-serving. The nation has the living structure but misdirects its output. The tree is fruitful and barren at the same time: productive in form, empty in covenant purpose.

### The Forest and the Field — Trees Among Trees

Once the equation is established, a pattern emerges throughout the prophets: trees appear in clusters, and the clusters represent international relations.

> *"All the **trees** of the field shall know that I the LORD have brought down the high **tree**, have exalted the low **tree**, have dried up the green **tree**, and have made the dry **tree** to flourish."* — Ezek 17:24

"All the trees of the field" = all the nations of the world. The high tree brought low = the arrogant empire humbled. The low tree exalted = the despised nation lifted up. The green tree dried = the flourishing nation judged. The dry tree made to flourish = the withered nation restored. God's sovereignty over nations is expressed as a gardener's authority over trees.

Jesus picks up this language directly:

> *"For if they do these things in a green **tree**, what shall be done in the dry?"* — Lk 23:31

If this is done to the flourishing, living tree (to a righteous one, to a nation still bearing life), what will happen to the dry tree — the nation already dead, already ready for the fire?

The green tree / dry tree contrast runs throughout prophetic imagery. Isaiah connects it to the individual within the nation:

> *"Neither let the son of the stranger, that hath joined himself to the LORD, speak, saying, The LORD hath utterly separated me from his people: neither let the eunuch say, Behold, I am a dry **tree**."* — Isa 56:3

Even the eunuch — barren, apparently unable to produce $fruit — should not call himself a "dry tree." God can graft in and make fruitful what seems dead. The same logic Paul will apply to Gentile branches grafted into the olive tree.

### Trees Grow on Mountains

The positional relationship between trees and $mountain is consistent and illuminating. Trees grow *on* mountains. Nations exist *under* kingdoms and ruling authorities.

> *"Upon a lofty and high **$[mountain]** hast thou set thy bed."* — Isa 57:7

> *"In the **$[mountain]** of the height of Israel will I plant it: and it shall bring forth boughs, and bear **$[fruit]**, and be a goodly cedar: and under it shall dwell all fowl of every wing."* — Ezek 17:23

God plants His tree (His nation) on His $mountain (His kingdom). The tree grows within the kingdom's jurisdiction, sheltered by its authority. The $mountain is the governing structure; the tree is the organic, living community that grows within it. When Babylon is called both a "destroying $mountain" (Jer 51:25) and a great tree (Dan 4), the two symbols occupy different registers: $mountain = Babylon's governmental authority; tree = Babylon's collective national body.

## Patterns

1. **Tree = nation, consistently.** Five explicit identifications across different authors and centuries: Isa 5:7 (Israel = vineyard), Dan 4:22 (Babylon = tree), Ezek 31:3 (Assyria = cedar), Hos 10:1 (Israel = vine), Rom 11:17–24 (Israel = olive tree). The convergence is remarkable.

2. **The anatomy resolves.** Root = foundation/origin (patriarchs). Branches = members/people. $fruit = works/deeds. Leaves = outward appearance. Shadow = shelter/protection. Every component maps to a function within a national body.

3. **Size indicates scale.** A great cedar reaching to heaven = a world empire. A vine in a vineyard = a smaller covenant community. The type and stature of the tree correspond to the scale and character of the nation.

4. **Tree species = national character.** Olive tree = covenant faithfulness (Rom 11). Vine = covenant fruitfulness (Isa 5, Jn 15). Cedar = imperial grandeur (Ezek 31). Fig tree = covenant standing (Mk 11:13–21). The specific tree reveals something about the specific nation's identity.

5. **Cut down = judged.** Dan 4:23, Mat 7:19, Lk 3:9 ("the axe is laid unto the root of the trees"). Judgment on a nation is consistently expressed as felling a tree.

6. **Trees stand between $mountain and $grass.** $mountain = kingdom/ruling authority (the governing structure). Tree = nation/people group (the collective body). $grass = individual mortal people (the common person). The hierarchy is spatial: $grass grows at the base, trees rise from the soil, $mountain towers above all.

## Connections

- **$mountain** — Kingdom / ruling authority. Trees grow *on* mountains; nations exist *under* governing powers. God plants His tree on His $mountain (Ezek 17:23). The tree is the organic community; the $mountain is the seat of power that oversees it.

- **$sea** — Nations as chaotic, unsanctified peoples. The $sea represents peoples in their disordered, turbulent state (Rev 17:15). The tree represents peoples in their *ordered*, rooted, cultivated state. Beasts rise from the $sea (Dan 7:3); beasts shelter under the tree (Dan 4:12). One is chaos; the other is structure.

- **$animal** — People / tribe / sub-state. Animals dwell *under* the tree (Dan 4:12, 21). When $animal and tree co-occur, the spatial relationship resolves their overlap: the tree is the dominant national structure; the $animal is the subordinate people group characterized by its nature within that structure.

- **$fruit** — Works / deeds. The visible output of the tree — what the nation produces. A good tree bears good $fruit; a corrupt tree bears evil $fruit (Mat 7:17). The $fruit is how the tree is evaluated and why it is either preserved or cut down.

- **$thorns** — Worldly, choking people. $thorns are a type of plant — a kind of tree — representing people characterized by "cares, riches, and pleasures of this life" (Lk 8:14). They are the anti-tree: instead of sheltering and bearing good $fruit, they choke out fruitfulness in others. The cursed tree (Gen 3:18) versus the blessed one.

- **$babylon** — Worldly kingdom, man's substitute system. Nebuchadnezzar's $babylon is explicitly identified as the great tree in Dan 4:20–22. The empire that shelters all nations is also the empire that gets cut down. $babylon is what happens when a tree reaches for heaven on its own terms (compare Gen 11:4).

- **$grass** — People / mortal people. If the tree is the collective nation, $grass is the individual mortal person within it. "All flesh is $grass" (Isa 40:6). When Nebuchadnezzar's tree is cut down, the king is reduced to eating $grass like an ox (Dan 4:33) — the great national structure collapses to the level of common, mortal humanity.
