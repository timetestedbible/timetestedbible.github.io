# Blind

## Summary

**The man whose lamp has gone dark: the [eye](/research/symbols/eye/) is the lamp of the body, the commandment is the lamp's light, and the blind man is the one who can no longer find the way — a condition morally caused (bribes, trusted idols, hatred), judicially sealed on the refuser, worn as a title by false guides, terminal in those who say "we see," and healed only by the LORD as the Messiah's own sign.**

The notebook trail is in [research-blind.md](https://github.com/timetestedbible/timetestedbible.github.io/blob/main/books/symbolic-language/research/research-blind.md).

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**The man whose lamp has gone dark — unable to find the way.** The sections below derive it; the definition is proposed for the glossary and awaits approval.

### Literal Biblical Definition

Sightlessness of the bodily eyes: protected by the law's mercy commands (Lev 19:14), disqualifying priest and sacrifice alike as a blemish (Lev 21:18; Mal 1:8), healed throughout the Gospels. The literal cases carry the figure rather than compete with it — every healing is enacted doctrine.

## Corpus and Method

The sweep is Strong's-complete, not English-form-only: Hebrew *ivver* (H5787) appears in 23 verses, the verb *avar* "to blind" (H5786) in 5, *ivvaron/avveret* "blindness" (H5788) in 3, and *sanverim* — the striking-blindness of Sodom and Dothan (H5575) — in 2; Greek *typhlos* (G5185) in 48 verses and the verb *typhloō* (G5186) in 3 (John 12:40; 2 Cor 4:4; 1 John 2:11). All counts verified against kjv_strongs.txt. The Hebrew Gospels render *typhlos* back as *ivver* at every checked site (Matt 15:14 ×4; 23:16, 24) — one lexeme across both testaments. Adjacent structural texts that carry the figure without the lexeme are used as support and flagged as such (Isa 6:10 "shut their eyes"; Isa 29:10; Ps 69:23); note that the KJV's "blinded/blindness" in Romans 11:7, 25 translates *pōroō/pōrōsis* — hardening — and is cited here only as the same doctrine, not the same word.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- **"Blindness is simple ignorance — a neutral lack of information."** The corpus refuses the neutrality: Scripture names moral causes (bribes, Ex 23:8; trusted idols, Ps 115:8; hatred, 1 John 2:11) and a moral verdict — "they shall walk like blind men, *because they have sinned*" (Zeph 1:17). The blind of Scripture had the light and lost the lamp.
- **"Blindness is innate inability — some are simply made unable to see."** The sealing texts always follow refusal (Isa 6:9–10 comes to a people already not hearing; 2 Cor 4:4 blinds "them which believe not"), and the condition carries time limits and exits — "for a season" (Acts 13:11), "until the fulness of the Gentiles" (Rom 11:25), healed at confession (John 9:38, 41). A fixed fate needs no Healer; this condition has one.
- **"Blind is merely the physical disability."** Then the covenant curse (Deut 28:28–29), the blind watchmen (Isa 56:10), the blind guides (Matt 15:14; 23:16–26), Laodicea (Rev 3:17), and "ye say, We see" (John 9:41) are all unintelligible — every one applies the word to men with working retinas.

## The Claim

When Scripture calls a man blind, it is rarely describing his retinas. The claim of this study: **the blind man is the man whose lamp has gone dark** — and Scripture supplies every link of that chain in its own words. The eye is the *lamp* of the body (Matthew 6:22 — the KJV's "light" is *lychnos*, a lamp, and the Hebrew text reads *ner*); the commandment *is* the lamp and the law the light (Proverbs 6:23; Psalm 119:105); and the lamp's whole work is the **way** — "a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path." Kill the lamp and the way goes dark: "if the light that is in you grows dark," the Hebrew Matthew reads, "**all your ways will be dark**" (Matthew 6:23).

## The Covenant Curse Decodes It

The curse of Deuteronomy names the condition and then films it in motion:

> "The LORD shall smite thee with madness, and **blindness**, and astonishment of heart: And thou shalt **grope at noonday**, as the blind gropeth in darkness, and thou shalt **not prosper in thy ways**." — Deuteronomy 28:28–29

Blindness, then groping at the hour with two lights overhead, then ways that fail — the exact anatomy of the dead lamp. Isaiah puts the same words in Israel's mouth as confession: "we grope for the wall like the blind… we stumble at noonday as in the night" (Isaiah 59:10), and Zephaniah states the cause: "they shall walk like blind men, **because they have sinned** against the LORD" (Zephaniah 1:17).

## What Blinds a Man

The causes Scripture names are moral, not optical.

- **Bribes.** The verb *avar*, "to blind," has exactly five uses in Scripture: three for the putting out of King Zedekiah's eyes, and these two — "the gift **blindeth** the wise" (Exodus 23:8); "a gift doth **blind** the eyes of the wise" (Deuteronomy 16:19). Loving gain darkens the judging eye.
- **Trusting sightless gods.** Idols "have eyes, but they see not… **they that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them**" (Psalm 115:5–8; 135:16–18). A man becomes what he trusts; trust a blind god, inherit its eyes. Of the idol-makers: "he hath shut their eyes" (Isaiah 44:18).
- **Hatred.** "He that hateth his brother… walketh in darkness, and knoweth not whither he goeth, because that darkness hath **blinded his eyes**" (1 John 2:11).
- **The god of this world** — "hath blinded the minds of **them which believe not**" (2 Corinthians 4:4): the blinding lands on a prior refusal.

## The Sealing

Isaiah 6:9–10 is the charter — "shut their eyes; **lest** they see with their eyes… and be healed" — and it is the Old Testament text the New Testament reaches for most widely: Matthew 13:14–15, John 12:40 ("he hath **blinded** their eyes"), Acts 28:26–27. The judgment follows the refusal; the sealing is of a choice already made. Isaiah 29 gives the result its image: a "spirit of deep sleep," closed eyes, and the whole vision become "a book that is sealed."

History then staged the doctrine at the kingdom's fall: the last thing Zedekiah saw was his sons slain; then Babylon "**put out the eyes** of the king of Judah" and led him away in chains (2 Kings 25:7). Judah went into exile behind a blinded head.

## Blind Guides

- "His **watchmen are blind**: they are all ignorant… sleeping, lying down, loving to slumber… they all look to their own way, every one for **his gain**" (Isaiah 56:10–11) — the bribe-mechanism seated in the watchtower.
- "They have wandered as blind men in the streets" — said of "her prophets and her priests, that have shed the blood of the just" (Lamentations 4:13–14).
- "Let them alone: they be **blind leaders of the blind**. And if the blind lead the blind, both shall fall into the ditch" (Matthew 15:14). The five woes of Matthew 23 hammer the one title — *blind guides, blind fools, blind Pharisee* — and the Hebrew text of 23:16 reads "**the council of the blind**": the blind occupying the seat.
- Torah embedded the blind-guide curse in advance: "**Cursed be he that maketh the blind to wander out of the way**" (Deuteronomy 27:18).

## The Terminal Case: "We See"

> "For judgment I am come into this world, that they which see not might see; and that they which see might be made blind… **If ye were blind, ye should have no sin: but now ye say, We see; therefore your sin remaineth**." — John 9:39–41

Laodicea "knowest not that thou art… blind" (Revelation 3:17); the fruitless believer "is blind… and **hath forgotten that he was purged**" (2 Peter 1:9). Claimed sight seals the condition. Confessed blindness is the one form the Healer can work with.

## The Sign of the Messiah

Opening blind eyes is the LORD's signature — "who maketh the seeing, or the blind? have not I the LORD?" (Exodus 4:11); "The LORD **openeth the eyes of the blind**" (Psalm 146:8) — and the restoration texts make it the mark of the day of healing: "the eyes of the blind shall see **out of obscurity**" (Isaiah 29:18 — *choshek*, out of the darkness); "Then the eyes of the blind shall be opened" (Isaiah 35:5); the Servant is sent "to open the blind eyes" and to "bring the blind by a way that they knew not" (Isaiah 42:7, 16). Jesus claims the office in those words (Luke 4:18; Matthew 11:5) and stages its meaning:

- **John 9** — clay from the ground, then "wash in the pool of Siloam, which is by interpretation, **Sent**": sight comes by obeying the Sent One, and the man's confession grows as his sight does — a man… a prophet… of God… "Lord, I believe."
- **Matthew 21:14** — "the blind and the lame came to him **in the temple**; and he healed them": the deliberate reversal of the Jebusite taunt that "the blind and the lame shall not come into the house" (2 Samuel 5:6–8).
- **Mark 8:22–25** — the two-stage healing ("I see men as trees, walking"): partial sight is a real, diagnosable state.
- **2 Kings 6:18–20** — the enemy army struck blind, led by the prophet into Samaria, and there given back its eyes — at a feast instead of a slaughter: blindness as leading-into-mercy.
- **Acts 13:11** — Elymas struck blind "**for a season**," groping for a hand: the adversary's blindness given a time limit, the same shape as "blindness in part… **until** the fulness of the Gentiles" (Romans 11:25).
- **Genesis 19:11** — Sodom's men, struck with blindness, "wearied themselves to find **the door**": the groping of the blind aimed at the one exit they refused.

## Counter-Texts Weighed

Literal blindness is real throughout, and the law guards the literal blind man (Leviticus 19:14) — the literal cases carry the sign rather than compete with it. The hard text is Isaiah 42:19 — "Who is blind, but my servant?… who is blind as he that is perfect?" — the Servant wearing the nation's blindness in the very oracle that sends him to open blind eyes; read within the range, the blind servant is the covenant nation failing its watchman office ("the blind people **that have eyes**," Isaiah 43:8), and the Healer takes the title to carry it away. No occurrence uses blindness positively; none requires a second sense.

## Relationship to Other Symbols

[Eye](/research/symbols/eye/) — the lamp of the body; the blind man's lamp is out. **Lamp** — the commandment, the light of the way. **Way** — what the lamp lights and the blind man loses ("maketh the blind to wander out of the way"). **Watchman** — the office blindness disqualifies. **Sleep** — the sealing's other name ("the spirit of deep sleep"). **Door** — what the blinded grope for and cannot find.

## Conclusion

Scripture's blind man is not short of light; he is short of a working lamp. The eye is the lamp of the body, the commandment is the light it burns, and every class of the word's 84 occurrences keeps that grammar: bribes and trusted idols and hatred put the lamp out; God seals the refuser's darkness "lest they see… and be healed"; blind guides seat the condition in the watchtower; the terminal case insists "we see"; and the opening of blind eyes is reserved to the LORD as the Messiah's identifying sign — given to those who confess the dark, withheld from those who claim the light. The covenant curse binds the whole study to one picture: blindness, then groping at noonday, then ways that do not prosper — the dead lamp, filmed in motion.
