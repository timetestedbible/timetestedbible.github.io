# Church / Ekklesia

**A called-out assembly. The Greek word translated "church" (G1577 *ekklesia*) IS the word the LXX uses for Israel's congregation (H6951 *qahal*). The text uses the same word from Sinai to Revelation without announcing a new entity.**

> *"This is he that was in the **ekklesia** in the wilderness with the angel which spake to him in mount Sina, and with our fathers."* — Acts 7:38

---

## The Key Insight

The KJV translates G1577 *ekklesia* as "church" 111 times and "assembly" 3 times. The English word "church" creates the impression of a new institution. But the Greek word simply means "called-out gathering" — from G1537 *ek* (out of) + G2564 *kaleo* (to call).

The defining verse is Acts 7:38. Stephen, in his defense before the Sanhedrin, calls $israel at Sinai "the **ekklesia** in the wilderness." Same G1577 that Paul uses for the communities in Corinth, Ephesus, and Rome. The word was not invented for the NT — it was already in use for Israel's assembly, and had been for centuries through the Septuagint's rendering of H6951 *qahal*.

The chain is traceable: Ps 22:22 — "in the midst of the **qahal** (H6951) will I praise thee." Heb 2:12 quotes this verse as: "in the midst of the **ekklesia** (G1577) will I sing praise." The "church" in Hebrews IS the "congregation" of the Psalms. Same word, quoted directly.

**Symbolizes:** The called-out assembly of God's covenant people

**Opposite:** Scattered / dispersed — the un-assembled state

**Connected:** $israel, $name, $rock, $trumpet, $firstborn

## Ekklesia Across Scripture

### Qahal — From the Patriarchs Through Sinai

The qahal concept appears before the law:

- Gen 28:3 — Isaac to $[jacob]: "be fruitful, that thou mayest be a **qahal** of people"
- Gen 35:11 — God to Jacob: "a nation and a **qahal** of nations shall be of thee"
- Gen 48:4 — Jacob recounts: "I will make of thee a **qahal** of people" — in the $ephraim blessing context

At Sinai, the qahal acts corporately at the founding event:

- Exo 12:6 — "the whole **qahal** of the *edah* of Israel shall kill it" — first Passover
- Deut 5:22 — "These words the LORD spake unto all your **qahal** in the mount"
- Deut 9:10 — "the day of the **qahal**" — Sinai is called "the day of the assembly"

The qahal is convoked by $trumpet (Num 10:7) — the same pattern as 1 Th 4:16.

### "My Ekklesia" = "Qahal of the LORD"

Jesus uses *ekklesia* twice:

> *"Upon this $[rock] I will build **my ekklesia**; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it."* — Mat 16:18

"My ekklesia" mirrors "qahal of the LORD" (Num 16:3) — possessive, the assembly belongs to God/Christ. His Jewish audience would hear the established term for God's people assembled. The word is not explained as new — it's assumed to be understood.

Mat 18:17 — "tell it unto the **ekklesia**" — dispute resolution function, same as the qahal in OT judicial contexts (Num 35:12,24-25).

### Acts 7:38 — The Bridge Verse

Stephen calls Israel at Sinai "the **ekklesia** in the wilderness." This is the most direct evidence that the NT word for "church" and the OT word for Israel's congregation are the same entity described with the same word. The text does not mark a break between the Sinai assembly and the later believing communities — the same G1577 covers both.

### The KJV Translation Creates the Divide

In Acts 19, the same G1577 appears three times for a pagan civic assembly at Ephesus:

- Acts 19:32 — "the **ekklesia** was confused" — KJV: "assembly"
- Acts 19:39 — "determined in a lawful **ekklesia**" — KJV: "assembly"
- Acts 19:41 — "he dismissed the **ekklesia**" — KJV: "assembly"

Same word. But when it refers to believers, the KJV translates it "church." The Greek makes no such distinction — the word means "called-out gathering" in every context. The English translation creates an institutional category the original does not.

### One Law, Stranger Included

The qahal operates under one law for native and stranger:

> *"One ordinance shall be both for you of the **qahal**, and also for the stranger that sojourneth with you."* — Num 15:15

The same inclusion pattern as Exo 12:48 (stranger joins $israel through covenant). The assembly was never genetically exclusive. See the $israel study for the full inclusion evidence from Torah to NT.

### Body of Christ

Paul develops the body metaphor:

- Eph 5:23-32 — Christ = head of the body, the ekklesia; husband-wife metaphor
- Col 1:18 — "head of the body, the ekklesia: the beginning, the $[firstborn] from the dead"
- 1 Tim 3:15 — "the ekklesia of the living God, the pillar and ground of the $[truth]"
- Heb 12:23 — "the ekklesia of the $[firstborn], which are written in heaven"

The assembly is described as a body with Christ as head, populated by firstborn-enrolled ones, connected to the heavenly Jerusalem (Heb 12:22).

## What the Text Says — and Doesn't Say

The word-data presents a clear chain: H6951 *qahal* → LXX *ekklesia* → G1577 *ekklesia*. Acts 7:38 explicitly confirms the link. Ps 22:22 → Heb 2:12 demonstrates the direct quotation chain.

Using an established word without redefining it normally means the established meaning holds. A change in meaning would require the text to announce it — "ekklesia now means something different from qahal" — and no such announcement exists. The word appears 116 times in the OT (as qahal) and 115 times in the NT (as ekklesia) in continuous usage.

The text does NOT resolve every question this raises. How exactly the Sinai assembly relates to the Corinthian assembly, what institutional form the ekklesia should take, and how the twelve-tribe structure maps onto Gentile-majority communities — these are areas where the text provides principles (one law, grafted in, one body) without specifying every detail. The word-data establishes the connection; the reader must work through the implications.

## Patterns

1. **Same word, Sinai to Revelation.** H6951 qahal → LXX ekklesia → G1577 ekklesia. Acts 7:38 confirms. Heb 2:12 quotes Ps 22:22 qahal as ekklesia. No break announced.

2. **KJV translation creates the divide.** Same G1577: "church" (111×) vs "assembly" (3× for pagan gatherings). The English creates a category distinction the Greek does not.

3. **"My ekklesia" = "qahal of the LORD."** Mat 16:18 mirrors Num 16:3. Same possessive ownership language.

4. **Trumpet convokes.** Num 10:7 — trumpet gathers the qahal. 1 Th 4:16 — trumpet at the Lord's descent. Same mechanism.

5. **Synagoge and ekklesia overlap.** Jas 2:2 uses *synagoge* for believers. Strong's defines both as covering Jewish and Christian assemblies. The sharp distinction is post-biblical.

6. **The assembly has judicial authority.** Mat 18:17 — dispute resolution. Same function as the qahal in Num 35, Josh 20. Not worship-only.

7. **One law for all members.** Num 15:15 — native and stranger under one ordinance in the qahal. The assembly was inclusive from its Torah charter.

## Connections

- **$israel** — The ekklesia IS Israel's assembly. Acts 7:38 makes this explicit. Gentiles join this assembly (Rom 11:17, Eph 2:12-13) through the same inclusion mechanism the Torah established (Exo 12:48).

- **$rock** — "Upon this rock I will build my ekklesia" (Mat 16:18). $rock = covenant. The assembly is built on the covenant.

- **$trumpet** — Num 10:7: trumpet gathers the qahal. 1 Th 4:16: trumpet at the Lord's coming. The assembly is convoked by trumpet in both testaments.

- **$firstborn** — Heb 12:23: "ekklesia of the firstborn." Col 1:18: Christ = "firstborn from the dead," head of the body/ekklesia. The assembly is characterized by firstborn-status.

- **$name** — Ps 22:22 (quoted in Heb 2:12): "I will declare thy $[name] in the midst of the ekklesia." The assembly is where God's name is declared.
