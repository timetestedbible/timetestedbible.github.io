# Issachar

## Summary

**The hireling who owns the [field](/research/symbols/field/): strong as the donkey to carry, humble enough to bow, settled in tents, discerning the appointed times, rich enough to feed a king, and willing to walk into the [valley](/research/symbols/valley/) when Israel must be saved. In the heavenly camp he is [Virgo](/research/symbols/virgin/) in the [east](/research/symbols/east/)—the place of beginnings and returns—and is identified with [sapphire](/research/symbols/sapphire/), the full [moon](/research/symbols/moon/).**

Issachar gathers an unusual number of the kingdom parables' images into one
tribal portrait. His name is born from **hire** when a husband returns from the
**field at evening**. He is a strong donkey who bows to carry, a tent-dweller
who understands the appointed times, a provider who feeds the king, and a
servant who goes down into the valley when others hesitate. Jewish tradition
then places Issachar's name on the sapphire and places the sun and moon on his
banner. The book's heavenly map sets him in Virgo in the east.

The result is not a pile of unrelated emblems. Issachar becomes a meeting place
for the field and pearl parables: the Son buys the field and the bride, comes as
a humble King riding a donkey, returns with His reward, and gathers His people
at the appointed time.

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**The hireling who owns the field: strong as the donkey to carry, humble enough to bow, settled in tents, discerning the appointed times, rich enough to feed a king, and willing to walk into the valley when Israel must be saved. In the heavenly camp he is Virgo in the east—the place of beginnings and returns—and is identified with sapphire, the full moon.**

### Literal Biblical Definition

**Jacob's ninth son and Leah's fifth, and the Israelite tribe descended from him that camped east of the tabernacle and received an inheritance in Canaan.**

### Webster's Definition

The locally preserved Webster's 1913 corpus has no standalone entry for
**Issachar**. That absence matters: a dictionary cannot supply the meaning of a
biblical proper name it does not define. The literal and symbolic definitions
must therefore be derived from Scripture, while later Jewish testimony is kept
visible as historical evidence rather than silently treated as a dictionary.

## Corpus and Method

The primary corpus is the KJV with Strong's numbers used throughout the site.
It prints **Issachar** 44 times in 41 verses. This study begins with every one
of those verses, then includes the continuation of a blessing or scene when the
next verse carries the predicate without repeating Issachar's name: Genesis
30:16–18; 49:14–15; Deuteronomy 33:18–19; and 1 Kings 4:7, 17.

The evidence is kept in three layers. **Direct biblical statements** define the
name, animal, burden, tents, times, provision, valley, and eastward station.
**Established symbol definitions** connect field, valley, east, moon, pearl,
and sapphire without redefining them for this study. **Historical Jewish
tradition** supplies the sapphire and sun-moon banner assignment. The last
layer is evidence of an old association, not proof that Exodus itself names the
stone beside Issachar.

## The Name Begins in the Field

Issachar enters Scripture in a scene whose details become important later.
Jacob comes from the field **at evening**. Leah meets him and says that she has
**hired** him. The son conceived that night is named from **hire** or
**reward**.

> *“And Jacob came out of the **field in the evening**, and Leah went out to meet him, and said, Thou must come in unto me; for surely I have **hired** thee with my son's mandrakes.”* — Genesis 30:16

> *“And Leah said, God hath given me my **hire** … and she called his name **Issachar**.”* — Genesis 30:18

The Hebrew name *Yissakar* is explained in the narrative by *sakar*, hire or
reward. The field, evening, hiring, husband, and resulting son are not supplied
by an etymological guess; they stand together in the birth account.

Jesus later gathers the same cluster into a parable of the kingdom. A
householder goes out to **hire laborers into his vineyard**, and **when evening
comes**, the lord of the vineyard orders that the laborers receive their
**hire** (Matthew 20:1, 8). Genesis is not thereby reduced to a disguised
version of Matthew's parable. It does establish that field, evening, lord,
hiring, labor, and reward already belong together around Issachar's name.

That reward-word also announces the Lord's return:

> *“Behold, the Lord GOD will **come** with strong hand … behold, his **reward** is with him.”* — Isaiah 40:10

> *“Behold, thy salvation **cometh**; behold, his **reward** is with him.”* — Isaiah 62:11

Revelation repeats the declaration: *“Behold, I come quickly; and my reward is
with me”* (Revelation 22:12). Issachar's name therefore reaches beyond wages
paid for ordinary labor. The same Hebrew reward-word attached to his birth is
carried to the coming King.

## The Donkey That Bears the Burden

Jacob's blessing gives Issachar a body:

> *“Issachar is a **strong ass**, couching down between two burdens: and he saw that **rest was good**, and the land that it was pleasant; and **bowed his shoulder to bear**, and became a servant unto tribute.”* — Genesis 49:14–15

The donkey is not a figure of stupidity. It is **strong**, able to carry what
another places upon it. Issachar is humble enough to bow his shoulder and
settled enough to recognize good rest and pleasant land. His service is not
produced by weakness; a powerful bearer willingly lowers himself.

Jesus speaks the same sequence in the language of discipleship:

> *“Take my **yoke** upon you, and learn of me; for I am **meek and lowly** in heart: and ye shall find **rest** unto your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my **burden** is light.”* — Matthew 11:29–30

Rest does not mean the absence of a burden. It means bearing the right yoke
under the right Master. Issachar sees good rest and bows to bear; Christ is
lowly, gives rest, and places His own yoke upon those who learn from Him.

The King then makes the donkey visible:

> *“Behold, thy **King** cometh unto thee: he is just, and having salvation; **lowly, and riding upon an ass**.”* — Zechariah 9:9

> *“Behold, thy King cometh unto thee, meek, and sitting upon an ass.”* — Matthew 21:5

Issachar is not the King in this picture. He is the strong, bowed bearer upon
whom the lowly King enters His city. The animal and its Rider together display
one kingdom principle: royal authority arrives through willing, humble service.

## Settled in the Tents

Moses pairs the outward movement of Zebulun with Issachar's settled place:

> *“Rejoice, Zebulun, in thy going out; and, **Issachar, in thy tents**. They shall call the people unto the **mountain**; there they shall offer sacrifices of righteousness: for they shall suck of the abundance of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand.”* — Deuteronomy 33:18–19

Issachar's settled life is not idleness. From the tents he calls peoples to the
mountain, participates in righteous worship, and receives abundance from sea
and sand. The tribe is stable enough to study, judge a season, store provision,
and act when the appointed moment arrives.

The tents also prepare the heavenly correspondence. Scripture describes the
[heavens](/research/symbols/heaven/) as a tent stretched out by God and as a
tabernacle in which the sun runs its circuit (Psalm 19:4–6; Isaiah 40:22).
Issachar dwelling in tents therefore fits the tribe whose heavenly figure is
Virgo and whose work is to understand the circuit of appointed times. The
physical tent does not prove the constellation mapping by itself, but it fits
the independent camp, calendar, and luminary evidence without changing shape.

## He Knows the Time—and Goes

Issachar's most familiar distinction is practical wisdom:

> *“And of the children of Issachar, which were men that had **understanding of the times**, to know **what Israel ought to do**; the heads of them were two hundred; and all their brethren were at their commandment.”* — 1 Chronicles 12:32

They do not merely calculate dates. They understand the time **in order to know
what Israel should do**. Their discernment produces direction, and their
brethren follow their command.

The Song of Deborah shows what that looks like under pressure:

> *“And the princes of **Issachar were with Deborah**; even Issachar, and also Barak: he was sent on foot **into the valley**. For the divisions of Reuben there were great thoughts of heart.”* — Judges 5:15

Reuben remains divided in thought; Issachar goes. The tribe that knows the time
does not stay in the tent once the time to act has come. It walks into the
[valley](/research/symbols/valley/)—the low, contested passage between ruling
heights—to participate in Israel's deliverance.

This keeps the definition from turning calendar knowledge into trivia.
Understanding an appointed time is proven by timely obedience.

## Rich Enough to Feed a King

Solomon organized Israel into twelve provisioning districts:

> *“And Solomon had **twelve officers** over all Israel, which provided victuals for the king and his household: **each man his month in a year** made provision.”* — 1 Kings 4:7

One of those twelve monthly districts was **in Issachar** (1 Kings 4:17). The
tribe known for understanding the times appears inside a twelve-part annual
circuit, and its land is productive enough to feed the king and his house for
its appointed month.

The provision becomes even more concrete when David receives the kingdom:

> *“They that were nigh them, even unto **Issachar**, and Zebulun and Naphtali, brought **bread on asses** … meal, cakes of figs, and bunches of raisins, and wine, and oil, and oxen, and sheep **abundantly**: for there was joy in Israel.”* — 1 Chronicles 12:40

The verse joins Issachar, abundant food, the king, and donkeys in one scene.
Deuteronomy had already promised abundance from seas and hidden treasures in
the sand. The tribe's wealth is not presented merely for private ease; it
sustains the king and the rejoicing kingdom.

## The East, Virgo, and the Full Moon

Issachar's camp position is explicit:

> *“And on the **east side toward the rising of the sun** shall they of the standard of the camp of Judah pitch … and those that do pitch next unto him shall be the tribe of **Issachar**.”* — Numbers 2:3, 5

Joshua also describes an inheritance meeting Issachar **on the east** (Joshua
17:10). The [east](/research/symbols/east/) is where light begins its visible
course and from which Scripture pictures return. Issachar therefore stands at
the place of beginning and return beside Judah, the royal tribe.

In the heavenly camp developed in *Sun, Moon, and Stars*, the eastern triad is
Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun: Lion, Virgin, and Scales. Issachar is represented
by [Virgo](/research/symbols/virgin/), the one woman in the twelvefold circuit.
The full moon standing with the woman supplies the faithful-witness and bridal
figure, while Issachar's understanding of the times supplies the calendar
office.

### The sapphire assignment

Scripture says that the high priest's twelve stones carried the names of the
twelve tribes (Exodus 28:17–21), but it does **not** state which name was
engraved on which stone. That distinction must remain visible.

Later Jewish tradition assigns **sapphire to Issachar**. Exodus Rabbah 38:11
also describes Issachar's banner as dark blue or black with the sun and moon
upon it, tied to the tribe's understanding of the times. The early twentieth-
century *Jewish Encyclopedia* preserves the same sapphire assignment, and a
modern overview of the breastplate notes that the precise stone identities and
tribal ordering remain disputed.

This is independent historical testimony, not a direct biblical definition.
It is useful because its two details—**sapphire** and **sun and moon**—meet
Scripture's direct description of Issachar as the tribe that understands the
times. The tradition can strengthen a converging case, but the study does not
pretend Exodus names Issachar beside the sapphire.

The fuller [Sapphire](/research/symbols/sapphire/) study identifies the stone
with the full moon in its enthroned form: sapphire appears at the place of
God's throne and as its likeness (Exodus 24:10; Ezekiel 1:26; 10:1). The
[Pearl](/research/symbols/pearl/) study identifies the pearl with that same
complete moon in its purchased and bridal form. Sapphire and pearl therefore
do not compete for Issachar's sign. They show the same full witness by two
properties: throne and great price.

## Issachar in the Field and Pearl Parables

Jesus places two purchases side by side:

> *“The kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a **field**; the which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof goeth and selleth all that he hath, and **buyeth that field**.”* — Matthew 13:44

> *“Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly **pearls**: who, when he had found **one pearl of great price**, went and sold all that he had, and bought it.”* — Matthew 13:45–46

Jesus has already said, *“the field is the world,”* and identified the sower as
the Son of Man (Matthew 13:37–38). The book's *Pearls of Wisdom* chapter reads
the adjoining purchases as one action: the Son gives all to purchase the field
for its treasure and the pearl as His bride. We are bought with a price; Christ
loved the assembly and gave Himself for it (1 Corinthians 6:20; Ephesians
5:25).

Issachar touches both sides of that reading:

1. His name is born when the husband returns from the **field at evening** and
   is **hired**.
2. He inherits pleasant land, bows to bear its burden, and supplies its fruit
   to the king.
3. His animal is the donkey that bears the humble King into Jerusalem.
4. He understands the appointed time and acts when Israel must be saved.
5. His traditional stone is sapphire—the full moon in its enthroned form—while
   the pearl is the full moon in its purchased bridal form.
6. His station is the east, and his heavenly figure is Virgo, the woman in the
   twelvefold circuit.

No single item must carry the whole conclusion. The force is cumulative: name,
field, evening, hire, reward, donkey, burden, rest, tents, appointed time,
kingly provision, valley, east, woman, sapphire, and moon all converge without
requiring one word to abandon its ordinary sense.

Issachar is therefore not simply “the calendar tribe.” He is the willing
bearer who knows when to serve, has provision ready for the King, and enters
the low place at the appointed time. In the parable's light, he pictures the
service by which the King comes for the field and its one precious pearl.

## Competing Definitions Tested

### “Issachar is merely the tribe of calendar scholars”

This captures 1 Chronicles 12:32 but leaves most of the portrait unused. It
does not explain the name's hire-and-field setting, the donkey and bowed
shoulder, tents and abundance, monthly provision for the king, or descent into
the valley. Understanding the times is central, but Scripture presents it as
one capacity of a much fuller servant.

### “The donkey means laziness or servile compromise”

Genesis does say that Issachar becomes a servant under tribute, so the warning
cannot be erased. Yet the animal is introduced as **strong**, the land as
pleasant, and the rest as good. Judges shows Issachar entering the valley when
Reuben hesitates, and Chronicles calls his families mighty men of valor (1
Chronicles 7:5). The complete figure is strong, settled, and willing to bear;
it cannot be reduced to laziness.

### “Sapphire is assigned to Issachar directly by Exodus”

That claim is too strong. Exodus names twelve stones and says that the tribal
names were engraved upon them, but it does not pair each tribe with a named
stone. Sapphire belongs to Issachar in later Jewish tradition. The tradition
is unusually relevant because it also gives Issachar a sun-and-moon banner,
but it must remain labeled as tradition.

### “Issachar is simply another name for Jesus”

The study does not claim an alias. In the triumphal entry Issachar's donkey
portrait corresponds to the **bearer**, while Jesus is the **King who rides**.
Issachar's pattern participates in Christ's kingdom—field, service, appointed
time, provision, reward, and return—without collapsing tribe, animal, Rider,
and purchased bride into one referent.

## Conclusion

**Issachar is the hireling who owns the field: strong enough to carry, humble enough to bow, settled enough to dwell in the tents, discerning enough to know the appointed times, rich enough to feed a king, and willing to enter the valley when Israel must be saved. His eastern station, Virgo correspondence, and traditional sapphire identify him with the full moon—the complete witness present at the time of the King's return.**

The kingdom parables give the portrait its destination. The Son buys the field
and the pearl, arrives as the humble King upon a donkey, and comes with His
reward. Issachar is the servant-sign standing where those images meet.
