# Light

## Approved Definition

**The law lived visibly as an example — obedient actions others can see.**
> *"Let your **light** so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven."* — Mat 5:16

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The law lived visibly as an example — obedient actions others can see.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **light** begins: “That agent, force, or action in nature by the operation of which upon the organs of sight, objects are rendered visible or luminous.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

Read Matthew 5:14–16 and then keep reading. Most people stop at "let your light shine." But the very next verse defines what the light *is*:

> *"Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil. For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled."* — Mat 5:17–18

"Let your light shine" → "they see your good works" → "the law and the prophets." The light that shines before men is not a warm feeling, not abstract goodness, not theological knowledge. It is the visible, public keeping of God's commandments — Torah lived out where people can watch.

The opposite confirms this. $darkness in Scripture is not ignorance. It is **concealed disobedience** — doing in secret what you would not do in the open. "Every one that doeth evil hateth the $[light], neither cometh to the $[light], lest his deeds should be reproved" (Jn 3:20). The person in $darkness is not confused about what is right. He is *hiding* what he does.

Light does not hide. Light, by its nature, is visible. And what becomes visible when it shines? Works. Deeds. Conduct. Obedience or its absence.

This single insight — light = visible obedience, darkness = hidden disobedience — unlocks every use of the symbol in Scripture, from Genesis to Revelation.

**Symbolizes:** Visible obedience — the open, demonstrable keeping of God's commandments that others can see

**Opposite:** $darkness — hidden, concealed disobedience; doing in secret what should be done openly

**Defining verses:** Mat 5:14–16, 1 Jn 1:5–7, Eph 5:8–13, Jn 3:19–21, Jn 8:12

**The surprise:** What we call "good example" is exactly what Scripture calls light. A secular reader hearing "light" thinks of illumination or knowledge. But every passage that defines light in Scripture ties it to *doing* — visible works, walking in obedience, conduct that can be watched and imitated. Light is not what you know. It is what you *do* where others can see it.

**Connected:** $darkness, $mountain, $truth, $way, $the-way, $fruit, $wickedness, $name

## Light Across Scripture

### The Doing That Comes to Light

The Gospel of John draws the line most starkly:

> *"And this is the condemnation, that **light** is come into the world, and men loved **$[darkness]** rather than **light**, because their deeds were evil. For every one that doeth evil hateth the **light**, neither cometh to the **light**, lest his deeds should be reproved. But he that doeth $[truth] cometh to the **light**, that his deeds may be made manifest, that they are wrought in God."* — Jn 3:19–21

Notice the structure. Two kinds of people. Both are defined by their *deeds*:

- The one who does evil → hates the light → stays in $darkness → because his deeds would be **exposed**.
- The one who does $truth → comes to the light → because his deeds can be **shown** — they are wrought in God.

The word "doeth truth" is striking. $truth in Scripture's symbolic grammar is not merely factual accuracy — it is God's commandments, His standard (Jn 17:17: "Thy word is truth"; Ps 119:142: "Thy law is the truth"). You can *do* truth because truth is something you obey. And the one who obeys comes to the light — he welcomes visibility, because his conduct bears examination.

Light, then, is not the information that reveals. Light is the *state of being visible* — the condition of living openly according to God's standard. $darkness is not confusion; it is concealment. The choice between light and darkness is the choice between living your obedience where others can see it and hiding your disobedience where they cannot.

### "God Is Light" — The Standard Made Visible

> *"This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is **light**, and in him is no **$[darkness]** at all."* — 1 Jn 1:5

If light means "visible obedience," then "God is light" means God is the perfect, visible example of His own standard. There is no concealed disobedience in Him — no hidden contradiction, no gap between what He commands and what He does. He is entirely open, entirely consistent, entirely visible.

John immediately applies this to human conduct:

> *"If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in **$[darkness]**, we lie, and do not the $[truth]: But if we walk in the **light**, as he is in the **light**, we have fellowship one with another, and the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin."* — 1 Jn 1:6–7

Walking in $darkness while claiming fellowship = lying. Walking in light = doing $truth = open, visible obedience. The blood of Christ cleanses those who walk in the light — not those who hide. Light is the condition of openness, of refusing to conceal. It is not perfection (we still need cleansing); it is *transparency* — the willingness to live visibly.

The same letter continues: "He that saith he is in the **light**, and hateth his brother, is in **$[darkness]** even until now" (1 Jn 2:9). Light is tested not by profession but by behavior. Hatred proves $darkness regardless of the claim. Visible obedience cannot coexist with concealed malice.

### A City on a Hill — Light on a Mountain

> *"Ye are the **light** of the world. A city that is set on an **$[mountain]** cannot be hid."* — Mat 5:14

Jesus does not say believers *have* light or *receive* light. He says they **are** light. The identity is total — just as "God is light" and Christ is "the light of the world" (Jn 8:12), so believers *are* light. The same symbol applies at every level: God, Christ, His people. This only works if the meaning is consistent: visible example of commandment-keeping.

The $mountain sharpens the image. A $mountain in Scripture's symbolic vocabulary represents a kingdom — a ruling authority (Rev 17:9, Dan 2:35). A city on a $mountain is a visible, public community set within a governing structure — impossible to conceal. Christ is saying: your obedience is not a private affair. It is positioned on a $mountain. Everyone can see it. This is not a warning — it is a description of what light *does*. It cannot not shine.

> *"Neither do men light a candle, and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth **light** unto all that are in the house."* — Mat 5:15

A covered light is a contradiction. Light that does not shine is not functioning as light. If visible obedience is hidden, it ceases to be what it is. The bushel is the attempt to keep obedience private — to follow God quietly without being seen. Jesus says: that defeats the purpose. The light exists *for others*.

And then the definition:

> *"Let your **light** so shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father which is in heaven."* — Mat 5:16

"Good works" = visible obedience. "Before men" = publicly. "See" = the defining function of light. "Glorify your Father" = the works point beyond the doer to the standard-giver. Light is not self-glorifying. The visible obedience demonstrates God's commandments and brings honor to the one who gave them.

### Walking in the Light — How Conduct Becomes a Path

John's Gospel repeatedly links light to *walking* — to the $way a person conducts their life:

> *"I am the **light** of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in **$[darkness]**, but shall have the **light** of life."* — Jn 8:12

> *"If a man walk in the day, he stumbleth not, because he seeth the **light** of this world. But if a man walk in the night, he stumbleth, because there is no **light** in him."* — Jn 11:9–10

> *"Yet a little while is the **light** with you. Walk while ye have the **light**, lest **$[darkness]** come upon you: for he that walketh in **$[darkness]** knoweth not whither he goeth. While ye have **light**, believe in the **light**, that ye may be the children of **light**."* — Jn 12:35–36

The pattern is consistent: light enables walking. $darkness produces stumbling and disorientation — "knoweth not whither he goeth." The $way — one's conduct, manner of life — depends on light. Without a visible example to follow, a person does not know where they are going.

This connects directly to the Psalms:

> *"Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a **light** unto my path."* — Ps 119:105

> *"O send out thy **light** and thy $[truth]: let them lead me; let them bring me unto thy holy hill."* — Ps 43:3

Light and $truth travel together because they are two aspects of the same reality: $truth is the standard (God's commandments), and light is that standard made *visible*. The word illuminates the path — it shows the $way to walk. Light leads to the holy $mountain — God's kingdom.

Luke records the same idea in Zechariah's prophecy:

> *"To give **light** to them that sit in **$[darkness]** and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the $[way] of peace."* — Lk 1:79

Those sitting in $darkness — concealed disobedience, ignorance of God's standard — receive light: a visible example that guides their feet into the $way of peace. Light does not merely inform. It guides conduct. It shapes the $way one walks.

### From Darkness to Light — The Transformation

> *"For ye were sometimes **$[darkness]**, but now are ye **light** in the Lord: walk as children of **light**."* — Eph 5:8

Paul does not say they were *in* darkness and now are *in* light. They *were* darkness and now *are* light. This is identity language, not location language. The person who conceals their disobedience *is* $darkness. The person who visibly obeys *is* light.

The passage then defines the $fruit of this identity:

> *"(For the $[fruit] of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and $[truth];) And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of **$[darkness]**, but rather reprove them. For it is a shame even to speak of those things which are done of them in secret. But all things that are reproved are made manifest by the **light**: for whatsoever doth make manifest is **light**."* — Eph 5:9–13

The $fruit — the works and deeds — of light are "goodness and righteousness and truth." The works of $darkness are "unfruitful" (producing nothing of value) and done "in secret" (concealed). Light's function is to *make manifest* — to expose, to bring into the open.

This is the transformation chain: a person moves from $darkness (concealed disobedience) to light (visible obedience). The mechanism is not merely learning new information. It is *coming out of hiding*. The same deeds that were concealed are now brought into the open — repented of, confessed, abandoned — and replaced by $fruit that can bear scrutiny. Acts 26:18 frames this as a transfer of dominion:

> *"To open their eyes, and to turn them from **$[darkness]** to **light**, and from the power of Satan unto God."* — Acts 26:18

$darkness is explicitly linked to Satan's power. Light is explicitly linked to God's. The turning from darkness to light is simultaneously a turning from $wickedness (lawlessness, covenant transgression) to covenant obedience — from concealment to visibility, from Satan's domain to God's.

### Children of Light — Armor and Identity

Paul expands the identity language to the church:

> *"Ye are all the children of **light**, and the children of the day: we are not of the night, nor of **$[darkness]**."* — 1 Th 5:5

> *"The night is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of **$[darkness]**, and let us put on the armour of **light**."* — Rom 13:12

The "armour of light" is remarkable. Armor protects in battle — and light is the armor. Visible obedience is the believer's defense. Hidden, concealed conduct is vulnerability. What cannot be seen cannot be defended; what is done openly can stand against accusation.

Philippians adds:

> *"That ye may be blameless and harmless, the sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom ye shine as **lights** in the world."* — Phil 2:15

"Shine as lights" — not by preaching, not by theological argument, but by being "blameless and harmless" and "without rebuke." The shining *is* the blameless conduct. The visible obedience stands out against the backdrop of a "crooked and perverse nation" the way a lamp stands out in a dark room. The contrast is the point. $darkness makes light visible.

### The Three-Tier Pattern

Scripture applies "light" at three levels, and the symbolic meaning must work at all three. It does:

**God is light** (1 Jn 1:5) — God is the perfect visible example of His own standard. No $darkness in Him — no inconsistency between His commands and His nature.

**Christ is the light of the world** (Jn 8:12, 2 Cor 4:6) — Jesus is the commandments made flesh, the $truth embodied, the $way lived out in human form where people could watch. "For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, hath shined in our hearts, to give the **light** of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Cor 4:6). The light that shines in the face of Christ is God's standard made visible in a person.

**Believers are light** (Mat 5:14, Eph 5:8, Phil 2:15) — those who follow the example become examples themselves. They are not the source of the standard; they are its visible demonstration. "Let your light so shine before men" — the light is derivative, reflective, dependent on the original. But it is real.

The same word, the same symbol, the same meaning at every level: visible example of commandment-keeping. God demonstrates His own standard perfectly. Christ embodies it in human life. Believers, walking in His $way, extend the demonstration to every nation under heaven.

### Righteousness Is the Content of the Light

The pipeline research closes a crucial loop: if light = visible obedience, obedience to *what*?

> *"And it shall be our righteousness, if we observe to do all these commandments before the LORD our God, as he hath commanded us."* — Deut 6:25

> *"My tongue shall speak of thy word: for all thy commandments are righteousness."* — Ps 119:172

Righteousness = the commandments. And the "way of righteousness" = "the holy commandment":

> *"For it had been better for them not to have known the $[way] of righteousness, than, after they have known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered unto them."* — 2 Pet 2:21

So the chain runs: light → visible good works (Mat 5:16) → righteousness (Deut 6:25) → the commandments (Ps 119:172) → the holy commandment (2 Pet 2:21). Light shines when commandments are kept visibly. $darkness falls when they are transgressed secretly.

## Patterns

1. **Light is what you DO, not what you KNOW.** Every defining passage ties light to conduct — good works (Mat 5:16), walking (1 Jn 1:7, Jn 8:12, Jn 12:35), doing truth (Jn 3:21), fruit of goodness and righteousness (Eph 5:9). Never once is light defined as information alone.

2. **Light is inherently PUBLIC.** A city on a $mountain cannot be hid. A candle is not placed under a bushel. Light "makes manifest" (Eph 5:13). The function of light is visibility — obedience done where others can see it. Private, hidden obedience is a contradiction in terms within this symbol.

3. **$darkness is not ignorance — it is concealment.** "Men loved darkness because their deeds were evil" (Jn 3:19). "Things done of them in secret" (Eph 5:12). Israel "did secretly those things that were not right" (2 Ki 17:9). David "didst it secretly" (2 Sam 12:12). The opposite of light is hiding, not confusion.

4. **Light and $truth always travel together.** "Send out thy light and thy truth" (Ps 43:3). "He that doeth truth cometh to the light" (Jn 3:21). "Walk in darkness… do not the truth" (1 Jn 1:6). $truth is the standard; light is the standard made visible. They are inseparable.

5. **Light enables others to walk.** Ps 119:105 — light on the path. Lk 1:79 — light guides feet into the $way. Jn 11:9–10 — seeing the light prevents stumbling. Light is not self-contained. It exists so that others can follow the $way.

6. **The same symbol applies to God, Christ, and believers.** God is light (1 Jn 1:5). Christ is the light of the world (Jn 8:12). Believers are the light of the world (Mat 5:14). The meaning must — and does — work for all three: visible example of commandment-keeping at every level.

7. **Coming to light = coming out of hiding.** The transformation from $darkness to light (Eph 5:8, Acts 26:18) is not primarily an intellectual shift but a behavioral one — ceasing to conceal, beginning to live openly. Repentance is the act of bringing hidden $wickedness into the light.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$darkness** — The defining opposite. $darkness = concealed disobedience; light = visible obedience. Every passage that mentions light defines it against $darkness: Jn 3:19–21, 1 Jn 1:5–7, Eph 5:8–13. The two symbols are a single pair — you cannot understand one without the other. The transformation from $darkness to light is the movement from hiding to openness, from $wickedness to covenant faithfulness.

- **$truth** — God's commandments, His standard (Ps 119:142, Jn 17:17). "He that doeth $truth cometh to the light" (Jn 3:21). $truth is what you obey; light is that obedience made visible. $truth is the content; light is the display. They always appear together because you cannot demonstrate what you do not have, and a standard no one can see might as well be hidden.

- **$mountain** — Kingdom, ruling authority (Rev 17:9, Dan 2:35). "A city set on a $mountain cannot be hid" (Mat 5:14). Light is positioned on the $mountain — visible obedience placed within the context of God's kingdom, seen by the nations. The Sermon on the Mount is Torah taught with kingdom authority — the light shining from the mountain.

- **$way** — Conduct, manner of life (Gen 18:19, Jn 14:6). "Thy word is a light unto my path" (Ps 119:105). Light illuminates the $way — it shows the pattern of conduct to follow. Without light, "he that walketh in darkness knoweth not whither he goeth" (Jn 12:35). Light and $way are inseparable: one is the example, the other is the conduct it produces.

- **$the-way** — Torah, God's commandments (Acts 24:14, 2 Pet 2:21). "The commandment is a lamp; and the law is light" (Prov 6:23). $the-way is the specific content of the light — the defined standard of right living. When believers "shine as lights," they are demonstrating $the-way in practice.

- **$fruit** — Works, deeds (Gal 5:19–22, Mat 7:16). "The $fruit of the Spirit is in all goodness and righteousness and truth" (Eph 5:9). The $fruit of light is visible conduct — the works that others can see and evaluate. Light produces $fruit; $darkness produces "unfruitful works" (Eph 5:11). The $fruit is the evidence that light is genuine.

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2, 2 Th 2:7–8). $wickedness is what $darkness conceals and what light exposes. Those who claim the $name while practicing $wickedness are called "workers of lawlessness" (Mat 7:23) — they took the covenant $name but lived in $darkness. Light and $wickedness cannot coexist.

- **$name** — Covenant, covenant relationship (2 Sam 7:13, Acts 4:12). To bear the $name is to be in covenant — and those in covenant are called to be light. Taking the $name "in vain" (Exod 20:7) is bearing the covenant while concealing disobedience — claiming light while walking in $darkness. The third commandment, reframed, is a command to be genuine light.

## For Further Study

- **$darkness** — The defining opposite. What does Scripture mean by darkness, and why is concealment — not ignorance — its core? Understanding darkness fully is essential to understanding light.
- **$truth** — The content of the light. If light is the visible demonstration, truth is the standard being demonstrated. How does Scripture define truth as commandments rather than mere facts?
- **$mountain** — The platform for the light. Why is the city set on a mountain, and what does it mean for visible obedience to be positioned within God's kingdom?
- **$fruit** — The evidence of the light. How do works and deeds function as the proof that light is genuine and not mere profession?
- **$name** — The covenant behind the light. Bearing God's name and being His light are connected — how does the third commandment relate to the call to shine?

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **light** begins:

> “velocity of about 186,300 miles per second; but it is now generally understood to consist, not in any actual transmission of particles or substance, but in the propagation of vibrations or undulations in a subtile, elastic medium, or ether, assumed to pervade all space, and to be thus set in vibratory motion by the action of luminous bodies, as the atmosphere is by sonorous bodies. This view of the nature of light is known as the undulatory or wave theory; the other, advocated by Newton (but long since abandoned), as the corpuscular, emission, or Newtonian theory. A more recent theory makes light to consist in electrical oscillations, and is known as the electro-magnetic theory of light.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The two citations support law as light and good works as visible light separately, but they do not conclusively establish that every biblical use of light means law embodied in works; major passages directly identify light with God or Christ.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: by defining light as human law-keeping rather than God/Christ, the book replaces the consensus's central referent (divine being) with a different referent (human conduct), and 'light is God' vs. 'light is our lived obedience' identify categorically different things. This is a serious objection, but it fails the contradiction test because the consensus explicitly treats believer-conduct and law-as-light as legitimate co-existing senses of the same polyvalent symbol, and the book's cited verses genuinely carry those senses; the disagreement is over centrality and scope, not mutually exclusive claims.

- In biblical theology, light overwhelmingly and fundamentally points to God (1 John 1:5) and Christ (John 8:12); defining it solely as human works or the law risks mistaking the reflection for the source.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The law lived visibly as an example — obedient actions others can see.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *light*, *lights*. The English-form sweep found **256 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
