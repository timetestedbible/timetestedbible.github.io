# Goat

**Self-will — the nature that sees the need, knows the shepherd, and chooses its own way.**

> *"And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his **sheep** from the **goats**: And he shall set the $[sheep] on his right hand, but the **goats** on the left."* — Mat 25:32–33

---

## The Key Insight

The goats in Matthew 25 are not condemned for blasphemy. They are not condemned for murder, theft, or idolatry. They call Jesus "Lord" (Mat 25:44). They know who He is.

They are condemned for *not acting*:

> *"For I was an hungred, and ye gave me no meat: I was thirsty, and ye gave me no drink: I was a stranger, and ye took me not in: naked, and ye clothed me not: sick, and in prison, and ye visited me not."* — Mat 25:42–43

Six needs. Six refusals. Not active rebellion — passive self-determination. The goats saw the need and chose not to respond. They operated on their own authority, their own priorities, their own schedule. The $sheep heard the shepherd's voice and moved; the goats heard the same voice and stayed where they were.

This is the defining contrast. The $sheep follows; the goat decides for itself. And the Hebrew words for "goat" encode this nature in their very roots.

**Symbolizes:** Self-will — the aggressive, independent nature that refuses to submit to covenant authority

**Opposite:** $sheep — trusting obedience, following the shepherd, covenant people who hear and respond

**Defining verses:** Mat 25:32–33, Ezek 34:17–21, Zech 10:3, Dan 8:5–8, Lev 16:8–22

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word H8163 *sa'iyr* — translated "goat" — is the *same word* translated "devil" and "satyr." Israel sacrificed to "devils" (Lev 17:7), and the word is *sa'iyr*: goats. The self-willed nature and the demonic share a single Hebrew term.

**Connected:** $sheep, $animal, $faith, $wickedness, $babylon, $grass

## Goat Across Scripture

### The Etymology That Tells the Whole Story

Three Hebrew words are translated "goat," and each one reveals a dimension of self-will:

**H5795 *ez*** — derives from H5810 *azaz*, meaning "to be strong, to be fierce." The goat is the *fierce* one. Strength turned inward, exercised for its own ends.

**H8495 *tayish*** — literally "given to butting." The male goat's defining behavior: head-down aggression, forcing its way through the flock. Not defending — *attacking* to maintain dominance.

**H8163 *sa'iyr*** — this is the most revealing. It means "he-goat," but it is *also* the word translated "devil" in Lev 17:7 and 2 Chr 11:15, and "satyr" in Isa 13:21 and Isa 34:14. One word carries all three meanings: goat, devil, satyr. The self-willed creature and the demonic entity share a single name in Hebrew.

And in Gen 27:11, Esau — the man who sold his birthright, who despised the covenant inheritance — is described as *sa'iyr*: "hairy." The same word. The man who traded covenant blessing for a bowl of stew wears the goat-word on his skin.

The Greek confirms the pattern. G2055 *eriphion* ("kid" in Mat 25:33) carries a Strong's note identifying it as symbolically denoting "a wicked person." The lexical evidence is unusually direct: every goat-word in both testaments encodes aggression, fierceness, or rebellion.

### Sheep on the Right, Goats on the Left

> *"When the Son of man shall come in his glory, and all the holy angels with him, then shall he sit upon the throne of his glory: And before him shall be gathered all nations."* — Mat 25:31–32

The judgment of the nations in Matthew 25 is not a parable. It is a direct description. And the criterion of separation is striking in what it does *not* include.

The $sheep are blessed because they acted: they fed the hungry, gave drink to the thirsty, took in the stranger, clothed the naked, visited the sick and imprisoned (Mat 25:35–36). They did this without knowing it was Christ they served — "When saw we thee?" (Mat 25:37–39). Their $faith — their trusting obedience — produced action without calculation.

The goats are cursed because they *did not act* (Mat 25:42–43). They knew. They saw. They chose not to. And their destination is "everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels" (Mat 25:41) — the same fire prepared for the ultimate self-willed being. The goat-nature and the devil share a destination just as H8163 *sa'iyr* gives them a shared name.

Notice: the goats call Jesus "Lord" (Mat 25:44). They recognize His authority. They simply did not submit to it. This is not ignorance — it is self-will. The capacity to know the shepherd's voice and choose not to follow it.

This is exactly what distinguishes the goat from the $sheep:

> *"My $[sheep] hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me."* — Jn 10:27

The $sheep hear *and follow*. The goat hears *and decides for itself*.

### The Goats Within the Flock

> *"And as for you, O my flock, thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I judge between cattle and cattle, between the rams and the **he goats**."* — Ezek 34:17

Ezekiel 34 places the goats *inside the flock*. They are not outsiders. They graze in the same pasture, drink from the same waters. But their behavior reveals their nature:

> *"Seemeth it a small thing unto you to have eaten up the good pasture, but ye must tread down with your feet the residue of your pastures? and to have drunk of the deep $[water]s, but ye must foul the residue with your feet?"* — Ezek 34:18

> *"Because ye have thrust with side and with shoulder, and pushed all the diseased with your horns, till ye have scattered them abroad"* — Ezek 34:21

The goat eats the good pasture — and *tramples what's left*. Drinks the deep water — and *muddies the rest*. Thrusts with side and shoulder — and *scatters the weak*. This is self-will turned aggressive: not content with having enough, but destroying what others need. The goat takes for itself and ruins the surplus.

The weak $sheep — the diseased, the vulnerable — are pushed aside by horns. The goat-nature within the flock does more damage than the wolves outside it.

Zechariah draws the same picture with sharper focus:

> *"Mine anger was kindled against the shepherds, and I punished the **he goats**: for the LORD of hosts hath visited his flock the house of Judah."* — Zech 10:3

God's anger falls on two groups: the shepherds (false leaders) and the *he goats*. The goats are paired with corrupt leadership — both exercise authority that belongs only to the shepherd. The goats are not the flock. They are within it, taking advantage of it, acting as though they own it.

### The He-Goat of Empire

> *"And as I was considering, behold, an **he goat** came from the west on the face of the whole earth, and touched not the ground: and the goat had a notable horn between his eyes."* — Dan 8:5

> *"And the rough **goat** is the king of Grecia: and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king."* — Dan 8:21

Daniel's vision scales the goat-nature up to the level of nations. The he-goat of Daniel 8 is explicitly identified as Greece — specifically Alexander the Great. And every detail encodes self-will:

**Speed without grounding** — the goat "touched not the ground" (Dan 8:5). Conquest so rapid it seems to fly, disconnected from the earth, from limitation, from restraint.

**Fury** — "he came to the ram... and ran unto him in the fury of his power" (Dan 8:6). Not measured force — unbridled aggression. The *tayish* nature ("given to butting") at civilizational scale.

**Self-magnification** — "the he goat waxed very great... he magnified himself" (Dan 8:8, 11). The $babylon pattern: "I am, and none else beside me" (Isa 47:8). The goat becomes its own authority, its own center.

And then the horn breaks. "When he was strong, the great horn was broken" (Dan 8:8). Self-will carries the seed of its own destruction — a pattern Scripture attributes to $wickedness universally (Isa 9:18–19). The empire fragments into four kingdoms, none with the original power. The goat-nature conquers spectacularly and cannot sustain what it conquers.

### The Devil-Word

> *"And they shall no more offer their sacrifices unto **devils** (H8163 *sa'iyr*), after whom they have gone a whoring."* — Lev 17:7

> *"And he ordained him priests for the high places, and for the **devils** (H8163 *sa'iyr*), and for the calves which he had made."* — 2 Chr 11:15

The same word. When Israel "went a whoring" after foreign gods, the object of their worship is called *sa'iyr* — goats. When Jeroboam established counterfeit priesthood, he ordained priests for *sa'iyr* — goats/devils. The self-willed nature is inseparable from the demonic in Hebrew vocabulary.

In the desolation prophecies, the same word appears as "satyr":

> *"But wild beasts of the desert shall lie there... and **satyrs** (H8163 *sa'iyr*) shall dance there."* — Isa 13:21

> *"The wild beasts of the desert shall also meet with the wild beasts of the island, and the **satyr** (H8163 *sa'iyr*) shall cry to his fellow."* — Isa 34:14

In the ruins of $babylon (Isa 13) and the desolation of Edom (Isa 34), the *sa'iyr* dances. The goat-word haunts the places where self-willed kingdoms have fallen. Where man's authority has been judged, the goat-nature that built it still lurks in the wreckage.

This chain — goat, devil, satyr — converges with Acts 7:43, where Stephen accuses Israel: "Yea, ye took up the tabernacle of Moloch, and the star of your god Remphan" (quoting Amos 5:26). The worship of self-willed, self-made deities — the goat-religion — runs through Israel's history like a thread.

### The Scapegoat — Self-Will Expelled

> *"And Aaron shall cast lots upon the two **goats**; one lot for the LORD, and the other lot for the scapegoat (H5799 *azazel*)."* — Lev 16:8

The Day of Atonement presents two goats — and their fates illuminate the symbol's full range.

The first goat is "for the LORD" — it is killed as a sin offering (Lev 16:15). This goat's self-willed nature is put to death. It becomes the sacrifice that purges the sanctuary.

The second goat is *azazel* (H5799). The etymology appears to combine H5795 *ez* (goat/fierce) with H235 *azal* (to go away/depart). The goat that *goes away*. Aaron lays both hands on its head and confesses "all the iniquities of the children of Israel, and all their transgressions in all their sins" (Lev 16:21). The goat bears the accumulated self-will of the people — their covenant violations — and carries them into the wilderness:

> *"And the **goat** shall bear upon him all their iniquities unto a land not inhabited: and he shall let go the **goat** in the wilderness."* — Lev 16:22

Self-will is identified with sin, loaded onto the self-willed creature, and *expelled from the community*. The scapegoat ritual does not merely illustrate atonement — it enacts the removal of the goat-nature from among the covenant people. The first goat dies; the second departs. Both outcomes involve the elimination of self-will from the assembly.

The wilderness is key. The goat goes where there is no flock, no shepherd, no pasture, no community. The self-willed nature ends up alone — which is, in a sense, what it chose all along.

## Patterns

1. **The goat is not an outsider.** Ezek 34:17 judges goats *within* the flock. Mat 25:44 — goats call Jesus "Lord." Zech 10:3 — goats are near the flock, punished alongside bad shepherds. Self-will operates inside covenant proximity, not outside it.

2. **Condemned for inaction, not for active evil.** Mat 25:42–43 lists six refusals. The goat's sin is the exercise of personal autonomy over covenant obligation — choosing not to serve. $faith (trusting obedience) produces action; the goat-nature withholds it.

3. **Self-will scales from individual to empire.** Ezek 34 = individual goats within a flock. Dan 8 = Greece as a goat-kingdom. The same nature — aggression, self-magnification, independence — operates at every level.

4. **The goat-word and the devil share a name.** H8163 *sa'iyr* = goat = devil = satyr. Mat 25:41 sends goats to fire "prepared for the devil and his angels." The lexical overlap is not accidental — self-will is the nature Scripture attributes to Satan himself.

5. **The goat must be removed.** Lev 16:21–22 — the scapegoat is expelled. Mat 25:41 — goats are sent away. Ezek 34:17–21 — God judges and separates. In every judgment context, the goat-nature is identified and cast out.

6. **Self-will destroys what it doesn't consume.** Ezek 34:18 — eats the pasture, tramples the rest. Dan 8:8 — conquers everything, then the horn breaks. The goat takes more than it needs and ruins the surplus. This mirrors the self-consuming quality of $wickedness (Isa 9:18–19).

7. **Esau is the goat-man.** Gen 27:11 — Esau is *sa'iyr* (hairy). He sells his birthright (Gen 25:34), despises the covenant inheritance, marries foreign wives, and becomes the father of Edom — whose desolation hosts satyrs (*sa'iyr*) in Isa 34:14. The goat-word covers Esau from skin to legacy.

## Connections

- **$sheep** — The goat's defining opposite. $sheep hear the shepherd's voice and follow (Jn 10:27); goats hear and choose for themselves. The judgment of Mat 25:32–33 hinges entirely on this contrast — the same flock, the same shepherd, opposite responses.

- **$animal** — $animal represents people or tribes characterized by their nature. The goat is a specific type within this pattern: the domestic $animal that behaves like a wild one — fierce, independent, aggressive. Within the flock but not of the flock's character.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience — the response of hearing God's word and acting on it. The goat is the anti-$faith creature: it sees the need, knows the Lord (Mat 25:44), and refuses to act. James 2:17 — "faith without works is dead" — describes precisely the goat's condition.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness is lawlessness, covenant transgression. The goat embodies $wickedness not as spectacular rebellion but as quiet self-legislation — deciding for oneself what to do, when to act, whom to serve. The same Greek concept: *anomia* (without law) = without external authority = self-willed.

- **$babylon** — $babylon is man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — the kingdom of self-determination. Daniel 8 casts an entire empire (Greece) as a goat. The goat-nature at civilizational scale produces $babylon: aggressive conquest, self-magnification ("I am, and none else"), and eventual self-destruction.

- **$grass** — $grass represents mortal people. The goats in Ezek 34:18 eat the good pasture and trample the rest — the self-willed consuming and destroying the common $grass-people. The goat feeds on the flock's provision while fouling what remains for others.

## For Further Study

- **$sheep** — The goat's opposite. What does it mean to be a sheep — not passive, but hearing and following? The contrast defines both symbols.
- **$faith** — If $faith is trusting obedience, then the goat is the portrait of $faith's absence. The goats of Mat 25 believed — they just didn't act. What does that reveal about what $faith actually is?
- **$wickedness** — The goat embodies $wickedness not as flamboyant sin but as quiet lawlessness — self-legislation. How does the Greek *anomia* illuminate the goat-nature?
- **$babylon** — When the goat-nature builds a civilization, it looks like $babylon: aggressive, self-magnifying, eventually self-consuming. Daniel 8 makes the connection explicit.
- **$animal** — Every $animal in Scripture characterizes a people by its nature. The goat is the $animal within the flock that acts like a predator. How do the other animal-symbols map to other postures toward covenant authority?
