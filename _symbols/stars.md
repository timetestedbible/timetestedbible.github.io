# Stars

## Approved Definition

**The righteous, who rule the [night](/research/symbols/night/) with the Son — “they that be wise shall shine… as the stars for ever”; the twelve stars are the twelve tribes, gathered into the twelve constellations, the Mazzaroth.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Sun, Moon & Stars](/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/), [research-sun-moon-stars.md](/books/symbolic-language/research/research-sun-moon-stars.md).

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The righteous, who rule the [night](/research/symbols/night/) with the Son — “they that be wise shall shine… as the stars for ever”; the twelve stars are the twelve tribes, gathered into the twelve constellations, the Mazzaroth.**

### Bible literal sense

**Lights in the firmament appointed to give light, rule the night, mark signs and seasons, and move in ordered courses.**

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **star** begins: “One of the innumerable luminous bodies seen in the heavens; any heavenly body other than the sun, moon, comets, and nebulæ.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The righteous, who rule the night with the Son (“they that be wise shall shine… as the stars for ever”); the twelve stars are the twelve tribes, gathered into the twelve constellations, the Mazzaroth.

> *“And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.”* — Daniel 12:3

> *“The moon and stars to rule by night: for his mercy endureth for ever.”* — Psalms 136:9

> *“And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.”* — Genesis 37:9

> *“And there appeared a great wonder in heaven; a woman clothed with the sun, and the moon under her feet, and upon her head a crown of twelve stars:”* — Revelation 12:1

> *“Canst thou bring forth Mazzaroth in his season? or canst thou guide Arcturus with his sons?”* — Job 38:32

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *stars*. The English-form sweep found **50 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.

## Evidence by Sense

The approved entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Approved core:** The righteous, who rule the [night](/research/symbols/night/) with the Son — “they that be wise shall shine… as the stars for ever”; the twelve stars are the twelve tribes, gathered into the twelve constellations, the Mazzaroth.
2. **Defining witnesses:** Dan 12:3; Ps 136:9; Gen 37:9; Rev 12:1; Job 38:32
3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The printed texts do not establish the full synthesis. Daniel 12:3 says the righteous shine as stars, not necessarily that stars generally denote the righteous; Psalm 136:9 says literal stars rule the night; Revelation 12:1 does not explicitly identify its twelve stars as tribes; and Job 38:32 neither equates Mazzaroth with exactly twelve constellations nor maps them to the tribes.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: the consensus's minimum core identification is angels (the only reading Scripture states explicitly, Rev 1:20), and the book's core—human righteous and tribes—is a different class of referent altogether; selecting it as the exclusive meaning implicitly rejects the consensus primary. Additionally, the Mazzaroth claim (twelve tribes gathered into twelve constellations) has no consensus counterpart and could push toward NOVEL for that component; Job 38:32 does not itself connect Mazzaroth to the tribes.

- The book entry neither cites nor accounts for Revelation 1:20, the only place Scripture explicitly equates stars with angels and the foundation of the consensus primary meaning.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
