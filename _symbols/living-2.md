## The Short Case

The Spirit and word of God going out to give life.

> *“For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.”* — Jeremiah 2:13

> *“He that believeth on me, as the scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water.”* — John 7:38

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- John 7:38, especially when interpreted by John 7:39, supports the Spirit, while Jeremiah 2:13 identifies God as the fountain of living waters. Neither printed citation directly identifies living water with God’s word, so the book’s added co-referent is not established by these references alone.

- One could argue MATCH: since the consensus already lists 'the word of God' as a recognized minority alternative and treats the various referents (Spirit, life, revelation) as overlapping rather than competing, the book's dual formulation may be mere wording within the same acknowledged complex rather than a material boundary change. Additionally, neither printed citation (Jer 2:13; John 7:38) explicitly supports the 'word' component, so the broadening rests on the definition alone.

- John 7:38-39 explicitly interprets 'rivers of living water' specifically as the Spirit. Treating the 'word of God' as a coordinate meaning might blur the specific referent provided by the evangelist.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
