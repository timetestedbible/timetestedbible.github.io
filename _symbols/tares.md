## The Short Case

Tares are enemy-sown counterfeits among the wheat: the children of the wicked one, resembling the true grain until harvest and then bundled and burned.

> *“But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat, and went his way.”* — Matthew 13:25

> *“But when the blade was sprung up, and brought forth fruit, then appeared the tares also.”* — Matthew 13:26

> *“So the servants of the householder came and said unto him, Sir, didst not thou sow good seed in thy field? from whence then hath it tares?”* — Matthew 13:27

> *“He said unto them, An enemy hath done this. The servants said unto him, Wilt thou then that we go and gather them up?”* — Matthew 13:28

> *“But he said, Nay; lest while ye gather up the tares, ye root up also the wheat with them.”* — Matthew 13:29

> *“Let both grow together until the harvest: and in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers, Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn.”* — Matthew 13:30

> *“The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one;”* — Matthew 13:38

> *“The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the world; and the reapers are the angels.”* — Matthew 13:39

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The phrase “counterfeit” might be read as narrowing the tares to deceptive false believers who outwardly mimic Christians, whereas Matthew’s explanation can encompass the wicked in the world more generally. In context, however, the book immediately gives the unrestricted scriptural identification “children of the wicked one,” so this does not establish a material boundary change.

- One could argue the book's explicit demarcation from chaff, and its emphasis on 'counterfeit' mimicry, slightly sharpens the referent toward false professors specifically (versus the wicked generally), which might suggest REFINED. But the consensus responses already include the counterfeit/false-believer framing as the primary meaning, and the chaff distinction concerns a different symbol, not the tares' extension, so the objection does not overturn MATCH.

- The explicit ‘distinct from winnowed chaff’ clause could be read as an extra restriction, yet it does not exclude any cases the consensus assigns to tares themselves.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
