# Wars and Rumors of Wars

**Covenant conflict — the visible fracture that appears when covenant order dissolves, a sign of the process but not the consummation.**

> *"And ye shall hear of **wars** and **rumours of wars**: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

---

## The Key Insight

Jesus says wars and rumors of wars are *not the end*. Most readers hear this as a reassurance: "Don't worry, the end is far off." But the actual force of the statement runs deeper. The Greek word for "end" is G5056 *telos* — not cessation but **goal**, the purpose arriving. Jesus is saying: covenant conflict is not the purpose. It is a symptom of something breaking, not the thing being built.

The sentence that unlocks the symbol is in 2 Chronicles — the Old Testament passage Jesus appears to be quoting. When the prophet Azariah describes the period of Israel's unfaithfulness, he uses the same formula:

> *"And in those times there was no peace to him that went out, nor to him that came in, but great vexations were upon all the inhabitants of the countries. And **nation** was destroyed of **nation**, and city of city: for God did vex them with all adversity."* — 2 Chr 15:5–6

"God did vex them." The fracture is not random. It is not merely political. God Himself is the agent. When covenant order breaks down — when the people abandon the terms of their relationship with God — the result is internal conflict. Nation turns on nation. City turns on city. Brother turns on brother. The wars are not the cause; they are the **evidence** that covenant dissolution is underway.

This reframes every "war" passage in Scripture. War is not a secular event that happens to appear in a prophetic list. It is the theological sign that the covenant fabric is tearing — and the tearing has a divine hand behind it.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant conflict — divine judgment manifesting as fracture within and between peoples, the visible sign that covenant order is dissolving

**Opposite:** $peace (*shalom*) — covenant wholeness, fulfillment, everything in its proper relationship

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:6, 2 Chr 15:5–6, Jer 51:46, Dan 9:26, Judg 5:8

**The surprise:** The Hebrew root of "war" (H3898 *lacham* — to fight, to devour) is the same root as "bread" (*lechem*). The word for covenant conflict and the word for covenant sustenance share an origin. Where $bread nourishes covenant life, war consumes it.

**Connected:** $nation-against-nation, $birth-pains, $famine, $earthquake, $pestilence, $the-end, $deceive, $babylon

## Wars Across Scripture

### The Root That Eats

H4421 *milchamah* (war, battle) occurs roughly 319 times in the Old Testament. It derives from H3898 *lacham* — a root with two faces: **to fight** and **to eat/devour**. The noun *lechem* (bread) comes from the same root.

This is not a lexical accident. The root carries a primal idea: consumption. $bread sustains — it is the covenant Word that feeds life (Deut 8:3). War devours — it is the covenant fracture that consumes life. They are the same root moving in opposite directions.

In the New Testament, the Greek equivalent is G4171 *polemos*, distinct from G3163 *machē* (a quarrel, a personal fight). *Polemos* is systemic conflict — organized, structural, the kind that tears apart nations and kingdoms. When Jesus and James and Revelation use *polemos*, they are describing something larger than an argument. They are describing the breakdown of order itself.

### When New Gods Were Chosen

> *"They chose new gods; then was **war** in the gates."* — Judg 5:8

This single verse from Deborah's song encodes the entire pattern. The causation is explicit: Israel chose new gods → war appeared in the gates. Not foreign invasion as punishment from outside, but conflict *at the gates* — the place of community governance, the seat of covenant order. Idolatry produces internal collapse.

The pattern repeats across the Old Testament with remarkable consistency:

**Idolatry → Fracture → War → Judgment → Restoration.**

> *"And I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians: and they shall fight every one against his brother, and every one against his neighbour; city against city, and kingdom against kingdom."* — Isa 19:2

Egypt's judgment is not foreign conquest. It is **self-destruction**. God sets Egyptians against Egyptians. The same people, devouring each other. The oracle continues: "the spirit of Egypt shall fail in the midst thereof; and I will destroy the counsel thereof: and they shall seek to the idols" (Isa 19:3). The cycle is clear: failed counsel → idolatry → internal war. The fracture radiates outward from a broken center.

Isaiah 9 shows the same pattern within Israel itself:

> *"Through the wrath of the LORD of hosts is the land darkened, and the people shall be as the fuel of the fire: no man shall spare his brother. And he shall snatch on the right hand, and be hungry; and he shall eat on the left hand, and they shall not be satisfied: they shall eat every man the flesh of his own arm: Manasseh, Ephraim; and Ephraim, Manasseh: and they together shall be against Judah."* — Isa 9:19–21

Tribe against tribe. Brother consuming brother. The *lacham* root in its darkest expression — not bread-eating but self-devouring. And the agent behind it all: "through the wrath of the LORD of hosts."

### The Template Jesus Quoted

The Olivet Discourse does not come from nowhere. When Jesus says "nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom" (Mat 24:7), he is using a formula his audience would recognize — the exact language of 2 Chronicles 15:

> *"And **nation** was destroyed of **nation**, and **city** of **city**: for God did vex them with all adversity."* — 2 Chr 15:6

The prophet Azariah speaks this to King Asa during a period of reform. He is describing what happened *before* the reform — the years when "Israel hath been without the true God, and without a teaching priest, and without law" (2 Chr 15:3). No Word, no priest, no Torah → no peace, and nation against nation.

The Greek of Matthew 24:7 matches the LXX of 2 Chronicles 15:6 closely: *ethnos* against *ethnos*. Jesus is not inventing new apocalyptic imagery. He is pointing back to a historical pattern and saying: this is what covenant dissolution looks like. It has looked this way before. It will look this way again. And it is *not the telos* — not the goal, not the consummation.

The distinction matters. Wars and rumors of wars are **process signs**, not arrival signs. They belong to what Jesus calls "the beginning of $birth-pains" (Mat 24:8) — the labor that precedes delivery, not the delivery itself. The covenant is fracturing, but the new order has not yet emerged.

### "The End Is Not Yet"

> *"And ye shall hear of **wars** and **rumours of wars**: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but **the end is not yet**."* — Mat 24:6

> *"For **nation** shall rise against **nation**, and **kingdom** against **kingdom**: and there shall be **$[famine]s**, and **$[pestilence]s**, and **$[earthquake]s**, in divers places. All these are the beginning of **$[birth-pains]**."* — Mat 24:7–8

Jesus clusters the signs: wars, $nation-against-nation, $famine, $pestilence, $earthquake. Then he names the category: "the beginning of sorrows" — G5604 *odin*, birth pains. These are the contractions, not the birth. They signal that something is coming, but the thing itself has not arrived.

Daniel makes the same distinction. After Messiah is "cut off," the period that follows is characterized by ongoing conflict:

> *"And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the end of the **war** desolations are determined."* — Dan 9:26

"Unto the end" — wars and desolations stretch across the entire inter-advent period. They are the sustained condition, not the climax. Dan 9:26 describes the landscape *between* the cutting-off and the consummation: a landscape defined by ongoing conflict. The wars are the terrain, not the destination.

### Rumors in the Land

> *"And lest your heart faint, and ye fear for the **rumour** that shall be heard in the land; a **rumour** shall both come one year, and after that in another year shall come a **rumour**, and violence in the land, ruler against ruler."* — Jer 51:46

Jeremiah's oracle against $babylon introduces the phrase "rumors in the land" — a year, then another year, ruler against ruler. The anxiety is perpetual. The instability self-reproducing. Wars and *rumors* of wars captures not only the conflict but the **atmosphere** of conflict — the constant expectation of fracture, the social fabric humming with threat even before the blow falls.

This is why Jesus says "see that ye be not troubled" (G2360 *throeō* — to cry aloud in alarm, to be frightened by noise). The rumors are designed to disturb. The sound of approaching fracture is itself a form of judgment. But Jesus' instruction is: do not let the noise of dissolution become the object of your attention. These things "must come to pass" — they are necessary stages in the process — "but the end is not yet."

### YHWH — Man of War

> *"The LORD is a **man of war**: the LORD is his name."* — Exod 15:3

The symbol carries an anomaly that must be faced. War in Scripture is not only a symptom of covenant fracture — it is also a divine instrument. YHWH wages war. He fights on behalf of His people against covenant-breakers.

The distinction is between **war as symptom** and **war as tool**. When covenant peoples fracture against each other (Isa 19:2, 2 Chr 15:6, Hos 7:11's Ephraim running to Assyria), war is the visible sign of dissolution — judgment manifesting as internal collapse. But when YHWH acts as "man of war" against Pharaoh (Exod 15:3), or when the angel of the LORD draws his sword (Josh 5:13–14), or when the heavenly armies ride out in Revelation 19:11–14, war is the *enforcement* of covenant terms against those who violate them.

Both are covenant conflict. The difference is directional. One tears apart what should be united. The other removes what refuses to submit. Both confirm that war in Scripture is never merely political. It is always theological — always a function of covenant relationship, whether dissolving or enforcing.

### The Cosmic War — Revelation's Polemos

Revelation takes *polemos* to its fullest scope:

> *"And there was **war** in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon; and the dragon fought and his angels."* — Rev 12:7

> *"And it was given unto him to make **war** with the saints, and to overcome them."* — Rev 13:7

> *"These shall make **war** with the Lamb, and the Lamb shall overcome them."* — Rev 17:14

> *"And I saw the beast, and the kings of the earth, and their armies, gathered together to make **war** against him that sat on the horse."* — Rev 19:19

Every instance of *polemos* in Revelation is covenant conflict — the beast against the saints, the dragon against heaven, the kings against the Lamb. There is no secular war in the Apocalypse. Every battle is between those within the covenant structure and those who have set themselves against it.

And the wars end. They are resolved. The beast is captured, the dragon bound, the kings defeated. The consummation is not more war but the cessation of war — $peace, the restoration of covenant wholeness.

### God Makes Wars Cease

> *"He maketh **wars** to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire."* — Ps 46:9

> *"Scatter thou the people that delight in **war**."* — Ps 68:30

The end of war is not a peace treaty. It is God breaking the instruments of conflict. The bow, the spear, the chariot — all consumed. This is not negotiation but divine intervention. War ceases because God removes the capacity for it.

This completes the theological arc: covenant violation → internal fracture → war as sign → divine judgment through war → divine cessation of war → restoration. The wars are never permanent. They are always penultimate — always pointing toward something beyond themselves.

### Wars from Within

> *"From whence come **wars** and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that **war** in your members?"* — Jas 4:1

James traces *polemos* to its interior origin. The wars among the covenant community arise from desires within — unfaithful desires, divided loyalties, the internal fracture that precedes the external one. This is the micro-version of the macro-pattern: idolatry of heart → fracture of community → visible conflict.

The same movement: Judg 5:8 ("they chose new gods; then was war in the gates") and Jas 4:1–4 ("ye adulterers and adulteresses, know ye not that the friendship of the world is enmity with God?"). War begins in the heart's divided allegiance and manifests in the community's visible fracture.

## Patterns

1. **War is a symptom, not the disease.** Every war cluster traces back to covenant abandonment: new gods (Judg 5:8), no teaching priest (2 Chr 15:3), internal desires (Jas 4:1). War is what covenant fracture *looks like* on the surface.

2. **"Not yet the end."** Jesus explicitly separates wars from the $the-end (G5056 *telos* — the goal). Wars are process, not consummation. They belong to the beginning of $birth-pains, not the delivery.

3. **God is the agent.** "God did vex them" (2 Chr 15:6). "I will set Egyptians against Egyptians" (Isa 19:2). The fracturing is not accidental — it is divine discipline for covenant unfaithfulness.

4. **Internal fracture, not external conquest.** The defining pattern is brother against brother, city against city, nation against nation within a people. The destruction is self-consuming. The *lacham* root (fight/devour) captures it: the people eat themselves.

5. **Rumors are part of the sign.** Jer 51:46 — rumor after rumor, year after year. The atmosphere of instability, the constant threat of collapse, is itself a form of judgment. Jesus addresses this directly: "see that ye be not troubled."

6. **Wars stretch across the inter-advent period.** Dan 9:26 — "unto the end of the war desolations are determined." The condition persists until the consummation. Wars are the sustained landscape of the age between Messiah's cutting-off and the goal's arrival.

7. **War ceases at restoration.** Ps 46:9, Isa 2:4, Rev 19:19–21. When God acts finally, wars do not taper off — they are ended. Instruments broken, peoples scattered, beast captured. The cessation of war is itself a covenant act.

8. **YHWH wages war as enforcement.** Exod 15:3, Rev 19:11. Not all war is symptom; some is divine tool. The distinction: symptom-war fractures covenant peoples internally; instrument-war removes covenant-breakers externally. Both are theological, never merely political.

## Connections

- **$nation-against-nation** — The specific form wars take in the prophetic formula. Jesus uses the 2 Chr 15:6 phrase verbatim: *ethnos* against *ethnos*. Nation-against-nation is the internal fracture pattern — peoples who should be unified devouring each other under divine discipline.

- **$birth-pains** — Jesus categorizes wars as "the beginning of sorrows" (Mat 24:8). Wars are contractions, not the birth — the painful process that precedes the new covenant order. They signal transition, not termination.

- **$famine** — Listed alongside wars in the Olivet Discourse and throughout the prophetic triad (sword, $famine, $pestilence). If $famine represents God's Word withdrawn, then war is what happens in the vacuum — conflict fills the space where covenant instruction once held order.

- **$earthquake** — Co-occurring Olivet sign. If $earthquake represents God shaking every established order, wars are one form of that shaking — the political and social upheaval dimension of God removing what is unfaithful (Heb 12:27).

- **$pestilence** — The third member of the prophetic triad (Jer 14:12, Ezek 5:12). Sword (external violence) → $famine (Word-withdrawal) → $pestilence (direct lethal consequence). The triad escalates: conflict, emptiness, death.

- **$the-end** — Jesus explicitly separates wars from the *telos*. Wars are not the goal — they are the landscape through which the covenant process moves toward its consummation. Dan 9:26 confirms: desolations are "determined" until the end, not identical with it.

- **$deceive** — The first sign Jesus names, *before* wars (Mat 24:4–5): "Take heed that no man $[deceive] you." Deception precedes war in the Olivet sequence — misrepresentation of God's commands fractures allegiance, and fractured allegiance produces conflict.

- **$babylon** — Jer 51:46 places "rumors in the land" within the oracle against $babylon. Babylon is the system that produces and sustains covenant conflict — ruler against ruler, violence perpetuated — until God judges the system itself.

- **$peace** — The direct opposite. H7965 *shalom* = covenant wholeness, completeness, everything in right relationship. War = covenant fracture. Peace = covenant fulfillment. "He maketh wars to cease" (Ps 46:9) is the restoration of *shalom*.

## For Further Study

- **$nation-against-nation** — The specific fracture formula Jesus borrows from 2 Chronicles. How does the internal-destruction pattern differ from foreign conquest, and why does Scripture emphasize the difference?

- **$birth-pains** — Wars are the *beginning* of birth pains. What is being born? The birth-pain symbol traces from Isaiah through the Olivet Discourse to Revelation 12.

- **$famine** — If famine represents the withdrawal of God's Word, what is the relationship between Word-absence and the outbreak of war? The prophetic triad (sword, famine, pestilence) may encode a causal sequence, not just a list.

- **$the-end** — Jesus says wars are "not yet the end." What *is* the end, and how does G5056 *telos* (goal/purpose) differ from mere cessation?

- **$babylon** — The system that sustains perpetual conflict. Jer 51:46's "rumors in the land" describes Babylon's atmosphere. How does Babylon's fall relate to the cessation of wars?

- **$peace** — The opposite of covenant fracture. How does *shalom* function as the resolution of everything war represents — and why does Ezekiel 13:10 warn against false prophets who say "peace" when there is none?
