# Wise

**Obedient — one who hears God's instruction and does it.**

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that **do** his commandments."* — Ps 111:10

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture defines wisdom three times in explicit terms. Each time the definition is the same — and it has nothing to do with intelligence.

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that **do his commandments**."* — Ps 111:10

> *"**Keep** therefore and **do** them; for this is your **wisdom** and your understanding in the sight of the nations."* — Deut 4:6

> *"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and **doeth** them, I will liken him unto a **wise** man, which built his house upon a $[rock]."* — Mat 7:24

Three passages. Three different authors. Three different centuries. The same equation: **wise = hears + does**. Not hears and understands. Not hears and discusses. Hears and *does*.

This is not a secondary theme Scripture hints at. It is a definition Scripture states outright — and then builds every wisdom parable upon.

**Symbolizes:** Obedient — one who receives and keeps God's commandments

**Opposite:** $fool — disobedient, one who hears but does not do

**Defining verses:** Ps 111:10, Deut 4:6, Mat 7:24

**The surprise:** The Greek word translated "wise" in the parable of the ten virgins and the two builders is not G4680 *sophos* (the philosopher's word) — it is G5429 *phronimos*, which means *practically sensible, prudent in action*. Jesus chose the word for applied wisdom, not theoretical knowledge.

**Connected:** $rock, $sand, $oil, $light, $faith, $truth

---

## Wise Across Scripture

### The Three Definitions

It is rare for Scripture to define a symbol explicitly. It is extraordinary for Scripture to define the same symbol three times in nearly identical terms.

In **Psalm 111:10**, the definition comes at the end of an acrostic psalm praising God's works: "The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that **do** his commandments." The word "do" (H6213 *asah*) is the pivot. Wisdom is not contemplation of the commandments — it is the doing of them.

In **Deuteronomy 4:6**, Moses addresses Israel before they enter the land: "Keep therefore and do them; for this is your **wisdom** (H2451 *chokmah*) and your understanding in the sight of the nations." The nations will look at Israel and say, "Surely this great nation is a wise and understanding people" (Deut 4:6). What will they see? Not philosophical schools. Not intellectual achievement. They will see a people who *keep the statutes*.

In **Matthew 7:24**, Jesus closes the Sermon on the Mount with the same definition: "whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and **doeth** them, I will liken him unto a **wise** (G5429 *phronimos*) man." The wise man is not the one who heard the sermon and found it profound. He is the one who went home and *did* it.

Three testaments of evidence — Torah, Psalms, Gospel — converging on a single definition. Wisdom is obedience. Obedience is wisdom.

### The Wise and the Fool — A Perfect Antithesis

Scripture never defines the wise without also defining the fool. The two always appear as a matched pair — and the contrast is always the same:

| Wise | Fool |
|------|------|
| Hears + **does** (Mat 7:24) | Hears + **does not** (Mat 7:26) |
| Receives commandments (Prov 10:8) | Falls (Prov 10:8) |
| Hearkens to counsel (Prov 12:15) | Right in his own eyes (Prov 12:15) |
| Fears the LORD (Prov 1:7) | Despises wisdom + instruction (Prov 1:7) |
| Had $oil (Mat 25:4) | Had no $oil (Mat 25:3) |
| Built on $rock (Mat 7:24) | Built on $sand (Mat 7:26) |

> *"The **wise** in heart will receive commandments: but a prating **fool** shall fall."* — Prov 10:8

The wise *receives* commandments. The Hebrew H3947 *laqach* (receive, take, accept) implies active reception — grasping hold of something offered. This is not passive hearing. It is the deliberate act of taking instruction into one's hands and carrying it out.

> *"The way of a **fool** is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is **wise**."* — Prov 12:15

The fool's defining trait is self-direction: "right in his own eyes." The wise person's defining trait is the opposite: hearkening (H8085 *shama* — to hear and obey) unto counsel. The fool has ears but uses only his own judgment. The wise person listens — and adjusts.

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but **fools** despise wisdom and instruction."* — Prov 1:7

The $fool does not lack access to instruction. He *despises* it (H936 *buz* — to hold in contempt). The issue has never been capacity. It is posture. The wise person bows before instruction. The fool stands over it.

And the extreme case:

> *"The **fool** hath said in his heart, There is no God."* — Ps 14:1

If wise = obedient to God's word, then the ultimate fool is the one who denies there is anyone to obey.

### Built on Rock, Built on Sand

> *"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a **wise** man, which built his house upon a **$[rock]**: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a **$[rock]**."* — Mat 7:24–25

> *"And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them **not**, shall be likened unto a **foolish** man, which built his house upon the **$[sand]**: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it."* — Mat 7:26–27

The parable is the definition in architectural form. Both builders *heard* the same words. Both experienced the same storm. The only variable is whether they *did* what they heard.

The $rock — the unchanging covenant — holds the wise man's house through the storm. The $sand — shifting ground, no covenant foundation — collapses under the foolish man's. Obedience connects a person to the $rock. Disobedience leaves them on $sand.

Notice: the storm comes to both houses. Wisdom does not prevent trials. It determines whether you survive them. The wise builder is not spared the rain — he is spared the collapse.

### The Ten Virgins — Wise Had Oil

> *"Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom. And five of them were **wise** (G5429 *phronimos*), and five were foolish."* — Mat 25:1–2

> *"They that were foolish took their lamps, and took no **$[oil]** with them: But the **wise** took **$[oil]** in their vessels with their lamps."* — Mat 25:3–4

Again: the same Greek word *phronimos*. Again: the contrast with the foolish. And again: the defining difference is action, not knowledge.

All ten virgins had lamps. All ten went out to meet the bridegroom. All ten waited. But only five had $oil — the proven works produced by tested obedience. The foolish had the equipment (lamps — commandments to keep) but not the accumulated record of having kept them ($oil — works tested through trial).

When the bridegroom finally arrives, the foolish ask the wise to share their $oil. The answer is telling: "Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you: but go ye rather to them that sell, and **buy** for yourselves" (Mat 25:9). Proven works cannot be borrowed. No one can be obedient on your behalf. The wise had prepared — the doing had been done in advance, over time, through faithfulness.

> *"**Watch** therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour."* — Mat 25:13

The conclusion is not "be smarter." It is "watch" — remain active, remain prepared, remain *doing*. The wise are those found doing when the master arrives.

### Become a Fool to Become Wise

> *"Let no man deceive himself. If any man among you seemeth to be **wise** (G4680 *sophos*) in this world, let him become a **fool**, that he may be **wise**."* — 1 Cor 3:18

Paul uses the world's word for wisdom — *sophos* — and inverts it. If the world's "wisdom" is self-directed reasoning (the fool of Prov 12:15, "right in his own eyes"), then becoming a "fool" by the world's standard means surrendering self-direction. And surrendering self-direction to God's instruction is precisely what Scripture calls wisdom.

The reversal only makes sense if wisdom = obedience. Worldly wisdom = following your own understanding. Godly wisdom = abandoning your own understanding to follow God's commands. To become wise, you must first stop being wise in your own eyes.

### Walk as Wise

> *"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as unwise, but as **wise** (G4680 *sophos*), redeeming the time, because the days are evil."* — Eph 5:15–16

Walking is the consistent biblical metaphor for conduct — for how one *lives*. Paul does not say "think as wise" or "believe as wise." He says *walk* as wise. Wisdom is a mode of living, not a mode of thinking. The wise walk carefully, attentively, making the most of every opportunity for obedience.

## Patterns

1. **Wisdom is always defined by doing.** Every explicit definition of wisdom in Scripture includes an action verb: *do*, *keep*, *receive*, *hearken*. Not once is wisdom defined as understanding alone.

2. **The fool hears the same words.** Mat 7:24–26 — both builders heard. Mat 25:1–4 — all ten virgins went out. Prov 1:7 — the fool has access to wisdom and instruction but despises it. The difference is never access to information. It is always response to instruction.

3. **Wise is always paired with its opposite.** Scripture rarely mentions the wise without also mentioning the fool. The pair functions as obedient/disobedient, doer/non-doer — never as smart/unintelligent.

4. **The wise build on $rock.** Obedience connects the hearer to the covenant foundation. Disobedience leaves the hearer standing on $sand — shifting, unstable, exposed to judgment.

5. **The wise have $oil.** The wise virgins' $oil represents proven works — the accumulated evidence of obedience. Wisdom is not a momentary decision but a pattern of faithful doing that produces reserves for the day of testing.

6. **Worldly wisdom is the fool's posture.** Being "right in his own eyes" (Prov 12:15) is the world's version of wisdom — autonomous reasoning, self-trust. Biblical wisdom begins where self-trust ends: "the fear of the LORD is the beginning" (Prov 1:7, Ps 111:10).

## Connections

- **$rock** — The wise man builds on the $rock (Mat 7:24). The $rock is the covenant — the unchanging foundation. Obedience binds a person to the covenant; the wise man's house stands because it is anchored in what does not shift.

- **$sand** — The fool builds on $sand (Mat 7:26). $sand = multitudes without covenant foundation. Hearing without doing leaves a person unanchored — exposed when the storm arrives.

- **$oil** — The wise virgins had $oil (Mat 25:4). $oil = proven works, the product of obedience tested through trial. Wisdom that does not *produce* something over time is the foolish virgins' empty lamp.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience — hearing God's word and acting on it. The wise man of Mat 7:24 is a portrait of $faith in action: he heard, he trusted, he did. Wisdom and $faith are the same posture viewed from different angles.

- **$truth** — $truth is God's commandment, the standard to be obeyed. The wise person is the one who receives $truth and acts on it. Without $truth, there is nothing to obey; without wisdom, $truth remains unapplied.

- **$light** — $light is the visible example produced by obedience. The wise person's doing becomes visible — "let your $[light] so shine before men, that they may see your good works" (Mat 5:16). Wisdom produces $oil, and $oil fuels the lamp that gives $light.

- **$fool** — The wise person's mirror image. Every quality of the wise is negated in the $fool: receives → rejects, hearkens → self-directs, does → neglects, fears the LORD → says "no God."

## Hebrew & Greek Reference

| Strong's | Word | Meaning | Notes |
|----------|------|---------|-------|
| H2450 | *chakam* | wise, skillful, experienced | Most common OT adjective for "wise" |
| H2451 | *chokmah* | wisdom, skill | Noun form — Ps 111:10, Deut 4:6 |
| G4680 | *sophos* | wise | Used in 1 Cor 3:18, Eph 5:15 — the "philosophical" term |
| G5429 | *phronimos* | prudent, sensible, practically wise | Used in Mat 7:24, Mat 25:2 — wisdom *in action* |
| H3684 | *kesiyl* | fool, stupid fellow | Common OT antonym of H2450 |
| H191 | *eviyl* | fool (morally perverse) | Prov 12:15 — "The way of a fool" |
| G3474 | *moros* | foolish, dull | Mat 7:26, Mat 25:2 — paired against *phronimos* |

KJV translates both G4680 and G5429 as "wise," but the distinction matters. G4680 *sophos* is the broader term — it is what the world claims. G5429 *phronimos* is the active, applied term — it is what Jesus uses in His parables of doing.

## For Further Study

- **$rock** — The wise man's foundation. What is the covenant that holds when the storms come, and why does building on it require *doing*, not merely knowing?
- **$sand** — The fool's foundation. What does it mean to stand among the multitudes without a covenant anchor?
- **$oil** — What the wise virgins had. How are proven works produced, and why can they not be borrowed?
- **$faith** — The posture behind the doing. How does Scripture equate belief and obedience — and unbelief and disobedience?
- **$truth** — The standard being obeyed. If wisdom is the doing, $truth is the content of what is done. What are God's commandments, and why does Scripture call them $truth?
- **$fool** — The wise person's opposite in every passage. What does the fool represent, and what is the consistent pattern of hearing without doing?
