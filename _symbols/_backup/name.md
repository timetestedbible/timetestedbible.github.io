# Name

**Covenant — the binding relationship between God and His people, not a label or pronunciation.**

> *"He shall build an house for my **name**."* — 2 Sam 7:13

---

## The Key Insight

David wants to build a house for the ark of God (2 Sam 7:2). God responds: "He shall build an house for my **name**" (2 Sam 7:13).

God substitutes *name* for *ark of the covenant*. The two terms sit in the same grammatical slot, referring to the same reality. The house David's son will build is not for a pronunciation. It is for the covenant itself.

Solomon understood. In the dedication prayer of 1 Kings 8, "the name" appears sixteen times — and every single instance is embedded in covenant language: forgiveness when the people repent, restoration when they return, faithfulness to the promises made to the fathers. The temple is "the house of the name" because it is the house of the covenant.

This single substitution — name for ark of the covenant — unlocks a pattern that runs through the entire Bible. Everywhere Scripture speaks of "the name," it is speaking of the covenant. And this resolves two of the most persistent paradoxes readers encounter in the text.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant — the binding relationship between God and a person or people

**Opposite:** Namelessness — being without covenant, having no binding relationship with God

**Defining verses:** 2 Sam 7:13, Exod 6:3, Acts 4:12, Mat 7:22–23, Exod 20:7, Deut 28:58

**The surprise:** The Third Commandment — "Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain" — may have nothing to do with how you pronounce a word. If name = covenant, then taking the name in vain = treating the covenant as empty, living as though it doesn't matter. This reframes the commandment from a speech rule into the most searching demand in Scripture.

**Connected:** $rock, $mark, $marriage, $bread, $wickedness, $faith, $believe, $the-way

## Name Across Scripture

### The Abraham Paradox

> *"And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my **name** JEHOVAH was I not known to them."* — Exod 6:3

This verse has troubled readers for centuries. God says the patriarchs did not know Him by the name YHWH — yet YHWH is spoken *to* Abraham repeatedly:

> *"I am the LORD (YHWH) that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees."* — Gen 15:7

> *"Is any thing too hard for the LORD (YHWH)?"* — Gen 18:14

> *"By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD (YHWH)."* — Gen 22:16

Abraham clearly heard the word YHWH. God clearly spoke it to him. So what does Exod 6:3 mean when it says the patriarchs did not *know* God by that name?

If name = pronunciation, this is a flat contradiction. But if name = covenant, the paradox dissolves. Abraham knew God as El Shaddai (God Almighty) — the God of promise. But the *covenant relationship* that YHWH represents — the national covenant, with Torah, priesthood, tabernacle, the full structure given at Sinai — was not yet established. The patriarchs experienced God's promises but did not yet enter the covenant reality that the name YHWH embodies.

"By my name YHWH was I not known to them" becomes: "The covenant relationship that I AM was not yet revealed to them." They knew the word. They did not yet have the covenant.

### "Lord, Lord" — The Name Without the Covenant

> *"Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy **name**? and in thy **name** have cast out devils? and in thy **name** done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity."* — Mat 7:22–23

Here is the opposite problem. These people invoke the name constantly. They prophesy, cast out demons, perform miracles — all "in thy name." Yet Jesus says *I never knew you* and calls them "workers of iniquity" — G458 *anomia*, literally **lawlessness**.

If name = a spoken word, then these people are using it correctly. They say the right name. They do impressive things. Why are they rejected?

If name = covenant, the answer emerges immediately. They invoke the covenant but do not *keep* it. They use the name but live as covenant-breakers — workers of $wickedness, which Deut 17:2 explicitly defines as "transgressing his covenant." The name is on their lips; the covenant is not in their lives.

This connects directly to the Third Commandment. They have taken the name — entered into the covenant, claimed its authority — and made it *vain* (H7723 *shav* = empty, worthless, desolate). They carry the covenant label with no covenant substance.

### The Third Commandment Reframed

> *"Thou shalt not take the **name** of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his **name** in vain."* — Exod 20:7

Conventional reading: don't say God's name carelessly. But the Hebrew *nasa* (H5375 — "take") means to lift up, bear, carry. It is the word used for bearing a burden, carrying a load. And *shav* (H7723 — "in vain") means emptiness, worthlessness, desolation.

If name = covenant: "You shall not *carry* the covenant of the LORD your God as an empty thing." Do not bear the covenant and treat it as nothing. Do not claim the relationship and live as though it imposes no obligations.

This is precisely what Mat 7:22–23 describes. The workers of lawlessness *took* the name — they bore it, carried it, invoked it — and made it vain. They emptied the covenant of its content while wearing its label.

The severity of the warning — "the LORD will not hold him guiltless" — makes far more sense as a covenant warning than as a pronunciation rule. The most serious divine threat in the Ten Commandments is not about speech habits. It is about the integrity of the covenant relationship itself.

### No Other Name — No Other Covenant

> *"Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other **name** under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."* — Acts 4:12

This is among the most frequently quoted verses in Scripture. If name = covenant, it becomes: *there is no other covenant under heaven given among men whereby we must be saved.*

This does not diminish the verse. It sharpens it. The claim is not merely that a particular word has saving power, but that a particular *covenant relationship* is the only path to salvation. The exclusivity is relational, not phonetic. There is one covenant, one mediator (1 Tim 2:5), one $rock (1 Cor 10:4).

### Fear This Name — Torah and the Covenant

> *"If thou wilt not observe to do all the words of this law that are written in this book, that thou mayest fear this glorious and fearful **name**, THE LORD THY GOD…"* — Deut 28:58

Moses connects Torah-keeping directly to the name. Observing the words of the law = fearing the name. If name = covenant, this is not two separate commands but a single statement: *keep the covenant terms so that you properly honor the covenant relationship.*

The entire structure of Deuteronomy 28 is blessings for covenant-keeping and curses for covenant-breaking. The name sits at the center of this structure — the relationship that the blessings protect and the curses enforce.

### Baptized in the Name — Entering Covenant

The phrase "in the name of" appears throughout the New Testament — baptism in the name, prayer in the name, authority in the name. In each case, the substitution of "covenant" illuminates:

Baptism in the name = entering into the covenant. This parallels $marriage, where the bride takes the husband's name — not merely learning a new label, but entering a binding relationship with all its obligations and privileges. When $believe is understood as trusting obedience and $faith as the active commitment that obedience requires, baptism in the name becomes the visible entry into covenant, the moment of publicly bearing the relationship.

Prayer in the name = appealing on the basis of the covenant. "Whatsoever ye shall ask in my name" (Jn 14:13) is not a formula appended to the end of prayers. It is an invocation of covenant standing — asking as one who is in the relationship, on the grounds of the relationship.

Authority in the name = exercising covenant authority. "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk" (Acts 3:6) — Peter acts within the covenant, wielding its authority. This is why Mat 7:22 is so devastating: those who exercised the authority without maintaining the relationship are ultimately disowned.

### Solomon's Prayer — The Name Fills the Temple

The dedication of the temple in 1 Kings 8 provides the most sustained meditation on the name in Scripture. Solomon prays:

> *"That thine eyes may be open toward this house night and day, even toward the place of which thou hast said, My **name** shall be there."* — 1 Ki 8:29

God's name — His covenant presence — dwells in the temple. Every petition in Solomon's prayer follows the same pattern: when the people sin, when they are defeated, when there is famine, when there is plague — *if they pray toward this house where your name dwells*, then hear and forgive.

The pattern is always: covenant breach → repentance → appeal to the name → restoration. The name is the anchor point. It is the relationship that makes restoration possible. Without the name in the temple — without the covenant in the meeting place — there is no mechanism for return.

This is why 2 Sam 7:13 carries such weight. The house is not built for a word. It is built for the covenant. The temple is the physical structure that houses the covenant relationship between God and Israel.

## Patterns

1. **Name = Ark of the Covenant.** The explicit substitution in 2 Sam 7:2 → 7:13. David says "ark of God." God responds "my name." The temple houses the name because it houses the covenant.

2. **Knowing the name = entering the covenant relationship.** Exod 6:3 — the patriarchs did not *know* the name YHWH, though they heard the word. Knowing = relational engagement with the covenant, not phonetic familiarity.

3. **Taking the name in vain = emptying the covenant.** Exod 20:7 — bearing the covenant as worthless. Mat 7:22–23 — invoking the name while practicing $wickedness (lawlessness = covenant transgression, Deut 17:2).

4. **No other name = no other covenant.** Acts 4:12 — the exclusivity claim is relational, not phonetic. One covenant, one mediator, one way of salvation.

5. **Fearing the name = keeping the covenant terms.** Deut 28:58 ties Torah observance directly to honoring the name. The $bread (Word/covenant terms) and the name (covenant relationship) are inseparable.

6. **"In the name" = within/upon the authority of the covenant.** Baptism, prayer, miraculous works — all operate within the covenant framework or not at all.

7. **The name resolves apparent contradictions.** Both the Abraham paradox (Exod 6:3 vs. Gen 15:7) and the "Lord, Lord" paradox (Mat 7:22–23) dissolve when name = covenant.

## Connections

- **$rock** — "He shall build a house for my name" (2 Sam 7:13). The $rock is the unchanging covenant; the name is the covenant relationship itself. Both point to the same reality from different angles — the $rock emphasizes its permanence, the name emphasizes its relational, binding nature.

- **$marriage** — The bride takes the husband's name. In $marriage, name-taking = entering the covenant bond. "Thy Maker is thine husband" (Isa 54:5) — the name of the husband is the covenant of the husband.

- **$mark** — The $mark is the visible practice that reveals which covenant you belong to. The name is the covenant itself; the $mark is the evidence of it. Rev 14:1 — the Father's *name* in their foreheads. Rev 13:16 — the beast's *mark* in hand and forehead. Name identifies the covenant; mark identifies the practice.

- **$wickedness** — Deut 17:2 defines $wickedness as "transgressing his covenant." Mat 7:22–23 — workers of $wickedness invoke the name but break the covenant. Wickedness is the antithesis of what the name demands.

- **$bread** — Deut 28:58 connects the words of the law ($bread = Word/covenant terms) to fearing the name. The $bread is the content of the covenant; the name is the relationship that the content defines.

- **$faith** — $faith is the trusting obedience that the covenant requires. Taking the name without $faith = taking it in vain. The name establishes the relationship; $faith is the response to it.

- **$believe** — To $believe on the name (Jn 1:12, 1 Jn 3:23) is to commit to obey the covenant. $believe is the verb of covenant entry — trusting commitment that produces action.

- **$the-way** — Torah is the path the covenant defines. The name is the relationship; $the-way is the walk within it. "All the words of this law… that thou mayest fear this name" (Deut 28:58).
