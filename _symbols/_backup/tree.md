# Tree

**A nation or people group — rooted in its origin, known by its $fruit, sheltering $animal peoples under its branches, and cut down when it produces nothing.**

> *"For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant."* — Isa 5:7

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not leave the meaning of the tree to guesswork. It defines it outright — repeatedly, across centuries of text, through prophets, kings, apostles, and Jesus himself.

Daniel interprets Nebuchadnezzar's dream: the great **tree** whose height reached to heaven, visible to all the earth, under which the beasts dwelt? "It is thou, O king" — and with the king, his kingdom (Dan 4:20–22). Ezekiel identifies the towering cedar: "Behold, the Assyrian was a **cedar** in Lebanon" (Ezek 31:3), and under its shadow "dwelt all great nations" (Ezek 31:6). Paul writes of the olive **tree** into which Gentiles are grafted and from which natural branches are broken off — and the tree is unmistakably $israel (Rom 11:17–24). Hosea opens his indictment: "Israel is an empty vine, he bringeth forth $[fruit] unto himself" (Hos 10:1). Isaiah names it outright: "the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel" (Isa 5:7).

A tree is a nation. Its root is the nation's origin. Its branches are the nation's people. Its $fruit is the nation's works. Its shadow is the nation's shelter. And when a tree is cut down, a nation has been judged.

Once this equation is in place, every tree passage in Scripture — from Eden's two trees to the cursed fig tree to John 15's vine — takes on structural clarity.

**Symbolizes:** A nation or people group — its character, its works, and its standing before God

**Opposite:** A cut-down tree, a dry tree — a nation under judgment, stripped of life and shelter

**Defining verses:** Dan 4:20–22, Isa 5:7, Ezek 31:3–6, Rom 11:17–24, Hos 10:1

**The surprise:** Every component of the tree maps to a component of national life. The root = the patriarchal or covenant origin (Rom 11:16–18). The branches = the members of the nation (Rom 11:17–21). The $fruit = the works or deeds the nation produces (Mat 7:17). The shadow = the protection and shelter the nation provides (Ezek 31:6, Dan 4:12). The leaves = outward prosperity (Dan 4:12). Not one detail is wasted.

**Connected:** $mountain, $sea, $animal, $fruit, $thorns, $babylon, $grass

## Tree Across Scripture

### The Explicit Identifications

Five passages make the equation impossible to miss. They do not compare a nation *to* a tree as metaphor — they declare that the tree *is* the nation.

**Daniel 4:20–22 — The tree is Nebuchadnezzar's kingdom.**

> *"The **tree** that thou sawest, which grew, and was strong, whose height reached unto the heaven, and the sight thereof to all the earth; Whose leaves were fair, and the **fruit** thereof much, and in it was meat for all; under which the **beasts** of the field dwelt, and upon whose branches the fowls of the heaven had their habitation: It is thou, O king."* — Dan 4:20–22

The tree = $babylon under Nebuchadnezzar. The $animal peoples dwell under it. The $fruit feeds all. When the tree is hewn down (Dan 4:23), the kingdom is judged — and the stump is left, bound with iron and brass, because a remnant survives.

**Ezekiel 31:3–6, 18 — The cedar is Assyria, then Egypt.**

> *"Behold, the Assyrian was a **cedar** in Lebanon with fair branches, and with a shadowing shroud, and of an high stature."* — Ezek 31:3

> *"All the fowls of heaven made their nests in his boughs, and under his branches did all the **beasts** of the field bring forth their young, and under his **shadow** dwelt all great nations."* — Ezek 31:6

The cedar's height = imperial greatness. Its shadow = the shelter of empire. The chapter ends with Pharaoh: "To whom art thou thus like in glory and in greatness among the **trees** of Eden?" (Ezek 31:18). The trees of Eden = the nations of the ancient world. Pharaoh's Egypt is one tree among many.

**Isaiah 5:7 — The vineyard is Israel.**

> *"For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of **Israel**, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant."* — Isa 5:7

No interpretation is needed. God planted a vineyard — He planted a nation.

**Romans 11:17–24 — The olive tree is the covenant community.**

> *"And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive **tree**, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive **tree**; Boast not against the branches."* — Rom 11:17–18

The cultivated olive tree = $israel, the covenant people. Natural branches broken off = Israelites removed through unbelief. Wild branches grafted in = Gentiles brought into the covenant community. The root = the patriarchs and promises (Rom 11:16). Paul is not using a metaphor loosely — he is working within the established symbolic grammar of Scripture.

**Hosea 10:1 — The vine is Israel.**

> *"Israel is an empty vine, he bringeth forth **$[fruit]** unto himself."* — Hos 10:1

An empty vine = a nation whose works serve only itself. Israel produces — but the $fruit goes nowhere productive. The vine exists; the $fruit is misdirected.

### Every Component Maps

What makes the tree symbol so structurally precise is that its parts are not decorative. Each component corresponds to an identifiable element of national life:

**Root** — the foundation, the origin. Paul writes: "If the root be holy, so are the branches" (Rom 11:16). The root of the olive tree = the patriarchs, the covenant promises. A tree's character begins underground, in what cannot be seen. A nation's character begins with its founding covenant.

**Branches** — the people who belong to the nation. "Some of the branches be broken off" (Rom 11:17) = members removed. "Thou wilt say then, The branches were broken off, that I might be graffed in" (Rom 11:19) = Gentiles replacing unfaithful Israelites. The branches receive life from the root. The people receive identity from the covenant.

**$fruit** — the works and deeds produced. "Every good **tree** bringeth forth good **$[fruit]**; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil $[fruit]" (Mat 7:17). $fruit = works (Gal 5:19–22 equates "works of the flesh" with "fruit of the Spirit"). A good nation produces good works. A corrupt nation produces corrupt works. And "every tree which bringeth not forth good $[fruit] is hewn down, and cast into the fire" (Mat 7:19) — a nation judged by what it produces.

**Shadow** — the protection the nation provides. "Under his shadow dwelt all great nations" (Ezek 31:6). "The beasts of the field had shadow under it" (Dan 4:12). A great empire shelters smaller peoples. When the tree is cut, the shadow vanishes, and the sheltered peoples are exposed.

**Leaves** — outward prosperity, visible flourishing. "Whose leaves were fair" (Dan 4:12). Leaves are not $fruit — they are the appearance of health, the visible markers of a thriving nation. They can exist without $fruit, which is precisely the problem Jesus addresses when He curses the fig tree with leaves but no figs (Mk 11:13–14).

### Known by Its $fruit

> *"Ye shall know them by their **$[fruit]**. Do men gather grapes of **$[thorns]**, or figs of thistles? Even so every good **tree** bringeth forth good $[fruit]; but a corrupt **tree** bringeth forth evil $[fruit]."* — Mat 7:16–17

Jesus' teaching about trees and $fruit is not generic moral instruction. It sits inside the symbolic grammar: trees = nations/people groups, $fruit = their works. You identify a nation by what it produces. A people group rooted in covenant faithfulness produces good works. A people group rooted in rebellion produces corrupt works.

> *"Every **tree** that bringeth not forth good **$[fruit]** is hewn down, and cast into the $[fire]."* — Mat 7:19

The judgment follows the same logic: nations that produce nothing of value are cut down. This is exactly what happens to Nebuchadnezzar's tree (Dan 4:23), to Assyria's cedar (Ezek 31:12), and to the barren fig tree (Lk 13:6–9).

The parable of the vine in John 15 extends this with an intimacy the prophets prepared:

> *"I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much **$[fruit]**: for without me ye can do nothing."* — Jn 15:5

If the vine = Israel's covenant community (Isa 5:7, Hos 10:1), then Jesus identifies Himself as the true vine — the true Israel. The branches (members of the community) bear $fruit only through connection to Him. Severed from the vine, they "are cast forth as a branch, and… withered" (Jn 15:6). This is the same imagery Paul develops in Romans 11: branches broken off through unbelief, branches grafted in through faith.

### Trees Among Trees — Nations Among Nations

When Scripture places multiple trees together, the image resolves as nations among nations:

> *"The **trees** of the LORD are full of sap; the cedars of Lebanon, which he hath planted."* — Ps 104:16

> *"All the **trees** of the field shall know that I the LORD have brought down the high **tree**, have exalted the low tree, have dried up the green tree, and have made the dry tree to flourish."* — Ezek 17:24

Ezekiel's language is political: bringing down the high tree and exalting the low one is what God does with nations — humbling empires and raising up the lowly. The "green tree" and "dry tree" = the flourishing nation and the withered nation. This is not about individual trees in a forest. It is about the rise and fall of peoples.

When the cedars of Lebanon appear throughout the prophets (Isa 2:13, Ezek 31:3, Zec 11:1–2), the reference is to the great empires — the towering nations of the ancient world, whose height and strength seemed permanent. And their fall is always the same: "Howl, fir tree; for the cedar is fallen" (Zec 11:2). When the great empire goes down, the smaller nations tremble.

### Trees Grow on $mountains

The symbolic geography is consistent: $mountains = kingdoms/ruling authorities. Trees grow on mountains. Nations exist under the authority of kingdoms.

The cedars of Lebanon grow on mountains. God's vine was planted on "a very fruitful hill" (Isa 5:1). Ezekiel's cedar was "in Lebanon" — elevated, visible, towering above the landscape. Daniel's tree reached to heaven — its height a picture of imperial authority that tries to touch the divine.

When $mountains are cast into the $sea (Mat 21:21), kingdoms fall into the chaos of the nations. When trees are cut down on the $mountain, the peoples under that kingdom lose their shelter. The tree-on-mountain structure parallels the $animal-under-tree structure: $mountain → kingdom → tree → nation → branches → people → $animal → specific characterized groups dwelling under the nation's shadow.

### The Cut-Down Tree — Judgment on the Nation

> *"Hew the **tree** down, and destroy it; yet leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth."* — Dan 4:23

The opposite of a flourishing tree is a cut-down tree — and it consistently signifies a nation under judgment:

- Nebuchadnezzar's tree is hewn down — $babylon's power is stripped (Dan 4:23)
- Assyria's cedar is felled — "I have delivered him into the hand of the mighty one of the heathen" (Ezek 31:11)
- "Every tree which bringeth not forth good $[fruit] is hewn down" (Mat 7:19)
- John the Baptist: "Now also the axe is laid unto the root of the **trees**: therefore every tree which bringeth not forth good $[fruit] is hewn down, and cast into the fire" (Mat 3:10)

John's language is striking: the axe is at the *root*. Not the branches, not the leaves — the root. This is not pruning. This is destruction at the foundation. A nation whose root produces no good $fruit is cut down entirely.

But Daniel preserves a detail of mercy: "leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth" (Dan 4:23). The stump survives. The roots remain. The nation can regrow. Isaiah echoes this: "the holy seed shall be the substance thereof" — literally, the stump (Isa 6:13). Even in judgment, the possibility of restoration persists in the root.

The green tree versus the dry tree carries the same weight:

> *"For if they do these things in a green **tree**, what shall be done in the dry?"* — Lk 23:31

Jesus speaks this on the way to the cross. If this happens to the flourishing nation (the green tree — Israel still standing), what will happen when Israel is dried up, barren, ready for the fire? The dry tree = a nation whose life has departed, ripe for judgment.

## Patterns

1. **Explicit identification, not inference.** The tree = nation equation is stated outright in at least five passages (Dan 4:22, Ezek 31:3, Isa 5:7, Rom 11:17, Hos 10:1). This is not a pattern deduced from context — it is declared by God, by prophets, by apostles.

2. **Component-level correspondence.** Root = origin/covenant foundation. Branches = members/people. $fruit = works/deeds. Shadow = shelter/protection. Leaves = outward prosperity. No part of the tree is wasted symbolically.

3. **Judgment = cutting down.** The axe at the root (Mat 3:10), the hewn cedar (Ezek 31:12), the hewn tree of Babylon (Dan 4:23), the cursed fig tree (Mk 11:14, 20–21). Every case: a nation whose works fail is removed.

4. **Scale is flexible.** The tree can represent a world empire ($babylon in Dan 4), a regional power (Assyria in Ezek 31), a covenant community ($israel in Rom 11), or a local people. What remains constant is the meaning: a collective body with roots, members, and works.

5. **$animal peoples dwell under the tree.** Dan 4:12 and Ezek 31:6 place the beasts beneath the tree's branches and in its shadow. If the $animal = people/tribes and the tree = the nation, then subordinate peoples shelter under imperial structures.

6. **Types of trees reveal character.** The olive tree = the covenant community (Rom 11). The vine = Israel in covenant relationship (Isa 5, Hos 10:1, Jn 15). The cedar = proud, towering empire (Ezek 31). The fig tree = Israel's covenant status. $thorns = worldly, choking people (Mat 13:22). The species identifies the nature.

7. **The stump remains.** Even a cut-down tree retains its roots and stump (Dan 4:23, Isa 6:13, Isa 11:1). National judgment is severe — but the possibility of regrowth persists. "There shall come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch shall grow out of his roots" (Isa 11:1).

## Connections

- **$mountain** — Trees grow on mountains. Nations exist under kingdoms. The cedars of Lebanon stand on elevated heights; God's vineyard is planted on a fruitful hill (Isa 5:1). $mountain = ruling authority; tree = the nation that exists under that authority.

- **$sea** — The chaotic opposite of the ordered, rooted tree. The $sea = restless, ungoverned nations (Rev 17:15, Isa 57:20). The tree is planted, cultivated, rooted in a specific place. The sea is formless and turbulent. Nations that become like the sea have lost their root structure.

- **$animal** — Peoples and tribes characterized by their nature. Animals dwell *under* the tree (Dan 4:12, Ezek 31:6) — subordinate peoples sheltered within an empire. When tree and $animal co-occur, the tree is the larger national structure, the animal is the characterized group within it.

- **$fruit** — Works and deeds (Gal 5:19–22). The tree is known by its $fruit (Mat 7:16–17). A nation is judged by what it produces. A tree without $fruit is cut down (Mat 7:19).

- **$thorns** — A specific type of plant/tree: the worldly, choking people (Mat 13:22). $thorns are not merely bad $fruit — they are a kind of tree that *prevents* $fruit in others, choking out the word with cares, riches, and pleasures. They are the weed-nation growing among the good planting.

- **$babylon** — The great tree of Daniel 4. Nebuchadnezzar's kingdom is the tree whose height reaches heaven, whose shadow covers the earth. When $babylon is cut down, the beasts scatter and the birds flee — the sheltered peoples lose their covering.

- **$grass** — If the tree = the nation as a collective body, $grass = the individual mortal person (Isa 40:6–7). The tree endures longer than the $grass; the nation outlasts the individual. But both can be cut down, and both wither under God's judgment.
