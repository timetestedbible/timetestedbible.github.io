# Tyre

**$babylon's commercial face — the trade-network that corrupts worship into merchandise and persons into goods.**

> *"By the multitude of thy merchandise they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned: therefore I will cast thee as profane out of the mountain of God: and I will destroy thee, O covering cherub, from the midst of the $[fire] stones."* — Ezek 28:16

---

## The Key Insight

A covering cherub becomes a merchant. That is the entire story of Tyre.

Ezekiel 28:14–16 describes a being who was "the anointed cherub that covereth" (H5526 *sakak* — to cover, protect, screen). This is the same root that gives us *sukkah* — the booth of covenant shelter. The function was protective: covering, shielding, guarding holiness. But then something happened:

> *"By the multitude of thy **traffick** (H7404 *rekullah*) they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned."* — Ezek 28:16

The word H7404 *rekullah* appears only four times in all of Scripture — Ezek 26:12, 28:5, 28:16, and 28:18 — every one of them in the Tyre oracles. This is *the* Tyre word. It means trade, merchandise, traffick — not exchange as neutral activity, but commerce as a system that overtakes everything else. The chain is explicit: traffick → violence → sin → cast out.

The covering cherub did not abandon its post. It *commercialized* it. Protection became a product. Holiness became a commodity. The function meant to shelter worship was converted into a mechanism for extraction. That is what Tyre represents: the moment when serving becomes selling.

**Symbolizes:** The commercial system that commodifies what was meant to be holy — turning covering into traffick, worship into trade, and persons into merchandise

**Opposite:** $jerusalem — the worship/offering economy where goods flow *toward* God, not away from covering

**Defining verses:** Ezek 28:14–18, Ezek 27:3–36, Isa 23:8–17, Rev 18:11–19

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word for Tyre (H6865 *Tsôr*) comes from H6696 *tsûr* — "rock, to confine, to besiege." God is called "the Rock" (H6697 *tsûr*) — the covenant foundation. Tyre is the *counterfeit* $rock: a fortress rising from the $sea, built on trade instead of covenant.

**Connected:** $babylon, $sea, $fire, $earthquake, $harlot, $fornication, $thorns, $island, $mountain, $lead, $sackcloth

## Tyre Across Scripture

### The Covering That Became Commerce

Ezekiel 28:12–19 is often read as a description of Satan's fall — and the pattern may well apply there — but the oracle is addressed to "the king of Tyrus" (Ezek 28:12), and every detail maps to what Tyre *does* in Scripture. The progression reveals a system, not merely a biography:

> *"Thou hast been in $[eden] the garden of God; every precious $[stone] was thy covering… the workmanship of thy tabrets and of thy pipes was prepared in thee in the day that thou wast created."* — Ezek 28:13

Nine stones are listed — a subset of the twelve on the high priest's breastplate. Musical instruments are built into its very making. This is a being designed for worship: adorned like a priest, equipped for praise, positioned in the garden.

> *"Thou art the anointed cherub that **covereth** (H5526 *sakak*); and I have set thee so: thou wast upon the holy **$[mountain]** of God."* — Ezek 28:14

The function: *covering*. The location: the holy $mountain — the seat of God's authority. This is a guardian of holiness, a protector of sacred space.

> *"Thou wast perfect in thy ways from the day that thou wast created, **till iniquity was found in thee**."* — Ezek 28:15

Then the turn:

> *"By the multitude of thy **traffick** they have filled the midst of thee with violence, and thou hast sinned."* — Ezek 28:16

The corruption is specific. Not rebellion in general — *traffick* (H7404 *rekullah*). Commerce filled the interior with violence. The word *rekullah* shares its root with H7402 *rakal* (to travel for trading), which also produces H7400 *rakiyl* — **talebearer, slanderer**. The same Hebrew root gives "merchant" and "gossip." Trading in goods and trading in words share a common essence: both traffic in what should not be commodified.

And then the judgment:

> *"Thou hast defiled thy sanctuaries by the multitude of thine iniquities, by the iniquity of thy **traffick**; therefore will I bring forth a **$[fire]** from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee."* — Ezek 28:18

The $fire does not fall from heaven. It comes *from within*. The traffick itself is the fuel. The system's own iniquity generates the combustion that consumes it. This is not Sodom, where fire rained down from above. Tyre burns from the inside out — a self-consuming economy.

### The Composite Ship — Built from All Nations

Ezekiel 27 develops Tyre's identity through an extraordinary metaphor: the city is a *ship* — constructed from the materials of every surrounding nation:

> *"O thou that art situate at the entry of the $[sea], which art a merchant of the people for many $[island]s… Thy builders have perfected thy beauty."* — Ezek 27:3–4

> *"They have made all thy ship boards of fir trees of Senir: they have taken cedars from Lebanon to make masts for thee. Of the oaks of Bashan have they made thine oars; the company of the Ashurites have made thy benches of ivory."* — Ezek 27:5–6

The ship-metaphor runs for eleven verses. Fir from Senir. Cedar from Lebanon. Oaks from Bashan. Fine linen from Egypt. Blue and purple from Elishah. Soldiers from Persia, Lud, and Phut. Builders from Arvad, Zidon, and Gebal. Tyre produces *nothing itself*. It assembles. It connects. It extracts. Every nation contributes material; Tyre takes the position at the center.

This is the pattern of a trade-network, not a producing nation. Tyre does not grow food (Acts 12:20 confirms this — "their country was nourished by the king's country"). It does not generate raw material. It *intermediates*. It sits at "the entry of the sea" and manages the flow.

### The Merchandise Catalog — And Its Final Item

Ezekiel 27:12–25 unfolds a staggering inventory of Tyre's trade goods: silver, iron, tin, $lead from Tarshish. Persons of men and vessels of brass from Javan, Tubal, and Meshech. Horses and mules from Togarmah. Ivory and ebony from Dedan. Emeralds, purple, coral, agate from Syria. Wheat, honey, $oil, and balm from Judah and Israel. $wine from Helbon. Wrought iron, cassia, and calamus from Dan and Javan. Precious cloths from Dedan. Lambs and goats from Arabia. Spices, stones, and $gold from Sheba. All the luxury goods of the known world pass through Tyre's hands.

But notice what sits quietly in the middle of the list:

> *"Javan, Tubal, and Meshech, they were thy merchants: they traded the **persons of men** and vessels of brass in thy fairs."* — Ezek 27:13

"Persons of men" (H5315 *nephesh adam* — souls of mankind). Human beings are one commodity among many. Listed alongside brass vessels. The catalog does not pause or flinch. It moves directly from human beings to horses and mules.

This detail becomes the climax of the entire Tyre-to-$babylon arc when Revelation 18 reproduces this catalog at eschatological scale — and places the same item *last*, where it cannot be missed:

> *"And cinnamon, and odours, and ointments, and frankincense, and $[wine], and $[oil], and fine flour, and wheat, and beasts, and $[sheep], and horses, and chariots, and slaves, and **souls of men**."* — Rev 18:13

The Greek makes it even more pointed. The list says *somata* (bodies) AND *psychas anthropon* (souls of men) — as if to say: you traded their bodies, yes, but you also traded their *souls*. The merchandise catalog begins with luxury and ends with humanity. That is the trajectory of unchecked commerce: everything becomes a commodity, and the human person is the final product.

### "I Am a God" — The Merchant-Prince Who Sits in Heaven's Seat

> *"Son of man, say unto the prince of Tyrus, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Because thine heart is lifted up, and thou hast said, **I am a God, I sit in the seat of God**, in the midst of the $[sea]s; yet thou art a man, and not God, though thou set thine heart as the heart of God."* — Ezek 28:2

The prince of Tyre claims divine status. This is not unique to Tyre — the same language appears in $babylon's mouth:

> *"I am, and **none else beside me**."* — Isa 47:8

And in Revelation's great $harlot:

> *"I sit a queen, and am no widow, and **shall see no sorrow**."* — Rev 18:7

Three voices, one claim. The self-deification of human systems — political ($babylon), commercial (Tyre), religious ($harlot) — converges on the same boast. What makes Tyre's version distinctive is the *mechanism* by which the heart was lifted up:

> *"With thy wisdom and with thine understanding thou hast gotten thee riches, and hast gotten $[gold] and $[silver] into thy treasures: **by thy great wisdom and by thy traffick hast thou increased thy riches**, and thine heart is lifted up **because of** thy riches."* — Ezek 28:4–5

The chain is clear: **wisdom → commerce → riches → pride → self-deification**. The corruption is not instantaneous. It is a progression. Wisdom is the starting capital. Commerce is the mechanism. Wealth is the product. Pride is the inevitable result. And the end of pride is the claim to sit in the seat of God.

Tyre is not merely a historical location — it is the prototype for $babylon's merchant dimension. When $babylon falls in Revelation, it falls *as Tyre fell*: suddenly, completely, mourned only by those who profited from it.

### The Harlot's Song — Commerce as Fornication

> *"And it shall come to pass in that day, that Tyre shall be forgotten seventy years, according to the days of one king: after the end of seventy years shall Tyre sing as an $[harlot]."* — Isa 23:15

> *"Take an harp, go about the city, thou $[harlot] that hast been forgotten; make sweet melody, sing many songs, that thou mayest be remembered."* — Isa 23:16

> *"She shall commit **$[fornication]** with all the kingdoms of the world upon the face of the earth."* — Isa 23:17

Isaiah identifies Tyre's commercial relationships as $fornication — the same word used for idolatry and covenant-breaking throughout Scripture. Trade alliances with "all the kingdoms of the world" are described in the language of sexual unfaithfulness. This is the exact vocabulary Revelation applies to $babylon's $harlot: "with whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication" (Rev 17:2).

The 70 years of Tyre's forgetting (Isa 23:15) mirrors $babylon's 70 years of Jewish exile — "according to the days of one king." The commercial system and the political system share the same timeline.

But Isaiah adds a detail that no other judgment oracle contains:

> *"And her **merchandise** and her hire shall be **holiness to the LORD**: it shall not be treasured nor laid up; for her merchandise shall be for them that dwell before the LORD, to eat sufficiently, and for durable clothing."* — Isa 23:18

Commerce itself is not condemned. *Commerce-as-idol* is condemned. When merchandise flows toward those who dwell before the LORD — when trade serves covenant rather than replacing it — even Tyre's hire can become holiness. This is the one oracle that holds open a door. The covering cherub's function was not evil. The traffick was the corruption.

### Hiram — When Trade Served the Temple

Before Tyre became a symbol of commercial corruption, it was an ally:

> *"And Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, and cedar trees, and carpenters, and masons: and they built David an house."* — 2 Sam 5:11

> *"And Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants unto Solomon; for he had heard that they had anointed him king in the room of his father: for Hiram was ever a lover of David."* — 1 Ki 5:1

Hiram's craftsmen and materials — cedar, gold, stone — built the temple. The artisan also named Hiram, sent from Tyre, worked in brass "filled with wisdom, and understanding, and cunning" (1 Ki 7:14) — language that parallels Bezalel, the Spirit-filled builder of the tabernacle (Exod 31:3). Every luxury material that appears in Revelation 18's catalog of condemnation was *first* deployed in service to God's house.

The pivot comes in 1 Kings 9:11–12. Solomon gives Hiram twenty cities in exchange for materials. Hiram inspects them and is displeased: "What cities are these which thou hast given me, my brother?" The economy has shifted. Instead of materials flowing toward the temple, *territory* flows toward the merchant. Commerce has begun to drive the covenant rather than serve it.

By Nehemiah 13:16, men of Tyre are selling fish *in Jerusalem on the Sabbath* — commerce operating inside the holy city, violating the covenant's own calendar. The covering has become the traffick.

### "More Tolerable for Tyre" — The Capacity for Repentance

> *"Woe unto thee, Chorazin! woe unto thee, Bethsaida! for if the mighty works, which were done in you, had been done in **Tyre** and Zidon, they would have repented long ago in $[sackcloth] and ashes."* — Mat 11:21

> *"But I say unto you, **It shall be more tolerable for Tyre** and Zidon at the day of judgment, than for you."* — Mat 11:22

Jesus declares that Tyre — the archetypal merchant city, the $harlot-singer of Isaiah 23 — was *more capable of repentance* than covenant cities that witnessed miracles and rejected them. This confirms Isaiah 23:18: commerce itself is not the terminal sin. Tyre's merchandise *can* become holiness. Covenant cities with greater light face greater judgment.

The Syro-Phoenician woman in Tyre's borders demonstrates this (Mat 15:21–28, Mk 7:24–30). Faith is found where the prophets pronounced doom. The merchant region produces a worshipper. The pattern holds: the corruption is in what commerce *replaces*, not in commerce itself.

## Patterns

1. **The covering-to-traffick corruption.** H5526 *sakak* (cover/protect) → H7404 *rekullah* (traffick). The covering cherub's function was to shelter holiness. Commerce converted that function into extraction. Every act of commodifying what is sacred — worship, persons, covenant goods — re-enacts the original corruption (Ezek 28:14–18).

2. **The self-consuming fire.** Tyre's judgment is unique: "I will bring forth a $fire from the midst of thee" (Ezek 28:18). Unlike Sodom ($fire from heaven) or Egypt (plagues from outside), Tyre generates its own destruction. The iniquity of traffick is the fuel. The system's internal contradictions — violence bred by commodification — ignite from within. Revelation 18:8 echoes this: "she shall be utterly burned with fire."

3. **The composite parasite.** Tyre builds itself from every nation (Ezek 27:4–11), trades with every nation (Ezek 27:12–25), but produces nothing of its own. It does not grow its own food (Acts 12:20). Its role is intermediation — connecting, extracting, profiting from position rather than production. It sits "at the entry of the $sea" (Ezek 27:3) and manages flow.

4. **"Persons of men" as final commodity.** Both Ezekiel 27:13 and Revelation 18:13 place human trafficking within a luxury catalog — and in Revelation, it is the last item listed. The trajectory of unchecked commerce: everything is commodified, and the person is the ultimate product. The catalog's structure makes this impossible to miss.

5. **"Never any more."** The phrase echoes across the oracles: "thou shalt be no more" (Ezek 26:21), "thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt thou be any more" (Ezek 27:36), "never shalt thou be any more" (Ezek 28:19), "shall be found no more at all" (Rev 18:21). Commerce-as-idol has no afterlife. Unlike $jerusalem, which is rebuilt, the merchant system receives terminal judgment.

6. **The counterfeit $rock.** H6865 *Tsôr* (Tyre) derives from H6696 *tsûr* — the same root as H6697 *tsûr*, the word for God as "the Rock." Tyre is a rock rising from the $sea — a fortress built on trade. God's rock is the covenant foundation. Tyre's rock is the commercial foundation. One endures; the other is cast into the waters (Ezek 26:12, Rev 18:21).

7. **The redemption anomaly.** Isaiah 23:18 stands alone among judgment oracles: Tyre's merchandise *can* become "holiness to the LORD." Jesus confirms this (Mat 11:21–22). The commercial function is not inherently condemned — only its displacement of covering/worship. Psalm 45:12 ("the daughter of Tyre shall be there with a gift") and Psalm 87:4 (Tyre counted among those who know God) further suggest that the merchant can be redeemed when the trade flows toward holiness rather than away from it.

## Connections

- **$babylon** — Tyre is $babylon's commercial dimension. Ezek 28:2 ("I am a God") = Isa 47:8 ("I am, and none else") = Rev 18:7 ("I sit a queen"). The political system (Babylon), the commercial system (Tyre), and the religious system ($harlot) converge in one boast of self-deification. Rev 18 *is* Ezek 27 at eschatological scale.

- **$sea** — Tyre sits "at the entry of the sea" (Ezek 27:3), in the midst of the $sea (Ezek 28:2), and is cast into the $sea at judgment (Ezek 27:34, Rev 18:21). The $sea = chaotic nations. Tyre's position *at the entry* of the nations makes it the gatekeeper of the trade-network. Its judgment: thrown back into the very element it exploited.

- **$fire** — "I will bring forth a fire from the midst of thee" (Ezek 28:18). Tyre's $fire is self-generated — the iniquity of traffick is the fuel. This distinguishes Tyre from Sodom (fire from above) and marks commerce-corruption as self-consuming. The same $fire that refines covenant-keepers consumes the system that replaced covenant with commerce.

- **$earthquake** — "Shall not the isles shake (H7493 *ra'ash*) at the sound of thy fall?" (Ezek 26:15). Tyre's collapse sends tremors through the $island (set-apart peoples) and $sea alike. The $earthquake at Tyre's fall is economic and political — the trade-network's implosion destabilizes everything connected to it.

- **$harlot** — Isaiah 23:16–17 calls Tyre a $harlot committing $fornication "with all the kingdoms of the world." Commercial alliances described in covenant-breaking language. Revelation's great $harlot sits on $sea and controls trade — the Tyre pattern universalized.

- **$mountain** — The covering cherub was "upon the holy mountain of God" (Ezek 28:14). Cast out from the $mountain = removed from the seat of authority. Tyre claimed a mountain-seat (divine authority) through commercial power; the judgment strips it.

- **$thorns** — Ezekiel 28:24 connects directly: "there shall be no more a pricking brier unto the house of Israel, nor any grieving thorn of all that are round about them." Tyre and its neighbors are the $thorns that choke $israel — worldly peoples whose commercial cares prevent covenant fruitfulness.

- **$lead** — Ezekiel 27:12 lists $lead among Tyre's merchandise from Tarshish. Lead = suppression-weight, the heaviest commodity, bottom of the metal hierarchy. Tyre trades in everything, even the densest earthly substance. In the refining context (Jer 6:29, Ezek 22:18–20), $lead that fails to purify becomes dross — paralleling the covering cherub that failed to cover.

- **$sackcloth** — Ezekiel 27:31 describes Tyre's mourners: "they shall gird themselves with sackcloth." Mat 11:21 — Tyre *would have* repented in $sackcloth. The mourning garment appears on both sides: those who lament Tyre's fall, and the hypothetical repentance Tyre could have embraced.
