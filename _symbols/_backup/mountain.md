# Mountain

**A kingdom — a seat of ruling authority from which law goes forth and over which dominion is exercised.**

> *"And the stone that smote the image became a great **mountain**, and filled the whole earth."* — Dan 2:35

---

## The Key Insight

An angel interprets the symbol directly. In Rev 17:9, the seven heads of the beast "are seven **mountains**." This is not geography — it is government. The heads represent seats of power, ruling kingdoms. Mountains *are* kingdoms.

Daniel sees the same thing from a different angle. A stone — cut without hands — strikes the feet of the great image and shatters it. Then the stone "became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth" (Dan 2:35). Daniel interprets: "the God of heaven set up a **kingdom**, which shall never be destroyed… it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever" (Dan 2:44). The stone becomes a mountain. The kingdom fills the earth. Mountain = kingdom.

Once this equation is visible, every mountain in Scripture begins to function the same way. Mountains are not scenery. They are thrones.

**Symbolizes:** Kingdom — ruling authority, seat of government and law

**Opposite:** The $sea — chaotic, restless, ungoverned peoples (Rev 17:15). Also the valley — low position, subjection, absence of authority.

**Defining verses:** Rev 17:9, Dan 2:35, Dan 2:44–45, Isa 2:2–3, Jer 51:25, Mic 4:1–2

**The surprise:** When Jesus says $faith can move mountains (Mat 21:21), he is not using a metaphor for difficult obstacles. He is describing the toppling of ruling powers — kingdoms cast into the $sea of chaotic nations.

**Connected:** $sea, $babylon, $jerusalem, $rock, $faith, $bread, $truth

## Mountain Across Scripture

### The Angel's Interpretation

> *"The seven heads are seven **mountains**, on which the woman sitteth."* — Rev 17:9

Revelation's angel does not leave the symbol uninterpreted. The beast's seven heads are seven mountains, and the woman who sits upon them is "that great city, which reigneth over the kings of the earth" (Rev 17:18). Mountains = seats of ruling power. The harlot $babylon sits atop multiple kingdoms — she rides upon governmental authority.

The $sea appears in the same passage: "The waters which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Rev 17:15). Mountains rise above the sea. Kingdoms rise above the peoples. The geography of the vision *is* the political structure: ungoverned peoples below ($sea), governing authorities above (mountains), and $babylon enthroned over both.

### The Stone That Becomes a Kingdom

> *"Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors; and the wind carried them away, that no place was found for them: and the stone that smote the image became a great **mountain**, and filled the whole earth."* — Dan 2:35

> *"And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed: and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever."* — Dan 2:44

The great image represents a succession of world kingdoms — Babylon, Persia, Greece, Rome. A stone (not cut by human hands) destroys them all and then *grows* into a mountain filling the earth. Daniel's interpretation makes the equation explicit: the mountain is the kingdom that God sets up.

Notice the progression: small $rock becomes great mountain. The $rock (covenant) expands into a ruling kingdom. The covenant itself becomes the governing authority over all the earth. This connects the mountain directly to the $rock — the covenant is the foundation; the kingdom is its visible, established form.

### The Mountain of the LORD's House

> *"And it shall come to pass in the last days, that the **mountain** of the LORD'S house shall be established in the top of the **mountains**, and shall be exalted above the hills; and all nations shall flow unto it."* — Isa 2:2

> *"And many people shall go and say, Come ye, and let us go up to the **mountain** of the LORD, to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths: for out of Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the LORD from $[jerusalem]."* — Isa 2:3

Micah 4:1–2 repeats this prophecy nearly word for word. Two prophets, one vision: God's mountain established above all other mountains — God's kingdom exalted above all other kingdoms. What follows is the behavior of government:

1. **Nations flow to it** — peoples submit to its authority
2. **Law goes forth from it** — legislation, the standard of governance ($truth — God's commandments)
3. **The word of the LORD proceeds from it** — $bread, covenant terms issued from the seat of power
4. **It is established in the top of the mountains** — supreme over lesser kingdoms

Mountains do what governments do. God's mountain does what God's government does — issues law, receives allegiance, exercises dominion, and stands above every competing authority.

> *"So shall ye know that I am the LORD your God dwelling in Zion, my holy **mountain**."* — Joel 3:17

God *dwells* in His mountain. The king occupies his throne. $jerusalem — "Zion, my holy mountain" — is both the covenant community and the seat of divine authority. The mountain is where God rules from and where His people are gathered.

### Babylon — The Destroying Mountain

> *"Behold, I am against thee, O destroying **mountain**, saith the LORD, which destroyest all the earth: and I will stretch out mine hand upon thee, and roll thee down from the rocks, and will make thee a burnt **mountain**."* — Jer 51:25

$babylon is directly called a "destroying mountain." This is not a topographic description — Babylon sat on a flat plain. The prophet identifies what Babylon *is*: a kingdom that destroys all the earth. A ruling authority wielding devastation.

And God's judgment against this mountain? To "roll thee down from the rocks" — dethrone the kingdom, strip it of authority. A burnt mountain is a kingdom reduced to ashes. The same pattern appears in Rev 18: $babylon falls, burns, and is "thrown down" like a great millstone cast into the $sea (Rev 18:21) — a kingdom hurled into the chaos of ungoverned peoples.

This establishes the dual application of the symbol. Mountains can be *God's* kingdom (Zion) or *man's* kingdom ($babylon). The symbol represents the seat of ruling authority regardless of who occupies it. The question is always: whose mountain? Whose government?

### Faith That Moves Mountains

> *"Jesus answered and said unto them, Verily I say unto you, If ye have **$[faith]**, and doubt not, ye shall not only do this which is done to the fig tree, but also if ye shall say unto this **mountain**, Be thou removed, and be thou cast into the **$[sea]**; it shall be done."* — Mat 21:21

> *"If ye have **$[faith]** as a grain of mustard seed, ye shall say unto this **mountain**, Remove hence to yonder place; and it shall remove; and nothing shall be impossible unto you."* — Mat 17:20

When Jesus speaks of $faith moving mountains, the symbolic meaning transforms the saying. Mountains are kingdoms. The $sea is the chaos of ungoverned nations. To cast a mountain into the sea is to topple a ruling authority into disorder — to bring down a kingdom through trusting obedience to God.

This is not a proverb about overcoming personal difficulties. It is a statement about the power of covenant obedience ($faith) to overthrow governing powers. Israel was a vassal people under Rome. Jesus tells his disciples that $faith — trusting obedience to God — can unmake kingdoms. Daniel's stone, after all, was not cut by human hands. The kingdom that fills the earth does not rise by political maneuvering but by the covenant itself.

### Every Mountain of Authority in Christ's Life

The mountain symbol saturates the gospel narratives, and in each case the connection to kingdom authority is present:

**Temptation on a mountain** — The devil takes Jesus up to "an exceeding high mountain, and sheweth him all the kingdoms of the world, and the glory of them" (Mat 4:8). The mountain is the vantage point of rulership. Satan offers kingdom authority *from* a mountain — the very place where authority is seated.

**The Sermon on the Mount** — Jesus "went up into a mountain" and taught (Mat 5:1). He teaches with authority, issuing the law of His kingdom — just as Isa 2:3 says, "out of Zion shall go forth the law." The pattern of Sinai repeats: God issues His covenant terms from a mountain.

**The Transfiguration** — On a high mountain, Jesus is revealed in the glory of His kingdom (Mat 17:1–2). Peter, witnessing this, later writes: "we were eyewitnesses of his majesty… in the holy mount" (2 Pe 1:16–18). The mountain is where the King's authority is unveiled.

**The Mount of Olives** — Zechariah prophesies that the LORD's feet "shall stand in that day upon the **mount** of Olives… and the mount of Olives shall cleave in the midst thereof" (Zech 14:4). The return of the King to His mountain — the establishment of His ruling authority — is so transformative that the mountain itself splits. The kingdom changes.

## Mountains Removed — Kingdoms Shaken

> *"For the **mountains** shall depart, and the hills be removed; but my kindness shall not depart from thee, neither shall the covenant of my peace be removed, saith the LORD that hath mercy on thee."* — Isa 54:10

> *"Though the **mountains** be carried into the midst of the **$[sea]**… though the **mountains** shake with the swelling thereof."* — Ps 46:2–3

When mountains shake, kingdoms tremble. When mountains are "carried into the midst of the sea," ruling authorities are overthrown into the chaos of unruly peoples. Isa 54:10 contrasts shifting mountains with God's immovable covenant — kingdoms may depart, but the $rock of God's kindness and covenant of peace will never be removed.

This is the consistent prophetic image: earthly kingdoms are unstable mountains, subject to removal. God's kingdom is the mountain that cannot be shaken (Heb 12:28 — "a kingdom which cannot be moved").

## Patterns

1. **Mountains are explicitly decoded as kingdoms.** Rev 17:9 — angelic interpretation. Dan 2:35, 44 — prophetic interpretation. Jer 51:25 — God's own speech. The symbol is not left to inference; Scripture defines it.

2. **Mountains do what governments do.** They issue law (Isa 2:3), receive the allegiance of nations (Isa 2:2, Mic 4:1), seat the ruler (Joel 3:17), and exercise dominion (Jer 51:25). Every function is governmental.

3. **God's mountain rises above all others.** Isa 2:2 — "established in the top of the mountains, exalted above the hills." Mic 4:1 — same. God's kingdom is supreme over all earthly governments. The stone-mountain fills the *whole earth* (Dan 2:35).

4. **Mountains cast into the sea = kingdoms overthrown.** Mat 21:21, Ps 46:2. The opposite of mountain is $sea — ordered authority versus chaotic peoples. A mountain cast into the sea is a government toppled into disorder.

5. **Mountain and $rock share a structural relationship.** The $rock (covenant) becomes the mountain (kingdom) in Dan 2:35. The covenant is the foundation; the kingdom is its visible, governing expression. "Roll thee down from the rocks" (Jer 51:25) — the destroying kingdom is stripped from its foundation.

6. **Mountains shake; God's covenant does not.** Isa 54:10 — mountains depart, but the covenant of peace remains. Earthly kingdoms are temporary mountains. God's kingdom is the one that "shall not be destroyed" (Dan 2:44).

## Connections

- **$sea** — The mountain's opposite. The $sea represents chaotic, ungoverned peoples (Rev 17:15). Mountains (kingdoms) rise above the sea (nations). A mountain cast into the sea (Mat 21:21) is a kingdom overthrown into chaos. Rev 17 places both in one vision: the beast rises from the $sea, the harlot sits on the mountains.

- **$babylon** — The world's mountain. Jer 51:25 calls $babylon a "destroying mountain" — a kingdom that devours all the earth. Rev 17:9 seats $babylon atop seven mountains. $babylon is the counterfeit ruling authority, the kingdom built by man against God's rule.

- **$jerusalem** — God's mountain. "Mount Zion, my holy mountain" (Joel 3:17). $jerusalem is the covenant community seated on God's mountain — the kingdom from which law goes forth and to which nations flow (Isa 2:2–3). $babylon sits on mountains; $jerusalem *is* the mountain.

- **$rock** — The mountain's foundation. The $rock (covenant) becomes the mountain (kingdom) in Dan 2:35. The unchanging covenant is what the kingdom is built upon. God's mountain stands because its $rock does not shift.

- **$faith** — The force that moves mountains. Trusting obedience to God topples ruling authorities (Mat 17:20, Mat 21:21). What human armies cannot accomplish, $faith does — because the stone that shatters kingdoms is "cut without hands."

- **$truth** — The law that proceeds from the mountain. "Out of Zion shall go forth the law" (Isa 2:3). God's commandments flow from God's kingdom. $truth is the standard issued from the mountain; the mountain is the authority behind the standard.

- **$bread** — The word that goes forth from the mountain. "The word of the LORD from Jerusalem" (Isa 2:3). $bread (the covenant terms, the Word of God) is proclaimed from the seat of authority. Sinai — the mountain where Torah was given — is the defining example: God's $bread delivered from God's mountain.

## For Further Study

- **$babylon** — The destroying mountain. How does the world's counterfeit kingdom operate, and what does it mean that God will "roll thee down from the rocks"?
- **$jerusalem** — God's mountain. The covenant community seated on divine authority — the mountain to which all nations will flow. How does $jerusalem function as bride and kingdom simultaneously?
- **$rock** — The mountain's foundation. The covenant that does not shift. How does the small stone become the great mountain, and what does this reveal about the relationship between covenant and kingdom?
