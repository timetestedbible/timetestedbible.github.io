# Wickedness

**Covenant transgression — lawlessness that defies God's standard, burns like $fire, and ultimately consumes itself.**

> *"If there be found among you… man or woman, that hath wrought **wickedness** in the sight of the LORD thy God, in transgressing his covenant"* — Deut 17:2

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not leave the meaning of wickedness to intuition. Deuteronomy 17:2 supplies an explicit definition: wickedness **is** transgressing God's covenant. Not general badness. Not cultural offense. Not hurting someone's feelings. Covenant transgression — the violation of a specific, binding agreement between God and His people.

The Greek New Testament sharpens this further. When Paul writes of the "mystery of iniquity" in 2 Thess 2:7, the word is G458 *anomia* — literally *a-* (without) + *nomos* (law). **Lawlessness.** The "Wicked" one revealed in 2 Thess 2:8 is G459 *anomos* — **the lawless one.** Not "the evil one" in some vague sense. The one who operates *without law*, outside the covenant.

These two definitions — Hebrew and Greek, Old Testament and New — converge on a single meaning: wickedness is the rejection of God's declared standard. It is not merely doing harm; it is doing *whatever one wills* in defiance of what God has spoken. The opposite is not niceness. The opposite is $righteousness — covenant faithfulness, keeping what God commanded.

What makes this definition powerful is what flows from it. Isaiah shows that lawlessness is not stable. It does not simply exist in a neutral state. It **burns**. It devours. And in the end, it devours itself.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant transgression — lawlessness, the rejection of God's standard

**Opposite:** Righteousness — covenant keeping, faithfulness to God's law

**Defining verses:** Deut 17:2, 2 Thess 2:7–8, Isa 9:18–20, Isa 57:20–21

**The surprise:** Wickedness is not merely destructive — it is *self*-destructive. Isaiah 9:18–20 describes it as a fire that consumes the very people who fuel it. The wicked become their own fuel.

**Connected:** $truth, $thorns, $sea, $fruit, $babylon, $fire

## Wickedness Across Scripture

### The Explicit Definition — Transgressing His Covenant

> *"If there be found among you, within any of thy gates which the LORD thy God giveth thee, man or woman, that hath wrought **wickedness** in the sight of the LORD thy God, **in transgressing his covenant**"* — Deut 17:2

The phrase structure here is not incidental. "Wrought wickedness" is immediately defined by the participial clause "in transgressing his covenant." This is not two separate charges. It is one charge stated and then explained. To work wickedness *is* to transgress the covenant.

This matters because it anchors wickedness to something concrete. The covenant has specific terms — commandments, statutes, ordinances. To transgress them is wickedness. This is not a moral feeling or a cultural judgment. It is a legal reality. The covenant exists; to violate it is to be wicked.

This also explains why Scripture can speak of someone being wicked even when their culture approves of their behavior. The standard is not cultural consensus. It is the covenant.

### The Greek Precision — Lawlessness

> *"For the mystery of iniquity doth already work: only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way. And then shall that **Wicked** be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness of his coming"* — 2 Thess 2:7–8

The word Paul uses for "iniquity" is G458 *anomia*: **without law**. Not *anti-law* (actively opposing), but *a-nomos* — operating as if the law does not exist. The lawless one is not someone who argues against God's commandments. He is someone who acts as though they are irrelevant — who sets up his own standard in place of God's.

The definite article matters: "that Wicked" (G459 *anomos*) — **the** lawless one. A specific figure who embodies the principle of lawlessness that was already at work in Paul's day. The "mystery of *anomia*" is not mysterious evil in general. It is the hidden operation of lawlessness — covenant rejection dressed up as something else, working beneath the surface before it is finally unmasked.

This Greek definition complements the Hebrew one perfectly. Deut 17:2 says wickedness = covenant transgression. Paul says wickedness = lawlessness. These are the same thing stated from two angles: one emphasizes the covenant violated, the other emphasizes the law rejected. Both point to the same reality — operating outside God's declared standard.

### The Fire That Eats Itself

> *"For **wickedness** burneth as the $[fire]: it shall devour the $[thorns] and briers, and shall kindle in the thickets of the forest, and they shall mount up like the lifting up of smoke."* — Isa 9:18

> *"Through the wrath of the LORD of hosts is the land darkened, and the people shall be as the fuel of the $[fire]: no man shall spare his brother. And he shall snatch on the right hand, and be hungry; and he shall eat on the left hand, and they shall not be satisfied: they shall eat every man the flesh of his own arm"* — Isa 9:19–20

Isaiah reveals what lawlessness *does*. It burns. It spreads. It devours. And the sequence in these verses is devastating:

First, wickedness burns as fire and devours the $thorns and briers — the worldly, the materialistic, those characterized by "cares, riches, and pleasures" (Lk 8:14). Lawlessness consumes its own constituency first.

Then "the people shall be as the fuel of the fire." The people themselves become the thing being burned. Not an external enemy consuming them — their own lawlessness consuming them from within. "No man shall spare his brother" — covenant loyalty has broken down. "They shall eat every man the flesh of his own arm" — they devour their own strength.

This is the behavioral signature of lawlessness: it is inherently self-destructive. A fire needs fuel, and when the fuel runs out, the fire dies. Wickedness burns through $thorns, then through the forest, then through the people themselves. The system collapses under its own logic. When there is no law, there is no restraint, and when there is no restraint, every man devours his neighbor until nothing remains.

The smoke that "mounts up" from this self-consumption anticipates the same image in Revelation — the smoke of $babylon ascending (Rev 18:9, 19:3). $babylon produces wickedness and is ultimately consumed by it. The fire comes "from the midst of thee" (Ezek 28:18).

### The Troubled Sea — No Rest for the Wicked

> *"But the **wicked** are like the troubled $[sea], when it cannot rest, whose waters cast up mire and dirt. There is no peace, saith my God, to the **wicked**."* — Isa 57:20–21

The $sea in Scripture represents chaotic, unsanctified peoples and nations (Rev 17:15, Isa 17:12). Isaiah's comparison is precise: the wicked are not merely *in* the sea — they are *like* it. Restless, turbulent, unable to be still.

"It cannot rest" — this is not a temporary state but a permanent condition. Lawlessness has no equilibrium. Without the covenant's boundaries, there is nothing to settle into. The $sea churns because it has no shore it will accept. The wicked churn because they have no standard they will submit to.

"Cast up mire and dirt" — the turbulence doesn't produce anything of value. It only brings filth to the surface. Contrast this with the $fruit that covenant-keeping produces. Lawlessness yields nothing but mire; righteousness yields $fruit.

"No peace to the wicked" — this is not a punishment imposed from outside. It is a diagnosis. Peace is the natural state of things rightly ordered under God's standard. Remove the standard, and restlessness is what remains. The wicked *cannot* have peace — not because God withholds it vindictively, but because peace is the product of covenant order, and they have rejected covenant order.

## Patterns

1. **Wickedness is explicitly defined, not inferred.** Deut 17:2 states the equation directly: wickedness = transgressing the covenant. G458 *anomia* confirms: wickedness = lawlessness. Scripture does not ask the reader to guess.

2. **Wickedness is the opposite of $truth.** If $truth is God's word, law, and commandments (Jn 17:17, Ps 119:142), then wickedness is their rejection. $truth sets the standard; wickedness transgresses it. The two are not merely different — they are antithetical.

3. **Wickedness burns and self-consumes.** Isa 9:18–20 — lawlessness acts like fire, devouring $thorns, kindling forests, and finally turning the people themselves into fuel. The wicked eat their own flesh. The system destroys itself.

4. **The wicked are like the $sea — restless, chaotic, without peace.** Isa 57:20–21 compares the wicked to the troubled sea. The $sea = chaotic peoples; the wicked share the sea's character. Without covenant order, there is no rest.

5. **Wickedness is $babylon's defining cargo.** Zechariah 5:5–11 shows a woman named "Wickedness" (H7564) sealed in an ephah and transported to Shinar ($babylon's birthplace). $babylon generates lawlessness — and lawlessness returns to $babylon as judgment.

6. **Wickedness applies at every scale.** Individuals transgress the covenant (Deut 17:2). The "lawless one" embodies it as a figure (2 Thess 2:8). $babylon institutionalizes it as a system. The principle is the same at every level: operating outside God's declared standard.

## Connections

- **$truth** — The direct opposite of wickedness in function. $truth = God's word, law, and commandments. Wickedness = transgressing that standard. Where $truth sanctifies and frees (Jn 17:17, Jn 8:32), wickedness enslaves and consumes.

- **$thorns** — The first fuel wickedness devours. "Wickedness burneth as the fire: it shall devour the $thorns and briers" (Isa 9:18). $thorns = worldly people characterized by cares, riches, and pleasures (Mat 13:22). Lawlessness produces this kind of people, then feeds on them.

- **$sea** — The wicked share the sea's nature. "The wicked are like the troubled sea" (Isa 57:20). Both are restless, both are chaotic, both cast up filth. The $sea = unsanctified nations; the wicked are their moral character.

- **$fruit** — Wickedness prevents and destroys $fruit. Where covenant-keeping produces works of righteousness (Gal 5:22), lawlessness chokes $fruit through $thorns (Mk 4:7) and burns what remains (Isa 9:18). "No peace to the wicked" means no settled harvest.

- **$babylon** — Wickedness personified and institutionalized. Zechariah's vision (Zech 5:5–11) shows wickedness physically transported to $babylon's homeland. $babylon is the system that runs on lawlessness — and is ultimately consumed by the fire it produces (Ezek 28:18, Rev 18:8).

- **$fire** — The element wickedness behaves like. "Wickedness burneth as the fire" (Isa 9:18). Not a metaphor chosen at random — fire both reveals and destroys. Lawlessness is visible and self-consuming. The judgment of the wicked comes in the form of their own nature turned back upon them.

## For Further Study

- **$truth** — If wickedness is the rejection of God's standard, what exactly is that standard? The $truth study traces the chain from word to law to commandment — the very thing wickedness transgresses.
- **$thorns** — The first thing wickedness burns through. Who are the $thorns, and why are they the natural fuel of lawlessness?

- **$fire** — Wickedness "burneth as the fire." How does $fire function across Scripture — as purifier, as judgment, and as the element that consumes what it tests?
