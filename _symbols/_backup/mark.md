# Mark

**Observable practice — the visible evidence of whose authority you live under.**

> *"And it shall be for a **sign** unto thee upon thine hand, and for a memorial between thine eyes, that the LORD's law may be in thy mouth."* — Exo 13:9

---

## The Key Insight

The KJV uses three different English words — "mark," "sign," and "token" — to translate a single Hebrew word: H226 *'owth*. Once you see that these are the same word, the whole pattern snaps into focus.

The rainbow is an *'owth* (Gen 9:12). Circumcision is an *'owth* (Gen 17:11). The blood on the doorposts is an *'owth* (Exo 12:13). The Sabbath is an *'owth* (Exo 31:13). And in Deuteronomy 6:8, the commandments bound on your hand and between your eyes are an *'owth*.

What do these have in common? They are not abstract ideas. They are all **things you do** — observable practices that identify which covenant you belong to. The rainbow is God's practice-of-promise made visible. Circumcision is a practice performed on the body. Passover blood is applied by hand. The Sabbath is kept or broken by what you do with your time. The commandments are lived out in action and thought.

The mark is never merely a brand stamped on passive flesh. It is the practice itself — what your hands do and what your mind dwells on — that constitutes the mark.

**Symbolizes:** Observable practice — what you do (hand) and what you think (forehead) that reveals your allegiance

**Opposite:** Unmarked / unidentifiable — no visible practice declaring any master

**Defining verses:** Exo 13:9, Deut 6:6–8, Exo 31:13, Rev 13:16, Rev 14:1, Ezek 9:4

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word H226 *'owth* — translated "mark" on Cain, "sign" at Passover, "token" for the rainbow and circumcision — is one word. What English renders as three different concepts, Hebrew treats as one: *the visible evidence of covenant relationship*.

**Connected:** $name, $truth, $faith, $bread, $fornication, $idolatry, $babylon

## Mark Across Scripture

### One Hebrew Word, Three English Masks

The moment you trace H226 *'owth* through the Old Testament, the artificial separation between "mark," "sign," and "token" collapses:

> *"And the LORD set a **mark** upon Cain."* — Gen 4:15 (H226)

> *"This is the **token** of the covenant which I make between me and you."* — Gen 9:12 (H226)

> *"It shall be a **token** of the covenant betwixt me and you."* — Gen 17:11 (H226, circumcision)

> *"And the blood shall be to you for a **token** upon the houses where ye are."* — Exo 12:13 (H226, Passover)

> *"Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a **sign** between me and you."* — Exo 31:13 (H226)

> *"Thou shalt bind them for a **sign** upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes."* — Deut 6:8 (H226)

Every instance is an observable indicator of covenant standing. The rainbow, circumcision, Passover blood, the Sabbath, the commandments on hand and forehead — all are *'owth*. All are practices (or practice-reminders) that identify who belongs to whom.

A secular reader encountering "sign" or "token" might think of a symbol, an omen, or an indicator. But the pattern in Scripture narrows the meaning: the *'owth* is specifically what you **practice** that declares your covenant allegiance.

### The Hand and the Forehead — What You Do and What You Think

Two passages spell out where the mark belongs and what it contains:

> *"And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children… And thou shalt bind **them** for a **sign** upon thine hand, and they shall be as frontlets between thine eyes."* — Deut 6:6–8

> *"And it shall be for a **sign** unto thee upon thine **hand**, and for a memorial between thine **eyes**, that the LORD's law may be in thy mouth."* — Exo 13:9

Notice what the "sign" *is*. In Deuteronomy 6:8, the word "them" refers back to verse 6 — "these words which I command thee." The commandments themselves, practiced and meditated upon, *are* the sign. In Exodus 13:9, the purpose clause makes it explicit: "that the LORD's law may be in thy mouth."

The hand represents action — what you **do**. The forehead (between the eyes) represents the mind — what you **think**, what governs your decisions. When both hand and forehead bear the mark, it means your practices *and* your thinking are aligned with that authority's commands.

This is not about a physical object strapped to the body. It is about whose instructions govern your life — in deed and in thought.

### The Sabbath as the Defining Mark

> *"Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep: for it is a **sign** between me and you throughout your generations; that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you."* — Exo 31:13

> *"It is a **sign** between me and the children of Israel for ever: for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed."* — Exo 31:17

Of all the *'owth* instances, the Sabbath may be the most revealing. God calls it a sign (H226) — the same word used for the mark, the token, the sign on hand and forehead. And He specifies its function: "that ye may **know** that I am the LORD."

The Sabbath is an observable practice. You either keep it or you don't. It cannot be hidden. It identifies, week after week, who acknowledges the Creator's authority over time itself. It is the mark of God's people in the most literal sense: the recurring practice that separates those who observe His calendar from those who do not.

This becomes critical when considering the contrast in Revelation — but the foundation is already here in Exodus. The Sabbath is the sign. The sign is on the hand (you rest from labor) and between the eyes (you remember the Creator). It is the $mark of the one who keeps God's $truth.

### Ezekiel's Tav — The Mark That Saves

> *"And the LORD said unto him, Go through the midst of the city, through the midst of Jerusalem, and set a **mark** upon the foreheads of the men that sigh and that cry for all the abominations that be done in the midst thereof."* — Ezek 9:4

> *"Come not near any man upon whom is the **mark**."* — Ezek 9:6

The word here is H8420 *tav* — the name of the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet. In the ancient paleo-Hebrew script, the *tav* was written as an X or a cross (+). Those who receive this mark are protected from the slaughter sweeping Jerusalem.

But notice *who* receives it: those who "sigh and cry for all the abominations." Not those wearing a badge. Not those with the right ancestry. Those whose hearts are grieved by the violation of God's standard — whose inner life (forehead/mind) is aligned with God's ways. Their response to $wickedness identifies them.

The mark is not arbitrary. It reveals what is already true about the person's allegiance. The angel does not create their faithfulness; he marks what already exists in their practice and conviction.

### Two Marks — Two Masters

Revelation's mark-of-the-beast imagery draws directly from the Exodus and Deuteronomy pattern, but inverts it:

> *"And he causeth all, both small and great, rich and poor, free and bond, to receive a **mark** in their right hand, or in their foreheads."* — Rev 13:16

> *"And I looked, and, lo, a $[lamb] stood on the mount Sion, and with him an hundred forty and four thousand, having his Father's **$[name]** written in their foreheads."* — Rev 14:1

The same locations — hand and forehead. But two different marks. One bears the beast's mark; the other bears the Father's $name. The parallel suggests the mark and the $name function identically in structure: both are identifiers of allegiance written in practice and thought.

What distinguishes the two groups? Revelation 14:12 answers:

> *"Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God, and the $[faith] of Jesus."* — Rev 14:12

Those with the Father's $name keep God's commandments. Those with the beast's mark worship the beast and his image (Rev 14:9). The mark, in both cases, is not a tattoo or a microchip. It is the visible practice-pattern that reveals whose authority you submit to — whose commands shape what your hands do and your mind thinks.

$babylon's mark is the practice of $babylon's system. God's mark is the practice of God's $truth. You take a mark by **observing** that master's ordinances.

### The Slave-Brand of Paul

> *"From henceforth let no man trouble me: for I bear in my body the **marks** of the Lord Jesus."* — Gal 6:17

The word here is G4742 *stigma* — a brand burned into the flesh of a slave to identify his owner. Paul uses the language of ownership. His scars — from beatings, stonings, imprisonments — are the visible evidence that he serves Christ. The marks are not decorative. They are the consequences of his practice, the proof of whose authority he lives under.

Even in this most physical usage, the logic holds: the mark is observable evidence of allegiance, inscribed by the life you live.

## Patterns

1. **One word, three translations.** H226 *'owth* appears as "mark" (Gen 4:15), "sign" (Exo 31:13, Deut 6:8), and "token" (Gen 9:12, Gen 17:11, Exo 12:13). English obscures what Hebrew unifies: these are all the same concept — the observable evidence of covenant relationship.

2. **Hand = action; forehead = thought.** The hand-and-forehead pattern (Exo 13:9, Deut 6:8, Rev 13:16, Rev 14:1) consistently represents the totality of allegiance: what you do and what you believe. A mark in both locations means full alignment with that authority.

3. **The mark is the practice, not a brand.** In Deut 6:6–8, the "sign upon thine hand" is explicitly the commandments themselves — practiced and internalized. The mark is not stamped onto a passive recipient; it is lived out by an active practitioner.

4. **Every mark identifies a master.** Cain's mark (Gen 4:15), God's Sabbath-sign (Exo 31:13), the Passover blood-token (Exo 12:13), Ezekiel's tav (Ezek 9:4), the Father's $name (Rev 14:1), the beast's mark (Rev 13:16) — in every case, the mark declares whose jurisdiction the bearer operates under.

5. **The mark that protects and the mark that condemns.** In Ezekiel 9, the tav spares those who bear it. In Revelation, the beast's mark brings wrath (Rev 14:9–10), while the Father's $name brings protection (Rev 7:3). The mark itself is neutral in structure — it is the authority behind it that determines the outcome.

6. **You take a mark by practicing.** You don't receive God's mark by intellectual assent and you don't receive the beast's mark by accident. Both marks are the accumulated evidence of whose ordinances you observe — whose calendar, whose commandments, whose system shapes your daily life.

## Connections

- **$name** — The $name is the covenant; the mark is the evidence of it. Rev 14:1 puts the Father's $name in the foreheads of the faithful — the same location as the beast's mark (Rev 13:16). Taking a $name (entering a covenant) and bearing a mark (practicing that covenant's terms) are two sides of the same relationship.

- **$truth** — $truth is God's standard — His commandments. The mark is the observable practice of that standard. Deut 6:6–8 makes the connection explicit: the commandments (= $truth) bound on hand and forehead *are* the sign (= mark). You know someone's mark by whether they practice God's $truth.

- **$faith** — $faith is the obedient response to God's word. The mark is the visible result of that response. Rev 14:12 links them directly: those with the Father's $name "keep the commandments of God, and the $faith of Jesus." $faith produces the mark; the mark proves the $faith.

- **$bread** — $bread is God's word / covenant terms. The mark is what happens when those terms are put into practice. Exo 13:9 — the sign on hand and forehead exists "that the LORD's law may be in thy mouth." Receiving the $bread (hearing the word) and bearing the mark (practicing it) form a cause-and-effect chain.

- **$fornication** — If the mark identifies whose practices you observe, $fornication is the act of switching practices — observing another master's ordinances while still bearing God's $name. The mark reveals the betrayal: your hands serve one authority while your lips claim another.

- **$idolatry** — $idolatry is self-rule — rejecting God's word as your standard. In mark-language, $idolatry is setting up a third mark: neither God's nor the beast's, but your own. You become your own authority, your practices answering to no one.

- **$babylon** — $babylon is the system whose mark stands opposite God's. Rev 13:16–17 describes a system that requires its mark for participation in commerce — a total economic and social order built on allegiance to the beast. $babylon's mark is the observable practice of $babylon's ways: its calendar, its laws, its values. The contrast with the Sabbath-sign (Exo 31:13) is structural.
