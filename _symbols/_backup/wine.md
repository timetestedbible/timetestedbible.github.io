# Wine

**Blood — covenant blood. The life poured out to ratify the agreement between God and man.**

> *"He washed his garments in **wine**, and his clothes in the blood of grapes."* — Gen 49:11

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not compare wine *to* blood. It defines wine *as* blood.

> *"He washed his garments in **wine**, and his clothes in the **blood of grapes**."* — Gen 49:11

> *"Of the pure **blood of the grape** thou didst drink **wine**."* — Deut 32:14

"Blood of grapes" is not metaphor — it is a definitional statement. The same liquid is named by both words. When Jacob blesses Judah, when Moses recounts God's provision, wine is called blood outright. This is the equation that unlocks every other appearance of wine in Scripture.

And blood, in turn, carries a definition of its own:

> *"For the life of the flesh is in the **blood**: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the **blood** that maketh an atonement for the soul."* — Lev 17:11

Blood = life. Wine = blood. Therefore wine = life — specifically, life *poured out*. And what is that life poured out *for*? To ratify a covenant.

**Symbolizes:** Blood / covenant blood — the life given to seal and ratify God's covenant

**Opposite:** Thirst / absence of covenant life

**Defining verses:** Gen 49:11, Deut 32:14, Exod 24:8, Mat 26:28, Rev 14:10

**The surprise:** The same wine that represents covenant joy *also* represents covenant wrath. Not two symbols — one symbol with two outcomes, depending entirely on one's standing within the covenant. The blood that brings life to those who receive it becomes blood-guilt upon those who reject it.

**Connected:** $bread, $fruit, $rock, $marriage, $babylon, $fornication, $harlot, $jerusalem

## Wine Across Scripture

### Blood of Grapes — The Definition

Two passages establish the equation before any ritual or ceremony takes place:

> *"Binding his foal unto the vine, and his ass's colt unto the choice vine; he washed his garments in **wine**, and his clothes in the **blood of grapes**."* — Gen 49:11

Jacob's blessing over Judah sets wine and "blood of grapes" in strict parallelism. The two halves of the verse say the same thing in different words: wine *is* the blood of grapes. This is the royal line from which the Messiah will come — and the imagery drenches the king in covenant blood.

> *"Butter of kine, and milk of sheep, with fat of lambs, and rams of the breed of Bashan, and goats, with the fat of kidneys of wheat; and thou didst drink the pure **blood of the grape**."* — Deut 32:14

Moses' Song — a covenant lawsuit — recalls God feeding Israel. The provision culminates in the "pure blood of the grape," which Israel *drank*. The Hebrew for "pure" here is H2561 *chemer*, denoting fermented wine in its full strength. God gave His people covenant blood to drink — and they took it for granted.

These are not poetic flourishes. They are the foundation on which every later reference builds. When Jesus lifts a cup at the Last Supper, He is not inventing a symbol. He is naming what wine has always been.

### The Blood That Seals the Covenant

If wine = blood, then wine's primary function in Scripture follows from blood's primary function: to ratify a covenant.

> *"And Moses took the blood, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the **blood of the covenant**, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words."* — Exod 24:8

The sequence at Sinai is telling:

1. Moses reads the covenant words to the people (Exod 24:7) — they hear the terms.
2. Moses sprinkles blood on the people (Exod 24:8) — the covenant is ratified.
3. The elders go up, see God, "and did eat and drink" (Exod 24:11) — the covenant meal.

$bread and wine appear together here in function, even before they appear together by name. The words are heard ($bread — the terms), the blood is applied (wine — the ratification), and the meal is shared (both together — covenant communion). This is the pattern that every covenant meal in Scripture echoes.

> *"By the **blood** of thy **covenant** I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit wherein is no water."* — Zech 9:11

> *"The God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the **blood** of the **everlasting covenant**."* — Heb 13:20

Blood seals the covenant in every age — Noah's (Gen 9:4–6), Abraham's (Gen 15:9–10), Sinai's (Exod 24:8), and the New (Mat 26:28). The blood of the everlasting covenant is what brings Christ back from the dead. The same blood, the same covenant, the same wine.

### "This Is My Blood of the New Covenant"

> *"For this is my **blood** of the **new testament**, which is shed for many for the remission of sins."* — Mat 26:28

> *"This cup is the **new testament** in my **blood**, which is shed for you."* — Lk 22:20

> *"This cup is the **new testament** in my **blood**: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me."* — 1 Cor 11:25

Jesus does not say wine *represents* His blood. He says "this is my blood of the new covenant." The disciples, steeped in Gen 49:11 and Deut 32:14, would not have heard this as innovation. They would have heard it as fulfillment — the blood of grapes has always been covenant blood. Now the one who pours it identifies Himself as the source.

The phrase mirrors Exod 24:8 precisely. Moses said: "Behold the blood of the covenant." Jesus says: "This is my blood of the new covenant." Same structure, same function — ratification through blood. The old covenant blood was animal blood applied externally. The new covenant blood is His own, received internally: *drink it*.

### Bread and Wine Together — The Complete Covenant

$bread and wine form a pair throughout Scripture, and the pairing is not arbitrary. If $bread represents the Word — the terms of the covenant — then wine represents the blood that seals those terms. Together they constitute the complete covenant transaction.

The Sinai sequence makes this explicit:
- **Covenant terms heard** (Exod 24:7 — "All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient") → $bread
- **Blood applied** (Exod 24:8 — "the blood of the covenant") → wine
- **Meal shared** (Exod 24:11 — "did eat and drink") → $bread + wine together

At the Last Supper, Jesus breaks $bread and says "this is my body" — the Word made flesh (Jn 1:14), the living covenant terms. Then He takes the cup and says "this is my blood of the new covenant" — the life poured out to ratify. The early church continued both: "they continued stedfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread" (Acts 2:42).

You do not have a covenant with terms alone (bread without wine) — that is words without commitment. You do not have a covenant with blood alone (wine without bread) — that is sacrifice without content. You need both. The terms must be heard, and the blood must seal them.

### Drink His Blood — Receive His Life

> *"Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his **blood**, ye have no life in you. Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my **blood**, hath eternal life."* — Jn 6:53–54

This saying scandalized Jesus' audience — and it still scandalizes readers who take it as literal biology rather than covenant language. But within the wine-as-covenant-blood framework, the meaning is precise:

To *drink His blood* is to receive His life (Lev 17:11 — the life is in the blood). To receive His life is to enter His covenant. To enter His covenant is to accept the terms ratified by that blood.

This is why Jesus adds in the same discourse: "the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life" (Jn 6:63). The $bread (His words/terms) and the wine (His blood/life) are two dimensions of one covenant reality. Eating and drinking = hearing the terms and receiving the life that seals them.

### Wine of Joy — Covenant Blessing

Within covenant relationship, wine's effect is joy — not mere pleasure, but the deep gladness of shared life between God and man.

> *"And **wine** that maketh glad the heart of man."* — Ps 104:15

> *"Go thy way, eat thy **$[bread]** with joy, and drink thy **wine** with a merry heart; for God now accepteth thy works."* — Eccl 9:7

> *"The new **wine** is found in the cluster, and one saith, Destroy it not; for a blessing is in it."* — Isa 65:8

Notice the Ecclesiastes passage: $bread and wine together, joy and acceptance together. The covenant meal is not solemn duty — it is celebration. God accepts your $fruit (works), so eat and drink with gladness. The joy comes *from* covenant standing. The wine makes glad because it represents covenant life flowing between partners.

This explains why wine features prominently at weddings. $marriage = covenant. The wine at the wedding feast = covenant blood that seals the union. When Jesus turns water into wine at Cana (Jn 2:1–11), the first miracle, He is enacting what He will later say plainly: the new covenant has come, and it surpasses the old as fine wine surpasses water.

### Wine of Wrath — Covenant Rejected

Here is where the symbol reveals its deepest logic. The same wine that represents blessing in covenant becomes wrath when the covenant is broken:

> *"The same shall drink of the **wine** of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation."* — Rev 14:10

> *"And great **$[babylon]** came in remembrance before God, to give unto her the cup of the **wine** of the fierceness of his wrath."* — Rev 16:19

> *"He treadeth the **winepress** of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God."* — Rev 19:15

This is not a different symbol. It is the same blood, producing a different outcome. Covenant blood, when received, brings life — that is wine's joy. Covenant blood, when rejected, produces blood-guilt — that is wine's wrath.

The phrase "poured out without mixture" (Rev 14:10) is striking. The Greek for "without mixture" relates to *kerannumi* (to mix) — the same root behind G185 *akeraios* (unmixed), used of the $dove in Mat 10:16. Undiluted wine. Full-strength covenant blood. There is no water to weaken it, no admixture to soften the judgment. The covenant blood that was offered for atonement now falls on those who refused it — pure, unmixed, undiluted.

$babylon drinks this wine, and makes all nations drunk on her own counterfeit version:

> *"For all nations have drunk of the **wine** of the wrath of her **$[fornication]**."* — Rev 18:3

$babylon produces a false wine — $fornication, the intoxication of $idolatry — and distributes it to the nations. But the true wine, the covenant blood she rejected, returns as judgment. The winepress image in Rev 19:15 depicts covenant blood flowing as judgment — life rejected becomes death administered.

### The Dual Nature — One Wine, Two Outcomes

This is not two symbols awkwardly wearing the same name. It is one symbol whose logic is entirely consistent:

**Wine = covenant blood = life poured out.**

- *Received* → atonement, forgiveness, life, joy, communion with God.
- *Rejected* → blood-guilt, wrath, death, judgment from God.

The same blood does both. The cross is the clearest illustration: the blood of Christ is poured out once. Those who drink it (Jn 6:53–54) receive eternal life. Those who trample it (Heb 10:29 — "counted the blood of the covenant… an unholy thing") receive "a certain fearful looking for of judgment."

The dual nature of wine thus resolves into a single meaning: **covenant blood**, whose effect depends entirely on the receiver's relationship to the covenant.

## Patterns

1. **Wine is defined as blood, not compared to it.** Gen 49:11 and Deut 32:14 use "blood of grapes" as a name for wine — a definitional equation, not a simile. This is the anchor that holds every other occurrence in place.

2. **Blood ratifies covenant in every dispensation.** Noah (Gen 9), Abraham (Gen 15), Sinai (Exod 24:8), the New Covenant (Mat 26:28), the everlasting covenant (Heb 13:20). Wine carries this ratifying function because wine *is* blood.

3. **$bread and wine form a covenant pair.** Terms + ratification. Word + blood. Hearing + receiving. They appear together at Sinai, at the Last Supper, and in the early church's practice. Neither is complete without the other.

4. **Wine's joy is covenant joy.** Ps 104:15, Eccl 9:7, Isa 65:8 — gladness flows from covenant relationship. The merry heart comes because "God now accepteth thy works." Remove the covenant, and wine's joy turns to something else entirely.

5. **Wine's wrath is covenant wrath.** Rev 14:10, 16:19, 19:15 — the same blood that atones becomes judgment when refused. This is not a second meaning but the necessary consequence of the first: blood that should have covered sin instead exposes it.

6. **Undiluted wine = full-strength covenant.** "Without mixture" (Rev 14:10) means no dilution, no softening. Compare G185 *akeraios* (unmixed) used for the $dove — pure, unadulterated loyalty. Covenant blood at full potency, whether for salvation or judgment.

7. **The winepress = blood poured out under pressure.** Rev 19:15 — treading the winepress is the act of extracting blood. The image applies to both Christ's suffering (Isa 63:3 — "I have trodden the winepress alone") and His judgment. The press is where wine is *made* — where life is released from its vessel.

## Connections

- **$bread** — Wine's constant companion. $bread = the Word / covenant terms; wine = the blood / covenant ratification. Together they form the complete covenant meal. Exod 24:7–11 establishes the sequence: terms heard, blood applied, meal shared.

- **$rock** — The $rock is the covenant itself; wine is the blood that seals it. The $rock struck in the wilderness produced water (Exod 17:6) — but the covenant it represents is sealed by blood. Deut 32:14 places "blood of the grape" in the same Song that calls God "the Rock" (Deut 32:4).

- **$fruit** — Wine is the product of the vine's $fruit. Grapes are the $fruit; wine is what flows when the $fruit is pressed. If $fruit = works/deeds, then wine = the life that flows from those works when they are brought to completion (or to judgment).

- **$marriage** — The $marriage feast features wine because $marriage = covenant. Wine at Cana, wine at the Last Supper, wine at "the marriage of the Lamb" (Rev 19:7–9). Covenant blood seals the union between the partners.

- **$babylon** — $babylon distributes counterfeit wine: "the wine of the wrath of her fornication" (Rev 18:3). She intoxicates the nations with false covenant — $idolatry dressed as communion. But the true wine returns as her judgment (Rev 16:19).

- **$fornication** — $fornication is $idolatry — broken covenant loyalty. $babylon's wine is the wine of $fornication: a false communion that substitutes other gods for the true covenant partner. The nations drink it and become drunk — disoriented, unable to distinguish true covenant from false.

- **$harlot** — The $harlot holds a cup "full of abominations and filthiness of her fornication" (Rev 17:4). This is wine corrupted — covenant blood turned into the drink of $idolatry. The $harlot's cup is the anti-communion.

- **$jerusalem** — $babylon's opposite. Where $babylon distributes false wine, $jerusalem receives the true wine of covenant communion. The marriage supper of the Lamb (Rev 19:7–9) is the consummation of the covenant that wine has always pointed toward.
