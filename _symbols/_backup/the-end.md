# The End

**Covenant fulfillment — the goal toward which God's covenant process drives, arriving at its appointed completion.**

> *"And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall **the end** come."* — Mat 24:14

---

## The Key Insight

The Greek word translated "the end" in the Olivet Discourse is G5056 *telos*. It does not mean cessation. It means **the goal arriving**.

Paul uses the same word in Romans 10:4 — "Christ is the *telos* of the law." If *telos* meant "termination," Paul would be saying Christ abolished the Torah. But *telos* means the **aim**, the **purpose**, the **thing the process was driving toward**. Christ is what the Torah was always pointing at.

Again in 1 Timothy 1:5 — "the *telos* of the commandment is love." Love is not where the commandment stops. Love is what the commandment *produces when it reaches its goal*.

This reframes everything Jesus says in Matthew 24. When the disciples ask about "the end," and Jesus answers "the *telos* is not yet" (Mat 24:6), he is not saying "the catastrophe hasn't started yet." He is saying **the purpose hasn't arrived yet**. Wars, $famine, $earthquake — these are not the goal. They are $birth-pains. The goal is something else entirely: the testimony completed, the $kingdom delivered, the covenant purpose fulfilled.

The difference between "the end is coming" and "the goal is arriving" is the difference between dread and hope.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant fulfillment — the appointed goal reached, the purpose achieved

**Opposite:** The beginning (H7225 *reshith* / G746 *arche*) — the starting point of what the end completes

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:6, Mat 24:13–14, Rom 10:4, 1 Cor 10:11, Dan 12:13, Rev 21:6

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word for "end" (H7093 *qets*) comes from H7112 *qatsats* — **to cut off**. The same cutting imagery used for covenant-making (H3772 *karath* = to cut a covenant). The "end" in Hebrew is a *cutting* — not destruction, but the decisive boundary where the covenant process reaches its appointed limit.

**Connected:** $birth-pains, $babylon, $earthquake, $famine, $pestilence, $faith, $kingdom

## The End Across Scripture

### Not the Same Word — Not the Same Meaning

English collapses at least seven distinct biblical words into one — "the end." But Scripture is precise, and the distinctions matter enormously:

**G5056 *telos*** — the goal, the aimed-at outcome. Mat 24:6, 14; Rom 10:4; 1 Tim 1:5. The purpose *arriving*.

**G4930 *synteleia*** — joint-completion, consummation. Mat 24:3 — the disciples ask about the *synteleia* of the age, the winding-up of the entire age-process. From G4931 *synteleo* = to complete together.

**H7093 *qets*** — cut, boundary, termination. Dan 8:17, 9:26, 12:13. The appointed cutting point of the covenant timetable.

**H319 *achariyth*** — the latter end, the aftermath, what comes *after*. Jer 29:11 — "to give you an *achariyth* and a hope." Even the ending has a future.

**H8503 *takliyth*** — completeness, perfection, purpose. Job 26:10. The completion-point where something reaches its fullness.

**G2078 *eschatos*** — last, uttermost. Rev 1:17 — "I am the first and the *eschatos*." The temporal extremity.

**G4009 *peras*** — limit, boundary, geographic edge. Rom 10:18 — "their words unto the *peras* of the world."

When Jesus says "the *telos* is not yet" (Mat 24:6, G5056), he means the *purpose hasn't arrived*. When Daniel speaks of "the time of the *qets*" (Dan 8:17, H7093), he means the appointed *cutting point*. When the disciples ask about the *synteleia* (Mat 24:3, G4930), they mean the *consummation* of the age. Collapsing all of these into a single English word — "the end" — and reading it as "the catastrophe" obscures what each text is actually saying.

### The Cutting Point — Daniel's "End"

> *"And he said, Behold, I will make thee know what shall be in the last **end** of the indignation: for at the time appointed the **end** shall be."* — Dan 8:19

Daniel's word for "end" is H7093 *qets*, from H7112 *qatsats* — to cut off. This is covenant language. The same semantic field as H3772 *karath*, the verb for cutting a covenant (Gen 15:18). Daniel's "end" is not random termination but an **appointed cutting point** — a boundary set in advance on God's timetable.

> *"And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the **end** thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the **end** of the war desolations are determined."* — Dan 9:26

The Messiah is "cut off" (H3772 *karath* — the covenant-cutting verb), and then the *qets* comes "like a flood." The cutting of the Messiah and the cutting-point of the age share the same root imagery. Desolations are "determined" — the Hebrew H2782 *charats* means decreed, decided in advance. Nothing about this "end" is accidental.

> *"But go thou thy way till the **end** be: for thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at the **end** of the days."* — Dan 12:13

Daniel is told to go his way until the *qets*. He will rest (die) and then "stand in his lot" — be raised, receive his covenant inheritance — "at the end of the days." The end is not destruction for Daniel. It is where he receives what was promised. The same word that means judgment for the unfaithful means **inheritance** for the faithful.

This dual outcome is the nature of covenant: the same boundary that executes judgment also delivers promise. The *qets* is where the covenant terms — all of them, blessings and curses — reach their completion.

### "Not Yet the End" — What Jesus Ruled Out

> *"And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but **the end** is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

> *"For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom: and there shall be famines, and pestilences, and earthquakes, in divers places. All these are the beginning of sorrows."* — Mat 24:7–8

Jesus lists the signs — $wars, $nation-against-nation, $famine, $pestilence, $earthquake — and then says explicitly: **the *telos* is not yet**. The goal has not arrived. These are not the fulfillment; they are the $birth-pains. The word "sorrows" in verse 8 is G5604 *odin* — labor pangs. The signs are contractions, not the delivery.

This distinction is critical. Wars and upheavals are not the *telos* — they are the process through which the *telos* arrives. A woman in labor is not experiencing the child; she is experiencing the pangs that produce the child. The signs listed in Matthew 24:4–8 are what the covenant process looks like *before* it reaches its goal.

Notice what Jesus says immediately after: "Then shall they deliver you up... and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake" (Mat 24:9). Then $wickedness increases. Then $love grows cold. Then:

> *"But he that shall endure unto **the end**, the same shall be saved."* — Mat 24:13

Endurance unto the *telos* = endurance unto the goal. $faith — trusting obedience — carried through to the purpose. And then the trigger:

> *"And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall **the end** come."* — Mat 24:14

The testimony completed → then the *telos* comes. The goal is not triggered by catastrophe. It is triggered by the **completion of witness**.

### Finished — Then the End

A pattern emerges across Scripture: the *telos* arrives when testimony reaches its completion. The verb G5055 *teleo* (to finish, to complete) shares the same root as G5056 *telos* (end/goal). Four passages use the same structure:

**Matthew 24:14** — The gospel preached for a witness (*marturion*) → "then (*tote*) the *telos* comes."

**Revelation 11:7** — "When they shall have finished (*telesōsin*, G5055) their testimony (*marturian*)" → the beast acts → the seventh trumpet sounds.

**Revelation 10:7** — "The mystery of God should be finished (*telesthē*, G5055), as he hath declared to his servants the prophets."

**1 Corinthians 15:24** — "Then (*eita*) the *telos*, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God."

All four describe the same sequence: the testimony/mystery reaches completion → the *telos* arrives → the $kingdom is transferred. The finishing (*teleo*) of the witness IS the trigger for the end (*telos*).

The anchor point for this pattern appears to be the seventh trumpet:

> *"The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever."* — Rev 11:15

This is 1 Corinthians 15:24 — "delivers the kingdom." This is Daniel 7:14 — "given him dominion." The *telos* is the moment the $kingdom arrives in full — not the moment history stops, but the moment history's purpose is achieved.

### Christ Is the Telos of the Torah

> *"For Christ is **the end** of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth."* — Rom 10:4

If *telos* meant termination, this verse would teach that Christ abolished the law. But *telos* means the **goal** — what the law was always driving toward. The Torah's instructions, its sacrifices, its types, its covenant structure — all of it pointed at Christ. He is the purpose it was designed to produce.

> *"Now **the end** of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned."* — 1 Tim 1:5

The *telos* of the commandment is love. Love is not where the commandment stops. Love is the fruit the commandment produces when it reaches its intended goal. The commandment was always aiming at love; love is its fulfillment, not its abolition.

This usage confirms the symbolic reading: THE END is not cessation but **purpose achieved**. Whether applied to the Torah, the age, or the testimony of the saints, the *telos* is the moment the aim arrives.

### The Ends of the Ages — Already Arriving

> *"Now all these things happened unto them for ensamples: and they are written for our admonition, upon whom **the ends** of the world are come."* — 1 Cor 10:11

Paul says the *tele* (plural of *telos*) of the ages have already reached (*katantao* — arrived at, met) the Corinthian church. The covenant purposes of previous ages — the Exodus, the wilderness, the patterns of judgment and deliverance — have reached their fulfillment-point in the present generation. The "ends" are not future catastrophes. They are **purposes arriving** in Paul's own day.

> *"But **the end** of all things is at hand: be ye therefore sober, and watch unto prayer."* — 1 Pet 4:7

Peter says the *telos* of all things is near — and his response is not panic but sobriety and prayer. And the very next verse: "judgment must begin at the house of God" (1 Pet 4:17). The *telos* = covenant accountability beginning with God's own people. This is covenant consummation, not cosmic destruction.

### The End as Outcome — Wisdom's Warning

> *"There is a way which seemeth right unto a man, but **the end** thereof are the ways of death."* — Prov 14:12

The $way a person walks has a *telos* — an outcome, a destination. The end is where the path leads. This usage strips away any ambiguity: the "end" is the **result of the process**, the place the road was always going. A road that seems right but leads to death has death as its *telos*.

> *"Then understood I their **end**."* — Ps 73:17

The psalmist enters the sanctuary and sees the outcome of the wicked — their *achariyth* (H319), their latter end, their destiny. This is not cessation. It is the full playing-out of covenant consequences. The "end" of the wicked is what their choices were always producing.

> *"Mark the perfect man, and behold the upright: for **the end** of that man is peace."* — Ps 37:37

The *achariyth* of the righteous is $peace. The end of the upright is not termination but arrival — the covenant promise fully realized. The same word that describes the wicked's doom describes the righteous person's destination.

### The End of Babylon

> *"O thou that dwellest upon many waters, abundant in treasures, thine **end** is come, and the measure of thy covetousness."* — Jer 51:13

> *"An **end** is come, the **end** is come upon the four corners of the land."* — Ezek 7:2

$babylon's *qets* arrives — its appointed cutting point. The "measure of thy covetousness" is telling: the end comes when the measure is full, when the cup of iniquity reaches its boundary. The *qets* is not arbitrary. It is the point where the covenant terms have been fully transgressed and the consequences fully ripen.

> *"And the **end** thereof shall be with a flood."* — Dan 9:26

The end of the sanctuary-destroying power comes "with a flood" — total, decisive, overwhelming. The same *qets* that means judgment for $babylon means deliverance for the faithful. One event, two outcomes — because covenant fulfillment executes both its blessings and its curses simultaneously.

### "I Am the Beginning and the End"

> *"And he said unto me, It is done. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and **the end**. I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely."* — Rev 21:6

> *"I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and **the end**, the first and the last."* — Rev 22:13

God Himself is the *telos*. He is not merely at the end — He IS the end. The beginning (*arche*) and the end (*telos*) are both contained in His person. The covenant process begins in Him and terminates in Him. Every purpose He initiates finds its fulfillment in His own nature.

"It is done" — G1096 *ginomai*, it has come into being, it has become. The same declaration as John 19:30 — *tetelestai*, "it is finished" (G5055 *teleo*). The verb of completion on the cross and the verb of completion at the renewal of all things share the same root as the noun *telos*. What was aimed at has arrived.

## Patterns

1. ***Telos* = purpose arriving, not cessation.** Every NT use of G5056 fits "goal/purpose/outcome" better than "termination." Christ is the goal of the Torah (Rom 10:4). Love is the goal of the commandment (1 Tim 1:5). The completed testimony triggers the goal (Mat 24:14). Endurance to the goal = salvation (Mat 24:13).

2. **The trigger is testimony, not catastrophe.** Mat 24:14 — gospel preached → *telos* comes. Rev 11:7 — witnesses finish (*teleo*) → seventh trumpet. Rev 10:7 — mystery finished (*teleo*). 1 Cor 15:24 — kingdom delivered. The *telos* arrives when the witness is complete, not when the suffering is worst.

3. **Hebrew "end" is covenant-cutting.** H7093 *qets* from H7112 *qatsats* (cut off). The end is a boundary — an appointed line where the covenant process reaches its limit. Daniel's *qets* is always "determined" (H2782 *charats*), always on schedule.

4. **Same end, two outcomes.** Dan 12:13 — Daniel receives his inheritance "at the end." Jer 51:13 — $babylon's end comes as judgment. Ps 37:37 — the righteous person's end is peace. Prov 14:12 — the wrong path's end is death. The *telos* delivers whatever the covenant terms dictate — blessing or curse.

5. **"Not yet" rules out the signs as the goal.** Jesus explicitly separates the signs ($wars, $famine, $earthquake, $nation-against-nation) from the *telos* (Mat 24:6). These are $birth-pains (Mat 24:8 — *odin*). Labor pangs are not the child; the delivery is. The signs indicate the process is underway, not that the goal has arrived.

6. **The ends have already come.** Paul tells the Corinthians that the *tele* of the ages have reached them (1 Cor 10:11). Peter says the *telos* is at hand (1 Pet 4:7). The "end" is not exclusively future — covenant purposes reach fulfillment in every generation that lives at a consummation point.

7. **God IS the End.** Rev 21:6, 22:13 — "I am the beginning and the end." The *telos* is not an event but a Person. All covenant processes terminate in God Himself.

## Connections

- **$birth-pains** — The signs of Mat 24:4–8 are explicitly "the beginning of *odin*" — labor pangs. The *telos* is the delivery, not the contractions. Birth pains are the painful covenant transition that *precedes* the goal.

- **$babylon** — "Thine end is come, and the measure of thy covetousness" (Jer 51:13). $babylon's *qets* arrives when its cup is full. The end of the counterfeit kingdom is the same event as the arrival of the true $kingdom.

- **$famine** — Listed among the not-yet-end signs (Mat 24:7). $famine = God's Word withdrawn. The Word-absence is a birth pang, not the goal. The *telos* comes when the testimony is *completed*, not when it is silenced.

- **$earthquake** — Also listed among the not-yet-end signs (Mat 24:7). God shakes what is shakeable so that what cannot be shaken remains (Heb 12:27). The shaking serves the *telos*: removing the unfaithful so the faithful covenant order stands.

- **$pestilence** — Part of the birth-pains catalog. Covenant death-consequence (H1698 *deber*, same consonants as *dabar* — "word"). The anti-Word is not the goal; it is the consequence of Word-rejection that precedes the goal's arrival.

- **$faith** — "He that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved" (Mat 24:13). $faith = trusting obedience. Endurance to the *telos* is not passive survival but active covenant faithfulness sustained through $birth-pains.

- **$wickedness** — "Because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold" (Mat 24:12). $wickedness = lawlessness/covenant transgression. The increase of *anomia* is a sign that the *telos* is approaching — but the *telos* itself is the triumph of testimony (Mat 24:14), not the triumph of lawlessness.

- **$kingdom** — "Then the end, when he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God" (1 Cor 15:24). The *telos* IS the kingdom transfer — the moment the sovereignty of this world becomes the sovereignty of God and His Christ (Rev 11:15).
