# Sea

**The chaotic mass of nations and peoples — turbulent, restless, ungoverned by covenant.**

> *"And he saith unto me, The **waters** which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues."* — Rev 17:15

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture interprets its own symbols — and the sea is one of the rare cases where it does so explicitly, without parable or inference.

In Rev 17:15, an angel turns to John and gives him the key: the **waters** where $babylon sits are *peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues*. Four terms. Not one metaphorical flourish — four distinct categories of humanity. The waters are not scenery. They are the thing itself.

Once this key is in hand, every sea in prophetic Scripture unlocks. The beasts of Daniel rise from a churning human mass, not a literal ocean. The "troubled sea" of Isaiah is not a weather report — it is a portrait of the wicked. And when John sees a new heaven and a new earth where there is "no more sea," the promise is not about geography. It is about the end of chaotic, rebellious, ungoverned humanity.

The sea is not merely *like* nations. According to Scripture's own interpreter, it **is** nations.

**Symbolizes:** The nations — the chaotic, turbulent mass of peoples ungoverned by God's covenant

**Opposite:** Dry land / $island — that which is separated *out of* the sea, ordered, set apart

**Defining verses:** Rev 17:15, Isa 57:20, Isa 17:12, Dan 7:2–3, Rev 21:1

**The surprise:** The very first act of creation after the initial forming is God *separating* the waters so that dry land appears (Gen 1:9–10). The creation pattern — order drawn out of chaos, land distinguished from sea — is the template for everything that follows. Covenant history is God calling a people *out of* the sea of nations.

**Connected:** $island, $animal, $babylon, $wickedness, $wind

## Sea Across Scripture

### The Angel's Definition

> *"And he saith unto me, The **waters** which thou sawest, where the whore sitteth, are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues."* — Rev 17:15

This is the interpretive cornerstone. The angel does not say the waters *represent* peoples in some loose analogy. He says they **are** peoples (G2992 *laos*), multitudes (G3793 *ochlos*), nations (G1484 *ethnos*), and tongues (G1100 *glossa*). Four words covering every dimension of human collective identity: ethnic, numerical, political, linguistic.

$babylon — the harlot system — *sits on* these waters. She rules over the nations. The imagery is positional: the sea is the base, the foundation on which the worldly kingdom rests. Remove the sea and the harlot has nothing to sit on.

This single verse authorizes every substitution that follows. When Scripture says "sea," the angel says: think *peoples*.

### The Wicked Are the Sea

> *"But the **wicked** are like the troubled **sea**, when it cannot rest, whose **waters** cast up mire and dirt. There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked."* — Isa 57:20–21

Isaiah does not say the wicked are *near* the sea, or *drowned by* the sea. The wicked **are** the troubled sea. The comparison is identity, not proximity. And the defining quality is restlessness — "it cannot rest." The sea churns perpetually, casting up mire and dirt from its depths.

The phrase "no peace to the wicked" appears twice in Isaiah (48:22, 57:21), both times as a summary verdict. $wickedness — covenant transgression, lawlessness — produces the same turbulence in human affairs that storm winds produce on water. There is no stillness in it, no resolution, no rest. The sea's nature is the wicked's nature: ceaseless agitation that surfaces what is foul.

### The Nations Rush Like Many Waters

> *"Woe to the multitude of many people, which make a noise like the noise of the **seas**; and to the rushing of nations, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty **waters**!"* — Isa 17:12

Here the parallel structure makes the equation transparent:

| Line A | Line B |
|--------|--------|
| multitude of many **people** | noise of the **seas** |
| rushing of **nations** | rushing of mighty **waters** |

People ‖ seas. Nations ‖ waters. This is Hebrew synonymous parallelism — the second line restates the first in different terms. Isaiah is not comparing nations *to* waters as an outside metaphor. He is using "seas" and "waters" as synonyms for "people" and "nations." The poetic structure confirms what Rev 17:15 states outright.

And the next verse completes the picture:

> *"The nations shall rush like the rushing of many waters: but God shall **rebuke** them, and they shall flee far off, and shall be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the **$[wind]**, and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind."* — Isa 17:13

God rebukes the sea — and the nations scatter. The $wind drives them like chaff. The same invisible power ($wind = spirit) that stirs the sea can also disperse it at God's command.

### Beasts Rise from the Sea

> *"Daniel spake and said, I saw in my vision by night, and, behold, the four **$[wind]s** of the heaven strove upon the great **sea**. And four great **$[animal]s** came up from the **sea**, diverse one from another."* — Dan 7:2–3

This is the symbolic mechanism by which empires are born. Four $winds (spirits / invisible powers) act upon the great sea (mass of nations). The result: four $animal beasts (kingdoms) rise out of the churning waters.

The sea does not produce the beasts on its own. It must be *stirred* — agitated by spiritual forces. The $wind strives upon the sea, and from that turmoil, political powers emerge. Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome — each rises from the same roiling mass of humanity, each driven to the surface by forces the nations themselves do not see.

> *"And I stood upon the sand of the **sea**, and saw a **$[animal]** rise up out of the **sea**, having seven heads and ten horns, and upon his horns ten crowns, and upon his heads the name of blasphemy."* — Rev 13:1

John sees the same pattern Daniel saw. A beast — a kingdom — rises *out of* the sea. The sea is the source-pool. The beast emerges from the peoples. Empires do not descend from heaven; they coalesce out of the turbulent mass of nations, shaped by invisible powers.

The sea is not merely a backdrop. It is the raw material from which worldly kingdoms are assembled.

### In the Beginning — Land Separated from Sea

> *"And God said, Let the **waters** under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so. And God called the dry land Earth; and the gathering together of the **waters** called he **Seas**: and God saw that it was good."* — Gen 1:9–10

Before there was a nation to call out or a beast to rise, the creation itself encoded the pattern. God's first act of ordering the material world is *separation*: waters gathered, dry land appearing. The land does not exist until it is distinguished from the sea.

This is the template for all of covenant history. God calls Abraham *out of* the nations. He separates Israel *from among* the peoples. He draws a remnant *out of* the world system. The creative act — land from sea, order from chaos, the set-apart from the mixed — is the same act repeated at every scale.

The sea is not evil in itself. God called it "good." But it is *undifferentiated* — a mass without boundary, without structure, without the separation that defines holiness (H6918 *qadosh* — set apart). The sea is what you are called *out of*. The land is where you stand.

### No More Sea

> *"And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was **no more sea**."* — Rev 21:1

The final vision of the renewed creation includes this striking detail: no more sea. If the sea is merely water, this is an odd geographic note in the middle of the most exalted passage in Scripture. But if the sea is the chaotic mass of ungoverned, rebellious nations — then "no more sea" is the climax of the entire biblical story.

No more turbulence. No more restlessness. No more mire cast up from the depths. No more beasts rising from the churning waters. The peoples are no longer a *sea* — they have been gathered, ordered, set apart. What began in Gen 1:9 when God separated land from water reaches its consummation here: the separation is complete, and the chaos is gone.

The substitution is simple: *"there were no more chaotic, rebellious nations."* The new creation is not missing an ocean. It is missing the condition that the ocean represents.

## Patterns

1. **The sea is explicitly defined.** Rev 17:15 does not leave the meaning to inference. Waters = peoples + multitudes + nations + tongues. This is one of Scripture's clearest symbolic keys, and it governs every other occurrence.

2. **The sea is restless.** Isa 57:20 — the troubled sea *cannot rest*. This is its defining character: perpetual agitation, no peace. The wicked share this quality because they *are* the sea — covenant-less humanity in ceaseless turmoil.

3. **The sea is stirred by invisible powers.** Dan 7:2 — the four $winds strive upon the sea. The nations do not churn in a vacuum; spiritual forces ($wind = spirit) drive them. Rev 7:1 — four angels *hold back* the four winds from blowing on the sea, restraining the agitation until God's purposes are ready.

4. **Kingdoms rise from the sea.** Dan 7:3, Rev 13:1 — $animal beasts emerge from the waters. The sea is the raw material of empires. Political powers coalesce from the mass of peoples when stirred by spiritual forces.

5. **The creation pattern is the covenant pattern.** Gen 1:9–10 — land separated from sea. The act of calling dry ground out of the waters is the same act God performs when He calls a people out of the nations. Separation = holiness.

6. **No more sea = the end of the story.** Rev 21:1 — the new creation eliminates the sea. The turbulent, ungoverned mass of humanity is resolved. Every pattern in Scripture that begins with "out of the sea" reaches its conclusion when the sea itself is no more.

## Connections

- **$island** — Land set apart *from* the sea. If the sea = unsanctified nations, then an island — dry ground distinguished from the surrounding waters — suggests a sanctified, set-apart people. The isles "wait for his law" (Isa 42:4); the very thing that sanctifies (Jn 17:17) is what the set-apart peoples await. Islands and $mountains are paired under judgment (Rev 6:14, Rev 16:20) — both represent ordered structures distinguished from the chaos of the sea.

- **$animal** — Beasts rise *from* the sea (Dan 7:3, Rev 13:1). The sea is the source-pool; the beast is the political power that emerges from it. When $wind stirs the sea, kingdoms are produced. The specific animal characterizes the kingdom; the sea is the formless mass from which it takes shape.

- **$babylon** — The harlot sits *on* the waters (Rev 17:1, 15). $babylon's authority rests upon the nations — she rules over peoples, multitudes, nations, and tongues. Remove the sea and the harlot-system collapses. Rev 18's merchants who traded by sea weep when the system falls.

- **$wickedness** — "The wicked are like the troubled sea" (Isa 57:20). $wickedness = lawlessness / covenant transgression (Deut 17:2, 2 Thess 2:7). The sea's restless character *is* the character of the lawless — perpetual agitation, no peace, surfacing what is foul.

- **$wind** — Spirit / invisible power. The four $winds strive upon the sea (Dan 7:2) and are held back from the sea (Rev 7:1). The relationship is cause and effect: $wind acts on the sea, producing or restraining the turbulence of the nations. Angelic forces dispatched from God's throne room drive the movements of peoples.
