# Firmament (sky, atmosphere, space, heaven)

## Approved Definition

**The divider between the restless [sea](/research/symbols/sea/) of peoples below and the gathered heavenly waters above; the place where the ruling lights are set.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Sea & Waters](/books/symbolic-language/sea-and-waters/).

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **firmament** begins: “Fixed foundation; established basis. [Obs.] Custom is the . . . firmament of the law.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The firmament is the divider God set between the two seas of people—the restless nations below and the gathered waters above (the heavenly assembly)—and the place where the lights are hung.

> *“And God said, Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters.”* — Genesis 1:6

> *“And God made the firmament, and divided the waters which were under the firmament from the waters which were above the firmament: and it was so.”* — Genesis 1:7

> *“And God called the firmament Heaven. And the evening and the morning were the second day.”* — Genesis 1:8

> *“And God said, Let there be lights in the firmament of the heaven to divide the day from the night; and let them be for signs, and for seasons, and for days, and years:”* — Genesis 1:14

> *“And let them be for lights in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth: and it was so.”* — Genesis 1:15

> *“And God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night: he made the stars also.”* — Genesis 1:16

> *“And God set them in the firmament of the heaven to give light upon the earth,”* — Genesis 1:17

> *“Praise him, ye heavens of heavens, and ye waters that be above the heavens.”* — Psalms 148:4

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **firmament** begins:

> “Fir”ma*ment, n. Etym: [L. firmamentum, fr. firmare to make firm: cf. F. firmament. See Firm, v. & a.] 1. Fixed foundation; established basis. [Obs.] Custom is the . . . firmament of the law. Jer. Taylor.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Genesis 1 directly supports a firmament dividing waters and holding lights, while Revelation 17:15 shows that waters can symbolize peoples in a different apocalyptic vision. None of the printed texts directly equates Genesis’s lower waters with restless nations or its upper waters with a heavenly assembly, so the decisive reclassification depends on an unestablished cross-context transfer.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: the consensus's divided waters are physical or chaotic cosmic waters, while the book makes them peoples/assemblies, and Rev 17:15 explicitly interprets a different vision's waters (those under Babylon's harlot), not the Genesis waters; transferring that decoding to Genesis 1 arguably replaces the consensus identification of what the firmament divides rather than refining it.

- Revelation 17:15 explicitly interprets the 'waters' in a specific apocalyptic vision (where the harlot sits) as peoples and nations. Applying this specific apocalyptic decoding backward to redefine the cosmic waters of Genesis 1 requires a significant interpretive leap that the texts themselves do not demand.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
