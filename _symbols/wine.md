# Wine

**Blood — covenant blood: life poured out to ratify, seal, and sustain the agreement between God and man.**

> *"He washed his garments in **wine**, and his clothes in the blood of grapes."* — Gen 49:11

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not compare wine to blood. It defines wine *as* blood.

> *"He washed his garments in wine, and his clothes in the **blood of grapes**."* — Gen 49:11

> *"Of the **blood of the grape** thou drankest **wine**."* — Deut 32:14

These are not metaphors. They are definitional equations. The Hebrew parallelism sets the terms as synonyms: wine *is* blood of grapes. And blood, according to the Torah, is one thing:

> *"For the life of the flesh is in the **blood**: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the **blood** that maketh an atonement for the soul."* — Lev 17:11

Blood = life. Wine = blood of grapes. Therefore wine = life — specifically, life poured out. And when life is poured out between two parties, it ratifies a covenant.

This single chain — wine → blood → life → covenant — unlocks every appearance of wine in Scripture, from blessing to wrath, from Melchizedek's table to the winepress of God's fury. The question is never *what* wine represents. The question is always: *whose covenant blood, and what is the drinker's standing before it?*

**Symbolizes:** Blood / Covenant blood — life poured out to ratify agreement

**Opposite:** Thirst / $water — the absence of covenant life, or the unstable, uncovenant element

**Defining verses:** Gen 49:11, Deut 32:14, Lev 17:11, Exod 24:8, Mat 26:28

**The surprise:** The same wine that "maketh glad the heart of man" (Ps 104:15) is "poured out without mixture" as wrath (Rev 14:10). It is not two different symbols. It is the same covenant blood — joy to those inside the covenant, judgment to those who reject it.

**Connected:** $bread, $fruit, $rock, $marriage, $babylon, $fornication, $harlot, $wickedness, $jerusalem

## Wine Across Scripture

### Blood of Grapes — The Definitional Equation

The symbolic meaning of wine is not hidden. It is stated twice, plainly, in the Torah:

> *"He washed his garments in **wine**, and his clothes in the **blood of grapes**."* — Gen 49:11

> *"Thou didst drink the pure **blood of the grape**."* — Deut 32:14

Jacob's blessing over Judah (Gen 49:11) drenches the royal line in wine-blood imagery. The garments of the coming king are stained with it. Deut 32:14 places the "blood of the grape" among the covenant blessings God lavished on Israel — honey from the rock, oil from the flinty $rock, butter and milk, fat of lambs, and the blood of the grape as wine.

The Hebrew parallelism in Gen 49:11 is synonymous: *wine* and *blood of grapes* are the same thing, placed side by side as equivalent terms. This is not a poetic flourish. It is how Hebrew definitional statements work. Scripture is naming wine's symbolic identity: **blood**.

And blood has already been defined:

> *"For the **life** of the flesh is in the **blood**: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an **atonement** for your souls."* — Lev 17:11

Blood contains life. Blood makes atonement. Blood is what God accepts on the altar. The chain is complete: wine → blood of grapes → blood → life → atonement.

### The Blood That Ratifies — Exodus 24

The fullest picture of wine's symbolic role appears in the covenant ceremony at Sinai — even though the word "wine" never appears. The *pattern* of covenant ratification reveals why wine later becomes the blood-symbol at every covenant meal:

> *"And Moses took the book of the covenant, and read in the audience of the people: and they said, All that the LORD hath said will we do, and be obedient."* — Exod 24:7

> *"And Moses took the **blood**, and sprinkled it on the people, and said, Behold the **blood of the covenant**, which the LORD hath made with you concerning all these words."* — Exod 24:8

> *"They saw God, and did **eat and drink**."* — Exod 24:11

Three steps: (1) Moses reads the covenant words — the terms are heard. (2) Blood is sprinkled — the covenant is sealed. (3) The elders eat and drink in God's presence — the covenant meal is shared.

This is the template. When $bread and wine appear together throughout Scripture, they mirror this sequence: $bread = the Word, the covenant terms spoken and received; wine = the blood, the covenant ratified and sealed. Together they form the complete covenant meal.

The phrase "blood of the covenant" (Exod 24:8) echoes across the entire biblical story:

> *"As for thee also, by the **blood of thy covenant** I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit."* — Zech 9:11

> *"The God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, through the **blood of the everlasting covenant**."* — Heb 13:20

Covenant blood does two things: it frees prisoners and it raises the dead. Wine, as the symbol of that blood, carries the same weight at every table where it appears.

### "This Cup Is the New Covenant in My Blood"

Jesus does not invent a new symbol at the Last Supper. He fulfills the one Scripture has been building since Genesis:

> *"For this is my **blood** of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins."* — Mat 26:28

> *"This cup is the new covenant in my **blood**, which is shed for you."* — Lk 22:20

> *"This cup is the new testament in my **blood**: this do ye, as oft as ye drink it, in remembrance of me."* — 1 Cor 11:25

The language is precise: *this cup is the new covenant in my blood*. Not "this cup represents" — *this cup IS*. Jesus identifies the wine in the cup as covenant blood, just as Gen 49:11 identified wine as the blood of grapes. The chain that began with Jacob's blessing reaches its terminus: the blood of grapes → the blood of the covenant → the blood of Christ.

And the sequence at the Last Supper mirrors Exod 24 exactly. Jesus speaks the covenant words ($bread — "this is my body"). He offers the covenant blood (wine — "this is my blood"). The disciples eat and drink in His presence. Covenant terms → covenant blood → covenant meal. The pattern has not changed since Sinai.

### Drink His Blood — Receive His Life

> *"Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and **drink his blood**, ye have no life in you. Whoso eateth my flesh, and **drinketh my blood**, hath eternal life."* — Jn 6:53–54

This passage scandalized many of Jesus' listeners, but within the wine-blood-life chain it is entirely consistent. If blood = life (Lev 17:11), and wine = blood of grapes (Gen 49:11), then drinking His blood = receiving His life. The one who drinks enters the covenant. The one who refuses has "no life" — not because of arbitrary exclusion, but because the life is *in* the blood, and refusing the blood means refusing the life.

The same verb structure — eat $bread, drink wine — is the covenant meal. Eating His flesh = receiving His word (Jn 6:63 confirms: "the words that I speak unto you, they are spirit, and they are life"). Drinking His blood = entering His covenant through the life He poured out. $bread and wine together = receiving the full covenant: terms and ratification, word and blood.

### Wine That Gladdens — Covenant Blessing

When the covenant relationship is intact, wine is joy:

> *"And **wine** that maketh glad the heart of man."* — Ps 104:15

> *"Go thy way, eat thy **$[bread]** with joy, and drink thy **wine** with a merry heart; for God now accepteth thy works."* — Eccl 9:7

> *"Destroy it not; for a **blessing** is in it."* — Isa 65:8

The joy of wine is not the pleasure of intoxication. It is the gladness of covenant life shared. "God now accepteth thy works" (Eccl 9:7) — the context is acceptance within the covenant relationship. The $bread and the wine are consumed together: the Word received and the blood shared. This is the covenant meal of blessing, the same pattern as Exod 24:11 — eating and drinking in God's presence.

Isa 65:8 speaks of new wine found in a cluster, with "a blessing in it." The word for blessing (H1293 *berakah*) carries covenant overtones throughout the Old Testament. The wine is not merely good — it is *covenantally* good. Life is in it.

### Wine of Wrath — The Same Blood, Rejected

Here is where the symbol reveals its full force. The same wine that gladdens the heart of the covenant-keeper becomes wrath poured out on the covenant-breaker:

> *"The same shall drink of the **wine** of the wrath of God, which is poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation."* — Rev 14:10

> *"And the great city was divided into three parts, and the cities of the nations fell: and great $[babylon] came in remembrance before God, to give unto her the cup of the **wine** of the fierceness of his wrath."* — Rev 16:19

> *"He treadeth the **winepress** of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God."* — Rev 19:15

The wine of wrath is not a different symbol from the wine of gladness. It is the same covenant blood, encountered by those who have rejected the covenant. In covenant, blood brings life, atonement, joy. Outside covenant, blood brings guilt, judgment, death.

The phrase "poured out without mixture" (Rev 14:10) is telling. The Greek behind "without mixture" involves *kerannumi* — the same root behind G185 *akeraios*, the word Jesus used for $dove-like disciples who are "unmixed" (Mat 10:16). The wrath-wine is *undiluted* — pure covenant blood, full strength, no mercy mixed in. The covenant that should have been a source of life becomes the instrument of judgment when its terms are spurned.

This dual nature — joy inside the covenant, wrath outside — is not a contradiction. It is the nature of blood itself. Blood on the doorposts at Passover meant life for those inside and death for those outside. The same blood. The same night. Different standing.

### The Winepress — Blood Poured Out

The winepress is where grapes are crushed and their blood flows. Scripture uses it consistently as a place of judgment — not because wine itself is negative, but because the winepress is where life is *extracted* by force:

> *"He treadeth the **winepress** of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God."* — Rev 19:15

The image is unmistakable: treading the winepress = blood flowing. The $fruit of the vine is crushed, and what comes out is blood. In Isa 63:2–3, the LORD's garments are stained red because He has trodden the winepress alone — recalling Gen 49:11 where the coming king washes his garments in wine, in the blood of grapes. The blood-stained garments of the Messiah connect the two roles: the one who pours out covenant blood for life, and the one who treads out covenant blood in judgment.

### Bread and Wine — The Complete Covenant Meal

$bread and wine appear together with remarkable consistency. Their pairing is not incidental — it represents the two halves of the covenant:

- **$bread** = the Word, the covenant terms — what is spoken and received
- **Wine** = the blood, the covenant ratification — what seals and gives life to the terms

Melchizedek brings $bread and wine to Abraham (Gen 14:18). The Sinai elders eat and drink in God's presence (Exod 24:11). Jesus breaks $bread and shares wine at the Last Supper (Lk 22:19–20). The early church devotes itself to "breaking of $bread" (Acts 2:42) — the ongoing covenant meal.

In each case, the sequence holds: the terms are given, the blood is shared, the covenant is in force. Without the $bread there are no terms to agree to. Without the wine there is no blood to seal them. The covenant meal requires both.

## The Chain

Wine → blood of grapes (Gen 49:11) → blood (Lev 17:11 — life is in the blood) → covenant blood (Exod 24:8 — sprinkled on the people) → new covenant blood (Mat 26:28 — shed for remission of sins) → received = life and joy (Jn 6:53–54; Ps 104:15) → rejected = wrath and judgment (Rev 14:10; Rev 19:15).

One substance. One meaning. Two outcomes — depending on covenant standing.

---

## Patterns

1. **Wine is defined as blood — not compared to it.** Gen 49:11 and Deut 32:14 use synonymous parallelism to equate wine with "blood of grapes." This is a definitional statement, not a metaphor.

2. **Blood ratifies covenant.** Exod 24:8 (Sinai), Zech 9:11 (prisoners freed), Heb 13:20 (resurrection), Mat 26:28 (new covenant). Every major covenant in Scripture is sealed with blood. Wine serves as this blood at the covenant table.

3. **$bread + wine = terms + ratification.** The pair appears together whenever the full covenant meal is enacted. $bread = the spoken Word; wine = the sealing blood. Separating them is separating the covenant into incomplete halves.

4. **Joy and wrath are the same wine.** Ps 104:15 (gladdens the heart) and Rev 14:10 (wrath without mixture) use the same symbol. The difference is not in the wine — it is in the drinker's covenant standing.

5. **"Without mixture" = undiluted blood.** The wrath-wine is *akerastos* — unmixed. Covenant blood at full strength. The same root (*kerannumi*) appears in G185 *akeraios* (the "harmless/unmixed" of Mat 10:16). Pure wine = pure blood = undiluted covenant force.

6. **The winepress connects to Gen 49:11.** Garments washed in wine (Gen 49:11) → garments stained from treading the winepress (Isa 63:2–3, Rev 19:13–15). The king's blood-stained clothes appear in both blessing and judgment — because both flow from covenant blood.

7. **Drinking blood = receiving life.** Jn 6:53–54 is not novel; it is the logical conclusion of Lev 17:11 (life is in the blood) + the covenant meal pattern (eat and drink in God's presence). To drink is to receive. To refuse is to have "no life in you."

## Connections

- **$bread** — Wine's constant companion. $bread = the Word / covenant terms; wine = the blood / covenant ratification. Together they form the complete covenant meal — terms spoken and sealed — from Sinai (Exod 24:7–11) to the Last Supper (Lk 22:19–20).

- **$rock** — The $rock is the unchanging covenant itself. Wine is the blood that ratifies that covenant. Deut 32:14 places the "blood of the grape" alongside blessings drawn from the $rock: "he made him to suck honey out of the rock, and oil out of the flinty rock… and thou didst drink the pure blood of the grape."

- **$fruit** — $fruit = works/deeds. The vine produces $fruit (grapes), and from $fruit comes wine (blood). The connection suggests that the works of the covenant — the $fruit of obedience — are what the covenant life flows through. The vine must bear $fruit before there can be blood.

- **$marriage** — $marriage = covenant picture. Wine at the wedding (Jn 2:1–10) is covenant blood at the covenant ceremony. Jesus' first sign — turning $water into wine — enacts the transformation from the old, inadequate element to the new covenant blood.

- **$babylon** — $babylon = the worldly kingdom, man's substitute system for God's covenant rule. $babylon is given "the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath" (Rev 16:19). The false kingdom that counterfeited covenant life receives the covenant blood as judgment.

- **$fornication** — $fornication = idolatry / serving other gods. "All nations have drunk of the wine of her fornication" (Rev 18:3). The nations drink $babylon's wine — false covenant blood — sealing their allegiance to the false system instead of to God. Counterfeit wine = counterfeit covenant.

- **$harlot** — The $harlot = idolater / covenant breaker. She holds a cup "full of abominations and filthiness of her fornication" (Rev 17:4). The harlot's cup is a parody of the covenant cup — offering false blood, false life, false allegiance.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness = lawlessness / covenant transgression. When the covenant is transgressed, the blood that should have sealed life becomes the blood of guilt. The wine of wrath (Rev 14:10) is the covenant blood turned against the lawless — the same blood, now un-atoning.

- **$jerusalem** — $jerusalem = bride / covenant community. The true covenant community is where the wine of blessing is shared — where covenant blood brings gladness, fellowship, and life. $jerusalem is the wedding feast; $babylon is the drunken counterfeit.
