# Way

**Conduct — manner of life. When definite ("THE way"), the pattern of conduct defined by God's commandments.**

> *"For I know him, that he will command his children and his household after him, and they shall keep the **way** of the LORD, to do justice and judgment."* — Gen 18:19

---

## The Key Insight

A road is made by walking it. In Hebrew, the word for "way" (H1870 *derek*) comes from H1869 *darak* — "to tread, to walk." A *derek* is not a road that exists before anyone walks it. It is the path *created by repeated treading*. The road is the walking.

This is why Scripture can use the same word for a physical journey and a moral life without any sense of metaphor. They are the same thing. A person's *way* is not the direction they are heading — it is **how they walk**. Their conduct. Their manner of life. The road they are wearing into the ground by their habits.

And when the definite article appears — *the* way, *the* way of the LORD — something shifts. The road is no longer self-made. It is the pattern God has laid down: His commandments, His justice, His judgment. The way of the LORD is explicitly defined in the very first time it appears:

> *"They shall keep the **way** of the LORD, **to do justice and judgment**."* — Gen 18:19

This verse does not explain the metaphor. It gives the equation. The way of the LORD *is* to do justice and judgment. Conduct defined by divine standard.

Every other appearance of "way" in Scripture — whether general or definite, whether in Proverbs or in John — builds on this foundation.

**Symbolizes:** Conduct / manner of life — and when definite ("THE way"), Torah-faithfulness / God's commandments as the pattern of conduct

**Opposite:** $wickedness (lawlessness, covenant transgression), crooked paths, the broad way

**Defining verses:** Gen 18:19, Prov 12:15, Isa 35:8, Mat 7:13–14, Jn 14:6, Acts 24:14, 2 Pet 2:21

**The surprise:** The early church was not called "Christians" first. They were called "the Way" (Acts 9:2, 19:9, 19:23, 24:14) — a name that identified them not by belief but by *manner of life*.

**See also:** $the-way — the definite form. When the article appears ("THE way"), the meaning narrows from generic conduct to the specific covenant pattern God defines and Christ embodies.

**Connected:** $the-way, $truth, $light, $highway, $fruit, $bread, $wickedness, $faith

## Way Across Scripture

### Two Kinds of Way — Indefinite and Definite

The word *derek* appears over 700 times in the Old Testament. It describes literal journeys, moral instruction, divine action, and human character — often all at once. But a critical distinction runs through every occurrence: **whose way is it?**

When the way belongs to a person — "his way," "their ways," "the way of a fool" — it means that person's conduct. Their pattern of behavior. How they live:

> *"The **way** of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise."* — Prov 12:15

> *"And $[ephraim] walked not in the **ways** of the LORD, but walked in the **way** of the kings of Israel."* — 2 Ki 21:22 (of Amon walking after Manasseh's pattern)

> *"For all the **ways** of a man are before the eyes of the LORD, and he pondereth all his goings."* — Prov 5:21

A person's "way" is their conduct reviewed — the sum of their choices, their habits, the path they have worn by repeated treading. A secular dictionary would call this "path" or "route." But the symbolic force is moral: Scripture never separates the physical road from the life lived on it.

When the way belongs to God — "the way of the LORD," "the way of truth," "the way of righteousness" — it means something more specific. It means **the commandments**, the divine pattern of conduct:

> *"They shall keep the **way** of the LORD, to do justice and judgment."* — Gen 18:19

> *"The **way** of righteousness"* ‖ *"the holy commandment delivered unto them."* — 2 Pet 2:21

Peter's parallelism is explicit. The way of righteousness *is* the holy commandment. They are the same thing stated twice.

This distinction — indefinite way (conduct in general) vs. definite way (God's commandments) — mirrors the same pattern found in $truth. A truth is a fact. *The* $truth is God's standard, His commandments (Ps 119:142, Jn 17:17). A way is how someone lives. *The* way is how God says to live.

### The Two Ways — Broad and Narrow

> *"Enter ye in at the strait gate: for wide is the gate, and broad is the **way**, that leadeth to destruction, and many there be which go in thereat: Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the **way**, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it."* — Mat 7:13–14

This is the most concise statement of the two-way pattern that runs throughout Scripture. Two gates. Two ways. Two outcomes. The choice is not between two destinations but between two *manners of walking*. Broad conduct — permissive, undisciplined, self-directed — leads to destruction. Narrow conduct — constrained, disciplined, Torah-shaped — leads to life.

The word "narrow" is G2346 *thlibo* — to press, to compress, to afflict. The narrow way is not merely less popular. It *presses in* on the one who walks it. It constrains. It shapes conduct by restriction, the way a riverbank shapes a river.

Judges 2:22 frames the same test in covenant terms:

> *"That through them I may prove Israel, whether they will keep the **way** of the LORD to walk therein, as their fathers did keep it, or not."* — Judg 2:22

God uses the nations remaining in the land as a test — will Israel keep the *way* of the LORD? "To walk therein" is not redundant. The way *is* walking. Keeping the way is maintaining the conduct. The test is whether Israel's manner of life will hold.

### "I Am THE Way" — Conduct Incarnate

> *"Jesus saith unto him, I am the **way**, the $[truth], and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."* — Jn 14:6

Three definite articles. THE way. THE $truth. THE life. If *the way* means God's pattern of conduct, and *the* $truth means God's commandments, then Jesus is claiming to be the Torah made flesh — the living embodiment of right conduct and divine standard. The third term, "the life," follows as consequence: right conduct according to God's standard *produces* life.

This is not an abstraction. Jesus walked perfectly. His manner of life (the way) was entirely shaped by the Father's commandments (the $truth), and the result was life — resurrection life, abundant life, the life that the narrow way leads to. He is not merely pointing to the road. He *is* the road. The road is made by walking, and He walked it perfectly.

The progression in John 14:6 parallels the logic of Proverbs:

> *"For the commandment is a $[lamp]; and the law is $[light]; and reproofs of instruction are the **way** of life."* — Prov 6:23

Commandment (= $truth) → law (= $light, visible example) → way of life (= conduct shaped by both). The triad in Proverbs and the triad in John are the same structure: God's standard, made visible, producing a manner of life.

### "The Way" — The First Name of the Church

Before believers were called "Christians" in Antioch (Acts 11:26), they had another name. Luke uses it six times in Acts, and it deserves attention:

> *"And Saul, yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, went unto the high priest, and desired of him letters to Damascus to the synagogues, that if he found any of this **way**…"* — Acts 9:2

> *"But when divers were hardened, and believed not, but spake evil of that **way** before the multitude…"* — Acts 19:9

> *"And the same time there arose no small stir about that **way**."* — Acts 19:23

> *"And I persecuted this **way** unto the death…"* — Acts 22:4

> *"But this I confess unto thee, that after the **way** which they call heresy, so worship I the God of my fathers, believing all things which are written in the law and in the prophets."* — Acts 24:14

> *"And when Felix heard these things, having more perfect knowledge of that **way**…"* — Acts 24:22

Paul's definition in Acts 24:14 is decisive. "The Way" is not a creed, not a belief system in the abstract. Paul defines it as *believing all things written in the law and the prophets* — Torah-faithfulness. The early believers were identified by their manner of life, their conduct, their adherence to the covenant pattern. They were "the Way" because they walked it.

This also explains why Apollos, who "knew only the baptism of John," was described as one who was "instructed in the way of the Lord" yet needed Priscilla and Aquila to "expound unto him the way of God more perfectly" (Acts 18:25–26). His conduct was right but incomplete. The way could be more fully known.

And when Paul speaks of "the way of salvation" (Acts 16:17), or when Peter warns of those who blaspheme "the way of truth" (2 Pet 2:2), the definite article carries the same force. THE way — the one manner of conduct that God prescribes. Not a road among roads, but the pattern.

### Journey as Conduct — Three Surprising Encounters "By the Way"

Physical journeys in Scripture function as conduct parables. Three episodes demonstrate this:

**Moses confronted (Exod 4:24):** After receiving his commission at the burning bush, Moses is traveling back to Egypt when the LORD meets him "by the way in the inn, and sought to kill him." Why? Moses had neglected to circumcise his son — a failure of covenant conduct. Zipporah acts, and Moses is spared. The literal journey is interrupted because the *manner of life* is wrong. The way (journey) reveals the way (conduct).

**Balaam blocked (Num 22:22–31):** The angel of the LORD stands "in the way" to oppose Balaam. The donkey sees the angel; Balaam does not. The angel says: "I went out to withstand thee, because thy way is perverse before me" (Num 22:32). The Hebrew *yarat* (perverse/reckless) describes Balaam's *conduct*, not his road. The donkey stops because the road itself has become impassable — a physical parable of conduct that God opposes.

**Joseph's warning (Gen 45:24):** As Joseph sends his brothers back to Canaan, he gives one instruction: "See that ye fall not out by the way." The literal journey to Canaan carries an implicit moral warning: maintain right conduct with each other. Do not let the old patterns of jealousy and deception return during the journey. The road home is the conduct on the way home.

In all three cases, the physical road and the moral life are inseparable. Scripture does not shift between literal and figurative uses of *derek* — it holds them together as a single reality.

### Way and Highway — How You Walk and Where You Walk

> *"And an **$[highway]** shall be there, and a **way**, and it shall be called The **way** of holiness; the unclean shall not pass over it."* — Isa 35:8

Isaiah places $highway (*mesillah*) and way (*derek*) together in the same verse, and the result is significant. The $highway is the covenant path — the elevated, prepared road of faithfulness (from H5549 *salal*, to raise up, to cast up). The way is the conduct on that path. Both must align. You can be on the right $highway with the wrong conduct, or walking rightly on no $highway at all. Isaiah's "way of holiness" unites them: the right path *and* the right manner of walking.

Proverbs 16:17 makes the same distinction in a single line:

> *"The **$[highway]** of the upright is to depart from evil: he that keepeth his **way** preserveth his soul."* — Prov 16:17

The $highway defines trajectory (departing from evil). The way defines ongoing conduct (preserving the soul). The $highway is WHERE you walk — the covenant path itself. The way is HOW you walk — the life you live on it.

When Isaiah says "the $[highway]s lie waste, the wayfaring man ceaseth: he hath broken the covenant" (Isa 33:8), the $highway's desolation is directly tied to covenant-breaking. No covenant faithfulness → no path → no travelers. The $highway disappears when the covenant is abandoned, and the way (conduct) that should have been walked on it disappears with it.

### The Way of God Taught Truly

> *"Master, we know that thou art true, and teachest the **way** of God in truth."* — Mat 22:16

The Pharisees mean this as flattery, but their words contain more $truth than they intend. "The way of God" — God's prescribed pattern of conduct — taught "in $truth" — according to God's commandments. Jesus does exactly this. He teaches how to live (the way) according to God's standard (the $truth). He does not separate doctrine from conduct, belief from behavior.

This is why "the way" and "the $truth" appear together so consistently. The $truth is the standard (God's commandments). The way is the conduct that follows from keeping that standard. They are inseparable: knowing the $truth produces the way; walking the way demonstrates the $truth.

Paul captures this chain in Romans:

> *"And the **way** of peace have they not known."* — Rom 3:17

They do not know the *conduct* that leads to peace — because they do not keep the commandments that define it. $wickedness (lawlessness, covenant transgression) is the absence of the way, the refusal to walk as God prescribes. "There is no peace, saith my God, to the wicked" (Isa 57:21) — the same principle from the opposite direction.

## Patterns

1. **Way = conduct, not destination.** The Hebrew root *darak* (to tread) means the way is *made by walking*. Scripture never uses *derek* to mean a place you arrive at — it means how you get there. The road and the walking are inseparable.

2. **Indefinite way = general conduct; definite way = God's commandments.** "His way" = his manner of life. "THE way of the LORD" = Torah-faithfulness. This parallels $truth (a truth = fact; THE $truth = God's standard).

3. **Journey = moral parable.** Moses, Balaam, and Joseph's brothers demonstrate that every physical journey in Scripture carries implicit moral weight. Being confronted "by the way" means being confronted about conduct.

4. **"The Way" as ecclesial identity.** Acts 9:2, 19:9, 19:23, 22:4, 24:14, 24:22 — the early church was known by its conduct, not merely its creed. Paul defines "the Way" as believing all things in the law and prophets.

5. **Way + $truth + life = a single chain.** Jn 14:6 unites conduct (way), standard ($truth), and result (life). Prov 6:23 unites commandment, $light, and way of life. The triad is consistent: God's standard → visible conduct → life.

6. **Two ways, two outcomes.** Mat 7:13–14 crystallizes the pattern: broad conduct → destruction; narrow conduct → life. Judg 2:22 frames it as the covenant test: will they keep the way or not?

7. **$highway + way = location + conduct.** Isa 35:8 and Prov 16:17 distinguish WHERE you walk ($highway = covenant path) from HOW you walk (way = conduct). Both must be present for holiness.

8. **Jesus IS the way.** Not merely a teacher of conduct, but conduct incarnate — the Torah walked perfectly. The road made by His walking is the road others are invited to walk.

## Connections

- **$truth** — The standard that defines the way. $truth = God's commandments; way = the conduct shaped by those commandments. "The way of truth" (2 Pet 2:2) is not a redundancy — it is the manner of life that follows from keeping God's standard. Jn 14:6 places them side by side because they are inseparable: the $truth defines, the way enacts.

- **$light** — The visible demonstration of walking in the way. $light = visible obedience, the example that others can see. Prov 6:23 chains commandment → $light → way of life. Those who walk the way *become* $light — their conduct is the visible example (Mat 5:16). "He that doeth $[truth] cometh to the $[light]" (Jn 3:21) — keeping the standard produces visible conduct.

- **$highway** — The covenant path the way is walked on. $highway = WHERE (the raised, prepared covenant road); way = HOW (the conduct of the walker). Isa 35:8 unites them as "the way of holiness." When the covenant is broken, the $highway lies waste (Isa 33:8) — no path, no walk, no way.

- **$bread** — The word that sustains the walker. $bread = God's word / covenant terms. "Man shall not live by $[bread] alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Mat 4:4). The manna was a test of conduct — would Israel walk in God's Torah or not (Exod 16:4)? $bread defines the way by providing the instruction for right conduct.

- **$fruit** — The evidence of which way was walked. $fruit = works / deeds. "By their $[fruit]s ye shall know them" (Mat 7:16) appears in the same sermon as the two ways (Mat 7:13–14). The broad way and the narrow way both produce $fruit — one kind reveals the way of life, the other reveals $wickedness.

- **$wickedness** — The opposite of the way. $wickedness = lawlessness, covenant transgression. "The way of peace have they not known" (Rom 3:17) is the absence of the way — conduct shaped by rejection of God's standard. The broad way is the way of $wickedness: self-directed conduct without covenant constraint.

- **$faith** — The posture of the walker. $faith = trusting obedience — the response to God's standard that produces conduct. Israel failed to enter rest because of unbelief, which Hebrews equates with disobedience (Heb 3:18–19). $faith is what makes the way walkable: trusting obedience to the commandments that define the path.
