# Moon

## Summary

**The faithful witness — the reflecting, submissive side of a relationship. It pictures the Son submitting to and reflecting the Father, the wife submitting to her husband, and the Church submitting to Christ as Christ submits to the Father.**

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **moon** begins: “The celestial orb which revolves round the earth; the satellite of the earth; a secondary planet, whose light, borrowed from the sun, is reflected to the earth, and serves to dispel the darkness of night.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The moon is the faithful witness—the Son—who has no light of His own but reflects the Father’s, the lesser light that rules the night and marks appointed times.

> *“It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a faithful witness in heaven. Selah.”* — Psalms 89:37

> *“And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood,”* — Revelation 1:5

> *“Then answered Jesus and said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, The Son can do nothing of himself, but what he seeth the Father do: for what things soever he doeth, these also doeth the Son likewise.”* — John 5:19

> *“I can of mine own self do nothing: as I hear, I judge: and my judgment is just; because I seek not mine own will, but the will of the Father which hath sent me.”* — John 5:30

> *“But I would have you know, that the head of every man is Christ; and the head of the woman is the man; and the head of Christ is God.”* — 1 Corinthians 11:3

> *“Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord.”* — Ephesians 5:22

> *“For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body.”* — Ephesians 5:23

> *“Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing.”* — Ephesians 5:24

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Evidence by Sense

The entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Core sense:** The faithful witness — the reflecting, submissive side of a relationship. It pictures the Son submitting to and reflecting the Father, the wife submitting to her husband, and the Church submitting to Christ as Christ submits to the Father.
2. **Defining witnesses:** Ps 89:37; Rev 1:5; John 5:19, 30; 1 Cor 11:3; Eph 5:22-24
3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **the Church reflecting Christ's light as the moon reflects the sun.**

- The printed texts establish that the moon is called a faithful witness, that Jesus is called the faithful witness, that light shines in darkness, and that the Son acts dependently upon the Father. However, none explicitly identifies the moon with Jesus or says that the Son reflects the Father's light; the argument depends on linking shared descriptions typologically. The references also do not directly support the entry's claims about the lesser light ruling the night and marking appointed times.

- One could argue REFINED: the book keeps the consensus's core category (moon = a person/body reflecting borrowed divine light, the lesser light ruling the night) and merely re-specifies which person, so the general referent-type survives. Additionally, the verbal link between Ps 89:37 ('faithful witness') and Rev 1:5 (Christ 'the faithful witness') gives the book a plausible textual bridge, which could be read as sharpening rather than overturning the tradition.

- Traditional allegory pairs sun=Christ / moon=Church; reassigning the moon to Christ severs that pairing and rests on an associative ‘faithful witness’ parallel rather than an explicit scriptural equation.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
