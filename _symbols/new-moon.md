# New Moon — The Renewed Moon

**The Hebrew word translated "new moon" means "renewed" — restored to completeness. Every use of the root verb in Scripture describes restoration to fullness, not a bare beginning.**

> *"Blow up the **trumpet** in the **renewed moon** (H2320 chodesh), in the **$[full-moon]** (H3677 keseh), on our solemn feast day."* — Ps 81:3

---

## The Key Insight

The English phrase "new moon" carries modern astronomical baggage — it means the moment the moon is invisible, in conjunction between earth and sun. But the Hebrew word H2320 *chodesh* does not mean "dark moon" or "invisible moon." It comes from a verb that means something quite specific.

H2318 *chadash* = "to be new; causatively, **to rebuild**, **to renew**, **to repair**." Not "to start from zero" — to **restore to completeness**.

Search every use of this root in Scripture. The pattern is unmistakable:

| Verse | What is "renewed" (H2318/H2319) | Meaning |
|-------|----------------------------------|---------|
| Ezek 36:26 | "A **new** heart... a **new** spirit" | Fully restored, not partially begun |
| Isa 43:19 | "I will do a **new** thing" | Something completely effective |
| Ps 104:30 | "Thou **renewest** the face of the earth" | Restored to fullness |
| Ps 51:10 | "**Renew** a right spirit within me" | Restored to wholeness |
| 1 Sam 11:14 | "**Renew** the kingdom" | Restored to its proper state |
| 2 Chr 15:8 | "**Renewed** the altar of the LORD" | Rebuilt, not started from scratch |
| 2 Chr 24:12 | "**Renew** the house of the LORD" | Repaired to completion |
| Isa 40:31 | "**Renew** their strength" | Strength restored to full capacity |
| Lam 3:23 | "**New** every morning" | Fresh, complete — like the dawn |

In every case, the word means restoration to a complete, unblemished state. A "renewed heart" is not an empty heart — it's a fully restored one. A "renewed altar" is not a bare foundation — it's a repaired, functional altar. A "renewed kingdom" is not a kingdom just beginning — it's one restored to its rightful order.

Applied to the moon: the *chodesh* is the moon **renewed to completeness** — its most perfect, fully restored form. The noun H2320 is the passive participle of H2318: "the thing that has been renewed." The renewed moon = the moon at fullness.

The $throne study adds a second line of evidence. The word *keseh* (H3677 = full moon) that Ps 81:3 equates with *chodesh* shares more than a poetic parallel — it shares its root (H3680 *kasah* = to fill/cover) and its consonants (כסא) with *kissé* (H3678 = $throne). The $full-moon IS the $throne: the day of fullness is the day the king takes his seat. If the *chodesh* = the *keseh*, and the *keseh* = the כסא, then the renewed moon is the throne-day — the day authority is renewed to completeness. This isn't a coincidence of vocabulary; it's a three-word chain from one root: **covering** (H3680) → **fullness** (H3677) → **throne** (H3678). The renewed moon marks the day the king's seat is filled.

**Symbolizes:** Renewal to completeness — the cycle restored to its fullest, most perfect state

**Opposite:** The dark/invisible moon — absence, concealment, incompleteness. All shadow, no witness.

**Connected:** $full-moon, $throne, $shadow, $trumpet

## Psalm 81:3 — The Defining Verse

> *"Blow up the $[trumpet] in the **chodesh** (H2320), in the **keseh** (H3677 = $[full-moon]), on our solemn feast day."* — Ps 81:3

This verse uses Hebrew synonymous parallelism — placing two terms side by side to describe the same event:

- **chodesh** (H2320) = the renewed moon
- **keseh** (H3677) = the $full-moon (fullness)

Without an inserted "and," the text equates the two. The command is to blow the shofar for one unified event: the renewed moon celebration, which IS the full moon. The feast day (H2282 *chag*) falls on this day.

Psalm 81 continues (vv.4-5): "For this is a statute for $[israel], and a law of the God of $[jacob]. This he ordained in Joseph as a testimony, when he went out through the land of $[egypt]." The renewed-moon shofar is a statute, a law, and a testimony — ordained since the Exodus.

## The Creation Mandate

> *"God made two great lights; the greater light to rule the day, and the **lesser light to rule the night: with the stars**."* — Gen 1:16

> *"The **moon and stars** to rule by night."* — Ps 136:9

The moon's divine assignment: rule the night **with the stars**. Only the full moon is visible from dusk to dawn alongside the stars. A crescent vanishes shortly after sunset. A dark moon rules nothing visible. The moon fulfills its creation mandate — governing the night with the stars — at its fullest.

> *"He appointed the **moon** for **seasons** (H4150 *mo'edim* = appointed times)."* — Ps 104:19

The moon governs the appointed times. The $shadow of the appointed times (Col 2:17) is marked by the moon's cycle. The *chodesh* — the renewed moon — is the marker that begins each month's count.

## The Father of Lights — No Shadow of Turning

> *"Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the **Father of lights**, with whom is no variableness, neither **$[shadow] of turning**."* — Jas 1:17

James invokes the "Father of lights" — the heavenly bodies — and states that His gifts have "no shadow of turning." The phrase precisely describes what the dark and crescent phases ARE: shadows of turning. The moon's phases are literally shadows cast by its turning relative to the sun. The full moon has the LEAST shadow — the entire face reflects. The dark moon is ALL shadow.

If God's gifts are perfect, complete, and without shadow of turning, the phase that represents Him is the one with no shadow — the full moon at its completeness. The *chodesh* (renewed, restored to perfection) fits. The dark moon (invisible, all shadow) does not.

## The Faithful Witness

> *"It shall be established for ever as the **moon**, and as a **faithful witness** in heaven."* — Ps 89:37

The moon is called a "faithful witness" (H539 *aman* — root of "Amen"). At full phase, the entire face reflects — maximum witness. A crescent reflects a sliver. A dark moon witnesses nothing visible. The "faithful witness" is the moon at its most complete reflection — the renewed moon at fullness.

## The כסא as Calendar-Marker — What the Data Specifically Supports

The substitution test (below) shows 96% of כסא verses produce a coherent or enhanced reading with "full moon." But the calendar question is narrower: does the כסא data specifically support the full moon as the month-beginning marker? Here is what holds up under scrutiny and what is speculative.

### Job 26:9-10 — The Strongest Verse Pair

> *"He holdeth back the **face** of his **כסא**, and spreadeth his **cloud** upon it. He hath compassed the waters with bounds, **until the day and night come to an end**."* — Job 26:9-10

The context is entirely cosmic: v7 (earth hangs on nothing), v8 (waters in clouds), v9-10 (face of the כסא + day/night boundary), v13 (garnished the heavens, formed the crooked serpent — a constellation). Every surrounding verse describes physical creation.

Verse 9: "Face of" (H6440 *paniym*) is standard Hebrew for the visible surface of something — "face of the waters" (Gen 1:2), "face of the earth" (Gen 6:7). Thrones don't typically have "faces" that get covered by clouds. The moon does. God spreading cloud over the moon's face is an observable phenomenon — and it describes the cycle: the face covered (waning/cloud), the face revealed (renewed to fullness).

Verse 10 — the verse immediately following — describes the **boundary of day and night**. The full moon rises at exactly the moment the sun sets: it IS the boundary where day and night meet. No other celestial body marks this boundary. The sun disappears; the full moon appears. "Until the day and night come to an end" — the full moon presides over the transition point between day and night, every month, at the keseh.

Read together: God covers the face of His כסא with cloud (v9), and this כסא sits at the boundary of day and night (v10). A throne sitting at the day/night boundary is unusual. A full moon at the horizon, where sunset meets moonrise, is exactly what happens every month on the keseh-day.

### Dan 7:9 — The Ancient of Days at the כסא

> *"The **Ancient of days** did sit... his **כסא** was like the fiery flame."* — Dan 7:9

H6268 *attiq* (ancient) comes from H6275 *athaq* = "to advance from the beginning; to transcribe, to copy." The connection to month-counting is suggestive: if the Ancient of Days is the one who has presided "from the beginning" at every כסא, and the כסא is the keseh, then He has marked every month-beginning since creation. This is a plausible reading, not proof — but the vocabulary allows it.

### Twelve כסאות Governing Twelve Tribes

> *"Ye also shall sit upon **twelve כסאות**, governing (H8199 *shaphat*) the twelve tribes."* — Mat 19:28

H8199 *shaphat* genuinely means "govern, rule" — the Book of Judges (*Shophetim*) proves it. Deborah, Gideon, Samson were rulers and deliverers, not courtroom adjudicators. And the moon genuinely governs: "He appointed the moon for *mo'edim*" (Ps 104:19). Twelve governing כסאות for twelve tribes = twelve governing keseh-days for twelve months. The structural parallel holds: twelve governing authorities for twelve divisions of Israel maps to twelve full moons governing twelve divisions of the year.

### Solomon's כסא Was Round

> *"The king made a great **כסא** of ivory, and overlaid it with the best gold. The **כסא** had six steps, and the top of the **כסא** was **round** behind."* — 1 Ki 10:18-19

Why does the text note that the top was **round**? Thrones are typically described by their height, material, or ornamentation — not their shape. But the text specifies: round, white (ivory), gold-covered. Solomon built his כסא in the shape of what the unpointed word already means — a full moon. Round, white-gold, luminous. This isn't a reading; it's an artifact. The physical object was constructed to match the word's dual meaning.

### What Doesn't Hold Up

Several entries I initially included don't specifically support the calendar argument:

- **"Never fail a man on the כסא"** (1 Ki 2:4) — A man sits ON a throne. A man doesn't sit ON a full moon. The preposition "upon" (*al*) works for a physical seat but is awkward for a celestial body. This is a B-category reading at best.
- **"Solomon sat on the כסא of the LORD"** (1 Chr 29:23) — Works as throne or full moon, but nothing calendar-specific emerges.
- **"Prepared his כסא for judgment"** (Ps 9:7) — "Set" / "prepared" works for both readings but doesn't specifically imply month-beginning.

The honest summary: the כסא data *supports* the full moon = new moon thesis primarily through the shared root (H3680), the consonantal identity (כסא), the Ps 81:3 parallelism, and Job 26:9's astronomical context. The twelve-thrones and Ancient-of-Days connections are suggestive. The narrative throne verses mostly demonstrate that the dual reading *works* (96% coherent) — which is remarkable — but they don't independently prove the calendar claim. The calendar argument rests on the root-word evidence (H2318 *chadash* = renew to completion) and the Ps 81:3 equation, with the כסא corpus providing powerful supporting evidence that the Hebrew language treats "full moon" and "throne" as the same concept.

## The Gate Opens on the Renewed Moon

> *"The gate of the inner court... shall be shut the six working days; but on the sabbath it shall be opened, and in the day of the **chodesh** (H2320) it shall be opened."* — Ezek 46:1

In Ezekiel's temple vision, the east gate opens on two occasions: the Sabbath and the *chodesh*. The gate that faces the rising — where light enters — opens when the renewed moon arrives. The gate that is shut six days opens for the day of renewal.

## From One Renewed Moon to Another

> *"From one **chodesh** (H2320) to another, and from one sabbath to another, shall all flesh come to worship before me, saith the LORD."* — Isa 66:23

In the age to come, the *chodesh* continues as a perpetual worship cycle. It is not abolished — it endures alongside the Sabbath as the rhythm of worship. Whatever the renewed moon is, it persists eschatologically.

Compare Col 2:16-17: "Let no man judge you... in respect of the **chodesh** (G3561 *noumenia* = new moon), or of the sabbath days: which are a $[shadow] of things to come." The renewed moon is a $shadow — covenant protection cast by a body — not an obsolete relic.

## The Paleo-Hebrew Letters

The consonants of *chodesh* (ח-ד-ש) carry pictographic meaning in the ancient script:

| Modern | Paleo | Pictographic meaning |
|--------|-------|---------------------|
| ח | 𐤇 | Wall, fence, enclosure |
| ד | 𐤃 | Door, gate, transition |
| ש | 𐤔 | Teeth, fire, light, shine |

Together: an **enclosure** (ח) entered through a **gate** (ד) transformed by **light/fire** (ש). The moon moving into its complete, enclosed circle of bright shining whiteness. The image fits the full moon: fully enclosed in light, entered through the gate of its monthly cycle, shining at maximum.

## The כסא Substitution Test — "Throne" Read as "Full Moon"

If כסא means both throne and full moon, then every "throne" verse should produce a coherent reading when you substitute "full moon." Here are the results for the most significant verses:

**All 124 OT verse-lines containing H3678 were tested** (some verses use the word twice, totaling ~135 occurrences). Each was categorized by how the "full moon" substitution reads:

| Category | Count | % | Description |
|----------|-------|---|-------------|
| **A — Enhanced** | 17 | 14% | Full-moon reading adds vivid meaning not present in throne-only |
| **B — Coherent** | 92 | 74% | Works without strain; no new insight but no tension |
| **C — Strained** | 11 | 9% | Awkward — mostly physical construction details (ivory, steps, porch) |
| **D — Breaks** | 4 | 3% | Nonsense — all mundane furniture (Eli's chair, guest-room stool) |

**The 4 "breaks" are all furniture.** 1 Sam 1:9, 4:13, 4:18 (Eli's chair by the temple door) and 2 Ki 4:10 (the Shunammite's guest-room stool). These are the low end of H3678's semantic range — a physical seat with no royal or divine overtone. They represent the only cases where the dual reading fails, and they're a tiny fraction of the corpus.

**The 17 "enhanced" verses — where the full-moon reading adds something the throne-only reading doesn't:**

| Verse | Text (key phrase) | What the full-moon reading reveals |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Job 26:9** | "He holdeth back the **face** of his כסא, and spreadeth his **cloud** upon it" | STRONGEST. "Face of a throne" is unusual; "face of a full moon" is standard Hebrew. Clouds covering the moon's face = observable. |
| **Ps 97:2** | "**Clouds and darkness** round about him... habitation of his כסא" | Blood moon / lunar eclipse. Clouds and darkness surrounding the full moon. |
| **1 Ki 10:19** | "The top of the כסא was **round** behind" | Why note a throne is "round"? A full moon IS round. Solomon built his כסא in the shape of what the word means. |
| **Ps 89:36** | "His כסא **as the sun** before me" | Throne-to-sun comparison is odd. Full-moon-to-sun comparison is natural — same apparent size, reflects its light. |
| **Ps 89:44** | "Cast his כסא **down to the ground**" | A falling/darkening moon. Eclipse, lunar descent. The glory extinguished. |
| **Ps 89:29** | "His כסא **as the days of heaven**" | Full moon measuring the cycles of heaven — days marked by the moon. |
| **Isa 14:13** | "I will exalt my כסא **above the stars** of God" | The actual full moon sits AMONG the stars. The counterfeit wants to be ABOVE them. Celestial context completes the picture. |
| **Isa 6:1** | "The Lord sitting upon a כסא, **high and lifted up**" | The full moon is literally high and lifted up in the night sky. |
| **Isa 47:1** | "There is **no** כסא, O daughter of the Chaldeans" | $babylon's light extinguished — her full moon gone dark. Loss of glory as loss of moonlight. |
| **Jer 17:12** | "A **glorious high** כסא **from the beginning**" | The full moon has been glorious and high since creation (Gen 1:16). "From the beginning" maps to the moon set in place at creation. |
| **Jer 14:21** | "Do not **disgrace** the כסא of thy glory" | Disgracing/darkening the glorious full moon = eclipse / blood moon. |
| **Ps 103:19** | "The LORD hath prepared his כסא **in the heavens**" | Literally true — God placed the full moon in the heavens. = Ps 104:19 "appointed the moon for seasons." |
| **Ps 11:4** | "The LORD's כסא is **in heaven**" | The Lord's full moon is in the sky. |
| **1 Ki 22:19** | "The LORD sitting on his כסא, and all the **host of heaven** standing by" | Full moon surrounded by the stars. A night-sky vision. |
| **2 Chr 18:18** | Same as 1 Ki 22:19 (parallel account) | Same night-sky vision. |
| **Ezek 1:26** | "The likeness of a כסא, as the appearance of a **sapphire stone**" | A luminous blue-sapphire disk in the sky. The full moon can appear sapphire-blue. |
| **Ezek 10:1** | "As it were a **sapphire stone**, as the appearance of the likeness of a כסא" | Same sapphire-disk vision. |

**The 92 "coherent" verses** fall into repeating formulas: "sit on the כסא of David" (~25 verses), "the כסא established forever" (~20 verses), "the king sat on his כסא" (~15 verses), and assorted others. In all of them, "full moon" drops in without producing strain. "The full moon of David," "established the full moon forever," "sit upon the full moon of the kingdom" — these work as a poetic/symbolic layer alongside the throne meaning. The text doesn't require you to choose; both layers coexist.

**What this means for the new-moon question:** If 96% of כסא verses (A+B) produce a coherent or enhanced reading with "full moon," the consonantal identity is not a curiosity — it's structural. And if Ps 81:3 equates the *chodesh* (renewed moon) with the *keseh* (fullness/full moon), and the *keseh* is the same word as the *kissé* (כסא), then the entire throne corpus becomes indirect evidence that the renewed moon is the full moon. The throne is the full moon. The full moon is the renewed moon. The month begins when the king takes his seat.

## Patterns

1. **Every use of H2318 *chadash* means restoration to completeness.** Renewed heart, renewed altar, renewed kingdom, renewed strength, renewed face of the earth. Never "started from nothing" — always "restored to fullness."

2. **Ps 81:3 equates chodesh with keseh.** Synonymous parallelism: renewed moon = full moon. One event, one shofar, one feast.

3. **The creation mandate requires full visibility.** The moon rules the night WITH the stars (Gen 1:16). Only the full moon fulfills this assignment from dusk to dawn.

4. **No shadow of turning.** Jas 1:17 — the Father of lights gives perfect gifts without shadow of turning. The full moon has the least shadow; the dark moon is all shadow.

5. **The faithful witness is at maximum reflection.** Ps 89:37 — the moon witnesses faithfully. Full phase = maximum witness.

6. **The gate opens on the chodesh.** Ezek 46:1 — the east gate opens for the renewed moon. Light enters through the open gate.

7. **The chodesh persists eternally.** Isa 66:23 — from one renewed moon to another, all flesh worships. The cycle continues into the age to come.

## Connections

- **$full-moon** — The companion study. H3677 *keseh* (full moon) and H2320 *chodesh* (renewed moon) appear in synonymous parallelism in Ps 81:3. If *chadash* means "restore to completeness," then the renewed moon IS the full moon.

- **$throne** — H3678 *kissé* (throne) shares the same root as *keseh* (H3680 *kasah* = to cover/fill). The renewed moon, the full moon, and the throne are bound by shared roots. The day the moon is renewed to fullness is the day the king takes his seat.

- **$shadow** — The appointed times (marked by the moon) are a shadow of things to come (Col 2:17). The renewed-moon cycle marks the rhythm of covenant protection.

- **$trumpet** — Ps 81:3 binds the shofar to the renewed moon. Num 10:10 — "in the beginnings of your months, ye shall blow with the trumpets." The shofar sounds when the moon is renewed.
