**A counterfeit congregation claiming [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) standing while gathered against the covenant's Lord. Korah's company; the brood of vipers and children of the Devil, the father of lies; the assembly surrounding the Messiah; and those who say they are Jews but are not display the pattern.**

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **assembly** begins: “A company of persons collected together in one place, and usually for some common purpose, esp. for deliberation and legislation, for worship, or for social entertainment.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

A counterfeit congregation that claims covenant standing while gathering against the covenant's Lord, exemplified by Korah's rebellion, the crowd at the crucifixion, and the opponents in Revelation.

> *“For dogs have compassed me: the assembly of the wicked have inclosed me: they pierced my hands and my feet.”* — Psalms 22:16

> *“I the LORD have said, I will surely do it unto all this evil congregation, that are gathered together against me: in this wilderness they shall be consumed, and there they shall die.”* — Numbers 14:35

> *“And they gathered themselves together against Moses and against Aaron, and said unto them, Ye take too much upon you, seeing all the congregation are holy, every one of them, and the LORD is among them: wherefore then lift ye up yourselves above the congregation of the LORD?”* — Numbers 16:3

> *“But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees come to his baptism, he said unto them, O generation of vipers, who hath warned you to flee from the wrath to come?”* — Matthew 3:7

> *“O generation of vipers, how can ye, being evil, speak good things? for out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh.”* — Matthew 12:34

> *“Ye are of your father the devil, and the lusts of your father ye will do. He was a murderer from the beginning, and abode not in the truth, because there is no truth in him. When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it.”* — John 8:44

> *“For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod, and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles, and the people of Israel, were gathered together,”* — Acts 4:27

> *“I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty,(but thou art rich) and I know the blasphemy of them which say they are Jews, and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan.”* — Revelation 2:9

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The entry may compress historically distinct groups and texts into one canonical type, and the printed excerpts alone do not establish every lexical nuance of “edah” or the inside/outside-camp distinction. Nevertheless, Numbers explicitly depicts an allegedly holy congregation gathered against God’s appointed leaders, Psalms depicts wicked assemblies, Acts identifies those gathered against Jesus, and Revelation names false covenant claimants the synagogue of Satan.

- One could argue MATCH: the consensus already recognizes 'assembly of the wicked'/'synagogue of Satan' as false covenant claimants opposing God, and the OT verses merely illustrate the same idea rather than materially extending it; Response A even notes the synagoge/assembly linguistic linkage. On that view the Numbers typology and inside/outside reading are elaboration, not boundary change. However, treating the wilderness congregation and Korah's company as instances of the term itself — and splitting Psalm 22:16 into insider/outsider persecutors — goes beyond what any consensus response asserts, so REFINED is the better fit.

- None.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
