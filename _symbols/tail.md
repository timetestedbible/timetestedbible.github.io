# Tail

## Summary

**The prophet that teacheth lies — Isaiah's own decode, with head and tail already defined by the [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/): obedience makes Israel “the head, and not the tail,” and the curse inverts it. The dragon fells a third of the [stars](/research/symbols/stars/) with its tail — the led destroyed by the misleading — and the trumpet armies' serpent-tails “had heads”: the liar enthroned. Moses is told to seize the serpent exactly there.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Fall of Babylon](/books/symbolic-language/the-fall-of-babylon/), [research-tail.md](/books/symbolic-language/research/research-tail.md).

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **tail** begins: “(Law) Defn: Limitation; abridgment.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The book makes Isaiah’s lying prophet the controlling identification: the tail is deceptive leadership that misleads and destroys followers. It then reads the covenant head-tail contrast, the dragon’s tail, the serpent-headed tails, and Moses’ seizure of the serpent’s tail through that framework.

> *“The ancient and honourable, he is the head; and the prophet that teacheth lies, he is the tail.”* — Isaiah 9:15

> *“For the leaders of this people cause them to err; and they that are led of them are destroyed.”* — Isaiah 9:16

> *“And the LORD shall make thee the head, and not the tail; and thou shalt be above only, and thou shalt not be beneath; if that thou hearken unto the commandments of the LORD thy God, which I command thee this day, to observe and to do them:”* — Deuteronomy 28:13

> *“He shall lend to thee, and thou shalt not lend to him: he shall be the head, and thou shalt be the tail.”* — Deuteronomy 28:44

> *“And his tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven, and did cast them to the earth: and the dragon stood before the woman which was ready to be delivered, for to devour her child as soon as it was born.”* — Revelation 12:4

> *“For their power is in their mouth, and in their tails: for their tails were like unto serpents, and had heads, and with them they do hurt.”* — Revelation 9:19

> *“And the LORD said unto Moses, Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it, and it became a rod in his hand:”* — Exodus 4:4

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **tail** begins:

> “Tail, n. Etym: [F. taille a cutting. See Entail, Tally.] (Law) Defn: Limitation; abridgment. Burrill. Estate in tail, a limited, abridged, or reduced fee; an estate limited to certain heirs, and from which the other heirs are precluded; -- called also estate tail. Blackstone.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Isaiah 9:15-16 strongly supports the lying-prophet identification, and Deuteronomy supports the head-tail status contrast, but Revelation 12:4, Revelation 9:19, and Exodus 4:4 do not themselves identify the tail with a liar. Applying Isaiah’s decode to all those texts requires an interpretive bridge not established by the printed citations alone.

- One could argue REFINED because the book subordinates the Deut 28 status meaning under the false-prophecy meaning (making one unified symbol rather than two context-dependent ones) and reclassifies Exodus 4:4 from literal narrative to symbolic instance, slightly broadening the symbol's coverage against the consensus minority caution that not all tail texts are symbolic.

- Responses that treat the Deuteronomy status idiom as the more widespread or primary meaning may view the book’s elevation of the Isaiah decode (and consequent re-reading of the other passages) as over-narrowing.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
