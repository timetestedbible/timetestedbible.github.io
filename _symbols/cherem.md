# Cherem

**God's total enclosure — irrevocable seizure by divine claim from which nothing escapes, nothing is redeemed, and nothing remains neutral.**

> *"Notwithstanding no **devoted thing**, that a man shall devote unto the LORD of all that he hath, both of man and beast, and of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed: every **devoted thing** is most holy unto the LORD."* — Lev 27:28

---

## The Key Insight

The English Bible scatters *cherem* across a half-dozen translations — "devoted thing," "accursed," "utter destruction," "ban," "net" — and in doing so hides the single mechanism that ties them all together. The Hebrew root H2763 *charam* means **to shut in, to enclose completely**. From this root come two applications that appear unrelated in English but are identical in Hebrew:

1. A **net** that physically encloses fish so none escape.
2. A **ban** that judicially encloses persons, cities, or goods into God's exclusive jurisdiction so none can be redeemed.

These are not homonyms. They are the same word because they are the same action — *total enclosure*. The net shuts fish inside mesh. The ban shuts the devoted thing inside God's grip. What the cherem catches, nothing releases.

This is why Leviticus defines the cherem-ban as the one offering that **cannot be bought back**:

> *"None **devoted**, which shall be **devoted** of men, shall be redeemed; but shall surely be put to death."* — Lev 27:29

Every other consecration in Leviticus can be reversed. Firstborn animals can be exchanged. Dedicated fields can be redeemed at a price. Tithes can be bought back with a twenty-percent surcharge. But not cherem. The cherem belongs to God with a finality that admits no negotiation, no substitution, no ransom.

The opposite of cherem is not freedom — it is **redemption**. The entire concept is defined against the possibility of being bought back (H1350 *gaal*, H6299 *padah*). Every time Scripture says something *can* be redeemed, it is distinguishing that thing from cherem. And every time it says "shall not be redeemed," it is announcing: *this has entered cherem — God's total enclosure*.

**Symbolizes:** Irrevocable seizure by divine claim — total enclosure from which nothing can be redeemed or escape

**Opposite:** Redemption (H1350 *gaal* / H6299 *padah*) — cherem is defined as that which *cannot* be redeemed (Lev 27:28–29)

**Defining verses:** Lev 27:28–29, Josh 6:17–18, Josh 7:1–13, Mal 4:6, Zech 14:11

**The surprise:** The same Hebrew word (H2764) means both "net" and "ban." This is not a coincidence of language — it is a single concept: enclosure. The $net that catches $fish and the ban that claims cities operate by the same mechanism. And the empire that wields the net-cherem on nations (Hab 1:15) eventually becomes the target of God's ban-cherem (Jer 50:21). The hunter's net becomes the hunter's doom.

**Connected:** $sword, $fire, $net, $babylon, $fall-of-babylon, $flying-scroll, $sudden-destruction

## Cherem Across Scripture

### Not Destruction — Transfer of Ownership

Modern readers encounter cherem mostly as "utterly destroy" in the conquest narratives and assume it means annihilation. But the law foundation in Leviticus reveals something more precise. Cherem is not primarily about *destroying* — it is about **transferring ownership irrevocably to God**.

> *"Notwithstanding no **devoted thing** (H2764), that a man shall devote unto the LORD of all that he hath, both of man and beast, and of the field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed: every **devoted thing** is most holy unto the LORD."* — Lev 27:28

"Most holy unto the LORD." This is consecration language — the same designation given to the holy of holies, the showbread, and the sin offering. Cherem does not simply destroy; it seizes for God. What happens *after* the seizure varies — persons are put to death (Lev 27:29), property passes to the priesthood (Num 18:14, Ezek 44:29) — but the defining act is the enclosure itself. Once something enters cherem, it belongs exclusively and permanently to God's jurisdiction.

This reframes the conquest narratives. When Jericho is declared cherem (Josh 6:17), the city has not merely been targeted for military defeat. It has been *claimed* — every person, every animal, every object transferred to God's exclusive domain. The silver and gold go into the treasury of the LORD's house (Josh 6:19). The rest is consumed. Nothing remains in human hands.

### The Contagion — Touching What God Has Claimed

The most unusual property of cherem is that it **transfers on contact**. No other category in Scripture works this way with such finality:

> *"And ye, in any wise keep yourselves from the **accursed thing** (H2764), lest ye make yourselves **accursed** (H2764), when ye take of the **accursed thing** (H2764), and make the camp of Israel a **curse** (H2764), and trouble it."* — Josh 6:18

Notice the triple escalation: take the cherem → *become* cherem → make the whole camp cherem. The ban is contagious. It spreads from object to person to community. This is not metaphorical — Joshua 7 demonstrates the mechanism operating in real time.

> *"Israel hath sinned, and they have also transgressed my covenant which I commanded them: for they have even taken of the **accursed thing** (H2764), and have also stolen, and dissembled also, and they have put it even among their own stuff."* — Josh 7:11

One man — Achan — took a garment, silver, and gold from Jericho's cherem. He hid them in his tent. And the result was corporate:

> *"Therefore the children of Israel could not stand before their enemies, but turned their backs before their enemies, because they were **accursed** (H2764): neither will I be with you any more, except ye destroy the **accursed** (H2764) from among you."* — Josh 7:12

Israel *became* what Achan touched. The entire nation absorbed the cherem-status of the devoted thing. The contagion was not symbolic — thirty-six men died at Ai because the cherem had spread to the camp. God's total enclosure does not recognize the boundaries between "my tent" and "your tent." What is inside the camp is the camp's.

This pattern holds retrospectively: "Did not Achan the son of Zerah commit a trespass in the accursed thing, and wrath fell on *all the congregation* of Israel? and that man perished not alone in his iniquity" (Josh 22:20).

The same principle appears in the law's general instruction:

> *"Neither shalt thou bring an abomination into thine house, lest thou be a **cursed thing** (H2764) like it: but thou shalt utterly detest it, and thou shalt utterly abhor it; for it is a **cursed thing** (H2764)."* — Deut 7:26

Bringing cherem into your house makes *you* cherem. The devoted thing radiates its status outward. This is not magical thinking — it is the logic of total enclosure. If God has claimed something, taking it means you are now holding what belongs to God. You are inside His net.

### The Substitution — Releasing What God Has Claimed

If touching cherem absorbs the ban, *releasing* cherem produces the inverse: you absorb what you freed.

> *"And he said unto him, Thus saith the LORD, Because thou hast let go out of thy hand a man whom I appointed to **utter destruction** (H2764), therefore thy life shall go for his life, and thy people for his people."* — 1 Ki 20:42

Ahab defeated Ben-hadad of Syria in battle — a king God had devoted to cherem. But Ahab made a treaty with him and let him go. The prophetic verdict is substitutionary: *your life for his life*. Releasing God's cherem does not cancel it — it redirects it. The ban must land somewhere.

Saul's failure follows the same logic. Commanded to execute cherem on Amalek — "utterly destroy all that they have, and spare them not" (1 Sam 15:3) — Saul instead spared King Agag and the best livestock:

> *"But Saul and the people spared Agag, and the best of the sheep, and of the oxen, and of the fatlings, and the lambs, and all that was good, and would not **utterly destroy** (H2763) them."* — 1 Sam 15:9

Samuel's verdict: "Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king" (1 Sam 15:23). Partial cherem is not cherem. It is disobedience. And the one who withholds God's claimed property absorbs the consequences — Saul lost the kingdom.

### The Net and the Ban — One Word, One Mechanism

The prophet Habakkuk watches $babylon sweep through nations and describes its power using the very word that means both net and ban:

> *"They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in their **net** (H2764 *cherem*), and gather them in their drag: therefore they rejoice and are glad. Therefore they sacrifice unto their **net** (H2764), and burn incense unto their **drag**: because by them their portion is fat, and their meat plenteous."* — Hab 1:15–16

Babylon's imperial dragnet catches nations the way a fisherman catches fish — indiscriminately, totally. And then Babylon does something extraordinary: it **worships its own net**. "They sacrifice unto their cherem." The empire deifies its own power to enclose. The mechanism of capture becomes the object of worship.

This is the clearest illustration of how H2764 unifies net and ban: the physical enclosure of nations *is* the judicial claim on them. Babylon's net *is* Babylon's cherem. When an empire gathers peoples under its dominion, it is performing its own version of the devoted ban — seizing everything, releasing nothing.

But the prophet's question hangs in the air: "Shall they therefore empty their net (H2764), and not spare continually to slay the nations?" (Hab 1:17). Will this go on forever?

The answer comes from Jeremiah: God appointed $babylon to execute cherem on the nations (Jer 25:9 — "I will utterly destroy them"). But then:

> *"Go up against the land of Merathaim, even against it, and against the inhabitants of Pekod: waste and **utterly destroy** (H2763) after them, saith the LORD, and do according to all that I have commanded thee."* — Jer 50:21

The executor of cherem *becomes* cherem. The empire that wielded God's net on others is now caught in God's net itself. Hunter becomes hunted. The same word — the same mechanism — reverses direction. This is the pattern that links cherem to the $fall-of-babylon: the system that encloses nations is finally enclosed by God.

### The Harlot's Heart Is Nets

Wisdom literature makes the connection between cherem, the $net, and the harlot explicit:

> *"And I find more bitter than death the woman, whose heart is snares and **nets** (H2764 *cherem*), and her hands as bands: whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her; but the sinner shall be taken by her."* — Eccl 7:26

The harlot's heart *is* cherem — nets that enclose, from which the sinner cannot escape. This places the harlot-system and the cherem-system in the same symbolic space. Consider: Revelation's Babylon is a harlot (Rev 17:5) whose merchants traffic in "bodies and souls of men" (Rev 18:13). She is the empire whose heart is cherem-nets — catching, enclosing, claiming what belongs to God.

And like every wielder of cherem who is not God, she will absorb what she has claimed.

### The Scroll, the Sword, and the Cherem

Isaiah 34 reveals a chain of judgment mechanisms that converge on cherem:

> *"And all the host of heaven shall be dissolved, and the heavens shall be rolled together as a **scroll** (H5612): and all their host shall fall down… For my **$[sword]** shall be bathed in heaven: it shall come down upon Idumea, and upon the people of my **curse** (H2764 *cherem*), to judgment."* — Isa 34:4–5

The heavens roll like a $flying-scroll — the covenant-judgment delivery mechanism carrying the written decree of curses. The $sword descends from heaven — bathed, saturated, dripping with judgment. And it lands upon "the people of my cherem." The three converge: scroll delivers, sword executes, cherem designates.

The same chain appears in the final paragraph of the Old Testament. Malachi 4:4 invokes "Horeb" (H2722 — from the same root as $sword, H2717 *charab*, desolation). Malachi 4:6 threatens the earth with "cherem" (H2764). Between them: $fire that burns like an oven (Mal 4:1), Elijah's mission to turn hearts (Mal 4:5–6). The desolation-mountain, the consuming $fire, and the total ban — all gathered into the Old Testament's closing words.

### The Bookends — From Threatened Ban to Lifted Ban

The Old Testament's last word is a warning:

> *"Lest I come and smite the earth with a **curse** (H2764 *cherem*)."* — Mal 4:6

Elijah must come first. Hearts of fathers must turn to children, children to fathers. If not — cherem. The total enclosure. The irrevocable ban on the earth itself.

But Zechariah's vision of the age to come pronounces the opposite:

> *"And men shall dwell in it, and there shall be **no more utter destruction** (H2764 *cherem*); but Jerusalem shall be safely inhabited."* — Zech 14:11

No more cherem. When YHWH is king over all the earth (Zech 14:9), the ban lifts. The net opens. What was irrevocable is finally resolved — not by human redemption (Lev 27:28 says that is impossible) but by the sovereign act of the One who imposed the ban in the first place.

Between Malachi's threat and Zechariah's promise lies the entire scope of the Day of the LORD.

### The Net Serves Life — Restoration

Even the cherem-net receives a positive resolution:

> *"And it shall come to pass, that the fishers shall stand upon it from En-gedi even unto En-eglaim; they shall be a place to spread forth **nets** (H2764 *cherem*): their fish shall be according to their kinds, as the fish of the great sea, exceeding many."* — Ezek 47:10

In Ezekiel's vision of the restored temple, the river of life flows outward and transforms the Dead Sea. Fishermen spread cherem-nets — and the nets serve abundance, not judgment. The mechanism of enclosure, in the hands of the kingdom, gathers for life. The $net that caught nations for destruction now catches fish by the multitude for sustenance.

This is the same transformation Jesus invokes when He calls fishermen to leave their nets and become "fishers of men" (Mat 4:19). The kingdom of heaven is like a great dragnet cast into the $sea, gathering of every kind (Mat 13:47). The net still encloses indiscriminately — but in the hands of the King, the catch is sorted on the shore, and what is good is kept.

### The New Testament Anathema

The Greek equivalent of cherem is G331 *anathema* — the Septuagint's standard translation of H2764. Paul uses it in ways that reveal the ban's continued operation:

> *"If any man love not the Lord Jesus Christ, let him be Anathema Maranatha."* — 1 Cor 16:22

*Anathema Maranatha* — "devoted to the ban; the Lord comes." The cherem awaits execution at the parousia. The ban has been declared; the Lord's coming will enforce it. This echoes the $sudden-destruction pattern: the judgment is certain but its execution is delayed until the appointed moment (compare 1 Th 5:3).

> *"But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed (G331 *anathema*)."* — Gal 1:8

Paul repeats it in the next verse for emphasis. The trigger is the same as Exodus 22:20 — "He that sacrificeth unto any god, save unto the LORD only, he shall be utterly destroyed (H2763)." Idolatry devoted to cherem in the Torah; a false gospel devoted to anathema in the epistle. The mechanism has not changed. A competing object of worship triggers the ban.

And then there is Paul's extraordinary wish:

> *"For I could wish that myself were accursed (G331 *anathema*) from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh."* — Rom 9:3

Paul wishes himself to *become* cherem — devoted to the ban, irrevocably enclosed in God's judgment — if it would redeem his people. This echoes the substitution principle: one life absorbing the ban for many. And it points toward the One who actually accomplished it — who was "made a curse for us" (Gal 3:13), who entered the total enclosure of God's judgment on the cross and broke it from within by resurrection.

> *"Wherefore I give you to understand, that no man speaking by the Spirit of God calleth Jesus accursed (G331 *anathema*)."* — 1 Cor 12:3

No one by the Spirit can declare Jesus cherem — because the resurrection has reversed the ban. He entered God's total enclosure and emerged. The net did not hold Him. The devoted thing was redeemed — the one thing Leviticus 27:28 said could not happen.

## Patterns

1. **Net = Ban = Enclosure.** H2764 means both "net" and "devoted ban." The root H2763 *charam* = to shut in. Physical enclosure (net) and judicial enclosure (ban) are the same mechanism — total seizure from which nothing escapes.

2. **Contagion.** Touching cherem makes you cherem. Josh 6:18 → 7:1–13: object → person → camp. Deut 7:26: house absorbs the status of what is brought inside. No other consecration category in Scripture works this way.

3. **Substitution.** Releasing cherem redirects the ban onto the one who freed it. 1 Ki 20:42: "thy life for his life." 1 Sam 15: Saul withholds cherem, loses the kingdom. The ban must land.

4. **Irreversibility.** Lev 27:28–29 explicitly distinguishes cherem from every redeemable offering. It defines cherem *by its inability to be bought back*. This is what makes the resurrection's reversal of the anathema so significant.

5. **Executor becomes target.** Babylon executes God's cherem on nations (Jer 25:9) → Babylon itself receives cherem (Jer 50:21). The same word, the same mechanism, reversed. The wielder of the net is caught in it.

6. **The bookends.** Malachi 4:6 threatens earth with cherem (last word of the OT). Zechariah 14:11 promises "no more cherem." Between them: the entire Day of the LORD.

7. **Positive resolution.** Ezek 47:10 — cherem-nets spread at the river of life to catch abundant fish. The enclosure mechanism, under God's restored rule, serves life rather than judgment.

## Connections

- **$net** — The same word (H2764) in its physical application. The net that encloses fish and the ban that encloses cities are one concept. Hab 1:15–16 makes the convergence explicit: Babylon's net *is* cherem, and the empire worships its own power to capture.

- **$sword** — The desolation-maker that executes what cherem designates. Isa 34:5: the $sword descends upon "the people of my cherem." The sword-root (H2717 *charab* = parch/desolate) and the cherem-root (H2763 = shut in) converge in the conquest narratives where both appear together: "they utterly destroyed (H2763) all… with the edge of the sword (H2719)" (Josh 6:21, 10:28, 11:11).

- **$fire** — God's holy standard in active contact — and cherem's companion in execution. Deut 13:16: an idolatrous city is devoted to cherem and then burned with $fire. The fire consumes what the ban has enclosed. In Isa 34, the cherem-people's land becomes "burning pitch" and "brimstone" — the ban triggers the fire.

- **$babylon** — Man's substitute kingdom, which wields its own version of cherem. Babylon's imperial dragnet (Hab 1:15) catches nations in cherem-nets and worships its own capturing power. But the executor-becomes-target pattern ensures Babylon itself is enclosed by God's cherem (Jer 50:21). The system that devours nations is devoured.

- **$fall-of-babylon** — The moment when the hunter-becomes-hunted pattern consummates. Babylon executed cherem on the nations; now Babylon *is* cherem. The harlot whose heart was nets and cherem (Eccl 7:26) is caught in her own enclosure. "Reward her even as she rewarded you, and double unto her double" (Rev 18:6).

- **$flying-scroll** — The covenant-judgment delivery mechanism. In Isa 34:4–5, the heavens roll as a scroll and the $sword descends on cherem-people. In Zech 5:1–4, the flying scroll carries the covenant curse (H423 *alah*) that enters houses and consumes them — the same total, inescapable operation as cherem. The scroll delivers what the ban designates.

- **$sudden-destruction** — Covenant judgment that falls without warning on those in false security. The cherem-ban, once pronounced, executes at God's appointed time — and those under it perceive no danger until the net closes. "When they shall say, Peace and safety; then sudden destruction cometh upon them" (1 Th 5:3). Anathema Maranatha: the cherem waits for the Lord's coming.
