# Evening

**The hour of sacrifice — the appointed time when atonement is made and uncleanness is removed.**

> *"The one $[lamb] thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other $[lamb] thou shalt offer at **even**."* — Exod 29:39

---

## The Key Insight

Dozens of times in Leviticus and Numbers, the same phrase appears: "unclean until the even." A person touches a carcass, contracts a skin condition, or comes into contact with death — and the verdict is the same. Wash with $water. Wait. You will be clean *at evening*.

Most readers assume this means sunset — the turning of the clock, a natural boundary. But Scripture never says darkness cleanses. On the contrary, $darkness in Scripture represents concealment and hidden disobedience — the opposite of what restoration requires.

What happens at evening?

A lamb dies.

> *"Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar; two lambs of the first year day by day continually. The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other lamb thou shalt offer at **even**."* — Exod 29:38–39

Every day in Israel, without exception, a lamb was slaughtered in the morning and another in the evening. The evening sacrifice — the *tamid* — was offered "between the two evenings" (H996 + H6153, *bein ha'arbayim*, Num 28:4), historically understood as roughly the ninth hour, around 3 p.m. This was not sunset. It was the hour of blood.

When Leviticus says "unclean until the even," it is not prescribing a waiting period measured by the sun. It is pointing to the sacrifice. The unclean person washes, waits, and is restored — not when darkness falls, but when the lamb's blood is offered. The evening *is* the sacrifice.

**Symbolizes:** The appointed time of atoning sacrifice — the hour when blood is shed and the unclean are made clean

**Opposite:** $morning — the beginning of the $day cycle, the hour of the first sacrifice and the start of work; evening is where the cycle resolves

**Defining verses:** Exod 29:39, Exod 29:41, Num 28:4, 1 Ki 18:36, Mat 27:46

**The surprise:** Jesus died at the ninth hour (Mat 27:46) — the exact time of the evening sacrifice. The phrase "unclean until the even" was always pointing forward to the hour when the true $lamb would die.

**Connected:** $darkness, $day, $bread, $wine, $jerusalem, $lamb

## Evening Across Scripture

### The Daily Rhythm — Morning and Evening

The *tamid*, the perpetual offering, structured every day in Israel around two lambs:

> *"The one $[lamb] thou shalt offer in the morning; and the other $[lamb] thou shalt offer at **even**: and with the one $[lamb] a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of an hin of beaten oil; and the fourth part of an hin of $[wine] for a drink offering."* — Exod 29:39–40

Notice what accompanies the evening $lamb: $bread (flour) and $wine. If $bread represents the covenant terms (the Word of God — Deut 8:3, Mat 4:4) and $wine represents covenant blood (Gen 49:11, Mat 26:28), then the evening sacrifice is a complete covenant picture: the lamb's life poured out, accompanied by the Word and the blood. Every single evening, Israel enacted a covenant renewal in miniature.

> *"The other $[lamb] thou shalt offer at **even**, and shalt do thereto according to the meat offering of the morning, and according to the drink offering thereof, for a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD."* — Exod 29:41

The morning sacrifice opened the $day — the time of exposure and accountability, when work was done in the light. The evening sacrifice *closed* it — resolving the day's defilement, covering what had been accumulated. The entire day was bookended by blood. No Israelite lived a single day outside the framework of atonement.

### "Unclean Until the Even" — What Actually Cleanses

The phrase appears throughout Leviticus 11–15 and Numbers 19 in a pattern so consistent it becomes unmistakable:

- Touch a carcass → wash clothes → "unclean until the **even**" (Lev 11:24–25)
- Bodily discharge → wash with water → "unclean until the **even**" (Lev 15:5–11)
- Contact with death → water of purification → "unclean until the **even**" (Num 19:19)

The three-step sequence is always the same: (1) the unclean thing occurs, (2) washing with water, (3) restoration "at even." It is the third step that matters most for understanding the symbol. If "evening" meant merely sunset — the passage of time — then the cleansing agent would be *time itself*, or worse, $darkness. But Leviticus 17:11 gives the interpretive key:

> *"For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh atonement for the soul."* — Lev 17:11

Blood makes atonement. Not time. Not nightfall. Not the rotation of the earth. The evening sacrifice — the lamb slain at the appointed hour — is what stands behind the phrase "unclean until the even." The person's physical washing prepared them; the sacrifice completed the restoration.

This reframes every "unclean until the even" passage in Leviticus and Numbers. They are not calendar notes about when contamination expires. They are pointers — dozens of them — all aimed at the same reality: a lamb must die for you to be clean.

### Elijah at the Time of the Evening Sacrifice

> *"And it came to pass at the time of the offering of the **evening** sacrifice, that Elijah the prophet came near, and said, LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and of Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel."* — 1 Ki 18:36

Elijah's confrontation with the prophets of Baal on Mount Carmel pivots on this moment. The prophets of Baal cried out from morning until "the time of the offering of the evening sacrifice" (1 Ki 18:29) — nothing happened. They cut themselves, bled, shouted. No fire.

Then Elijah steps forward. Not at a random moment but at the *evening sacrifice time* — the hour when the lamb would normally die in $jerusalem at the temple. He drenches the altar with water, calls on the LORD, and fire falls.

The text marks the time twice (1 Ki 18:29, 36) because the timing *is* the theology. God answers at the hour of atonement. The false gods produce nothing — not at morning, not at midday, not ever. The true God responds at the appointed time of sacrifice.

### The Ninth Hour — When the Lamb of God Died

> *"And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?"* — Mat 27:46

The ninth hour. Roughly 3 p.m. The exact time of the evening sacrifice.

While the $lamb was being slaughtered in the temple for the daily *tamid*, the Lamb of God was dying on the cross outside the walls of $jerusalem. John the Baptist had identified Him at the beginning of His ministry:

> *"Behold the $[lamb] of God, which taketh away the sin of the world."* — Jn 1:29

Every "unclean until the even" in Leviticus was a shadow cast backward from this hour. Every evening lamb for fifteen centuries pointed forward to this one. The timing was not incidental — it was the fulfillment of the pattern. The evening sacrifice that cleansed Israel daily found its substance in the death of Christ at the ninth hour.

The early church recognized the hour. When Peter and John went to the temple in Acts 3:1, they went "at the hour of prayer, being the ninth hour" — the evening sacrifice time. The hour of atonement had become the hour of prayer, and the church gathered at it.

### Daniel's "Evenings and Mornings" — Counting Sacrifices, Not Sunsets

> *"And he said unto me, Unto two thousand and three hundred **evenings** and mornings; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed."* — Dan 8:14 (literal Hebrew)

The Hebrew reads עֶרֶב בֹּקֶר (*erev boqer*) — "evening morning," not "days" (H3117 *yom*). Daniel could have used *yom* if he meant days. He did not. He used the vocabulary of the daily sacrifice.

The context confirms this. In Daniel 8:11, the "daily sacrifice" (*tamid*) is taken away. In Daniel 8:13, the question is explicitly: how long will the daily sacrifice be removed and the sanctuary trodden down? The answer comes in 8:14 — 2,300 "evening mornings."

If each "evening morning" is one sacrifice (one evening offering + one morning offering = one day's pair), then 2,300 individual sacrifices = 1,150 days — approximately three and a half years. This aligns remarkably with the historically documented period of Antiochus Epiphanes' desecration of the temple (167–164 BC), when the daily sacrifice was suspended.

The Septuagint translators (c. 250 BC), working before this history unfolded, rendered the phrase literally as "evening and morning" rather than converting it to "days." They understood the sacrificial vocabulary.

When evening means *sacrifice time*, Daniel's prophecy clarifies. The prophet is not counting sunsets. He is counting missed sacrifices — the number of times the lamb did not die at the appointed hour — and asking: when will atonement be restored?

## Patterns

1. **Evening = sacrifice, not sunset.** The evening is defined by what happens in it (the lamb dies), not by the astronomical event (the sun sets). Time in Israel's worship was structured around blood, not light cycles.

2. **"Unclean until the even" = unclean until atonement.** Every occurrence of this phrase in Leviticus and Numbers is a signpost pointing to the same reality: blood cleanses, not time.

3. **The ninth hour is the hinge.** Elijah's fire falls at the evening sacrifice time (1 Ki 18:36). Jesus dies at the ninth hour (Mat 27:46). The apostles pray at the ninth hour (Acts 3:1). The most significant divine interventions cluster at the hour of the evening lamb.

4. **Morning and evening frame the $day.** The morning sacrifice opens the period of accountability; the evening sacrifice closes it with atonement. Between them is the work of the $day. The entire biblical day is a cycle from blood to blood.

5. **$bread and $wine accompany the evening lamb.** The evening sacrifice includes flour (covenant terms) and wine (covenant blood) alongside the lamb (Exod 29:40–41). The Last Supper — held in the evening — mirrors this exactly: $bread broken, $wine poured, the Lamb about to be slain.

6. **Daniel counts sacrifices, not days.** When evening is understood as sacrifice time, prophetic time-references built on "evening and morning" (Dan 8:14) resolve into counts of missed sacrifices rather than ambiguous spans of days.

## Connections

- **$darkness** — Darkness represents hidden and concealed disobedience — the very thing atonement addresses. Evening is not the onset of $darkness; it is the hour that *prevents* the need to enter $darkness unclean. The sacrifice at evening resolves defilement before the night arrives. Without it, one carries uncleanness into concealment.

- **$day** — The $day represents exposure and accountability. Evening closes the $day with atonement — the daily work is done, the defilement is covered, the account is settled. The morning sacrifice opens the time of accountability; the evening sacrifice resolves it. Together they define the $day's boundaries.

- **$bread** — The $bread (covenant terms / Word of God) accompanies the evening lamb as a grain offering (Exod 29:40). When $bread and $wine appear alongside the evening sacrifice, the full covenant pattern is present: the Word is declared, the blood is poured, the life is offered.

- **$wine** — The drink offering of $wine (covenant blood) is poured out with the evening lamb (Exod 29:40). Wine is "the blood of grapes" (Gen 49:11); the evening sacrifice is where that blood is shed. The Last Supper's cup — "this is my blood of the $renewed-covenant" (Mat 26:28) — occurs in the evening, completing the pattern.

- **$jerusalem** — The $jerusalem (covenant community / the bride) is where the evening sacrifice was offered. The temple, the altar, the lamb, the $bread, the $wine — all converged at the appointed hour in the appointed place. When Daniel asks how long the sanctuary will be trodden down (Dan 8:13), the concern is that the evening sacrifice has ceased in $jerusalem — the covenant community has been cut off from its source of atonement.
