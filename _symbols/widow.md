### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **widow** begins: “A woman who has lost her husband by death, and has not married again; one living bereaved of a husband.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

God's covenant people cut off from their Husband.

> *“How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people! how is she become as a widow! she that was great among the nations, and princess among the provinces, how is she become tributary!”* — Lamentations 1:1

> *“For thy Maker is thine husband; the LORD of hosts is his name; and thy Redeemer the Holy One of Israel; The God of the whole earth shall he be called.”* — Isaiah 54:5

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Lamentations 1:1 portrays Jerusalem as being like a widow, and Isaiah 54:5 calls her Maker her Husband, but neither cited verse alone explicitly states that God’s covenant people have been covenantally cut off from him. Isaiah 54:5 may instead stress the continuing identity of God as Husband and Redeemer, so the stronger claim of separation depends on surrounding context and inference.

- One could argue for DIVERGENT: the consensus's central referent (a vulnerable human person under God's protection) is effectively replaced by a corporate covenantal referent, and 'cut off from their Husband' introduces a relational-severance emphasis (loss of God as Husband) rather than mere social vulnerability. If the book truly denies the individual social-justice sense any symbolic standing, the center has been swapped rather than narrowed. However, since the book's reading is explicitly catalogued as a common alternative in every consensus response, replacement of the primary by a listed alternative fits REFINED better than DIVERGENT.

- The vast majority of biblical occurrences concern social justice for actual widows rather than the corporate marriage metaphor; treating the latter as definitional displaces the dominant ethical motif without warrant.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
