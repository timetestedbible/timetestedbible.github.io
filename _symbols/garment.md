### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **garment** begins: “Any article of clothing, as a coat, a gown, etc.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

A garment symbolizes righteousness, specifically defined as the righteous acts of the saints.

> *“For your hands are defiled with blood, and your fingers with iniquity; your lips have spoken lies, your tongue hath muttered perverseness.”* — Isaiah 59:3

> *“Now Joshua was clothed with filthy garments, and stood before the angel.”* — Zechariah 3:3

> *“And he answered and spake unto those that stood before him, saying, Take away the filthy garments from him. And unto him he said, Behold, I have caused thine iniquity to pass from thee, and I will clothe thee with change of raiment.”* — Zechariah 3:4

> *“Thou hast a few names even in Sardis which have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with me in white: for they are worthy.”* — Revelation 3:4

> *“And to her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints.”* — Revelation 19:8

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **garment** begins:

> “Gar”ment, n. Etym: [OE. garnement, OF. garnement, garniment, fr. garnir to garnish. See Garnish.] Defn: Any article of clothing, as a coat, a gown, etc. No man putteth a piece of new cloth unto old garment. Matt. ix. 16.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Evidence by Sense

3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Because the sole citation is specifically Revelation 19:8, where the fine linen is directly identified with the saints' righteousness or righteous deeds, the book may simply match the consensus for that particular context rather than propose a restrictive meaning for every garment occurrence.

- One could argue for REFINED on the grounds that 'the righteous acts of the saints' narrows righteousness to sanctified deeds and implicitly rejects the imputed-righteousness reading favored in much Protestant teaching (Isaiah 61:10, Zechariah 3:3-5), thereby excluding cases where the garment is a divine gift rather than the believer's works. But since the consensus explicitly houses both construals within its primary meaning, and the book's gloss is simply Rev 19:8's own wording, this objection does not establish a material boundary change.

- The definition is so closely aligned with Revelation 19:8 (and the broader consensus category of 'righteousness') that it could be considered a MATCH rather than REFINED, as the book is simply citing the biblical gloss for 'fine linen' which is synonymous with garments.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
