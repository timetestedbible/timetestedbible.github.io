# Valley

**The low place — subjection, humbling, death, and decision; the pole opposite the ruling $mountain, and the ground from which God exalts the lowly.**

> *"Every **valley** shall be exalted, and every **mountain** and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain."* — Isa 40:4

---

## The Key Insight

Valley and $mountain are not two unrelated images. They are the **two ends of a single axis: elevation = status and authority.** The $mountain is *lifted up* — a kingdom, a throne, a seat of rule. The valley is *brought low* — the place with no elevation, and therefore no rule: subjection, lowliness, death.

One verse sets them face to face and tells you what God does with the axis:

> *"Every **valley** shall be exalted, and every **mountain** and hill shall be made low…"* — Isa 40:4 (cf. Luke 3:5)

This is the leveling that "prepares the way of the LORD": the proud heights pulled down, the low places raised. Read the two symbols together and a single law appears — **God lowers the high and lifts the low.** The valley is where that lifting begins.

**Symbolizes:** the low place — subjection, humbling, death, trial, and the valley of decision; the inverse of the ruling $mountain, and the ground from which God raises the lowly

**Opposite:** $mountain — a kingdom, a seat of rule, the exalted

**Defining verses:** Isa 40:4, Luke 3:5, 1Ki 20:28, Psa 23:4, Eze 37:1, Eze 37:12, Joel 3:14, Hos 2:15, Eze 21:26, Luke 1:52, Luke 14:11, Php 2:8–9

**The surprise:** the deepest valley is the road to the highest $mountain. Christ is "obedient unto death" (Php 2:8 — the valley) and then "highly exalted" (Php 2:9 — the mountain). The valley full of bones (Eze 37) is not the end but the place of resurrection.

**Connected:** $mountain, $sea, $rock, $water, $harvest

## Valley Across Scripture

### The Great Leveling

> *"Every **valley** shall be exalted, and every **mountain** and hill shall be made low: and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough places plain."* — Isa 40:4

> *"Every **valley** shall be filled, and every **mountain** and hill shall be brought low; and the crooked shall be made straight, and the rough ways shall be made smooth."* — Luke 3:5

Both Testaments place the valley and the $mountain in the same sentence, moving in opposite directions. The valley rises; the mountain falls. The same height that makes a $mountain a throne makes a valley a place of subjection — so to "prepare the way of the LORD" is to flatten the difference: no kingdom left exalted above His, no lowly one left in the dust. The axis itself is leveled before the King.

### The God of the Valleys Too

> *"…Because the Syrians have said, The LORD is God of the hills, but he is not God of the **valleys**, therefore will I deliver all this great multitude into thine hand…"* — 1Ki 20:28

Pagan Syria assumed Israel's God was a mountain-god — strong on the heights, helpless in the low country. God answers by winning in the valley. The lesson is the symbol in miniature: He does not only rule the $mountain (the seats of power); He rules the depths as well. There is no place low enough to be outside His government.

### The Valley of the Shadow of Death

> *"Yea, though I walk through the **valley of the shadow of death**, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me…"* — Psa 23:4

The valley is the lowest point a soul can travel — darkness, danger, death. Note that it is walked *through*, not dwelt in: the valley is a passage, not a destination. The Shepherd does not keep His sheep on the heights; He leads them down into the valley and out the other side.

### The Valley of Dry Bones — Death into Resurrection

> *"…and set me down in the midst of the **valley** which was full of bones…"* — Eze 37:1

> *"…I will open your graves, and cause you to come up out of your graves, and bring you into the land of Israel."* — Eze 37:12

The valley is where the dead lie — *and* where God raises them. Lowness is exactly the condition God reverses: the place of bones becomes the place of resurrection. This is why the valley, though it pictures death, is never hopeless in Scripture; it is the ground the Spirit breathes upon.

### The Valley of Decision

> *"Multitudes, multitudes in the **valley of decision**: for the day of the LORD is near in the valley of decision."* — Joel 3:14

Two verses earlier the sickle is thrust in because "the $harvest is ripe" (Joel 3:13). The valley is where the nations are gathered down to be judged — the low ground of reckoning. The high are brought *here* to be measured.

### The Valley of Achor — A Door of Hope

> *"…and the **valley of Achor** [trouble] for a door of hope: and she shall sing there…"* — Hos 2:15

Achor means *trouble* — the valley where sin was judged (Josh 7). Yet God turns the valley of trouble into "a door of hope." The pattern repeats: the low place is precisely where God opens the way up.

### The Law of the Axis — Abased and Exalted

The valley/$mountain contrast is not only geography; it is a moral and governmental law, stated again and again:

> *"…every one that is proud and lofty… and every one that is lifted up… shall be brought low."* — Isa 2:12

> *"…exalt him that is low, and abase him that is high."* — Eze 21:26

> *"He hath put down the mighty from their seats, and exalted them of low degree."* — Luke 1:52

> *"…whosoever exalteth himself shall be abased; and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted."* — Luke 14:11

Mountains (seats, thrones, the mighty) are cast down; valleys (the low degree) are lifted up. Luke 1:52 is the axis applied to persons: the high seats emptied, the low raised. The disciple is told to choose the valley on purpose — humble yourself, and be exalted (Jas 4:10; 1Pe 5:6).

### Valley → Mountain: The Pattern of Christ

> *"…he **humbled himself**, and became obedient unto **death**, even the death of the cross."* — Php 2:8

> *"Wherefore God also hath **highly exalted him**, and given him a name which is above every name."* — Php 2:9

The gospel itself travels the axis: down to the deepest valley (the cross, death), then up to the highest $mountain (the name above every name). Descent *before* ascent is the shape of the King's path — and the shape He sets for everyone who would follow Him.

## Patterns

1. **One axis, two poles.** Valley and $mountain are paired by height = authority. The $mountain is the exalted (kingdom, throne); the valley is the low (subjection, death). Isa 40:4 names them together.

2. **God reverses the poles.** The proud $mountain is made low; the lowly valley is exalted (Isa 40:4; Eze 21:26; Luke 1:52; Luke 14:11). The drama of Scripture is this leveling.

3. **The valley is a passage, not a pit.** It is walked *through* (Psa 23:4), and it is where the dead are *raised* (Eze 37:12). Lowness is the condition God exists to reverse.

4. **The valley is where the high are judged.** The nations are gathered *down* into the valley of decision (Joel 3:14) and the valley of trouble becomes a door of hope (Hos 2:15). Judgment and mercy both happen in the low place.

5. **He rules the depths too.** God is "God of the valleys" (1Ki 20:28) — no place is low enough to escape His government.

6. **Descent before ascent.** Christ goes valley→mountain (Php 2:8–9), and calls His people the same way (Luke 14:11; Jas 4:10).

## Connections

- **$mountain** — the valley's opposite and partner on the axis. The $mountain is a kingdom, a seat of rule; the valley is the ground beneath rule — subjection. Isa 40:4 levels them together; God lowers the one and lifts the other.

- **$sea** — the other "low," but a different kind. The $sea is chaotic, ungoverned peoples; the valley is low *ground* — the place of humbling and death within the land. Where the $mountain rises above the $sea (Rev 17:9, 15), the valley lies below the $mountain.

- **$rock** — what waits to be found in the low place. The covenant ($rock) and its waters are struck and flow *down*; valleys are where water gathers and life returns (Psa 104:8).

- **$water** — the valley collects it. Streams run "down by the valleys" (Psa 104:8); the low place is where $water — the word, cleansing, life — pools and makes fruitful. The humbled ground is the watered ground.

- **$harvest** — the valley of decision *is* a harvest. "Put ye in the sickle, for the $harvest is ripe" stands beside "the valley of decision" (Joel 3:13–14). The low ground is the threshing floor of the nations.

## For Further Study

- **$mountain** — the ruling pole. If the valley is subjection, the $mountain is rule. How does every "exalted/abased" passage map onto the two?
- **$water** — what fills the low place. Why does Scripture send its rivers *down* into the valleys, and what does the watered valley produce?
- **$harvest** — the valley of decision as a threshing floor. How does the sickle of Joel 3:13 relate to the gathering of the nations in 3:14?
- **$rock** — struck so that water runs down. How does the covenant flow from the height into the valley below?
