## The Short Case

Branches symbolize the members of a nation who can be broken off or grafted in while the tree remains standing.

> *“And if some of the branches be broken off, and thou, being a wild olive tree, wert graffed in among them, and with them partakest of the root and fatness of the olive tree;”* — Romans 11:17

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Romans 11:17 supports broken and grafted branches, but the verse alone does not explicitly identify the tree as a nation or all branches as national members; that interpretation depends on the surrounding discussion of Israel and Gentiles and may understate the covenantal or patriarchal-root dimensions of the metaphor.

- One could argue for MATCH, since the book's definition is essentially the consensus's Romans 11 alternative stated verbatim ('broken off... grafted in'), and glossary brevity rather than doctrinal disagreement may explain the omission of John 15. Alternatively, one could argue the 'nation' framing subtly reclassifies the olive tree from a spiritual covenant community to a national/political body, pushing toward a larger disagreement than a mere narrowing.

- Excluding the John 15 imagery of disciples abiding in Christ the Vine leaves out the most prominent New Testament use of the symbol.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
