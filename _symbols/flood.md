# Flood

## Approved Definition

**Destroying waters — peoples in motion: a nation invading, or the Lord's own host sweeping the enemy away.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Sea & Waters](/books/symbolic-language/sea-and-waters/).

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**Destroying waters — peoples in motion: a nation invading, or the Lord's own host sweeping the enemy away.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **flood** begins: “A great flow of water; a body of moving water; the flowing stream, as of a river; especially, a body of water, rising, swelling, and overflowing land not usually thus covered; a deluge; a freshet; an inundation.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

An army in motion—a nation invading, or the Lord’s own host sweeping the enemy away.

> *“Now therefore, behold, the Lord bringeth up upon them the waters of the river, strong and many, even the king of Assyria, and all his glory: and he shall come up over all his channels, and go over all his banks:”* — Isaiah 8:7

> *“Thus saith the LORD; Behold, waters rise up out of the north, and shall be an overflowing flood, and shall overflow the land, and all that is therein; the city, and them that dwell therein: then the men shall cry, and all the inhabitants of the land shall howl.”* — Jeremiah 47:2

> *“And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the end of the war desolations are determined.”* — Daniel 9:26

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The strongest objection is that the book may be defining only prophetic metaphorical uses of “flood,” not Noah’s Flood, in which case its claim is a context-specific refinement rather than a contradiction. Even so, presented as the glossary’s unqualified core entry, it displaces the settled Noahic identification.

- One could argue REFINED rather than DIVERGENT: the book's 'army as flood' can be read as a specification of the broader consensus category of divine judgment (Isa 8:7 explicitly has the LORD bringing the Assyrian 'waters'), and enemy-invasion flood imagery already appears as a common alternative in the blind responses, so the book arguably narrows within an acknowledged strand rather than contradicting the tradition outright.

- By defining 'Flood' purely as an invading army, the book ignores the most prominent biblical flood (Noah's) and its explicit New Testament interpretations, omitting major theological themes of judgment, salvation, and new creation.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
