# Pit / Abyss

**Enforced containment that removes an entity from exerting influence — temporary holding, not terminal; sealed but openable.**

> *"And I saw an angel come down from heaven, having the $[key] of the **bottomless pit** and a great $[chain] in his hand. And he laid hold on the $[dragon], that old $[serpent], which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years, and cast him into the **bottomless pit**, and shut him up, and set a $[seal] upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season."* — Rev 20:1–3

---

## The Key Insight

The pit is not the grave. The pit is not the lake of $fire. The pit is a *holding cell*.

Everything that goes into a pit in Scripture can come back out. Joseph went down into a pit — and came up to Pharaoh's throne (Gen 37:24; 41:14). Jeremiah was lowered into a miry pit — and was pulled out by ropes (Jer 38:6–13). The psalmist was brought up from a "horrible pit" — and his feet were set upon a $rock (Ps 40:2). Christ descended into the abyss — and rose again (Rom 10:7). Satan is cast into the abyss — and after a thousand years, he is *loosed* (Rev 20:7).

This is what separates the pit from every other downward destination in Scripture. Sheol receives the dead; the lake of $fire is permanent destruction ("tormented day and night for ever and ever," Rev 20:10). But the pit has a $key (Rev 9:1, 20:1). It has a $seal (Rev 20:3). It can be *opened* (Rev 9:2). Entities *emerge* from it (Rev 9:2, 11:7, 17:8, 20:7).

The function of the pit is not to punish but to *contain*. To remove the confined entity from the world of the living — to strip it of influence, voice, and power — for a defined period. Joseph in the pit could not speak to his father. Jeremiah in the dungeon could not prophesy to the city. Satan in the abyss cannot deceive the nations. The pit silences.

And the pit *releases*.

**Symbolizes:** Enforced containment removing influence — the temporary holding of an entity, whether righteous or wicked, in a place of powerlessness until the appointed time

**Opposite:** $throne / exaltation — the seat of maximum influence. "I will exalt my throne above the stars of God… Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the **pit**" (Isa 14:13, 15).

**Defining verses:** Gen 37:24, Isa 24:22, Rev 20:1–3, Ps 40:2, Zech 9:11

**The surprise:** The pit is *waterless*. "The pit was empty, there was no water in it" (Gen 37:24). "The pit wherein is no water" (Zech 9:11). A cistern *with* water sustains life — "drink waters out of thine own cistern" (Prov 5:15). A cistern *without* water is a prison. The same structure, emptied of its life-giving content, becomes a containment vessel.

**Connected:** $darkness, $sea, $fire, $smoke, $seal, $serpent, $fall-of-babylon

## Pit / Abyss Across Scripture

### Joseph and the Pattern of Descent-Before-Exaltation

> *"Come, and let us slay him, and cast him into some **pit**, and we will say, Some evil beast hath devoured him."* — Gen 37:20

> *"And Reuben said unto them… cast him into this **pit** that is in the wilderness, and lay no hand upon him… And they… cast him into a **pit**: and the **pit** was empty, there was no water in it."* — Gen 37:22, 24

Joseph's brothers strip him of his coat and throw him into a dry cistern (H953 *bôwr*). The detail "no water" matters: this is not a well but a holding cell. Joseph cannot climb out. He cannot be heard. His voice — the voice that told dreams of authority — is silenced.

From the pit, he goes to Egypt. From Egypt, to the dungeon (Gen 40:15 uses the same word H953: "they put me into the **dungeon**"). From the dungeon, Pharaoh sends for him and he is raised to the throne (Gen 41:14). The trajectory is: voice silenced → influence removed → containment endured → sudden exaltation to supreme authority.

This becomes the defining narrative pattern of the pit in Scripture. The same arc appears again and again:

- **Jeremiah** cast into the miry pit (Jer 38:6) → pulled out, vindicated as true prophet
- **David** in the "horrible pit, the miry clay" → feet set upon a $rock (Ps 40:2)
- **Jonah** in the depths: "earth with her bars was about me for ever: yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption" (Jonah 2:6, H7845 *shachath*)
- **Christ** — "Who shall descend into the deep [G12 *abyssos*]? (that is, to bring up Christ again from the dead)" (Rom 10:7)
- **Israel** as prisoners in a waterless pit, released by covenant blood (Zech 9:11)

The pit is the lowest point before the highest exaltation. It is not the end of the story — it is the hinge.

### "Wherein Is No Water"

> *"As for thee also, by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the **pit** wherein is no water."* — Zech 9:11

The waterless pit appears three times as a distinct marker:

1. Gen 37:24 — "the pit was empty, there was no water in it" (Joseph)
2. Jer 38:6 — "no water in the dungeon, but mire" (Jeremiah)
3. Zech 9:11 — "the pit wherein is no water" ($israel as prisoners)

Water in a cistern makes it a *well* — a source of life (Prov 5:15; John 4:11–12; 2 Ki 18:31). The same structure *without* water becomes something entirely different: a dry hole with no sustenance, no productive capacity, total isolation. The contained entity has nothing to draw on.

Zechariah's passage is remarkable because it names the mechanism of release: "by the blood of thy covenant." The prisoners do not escape. They are *sent forth* — liberated by covenant authority. The pit holds them; the covenant blood opens it. This parallels the $key and $seal imagery of Revelation: the containment is real, but so is the release, and it comes by sovereign authority.

### The Isaiah-Revelation Hinge

> *"And it shall come to pass in that day, that the LORD shall punish the host of the high ones that are on high, and the kings of the earth upon the earth. And they shall be gathered together, as prisoners are gathered in the **pit**, and shall be shut up in the prison, and **after many days** shall they be **visited**."* — Isa 24:22

Compare this with what John saw roughly seven hundred years later:

> *"And he laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent… and cast him into the **bottomless pit**, and **shut him up**, and set a **seal** upon him… till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be **loosed**."* — Rev 20:1–3

The structural parallels suggest these are not merely similar — they may describe the same event from two vantage points:

| Isaiah 24:22 | Revelation 20:1–3 |
|---|---|
| Gathered in the **pit** (H953) | Cast into the **abyss** (G12) |
| **Shut up** in prison (H4525) | **Shut him up** (G2808) + sealed (G4972) |
| **After many days** (H7230 + H3117) | **Thousand years** |
| **Visited** (H6485 *paqad*) | **Loosed** (G3089) |

The word *paqad* (H6485) is worth pausing on. It means "to visit, inspect, attend to" — and it carries both positive and negative force. God *visits* to release (Exod 3:16) or to judge (Jer 6:15). The ambiguity is fitting: after the containment period, the entities are *attended to* — and what happens next depends on what they are.

### What the Pit Contains: Voices and Powers

The pit does not merely hold bodies. It holds *influence*. Every pit-narrative involves the silencing or restraining of a force that was active in the world:

**Joseph** — whose dreams declared future authority. The pit removed that voice from his family.

**Jeremiah** — whose prophecies condemned Jerusalem's leadership. "They have cast me into the dungeon" (Jer 38:6). Silencing the prophet.

**Satan** — "that he should deceive the nations no more" (Rev 20:3). The explicit purpose of his containment is the removal of his deceptive influence. Not punishment — *muzzling*.

**The beast** — "ascendeth out of the bottomless pit" (Rev 11:7, 17:8). The beast regains influence when it emerges. Before that, it was *contained* — powerless to act against the witnesses.

**Demons** — "And they besought him that he would not command them to go out into the deep [G12 *abyssos*]" (Luke 8:31). The demons fear the abyss because it would strip them of their ability to operate. They would rather enter swine than be rendered inactive.

This last detail is telling. The demons do not beg to avoid punishment — they beg to avoid *containment*. The abyss is not a place of torment for them (that is the lake of $fire; see Mat 25:41). It is a place where they can do nothing. For entities whose entire purpose is to act upon the world — to deceive, to possess, to destroy — being sealed in powerlessness is the one thing they cannot bear.

### Nations Go Down to the Pit

> *"I will make thee a terror, and thou shalt be no more: though thou be sought for, yet shalt thou never be found again, saith the Lord GOD."* — Ezek 26:21

Ezekiel uses the phrase "go down to the pit" repeatedly for nations that lose their power on the world stage:

- **Tyre** — "I shall bring thee down with them that descend into the pit, with the people of old time" (Ezek 26:20). Removed from "the land of the living."
- **Tyre's prince** — "They shall bring thee down to the pit, and thou shalt die the deaths of them that are slain in the midst of the seas" (Ezek 28:8).
- **Assyria** (as the great cedar) — "I cast him down to hell… I made the nations to shake at the sound of his fall, when I cast him down to the pit" (Ezek 31:16).
- **Egypt** — "Whom dost thou pass in beauty? go down, and be thou laid with the uncircumcised" (Ezek 32:19).
- **Elam, Edom, Zidon** — all catalogued as "gone down to the pit" (Ezek 32:23–30).

The pattern is consistent: a nation exalts itself → God brings it down to the pit → it is removed from "the land of the living" — that is, from active geopolitical influence. The nation is not annihilated; it is *sidelined*. Its voice goes silent on the world stage.

This connects directly to the defining contrast in Isaiah 14:

> *"How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! … For thou hast said in thine heart, I will exalt my **throne** above the stars of God… Yet thou shalt be brought down to hell, to the sides of the **pit**."* — Isa 14:12–15

$throne above the stars versus sides of the pit. Maximum exaltation versus maximum containment. The two poles of the symbol.

### When the Pit Opens — Smoke, Darkness, and the Beast

> *"And he opened the **bottomless pit**; and there arose a $[smoke] out of the **pit**, as the $[smoke] of a great furnace; and the sun and the air were $[darkness] by reason of the $[smoke] of the **pit**."* — Rev 9:2

When the pit is *opened*, its contents pour out — and those contents produce $darkness. The $smoke that rises from the pit is the visible, ascending evidence of $fire's application; it obscures the sun and air, darkening the structures of authority. Hidden disobedience ($darkness) spreads as what was contained is released.

Out of the $smoke come locusts with a king: "the angel of the bottomless pit, whose name in the Hebrew tongue is Abaddon (H3 — 'destruction'), but in the Greek tongue hath his name Apollyon (G623 — 'destroyer')" (Rev 9:11). The pit has a ruler — destruction personified. This is not Satan (Satan is *cast into* the pit in Rev 20:1–3; the angel of the pit *rules* it). They are distinct.

The beast itself ascends from the abyss:

> *"And when they shall have finished their testimony, the beast that ascendeth out of the **bottomless pit** shall make war against them, and shall overcome them, and kill them."* — Rev 11:7

> *"The beast that thou sawest was, and is not; and shall ascend out of the **bottomless pit**, and go into perdition."* — Rev 17:8

The beast's trajectory is: *was* (active) → *is not* (contained) → *shall ascend* (released) → *go into perdition* (terminated permanently in the lake of $fire). The pit is the middle stage — the period of powerlessness between active influence and final destruction. It is not the destination. It is the holding cell on the way to the courtroom.

This sequence matters for the $fall-of-babylon chain. The contained forces emerge. The $smoke of their emergence darkens the world. The $beast and $serpent exercise their brief, loosed authority. And then comes the final judgment — not back into the pit, but into the lake of $fire, where there is no $key, no $seal to break, and no release.

### The *Tehom* Beneath — What the Abyss Actually Is

The word G12 *abyssos* ("depthless, bottomless") is not a New Testament invention. In the Septuagint (LXX), it is the standard translation of H8415 *tehom* — the primordial "deep" of Genesis 1:2:

> *"And the earth was without form, and void; and $[darkness] was upon the face of the **deep** (H8415 *tehom*). And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the $[waters]."* — Gen 1:2

When John writes *abyssos* in Revelation, a Hebrew ear hears *tehom*. And the two-word phrase in Rev 9:1 — "the **pit** (G5421 *phrear*) of the **abyss** (G12 *abyssos*)" — distinguishes two things: the *phrear* is the physical shaft (a well, a cistern — the same word used for Jacob's well in Jn 4:11), and the *abyssos* is the depthless realm below it. The "bottomless pit" is literally **the well that opens into the primordial deep**. The KJV collapses this into one phrase, but the Greek keeps them separate. John even flags the Hebrew layer explicitly: the king of the abyss has a name "in the **Hebrew tongue** — Abaddon" (Rev 9:11).

H8415 *tehom* appears 36 times in the OT. It is always associated with vast subterranean waters — the deep that lies *beneath* everything:

- Gen 49:25 — *"blessings of the **deep** (tehom) that **coucheth beneath**"*
- Deut 33:13 — *"the **deep** that coucheth beneath"*
- Ps 78:15 — *"gave them drink as out of the great **depths** (tehom)"*
- Prov 8:24, 27–28 — Wisdom present when God set bounds on the *tehom*

The *tehom* is distinct from the $sea (H3220 *yam*). The sea is the gathered surface waters — the chaotic mass of nations. The *tehom* is deeper: the subterranean supply, the primordial ocean beneath the earth, the pre-creation waters over which God's Spirit brooded. In the vertical structure of creation: waters above the firmament → firmament (heaven) → seas (surface) → dry land → the *tehom* (beneath all).

Genesis 1:2 presents a parallel structure that illuminates what *tehom* is:

| Line A | Line B |
|--------|--------|
| $darkness was upon the face of | the Spirit of God moved upon the face of |
| **the deep** (*tehom*) | **the waters** (*mayim*) |

If this is synonymous parallelism — and the verse structure suggests it is — then the *tehom* is the waters, and the "darkness" over it corresponds to the Spirit of God. Psalm 139:12 confirms this: *"the $[darkness] is as $[light] to you."* The "darkness" over the *tehom* is not the absence of God — it is the **concealed presence** of God's Spirit before revelation begins. The "letting" of light is the *uncovering* of what was already there.

This reframes the abyss entirely. The *tehom/abyssos* is not an inherently evil realm. It is the **pre-ordered state** — the raw creation material over which God broods before He begins separating, naming, and structuring. What makes it dangerous is not its nature but its *unboundedness*. God's creative acts in Genesis 1 are acts of *containment* — separating light from darkness, waters above from waters below, sea from land. The *tehom* is what exists before those separations. When its boundaries fail, chaos returns.

This is precisely what happens in the flood:

> *"The fountains of the great **deep** (tehom) were **broken up**."* — Gen 7:11

The *tehom* erupts through its containment. The separations of Day 2 (waters above / waters below) and Day 3 (sea / land) collapse. Creation runs backward. The deep reclaims everything.

And this is what happens in Revelation:

> *"He opened the **well** (phrear) of the **abyss** (abyssos); and there arose **smoke** out of the well."* — Rev 9:2

The same structure — the deep breaking through its seal. But now what surges up is not water but $smoke, $darkness, and demonic powers. The flood was the physical un-creation. Revelation's opening of the abyss is the spiritual un-creation — the pre-ordered state erupting back through the boundaries God set. Both are the *tehom* unleashed.

And both are reversed. After the flood, God re-establishes the boundaries (Gen 8:2 — "the fountains of the deep were **stopped**"). After Revelation's abyss erupts, the $serpent is bound, the abyss is sealed again (Rev 20:1–3), and eventually the entire structure is replaced by a new heaven and new earth where there is "no more $sea" (Rev 21:1) — and, by implication, no more *tehom* to erupt.

### The Fear–Pit–Snare Triad

> *"Fear, and the **pit**, and the snare, are upon thee, O inhabitant of the earth."* — Isa 24:17

> *"It shall come to pass, that he who fleeth from the noise of the fear shall fall into the **pit**; and he that cometh up out of the midst of the **pit** shall be taken in the snare."* — Isa 24:18

The identical triad appears in Jer 48:43–44: fear (H6343 *pachad*) → pit (H6354 *pachath*) → snare (H6341 *pach*). The Hebrew creates a deliberate sound-pattern: *pachad–pachath–pach*. Three words from virtually the same consonants, signaling three-stage inescapable judgment.

Flee the dread — fall into the containment. Climb out of the containment — be caught in the trap. The pit in this triad is the *middle* stage of divine judgment: the temporary holding from which escape only leads to a final capture. There is no exit from God's judgment sequence.

### The One Who Digs It Falls In

> *"He made a **pit**, and digged it, and is fallen into the ditch which he made."* — Ps 7:15

> *"Whoso diggeth a **pit** shall fall therein."* — Prov 26:27

> *"He that diggeth a **pit** shall fall into it."* — Eccl 10:8

A recurring wisdom principle: the containment designed for others becomes the container's own confinement. Ps 9:15 — "The heathen are sunk down in the **pit** that they made: in the net which they hid is their own foot taken." Ps 35:7–8 — "For without cause have they hid for me their net in a **pit**… into that very destruction let him fall." Ps 57:6 — "They have digged a **pit** before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves."

This is not irony. It is structure. The one who designs containment for others is himself subject to containment. The $serpent who ensnares is ensnared. The accusers of Daniel are thrown into the den they prepared for him (Dan 6:24). Haman hangs on the gallows he built for Mordecai (Est 7:10). The pit is reversible — and the reversal often comes for the one who dug it.

### Brought Up — The Psalm of Deliverance

> *"He brought me up also out of an horrible **pit**, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a $[rock], and established my goings."* — Ps 40:2

The Psalms use the pit as the image of maximum powerlessness — the place from which only God can extract:

- Ps 28:1 — "Unto thee will I cry, O LORD my rock; be not silent to me: lest, if thou be silent to me, I become like them that go down into the **pit**."
- Ps 30:3 — "O LORD, thou hast brought up my soul from the grave: thou hast kept me alive, that I should not go down to the **pit**."
- Ps 88:4, 6 — "I am counted with them that go down into the **pit**… Thou hast laid me in the lowest **pit**, in $[darkness], in the deeps."
- Ps 143:7 — "Hear me speedily, O LORD: my spirit faileth: hide not thy face from me, lest I be like unto them that go down into the **pit**."

Note the association between the pit and $darkness in Ps 88:6 — the one cast into the pit is placed "in darkness, in the deeps." Hidden, concealed, removed from sight. And note the rescue in Ps 40:2: out of the pit, onto the $rock. From enforced containment to covenant foundation. From powerlessness to established steps.

Job 33 provides the fullest theology of pit-rescue:

> *"He keepeth back his soul from the **pit**, and his life from perishing by the sword."* — Job 33:18

> *"Yea, his soul draweth near unto the **pit**… If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to shew unto man his uprightness: Then he is gracious unto him, and saith, Deliver him from going down to the **pit**: I have found a ransom."* — Job 33:22–24

> *"He will deliver his soul from going into the **pit**, and his life shall see the light."* — Job 33:28

Three times in one chapter (vv. 18, 24, 28, 30): God delivers from the pit. The mechanism in v. 24 is a *ransom* (H3724 *kopher* — a covering price, atonement). Compare Zech 9:11: "by the blood of thy covenant I have sent forth thy prisoners out of the pit." Ransom, covenant blood, deliverance — the pit is opened by substitutionary payment.

## Patterns

1. **The pit is temporary; the lake of fire is permanent.** The pit has a $key (Rev 9:1, 20:1), a $seal (Rev 20:3), and can be opened. Entities emerge from it (Rev 9:2, 11:7, 17:8, 20:7). The lake of $fire has no key, no exit, no release. "Tormented day and night for ever and ever" (Rev 20:10). Different symbols, different functions. The pit is the holding cell; the lake is the sentence.

2. **The pit removes influence, not life.** Joseph is alive in the pit but silenced. Jeremiah is alive in the dungeon but cannot prophesy. Satan is alive in the abyss but cannot deceive. Demons fear it not because it torments but because it *disables*. The containment is functional, not punitive.

3. **Waterless pit = containment; water-filled cistern = life source.** The same structure operates opposite ways based on presence or absence of water. Gen 37:24, Zech 9:11, Jer 38:6 — all specify "no water." Prov 5:15, John 4:11–12, 2 Ki 18:31 — cisterns/wells with water are blessings.

4. **The descent-exaltation pattern.** Pit → throne. Joseph (Gen 37 → 41). David (Ps 40:2 → feet on rock). Jonah (Jon 2:6 → preaches to Nineveh). Christ (Rom 10:7 → resurrection). The pit is the low point before sudden elevation.

5. **The digger falls in.** Ps 7:15, 9:15, 35:7–8, 57:6, Prov 26:27, Eccl 10:8. Containment designed for others becomes the designer's own prison. A wisdom principle with cosmic scope: the $serpent who devised the trap is trapped (Rev 20:1–3).

6. **Pit opened → smoke → darkness.** Rev 9:2 traces the chain. The $fire within the pit produces $smoke when the $seal is broken. The $smoke darkens the visible authorities (sun and air). The $darkness — concealed disobedience — spreads through the release of what was contained. Fire → smoke → darkness is the invariable sequence.

7. **"After many days" = a set period.** Isa 24:22 ("after many days shall they be visited") and Rev 20:3 ("till the thousand years should be fulfilled") both specify a fixed term. The containment is not indefinite — it is *appointed*. The same principle of ordered timing that governs the $seal governs the pit.

## Connections

- **$darkness** — "Thou hast laid me in the lowest pit, in darkness, in the deeps" (Ps 88:6). $darkness is hidden, concealed disobedience. The pit is the *place* of that hiddenness — where entities are removed from sight and stripped of visible influence. When the pit opens (Rev 9:2), $darkness spreads as the concealed forces are released into the world.

- **$sea** — The $sea represents chaotic nations and peoples on the surface. The *tehom* (H8415) is deeper — the primordial subterranean waters beneath everything, which the LXX renders as G12 *abyssos*. Beasts emerge from the $sea (Dan 7:3, Rev 13:1) and from the abyss (Rev 11:7, 17:8) — both are reservoirs from which powers arise, but the $sea is the surface chaos of nations, while the *tehom/abyssos* is the deeper, pre-creation chaos that predates even the sea. The pit is the containment structure over the *tehom*; the $sea is what gathers on the surface above it.

- **$water** — The *phrear* (G5421) in Rev 9:1 — "well of the abyss" — is the same word used for Jacob's well (Jn 4:11), where Jesus offers living $water. The well can reach two very different sources: a spring of living water (the fountain of life) or the *tehom/abyssos* (the primordial deep). Same physical structure — opposite destinations. The cistern-well-fountain spectrum connects the $water study, the $sheol study, and the pit-abyss: fountain → well → cistern → pit → *tehom*.

- **$fire** — $fire is God's covenant standard in active contact. The pit contains $fire — when opened, $smoke arises "as the smoke of a great furnace" (Rev 9:2). The pit is not the $fire itself but the vessel that holds the $fire's effects in check. The lake of $fire is the pit's permanent successor — the containment without a $key.

- **$smoke** — $smoke is the ascending evidence of $fire's application. Rev 9:2 traces the chain: pit opened → $smoke rises → sun darkened. The $smoke of the pit is the bridge between the $fire of judgment and the $darkness that results. It also connects to the $fall-of-babylon sequence: Babylon's burning produces the $smoke that darkens the world (Rev 18:9, 18; 19:3).

- **$seal** — The $seal is authority's binding claim — sovereign identity that secures what it marks. In Rev 20:3, God sets a $seal upon the pit to restrain Satan. The $seal answers *whose authority governs this containment* — it is God's sovereign claim, not a lock that might be picked. When the appointed time comes, the same authority that sealed also loosens.

- **$serpent** — The $serpent is the deceiver working through cunning speech. Rev 20:2 identifies the dragon, the old $serpent, the Devil and Satan as the entity cast into the pit. The pit's explicit function is to stop the $serpent's deception: "that he should deceive the nations no more." The cunning whisperer is muzzled. The $serpent who dug pits for others (Ps 57:6 principle) is himself confined in one.

- **$fall-of-babylon** — The collapse of man's self-ruling system connects to the pit sequence. The $beast ascends *from* the pit (Rev 11:7, 17:8) and exercises its brief authority before Babylon falls. The $smoke of the pit (Rev 9:2) anticipates the $smoke of Babylon's burning (Rev 18:9). The pit releases what will ultimately self-consume — Babylon is destroyed by her own apparatus (Rev 17:16–17), then the powers that animated her are returned to final containment in the lake of $fire.

## For Further Study

- **$fire** — The pit contains fire; the lake of fire is the pit's permanent successor. How does the same holy standard produce both temporary containment and terminal judgment?
- **$seal** — The mechanism that holds the pit shut. What does it mean that God's sovereign authority both seals and unseals — and what governs the timing?
- **$serpent** — The pit's most prominent prisoner. How does the deceiver who dug pits for others end up confined in one — and what happens when the containment period ends?
- **$darkness** — The pit's interior condition. What is the relationship between enforced containment and concealed disobedience? Why does opening the pit spread darkness rather than light?
- **$fall-of-babylon** — The beast ascends from the pit, Babylon falls, and the beast returns to perdition. How does the pit function as the staging ground for the final sequence of God's covenant judgment?
- **$water** — The *phrear* ("well") of the abyss (Rev 9:1) uses the same word as Jacob's well (Jn 4:11), where Jesus offers living water. A well can reach a spring of life or the primordial *tehom*. How does the fountain → well → cistern → pit → *tehom* spectrum illuminate the choice between life and death in Jer 2:13?
- **$sheol** — Sheol (H7585) and the pit (*bowr*) parallel in Hebrew poetry (Ps 30:3, Isa 14:15), but sheol is death's demand on flesh while the pit contains the living in powerlessness. Where do these categories merge, and what does it mean that the *tehom* underlies both?
