## The Short Case

A great city of the sea representing gentile peoples who repent and rise in judgment.

> *“The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation, and shall condemn it: because they repented at the preaching of Jonas; and, behold, a greater than Jonas is here.”* — Matthew 12:41

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Matthew 12:41 directly supports repentance and rising in judgment, but the printed citation alone does not establish “city of the sea” and only indirectly supports extending the historical men of Nineveh into a general symbol of Gentile peoples.

- One could argue for REFINED on the grounds that the book collapses Nineveh's well-attested dual symbolism into only the repentance strand, implicitly excluding the Nahum 'bloody city under judgment' meaning that consensus rates as common; additionally, 'city of the sea' is a geographically inaccurate descriptor unsupported by the printed citation, suggesting the book may be reworking the referent in ways the glossary line alone does not reveal.

- The dual (repentance + irreversible judgment) portrait is settled across the consensus; omitting half of it plus the unsupported ‘city of the sea’ label makes the entry incomplete relative to the shared tradition.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
