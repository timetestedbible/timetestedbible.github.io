# Tower

## Approved Definition

**The stronghold raised by a [Name](/research/symbols/name/) around its house — refuge, watchtower, and lighthouse in one: high to see danger, giving [light](/research/symbols/light/) to guide the house, and covered to shelter those who come inside. A strong tower is an intact Name protecting its people; an unfinished tower is a Name begun without depth to endure its cost; a fallen tower is a [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) stronghold broken, emptied, or judged.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Tower](/books/symbolic-language/tower/), [research-tower.md](/books/symbolic-language/research/research-tower.md).

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **tower** begins: “(Arch.) (a) A mass of building standing alone and insulated, usually higher than its diameter, but when of great size not always of that proportion. (b) A projection from a line of wall, as a fortification, for purposes of defense, as a flanker, either or the same height as the curtain wall or higher. (c) A structure appended to a larger edifice for a special purpose, as for a belfry, and then usually high in proportion to its width and to the height of the rest of the edifice; as, a church towe”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

A tower is a covenantal or communal stronghold constituted by a Name: when intact it shelters, watches, and guides its people; when unfinished it signifies identity without sufficient depth or endurance, and when fallen it signifies a broken, emptied, or judged stronghold.

> *“The name of the LORD is a strong tower: the righteous runneth into it, and is safe.”* — Proverbs 18:10

> *“And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of a cloud, to lead them the way; and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light; to go by day and night:”* — Exodus 13:21

> *“And the LORD will create upon every dwelling place of mount Zion, and upon her assemblies, a cloud and smoke by day, and the shining of a flaming fire by night: for upon all the glory shall be a defence.”* — Isaiah 4:5

> *“And there shall be a tabernacle for a shadow in the daytime from the heat, and for a place of refuge, and for a covert from storm and from rain.”* — Isaiah 4:6

> *“I will stand upon my watch, and set me upon the tower, and will watch to see what he will say unto me, and what I shall answer when I am reproved.”* — Habakkuk 2:1

> *“And the whole earth was of one language, and of one speech.”* — Genesis 11:1

> *“And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a plain in the land of Shinar; and they dwelt there.”* — Genesis 11:2

> *“And they said one to another, Go to, let us make brick, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for stone, and slime had they for morter.”* — Genesis 11:3

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **tower** begins:

> “Tow”er, n. Etym: [OE. tour,tor,tur, F. tour, L. turris; akin to Gr. twr a tower, Ir. tor a castle, Gael. torr a tower, castle. Cf. Tor, Turret.] 1. (Arch.) (a) A mass of building standing alone and insulated, usually higher than its diameter, but when of great size not always of that proportion. (b) A projection from a line of wall, as a fortification, for purposes of defense, as a flanker, either or the same height as the curtain wall or higher. (c) A structure appended to a larger edifice for a special purpose, as for a belfry, and then usually high in proportion to its width and to the height of the rest of the edifice; as, a church tower.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The printed passages support refuge, vigilance, guidance, Babel’s name-making, unfinished construction, and a warning associated with a fallen tower separately, but they do not clearly establish that every tower is a Name raised around a covenant house. In particular, Exodus 13 and Isaiah 4 do not mention towers, Matthew 13 supplies the added idea of depth only by analogy, and Luke 13 does not identify the fallen Tower of Siloam as a broken covenant stronghold.

- One could argue for MATCH: the book's primary anchor is the same Prov 18:10 Name-as-tower reading the consensus lists first, and its unfinished/fallen states merely restate the consensus's Babel, Luke 14, and Luke 13 alternatives in different vocabulary — so all differences might be dressing rather than boundary change. Against this, the book's generalization from God's Name to 'a Name,' its lighthouse function built on non-tower texts (Ex 13:21; Isa 4:5-6; Matt 13:20-21 mentions no tower at all), and its recasting of Babel from pride to insufficient depth are substantive re-drawings of the symbol's shape that no consensus response anticipates.

- The synthesis of pillar-of-fire/cloud and Isa 4 defence imagery as ‘lighthouse-tower,’ plus the mapping of stony-ground discipleship onto unfinished towers, is an over-extension not licensed by the plain texts or by any consensus strand.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
