# Fool

## Approved Definition

**One who hears God's word and will not do it — the lawless, not the unintelligent.**
> *"For we ourselves also were sometimes **foolish**, disobedient, deceived, serving divers lusts and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, and hating one another."* — Titus 3:3

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**One who hears God's word and will not do it — the lawless, not the unintelligent.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **fool** begins: “A compound of gooseberries scalded and crushed, with cream; -- commonly called gooseberry fool.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

Paul does not write "foolish *and* disobedient" as though naming two different conditions. He writes them as synonyms — a cascade of terms describing the same state from different angles. Foolish. Disobedient. Deceived. Serving lusts. This is one portrait, not a list.

The secular world hears "fool" and thinks *stupid*. Scripture hears "fool" and means *rebel*.

The wise man and the foolish man in Jesus' parable both *hear* the same words (Mat 7:24–26). Intelligence is not the variable. The wise man hears and **does**. The foolish man hears and **does not**. The difference is not comprehension — it is compliance. One builds on the $rock (covenant); the other builds on $sand (shifting ground without covenant binding). Both heard. Only one obeyed.

This reframes every proverb, every parable, every prophetic rebuke involving the fool. The question is never "Does he understand?" The question is always "Will he submit?"

**Symbolizes:** Disobedience — the self-willed rebel who follows his own $way rather than God's

**Opposite:** The wise — the obedient one who does God's commandments (Ps 111:10, Prov 10:8)

**Defining verses:** Titus 3:3, Mat 7:26, Gal 3:1, Ps 14:1, Prov 1:7, Prov 12:15, Prov 28:26

**The surprise:** The Greek word translated "harmless" or "foolish" in Mat 7:26 — G3474 *mōros* — does not mean unintelligent. It means *heedless*: one who hears and disregards. And the Hebrew words for fool consistently point not to mental deficiency but to moral rebellion — H5036 *nabal* means vile, wicked; H191 *eviyl* means one who despises instruction; H3684 *kesiyl* means one who finds sport in mischief.

**Connected:** $faith, $believe, $rock, $sand, $truth, $idolatry, $wickedness, $oil, $virgin, $bread, $fruit, $way, $the-way

## The Fool Across Scripture

### The Opposite Defines the Meaning

The fastest route to understanding what the fool *is* runs through what the fool is *not*. Scripture defines the wise and the fool as a strict antithetical pair — and the wise man is never described as merely intelligent. He is described as *obedient*.

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that **do his commandments**."* — Ps 111:10

Wisdom begins with the fear of the LORD. But notice what the second clause says: good understanding belongs to those who *do his commandments*. Wisdom is not knowing — it is doing. The wise man is the commandment-keeper.

> *"The wise in heart will receive commandments: but a prating **fool** shall fall."* — Prov 10:8

The parallel is precise. Wise = receives commandments. Fool = does not. The fool prates — he talks — but does not receive instruction. The issue is never capacity. It is posture.

> *"The way of a **fool** is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is wise."* — Prov 12:15

The fool's defining trait: he is right *in his own eyes*. He has a standard — it is simply his own. He has a $way — it is simply not God's $way. The wise man hearkens; the fool self-legislates. This is the root of $idolatry — self-rule, following one's own judgment instead of God's word (1 Sam 15:23).

> *"A **fool** despiseth his father's instruction: but he that regardeth reproof is prudent."* — Prov 15:5

The fool *despises* instruction. Not misunderstands — **despises**. The Hebrew H191 *eviyl* carries contempt for external authority. This is an act of will, not a failure of intellect.

### "The Fool Hath Said in His Heart"

> *"The **fool** hath said in his heart, There is no God. They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good."* — Ps 14:1

The word here is H5036 *nabal* — not "simpleton" but *vile, wicked*. The same word names Nabal in 1 Samuel 25, the man whose very name his wife explains: "as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him" (1 Sam 25:25). Nabal refused to honor David's claim, kept everything for himself, and threw a feast like a king in his own house. His folly was not ignorance of David — it was defiant self-will.

The fool of Psalm 14 says "no God" — not as an intellectual proposition, but as a practical policy. The next words prove it: "They are corrupt, they have done abominable works, there is none that doeth good." The denial of God produces — or rather *is* — corrupt works. Saying "no God" and doing abominable works are the same act described from two angles. The fool's atheism is behavioral, not philosophical. He lives as though no one has authority over his conduct.

This is $idolatry at its root: self-rule, the rejection of God's word as the governing standard. The fool has not failed to find God. He has declined to submit to Him.

### The Parable That Defines the Word

> *"And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and **doeth them not**, shall be likened unto a **foolish** man, which built his house upon the $[sand]."* — Mat 7:26

Jesus defines the fool with clinical precision: one who **hears** and **does not do**. The Greek G3474 *mōros* — the word from which English derives "moron" — means *heedless*, not *stupid*. It describes a person who has access to the word and disregards it.

The wise man and the foolish man in this parable are identical in several respects. Both hear the same sayings. Both build a house. Both face the same storm. The single variable is action: one does, the other does not.

What does each build on? The wise man builds on the $rock — the covenant, the unchanging foundation (1 Cor 10:4, "that Rock was Christ"). The foolish man builds on $sand — the multitudes at the covenant boundary who have not bound themselves to the $rock. $sand is what $rock would be without covenant: separate grains, shifting, unable to bear weight. The parable is a picture of two lives that look similar on the surface — both have a house, both heard the word — but one is anchored in covenant obedience and the other is not.

The storm reveals the difference. Not the hearing. The doing.

### "O Foolish Galatians — Who Hath Bewitched You?"

> *"O **foolish** Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that ye should **not obey** the $[truth]?"* — Gal 3:1

Paul's rebuke is the equation written out in full. Foolish = not obeying the $truth. And $truth in Scripture is not abstract factual accuracy — it is God's standard, His commandments (Jn 17:17, "Thy word is truth"; Ps 119:142, "Thy law is the truth"). To not obey $truth is to not keep God's commandments. To not keep God's commandments is to be a fool.

The word here is G453 *anoētos* — literally "without understanding," from *a-* (not) + *noeō* (to perceive, think). But Paul immediately defines what he means by this "lack of understanding": it is the failure to obey. The Galatians were not uninformed. They had received the gospel. They had believed. They were now turning from obedience to self-directed religion — the very pattern of $idolatry, which is self-rule dressed in spiritual clothing.

### The Rich Fool — Storing for Self

> *"But God said unto him, Thou **fool**, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided for thyself?"* — Lk 12:20

The word God uses is G878 *aphrōn* — senseless, without reason. But the rich man's business plan was perfectly rational by worldly standards. His barns were full. His strategy was sound. His retirement was funded. What made him a fool?

> *"So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God."* — Lk 12:21

He stored for *himself*. His entire framework was self-directed. He consulted his own desires ("I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods"), built bigger barns to serve his own comfort, and made plans that centered entirely on his own future. God's standard, God's purposes, God's word — none entered the calculation. This is not stupidity. This is $idolatry: the ultimate idol is the self, and the rich fool served it with impressive efficiency.

### The Foolish Virgins — No Oil

> *"And five of them were wise, and five were **foolish**. They that were **foolish** took their lamps, and took no $[oil] with them."* — Mat 25:2–3

Ten $virgins await the bridegroom. All ten have lamps. All ten go out to meet him. All ten fall asleep. The difference? Five have $oil; five do not.

$oil in the pipeline represents proven works — the result of $fruit (works/deeds) pressed through trials and testing. It cannot be borrowed ("go ye rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves" — Mat 25:9). It cannot be transferred. It is personal, earned through sustained obedience.

The foolish $virgins had the outward form — lamps, attendance, the right location — but lacked the proven works that come from doing. They looked like the wise. They positioned themselves among the faithful. But when the moment of testing arrived, there was nothing inside the lamp. The wise $virgins' $oil was the accumulated evidence of $faith — trusting obedience acted out over time. The foolish had the posture without the practice.

When the foolish arrive late and cry "Lord, Lord, open to us," the bridegroom answers: "I know you not" (Mat 25:12). Compare this with the passage immediately preceding Jesus' $rock/$sand parable:

> *"Not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that **doeth** the will of my Father."* — Mat 7:21

The same pattern. The same word. Profession without obedience. Lamps without $oil. Hearing without doing. This is the fool.

### The Fool's Signature: Trust in Self

A thread runs through every Hebrew term for "fool" — and it is always the same thread.

> *"He that trusteth in his own heart is a **fool**: but whoso walketh wisely, he shall be delivered."* — Prov 28:26

> *"A **fool** hath no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself."* — Prov 18:2

> *"**Fools** make a mock at sin."* — Prov 14:9

> *"It is an abomination to **fools** to depart from evil."* — Prov 13:19

> *"Woe unto the **foolish** prophets, that follow their own spirit, and have seen nothing!"* — Ezek 13:3

The pattern: the fool trusts his own heart (Prov 28:26), delights in his own discovery (Prov 18:2), mocks the very concept of sin (Prov 14:9), considers it *abominable* to depart from evil (Prov 13:19), and follows his own spirit rather than God's word (Ezek 13:3). Every line points the same direction: self-rule. The fool is his own god, his own standard, his own $way.

This is precisely the definition of $wickedness — lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2). And it is the behavioral definition of $idolatry — not bowing to a carved image but setting up the self as the governing authority (1 Sam 15:23, "stubbornness is as idolatry… because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD").

The fool does not lack a standard. He has one. It is himself.

### "Become a Fool, That He May Be Wise"

> *"Let no man deceive himself. If any man among you seemeth to be wise in this world, let him become a **fool**, that he may be wise."* — 1 Cor 3:18

> *"We are **fools** for Christ's sake."* — 1 Cor 4:10

If the fool is the disobedient — the one who rejects the governing authority's standard — then "becoming a fool" to the world means becoming *disobedient to the world's standard*. The worldly wise follow the world's $way. To abandon that $way for God's $the-way looks like foolishness to those still operating under the old authority.

Paul's paradox confirms the meaning by inversion. The world calls covenant obedience foolish. Therefore, to be a "fool for Christ" is to be disobedient to the world's wisdom *precisely because* one is obedient to God's. The word does not change meaning — it changes reference frame. The fool is always the one who disobeys the reigning standard. The question is: whose standard reigns?

### Walk Circumspectly

> *"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as **fools**, but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil. Wherefore be ye not unwise, but understanding what the will of the Lord is."* — Eph 5:15–17

Paul's instruction closes the circle. Walking as fools = not understanding the will of the Lord. Walking as wise = understanding and *doing* the will of the Lord. "Understanding" here is not intellectual comprehension but practical alignment — the same distinction embedded in every occurrence of the fool.

The will of the Lord is His $truth — His commandments, His standard. To walk as a fool is to walk without reference to that standard. To walk as wise is to walk in $the-way, according to that standard. "Redeeming the time" echoes Jeremiah's turtledove, who observes the appointed times when God's people do not (Jer 8:7). The wise know what season it is. The fool is oblivious — not because he cannot read the clock, but because he is not looking at it.

## Patterns

1. **The fool hears but does not do.** Mat 7:26, Prov 10:8, Prov 15:5, Jas 1:22–25. The defining action of the fool is not ignorance but refusal. He has the information and rejects the instruction.

2. **The fool is right in his own eyes.** Prov 12:15, Prov 28:26, Prov 18:2, Ezek 13:3. Self-trust is the root of foolishness. The fool measures by his own standard rather than God's — the practical definition of $idolatry.

3. **Foolish and disobedient are synonyms.** Titus 3:3 pairs them directly. Gal 3:1 equates "foolish" with "not obeying the truth." The Hebrew and Greek terms converge on the same point: rejection of instruction.

4. **The fool is the $rock/$sand contrast made personal.** Mat 7:24–27 is the parable of the fool: same hearing, different doing, different foundation. The wise man anchors in covenant ($rock); the fool drifts on $sand.

5. **The fool's opposite is always defined by doing.** Ps 111:10 (do commandments = wisdom), Prov 10:8 (receive commandments = wise), Eph 5:17 (understand the Lord's will = wise). Wisdom is never mere knowledge; it is always active obedience.

6. **"Become a fool" inverts the reference frame.** 1 Cor 3:18, 1 Cor 4:10. Disobedience to the world's standard = obedience to God's. The term "fool" always means "the one disobeying the ruling standard" — the question is which standard rules.

7. **Every Hebrew word for fool points to rebellion, not ignorance.** H5036 *nabal* = vile/wicked; H3684 *kesiyl* = one who sports in mischief; H191 *eviyl* = despiser of instruction; H5530 *sakal* = follower of own understanding. No term suggests intellectual deficiency.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience (Rom 1:5, "obedience of faith"). The fool is the anti-type of $faith: he hears but will not commit, will not act, will not obey. Where $faith produces action, the fool produces only words (Prov 10:8, "a prating fool shall fall").

- **$believe** — To $believe is to commit to obey (Jn 3:36, where "believeth not" = G544 *apeitheō*, to disobey). The fool is the one who refuses to $believe — not because he doubts intellectually, but because he will not commit his conduct to God's authority.

- **$rock** — The $rock is the covenant, the unchanging foundation (1 Cor 10:4). The wise man builds on it (Mat 7:24). The fool refuses to build on $rock because building on $rock requires *doing* what was heard — submitting to the covenant's terms.

- **$sand** — $sand represents multitudes at the covenant boundary — people who have not bound themselves to the $rock. The fool builds on $sand: he dwells among the people of God, hears the words of God, but never binds himself to the covenant through obedience. His house stands until the storm.

- **$truth** — $truth is God's standard, His commandments (Jn 17:17, Ps 119:142). The fool does not obey $truth (Gal 3:1). He may acknowledge it, debate it, even quote it — but he will not *do* it.

- **$idolatry** — $idolatry is self-rule, rejecting God's word as the governing standard (1 Sam 15:23). The fool *is* the idolater in practice: right in his own eyes, trusting his own heart, following his own spirit. Foolishness and $idolatry share the same root — the self on the throne.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness is lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2, 2 Thess 2:7). The fool's disobedience, when it hardens into a settled pattern, becomes $wickedness — the systematic rejection of God's covenant terms.

- **$oil** — $oil is proven works, the product of $fruit pressed through testing. The foolish $virgins lacked $oil (Mat 25:3) — not because they could not obtain it, but because they had not done the sustained work of obedience that produces it. No doing, no $oil.

- **$virgin** — The $virgin represents exclusive devotion, faithfulness to one master (Rev 14:4, 2 Cor 11:2). The wise $virgins embody this; the foolish $virgins have the form of devotion without the substance. They are positioned among the faithful but have not done what the faithful do.

- **$bread** — $bread is the Word, God's covenant terms (Deut 8:3, Jn 6:35). The fool receives $bread — he hears the word — but does not eat it. He does not internalize and act upon it. The famine of Amos 8:11 is not the fool's problem; the fool has $bread and ignores it.

- **$fruit** — $fruit is works and deeds (Gal 5:19–22, Mat 7:16). The fool is known by his $fruit — or rather, by its absence. "By their fruits ye shall know them" applies to the fool as surely as to the false prophet: the disobedient life produces corrupt or absent $fruit.

- **$way** — $way is conduct, manner of life (Prov 12:15). "The way of a fool is right in his own eyes" — the fool has a $way, but it is self-directed. He walks his own path instead of $the-way, which is Torah, God's commandments.

## For Further Study

- **$rock** — The foundation the fool refuses to build on. What does it mean that the $rock is the covenant — and what does the wise man's house look like when it stands?
- **$sand** — What the fool builds on instead. How do multitudes at the covenant boundary become $rock? The transformation from $sand to living stones (1 Pet 2:4–5) is the story of the fool becoming wise.
- **$faith** — The fool's direct opposite in action. If the fool is disobedient, and $faith is trusting obedience, then every act of $faith is an act of un-fooling.
- **$idolatry** — The fool's theological root. Self-rule is the essence of $idolatry (1 Sam 15:23), and the fool is the self-ruler in daily practice. These two studies are two angles on the same rebellion.
- **$oil** — What the foolish virgins lacked. How are proven works accumulated, and why can they not be borrowed? The $oil study traces the production chain from $fruit through pressing to visible $light.
- **$truth** — What the fool refuses to obey. The $truth study traces God's commandments through Scripture and shows why "doing truth" (Jn 3:21) is the opposite of the fool's posture.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **fool** begins:

> “Fool, n. Etym: [Cf. F. fouler to tread, crush. Cf. 1st Foil.] Defn: A compound of gooseberries scalded and crushed, with cream; -- commonly called gooseberry fool.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Biblical wisdom literature applies fool-language to several kinds of moral and practical failure, and not every such text specifies that the person first heard God’s word and then refused it. The book may therefore overrestrict a broader biblical category.

- One could argue REFINED on the ground that the book narrows the fool to those who have actually heard God's word and refused it, thereby excluding the merely naive or the practical atheist who never received instruction, and that 'lawless' is a sharper juridical category than the consensus's broader 'practical godlessness.' If that condition is taken as strictly definitional rather than illustrative, the extension is a genuine subset of the consensus class.

- One could claim the book's focus on 'hears and will not do' slightly privileges the Matt 7:26 case over pure practical atheism or Proverbs gradations, yet consensus itself unifies these under the identical moral core and cites the identical verses.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**One who hears God's word and will not do it — the lawless, not the unintelligent.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *fool*, *fools*, *foolish*, *foolishness*, *simple*, *simpleton*. The English-form sweep found **194 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
