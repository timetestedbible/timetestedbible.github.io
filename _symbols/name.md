# Name

## Approved Definition

**The [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) identity of a house, consisting of its Head's Word, covenant law, and fruitful priesthood. Substituting any one creates a counterfeit Name; rejecting, replacing, or removing any one takes the Name in vain.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Name](/books/symbolic-language/the-name/).

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **name** begins: “The title by which any person or thing is known or designated; a distinctive specific appellation, whether of an individual or a class.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The Name is the covenant identity of a household under its Head, constituted by the divinely provided Word, covenant law, and fruitful priesthood. Taking the Name includes joining that house under its authority; taking it in vain means rejecting or replacing one of those constituent elements.

> *“And David arose, and went with all the people that were with him from Baale of Judah, to bring up from thence the ark of God, whose name is called by the name of the LORD of hosts that dwelleth between the cherubims.”* — 2 Samuel 6:2

> *“That the king said unto Nathan the prophet, See now, I dwell in an house of cedar, but the ark of God dwelleth within curtains.”* — 2 Samuel 7:2

> *“He shall build an house for my name, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever.”* — 2 Samuel 7:13

> *“And the LORD hath performed his word that he spake, and I am risen up in the room of David my father, and sit on the throne of Israel, as the LORD promised, and have built an house for the name of the LORD God of Israel.”* — 1 Kings 8:20

> *“And I have set there a place for the ark, wherein is the covenant of the LORD, which he made with our fathers, when he brought them out of the land of Egypt.”* — 1 Kings 8:21

> *“Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron's rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant;”* — Hebrews 9:4

> *“And he humbled thee, and suffered thee to hunger, and fed thee with manna, which thou knewest not, neither did thy fathers know; that he might make thee know that man doth not live by bread only, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of the LORD doth man live.”* — Deuteronomy 8:3

> *“And he declared unto you his covenant, which he commanded you to perform, even ten commandments; and he wrote them upon two tables of stone.”* — Deuteronomy 4:13

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **the person's character, authority, and reputation.**

- The cited texts establish that God’s name is associated with the temple, ark, covenant, and covenantal belonging, and Hebrews 9:4 lists the manna, tables, and Aaron’s rod. They do not by themselves state that these three items constitute the meaning of “Name,” nor that rejecting any one is what Exodus 20:7 means by taking the name in vain. The entry’s defining synthesis therefore exceeds its printed citations.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: the consensus says the name IS the person (his character and presence), while the book says the Name IS three objects/institutions appointed within a house — a different kind of referent — and its reading of Exodus 20:7 effectively rejects the universal understanding of that commandment. If the shift from 'person's character' to 'house's appointed contents' is judged a replacement of the referent rather than a re-specification of covenant identity, the verdict would be DIVERGENT. The citations, moreover, never state the equation name = manna + tables + rod; it is built by inference across Heb 9:4, Deut 8:3, Deut 4:13, and Num 17, so a critic could rate support INSUFFICIENT rather than PARTIAL.

- The triad could be read as a typological unpacking of God’s character, word, and authority (already inside the consensus), making the entry a specialized REFINED application rather than a replaced referent; the printed verses link Name to house/ark without an explicit equation of Name = the three items.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
