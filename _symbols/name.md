# Name

**Covenant — the binding relationship between God and His people.**

> *"He shall build an house for my **name**, and I will stablish the throne of his kingdom for ever."* — 2 Sam 7:13

---

## The Key Insight

David wants to build a house for *the ark of the covenant* (2 Sam 7:2). God responds: "He shall build an house for my **name**" (2 Sam 7:13).

God substitutes one word for the other. Where David says *ark of the covenant*, God says *my name*. They are the same thing. The name *is* the covenant.

This single exchange unlocks a pattern that runs from Genesis to Revelation. Every time Scripture speaks of God's "name" — invoking it, fearing it, profaning it, believing in it, being baptized into it — it is speaking about His *covenant*. Not a word to pronounce. Not a title to memorize. A relationship to enter and keep.

And this means the Third Commandment is not about what comes out of your mouth. It is about what you do with your life.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant — the binding relationship between God and His people

**Opposite:** Nameless / covenant-less — having no covenant, belonging to no master

**Defining verses:** 2 Sam 7:13, Exod 6:3, Exod 20:7, Acts 4:12, Mat 7:22–23

**The surprise:** People in Mat 7:22 prophesy, cast out demons, and do mighty works *in His name* — and He says "I never knew you." They invoked the covenant but never kept it. They took the name in vain.

**Connected:** $rock, $wickedness, $bread, $mark, $marriage, $the-way, $faith, $believe

## Name Across Scripture

### The Abraham Paradox — Name ≠ Pronunciation

> *"And I appeared unto Abraham, unto Isaac, and unto Jacob, by the name of God Almighty, but by my **name** JEHOVAH was I not known to them."* — Exod 6:3

This verse has troubled readers for centuries. God says the patriarchs did not know Him by His name YHWH — yet Genesis records Him speaking that name directly to Abraham:

> *"I am the LORD (YHWH) that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees."* — Gen 15:7

> *"Is any thing too hard for the LORD (YHWH)?"* — Gen 18:14

> *"By myself have I sworn, saith the LORD (YHWH)."* — Gen 22:16

Abraham heard the name. Isaac heard it. Jacob heard it. The pronunciation was not hidden. So what does Exod 6:3 mean?

If "name" means *covenant*, the paradox dissolves. God appeared to the patriarchs by the covenant of El Shaddai (God Almighty) — the promises of land, seed, and blessing. But by His covenant *as YHWH* — the covenant of redemption from bondage, the covenant ratified at Sinai with blood and fire — they had not yet experienced that relationship. They knew the word. They had not yet entered the reality.

The name is not the pronunciation. The name is the covenant behind it.

### The House for the Name

The exchange in 2 Samuel 7 is the clearest equation in Scripture:

> *"I was minded to build an house for the **ark** of God"* — 2 Sam 7:2 (see 1 Chr 28:2)

> *"He shall build an house for my **name**"* — 2 Sam 7:13

David says *ark of the covenant*. God says *my name*. The substitution is deliberate. The house Solomon builds is not a shrine for a pronunciation — it is a dwelling for the covenant itself.

Solomon understood this. In his dedicatory prayer (1 Ki 8), the word "name" appears sixteen times. Every instance sits in a covenant context — forgiveness, repentance, hearing prayer, restoring the people. Consider:

> *"That thine eyes may be open toward this house night and day, even toward the place of which thou hast said, My **name** shall be there: that thou mayest hearken unto the prayer which thy servant shall make toward this place."* — 1 Ki 8:29

> *"When thy people Israel be smitten down before the enemy, because they have sinned against thee, and shall turn again to thee, and confess thy **name**, and pray, and make supplication unto thee in this house…"* — 1 Ki 8:33

To "confess thy name" is to return to the covenant. To pray "toward the place where thy name dwells" is to orient oneself toward the covenant. The $rock upon which the temple sat was the covenant itself — and the name dwelling in it was the same reality in different language.

### The Third Commandment — What "In Vain" Actually Means

> *"Thou shalt not take the **name** of the LORD thy God in vain; for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his **name** in vain."* — Exod 20:7

The word "vain" is H7723 *shav* — emptiness, worthlessness, nothing. To take the name *in vain* is to take it *for nothing* — to render it empty, to make it meaningless.

If the name is the covenant, then taking it in vain is **emptying the covenant of its substance**. Claiming the covenant relationship while living as though it does not exist. Wearing the name of God's people while disregarding God's terms.

This reframes the commandment entirely. It is not primarily about careless speech. It is about careless living. The person who says "I belong to God" while ignoring His instructions has taken His name — His covenant — for nothing.

And this is precisely what Jesus describes in Mat 7:

> *"Many will say to me in that day, Lord, Lord, have we not prophesied in thy **name**? and in thy **name** have cast out devils? and in thy **name** done many wonderful works? And then will I profess unto them, I never knew you: depart from me, ye that work iniquity."* — Mat 7:22–23

The word "iniquity" here is G458 *anomia* — $wickedness, lawlessness. These people invoked the covenant. They performed signs under its banner. But they were *workers of lawlessness* — covenant transgressors. They took the name and made it nothing. They are the living illustration of the Third Commandment broken.

### "No Other Name" — No Other Covenant

> *"Neither is there salvation in any other: for there is none other **name** under heaven given among men, whereby we must be saved."* — Acts 4:12

If name means covenant, this reads: *there is no other covenant under heaven by which we must be saved*. Not another system, not another agreement, not another mediator. One covenant, one way of salvation.

This aligns with the entire biblical witness. The $rock in 1 Cor 10:4 — "that Rock was Christ" — points to Christ as the covenant itself, the unchanging foundation. Acts 4:12 says the same thing in name-language: this is the only covenant that saves.

The early church understood "the name" in covenantal terms. To be baptized "in the name" was not a formula of pronunciation — it was an act of entering the covenant, just as $marriage involves taking a husband's name. When Paul says believers are "called by his name," the language echoes the $marriage covenant: "thy Maker is thine husband" (Isa 54:5). The bride takes the name of the bridegroom because she has entered his covenant.

### "Fear This Glorious and Fearful Name"

> *"If thou wilt not observe to do all the words of this law that are written in this book, that thou mayest fear this glorious and fearful **name**, THE LORD THY GOD…"* — Deut 28:58

Notice the structure: *do all the words of this law* → *fear this name*. The law and the name are bound together. The $bread — the covenant terms, the words of the Torah — is what gives the name its substance. To fear the name is to observe the covenant terms. To disregard the terms is to despise the name.

This is why $the-way (Torah / God's commandments) and $name (covenant) are inseparable. The Torah is the content of the covenant. The name is the covenant relationship. You cannot honor one while emptying the other.

### Believing "On His Name"

> *"But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that **believe** on his **name**."* — Jn 1:12

If $believe means to obey — to commit oneself through trusting action — and the name means covenant, then to "believe on his name" is to *commit oneself to his covenant in trusting obedience*. This is not mental acknowledgment of a title. It is covenant allegiance demonstrated by action.

The same pattern appears throughout John's gospel:

> *"He that **believeth** on him is not condemned: but he that **believeth** not is condemned already, because he hath not **believed** in the **name** of the only begotten Son of God."* — Jn 3:18

To believe in the name = to enter the covenant. To refuse belief = to remain outside it. The condemnation is not for ignorance of a pronunciation but for refusal of a covenant.

## Patterns

1. **Name substitutes for covenant.** 2 Sam 7:2 → 7:13 (ark of covenant → name). 1 Ki 8 throughout. Wherever "the name" dwells, the covenant is present. The terms are interchangeable.

2. **"In the name" = invoking covenant authority.** Prophesying, casting out demons, healing, baptizing "in the name" = acting under the covenant's authority. The power comes from the covenant relationship, not from the word spoken.

3. **"For his name's sake" = covenant obligation.** When God acts "for his name's sake" (Ps 23:3, Ezek 36:22), He acts because His covenant binds Him. His reputation is His covenant faithfulness.

4. **Taking the name = entering the covenant.** Baptism "in the name" parallels $marriage — the bride takes the bridegroom's name by entering the covenant. Israel is called by God's name (2 Chr 7:14) because they are in covenant.

5. **Profaning the name = breaking the covenant.** Lev 18:21, 19:12, 20:3, 22:2 — to profane the name is to act in ways that violate the covenant's terms, making the relationship meaningless.

6. **The name requires the law.** Deut 28:58 binds them: observe the law → fear the name. The $bread (covenant terms) and the name (covenant relationship) are inseparable. You cannot have one without the other.

7. **Workers of lawlessness prove name ≠ speech.** Mat 7:22–23 is the decisive test. People who speak the name constantly but practice $wickedness (lawlessness / covenant transgression) are rejected. The name is kept by obedience, not invocation.

## Connections

- **$rock** — The $rock is the covenant itself — unchanging, unmovable. The name is the covenant relationship. David wanted a house for the ark (covenant); God said it would be a house for His name. The $rock and the name point to the same reality: the covenant between God and His people, one emphasizing its unchanging nature, the other its relational bond.

- **$wickedness** — $wickedness is lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2). The workers of $wickedness in Mat 7:22–23 are those who invoke the name (claim the covenant) while transgressing its terms. $wickedness is the name taken in vain — the covenant emptied of its content.

- **$bread** — $bread is the Word — the covenant terms themselves. Deut 28:58 ties the two together: observe all the words of this law ($bread) → fear this name (covenant). The $bread fills the name with content; without the $bread, the name is empty.

- **$mark** — The $mark is the visible practice that identifies which covenant you belong to. The name is the covenant; the $mark is the evidence of it. God's people carry His name (covenant) and are identified by His $mark (Sabbath, commandments). Rev 14:1 — the Father's name in their foreheads parallels the $mark of God's observances.

- **$marriage** — $marriage is the covenant picture — God as husband, Israel/Church as bride. Taking a husband's name = entering his covenant. Being "called by his name" (2 Chr 7:14, Isa 4:1) = being in covenant. The $marriage metaphor shows that the name is fundamentally relational, not linguistic.

- **$the-way** — $the-way is Torah, God's commandments — the specific path of covenant living. The name is the covenant relationship; $the-way is the covenant walk. Deut 28:58 says the law and the name are bound together.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience — the active response to the covenant. "Believing on his name" = committing oneself in obedient trust to his covenant. $faith without works is dead (Jas 2:26); the name without obedience is vain (Mat 7:22–23).

- **$believe** — $believe is the verb form of $faith: to obey, to commit to obey. "Believe on his name" (Jn 1:12, 3:18) = commit yourself to his covenant. The opposite (G544 *apeitheo* — to disobey) confirms that belief in the name is covenant obedience, not mental acknowledgment.
