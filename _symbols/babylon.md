# Babylon

**Man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — the kingdom built on bricks instead of $rock, reaching for a $name instead of receiving one.**

> *"And they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven; and let us make us a **name**, lest we be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth."* — Gen 11:4

---

## The Key Insight

The first thing Babylon builds is not a tower. It is a *brick*.

> *"And they said one to another, Go to, let us make **brick**, and burn them throughly. And they had brick for **$[rock]**, and slime had they for morter."* — Gen 11:3

Genesis is careful to note the substitution: brick *for stone*. Man-made material replacing God-given material. The $rock in Scripture is the covenant — the unchanging foundation (Deut 32:4, 1 Cor 10:4, Mat 7:24–25). Babylon's founding act is to manufacture a replacement for it. Not to reject the idea of a foundation — but to make one of its own.

Then comes the tower. Then comes the $name. "Let us make us a name" — not receive one from God, but fabricate one by collective human effort. The $name in Scripture is the covenant relationship (2 Sam 7:13, Acts 4:12). Babylon builds its own.

This is the pattern that repeats across every empire, every system, every age that Scripture calls "Babylon": man reaching up to where God comes down. Man-made covenant replacing God-given covenant. A system that looks like a kingdom but is built on bricks.

**Symbolizes:** Man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — worldly kingdom

**Opposite:** $jerusalem — the covenant community that descends from God (Rev 21:2), receives a $name (Isa 62:2), and is built on $rock (Mat 16:18)

**Defining verses:** Gen 11:3–4, Isa 47:8, Dan 4:30, Rev 17:5, Rev 18:2

**The surprise:** Babylon is not just one city. Scripture uses at least five names — Babel, Shinar, Egypt, Sodom, Tyre — for the same system. Revelation 11:8 calls the place where the Lord was crucified "spiritually Sodom and Egypt." The names are functional, not geographic. Wherever the pattern appears, *that* is Babylon.

**Connected:** $jerusalem, $rock, $name, $mountain, $harlot, $fornication, $wine, $sea, $fire, $thorns, $wickedness

## Babylon Across Scripture

### One System, Five Names

Scripture does not restrict this pattern to the city on the Euphrates. The same system wears different faces:

**Shinar** (H8152) — Babylon's origin-name. Nimrod's kingdom begins in the land of Shinar (Gen 10:10). The tower is built in the plain of Shinar (Gen 11:2). And in Zechariah's vision, $wickedness personified as a woman in an ephah is carried back "to the land of **Shinar**" (Zech 5:11) — wickedness returning to its birthplace.

**Egypt** — The first national-scale Babylon pattern. Pharaoh declares "My river is mine own, and I have made it for myself" (Ezek 29:3) — the same self-deification as Babylon's "I am, and none else beside me" (Isa 47:8). Egypt enslaves God's people, and God extracts them.

**Sodom** — Babylon's moral face. Lot is extracted from Sodom before $fire falls — the same "come out of her, my people" pattern (Rev 18:4, Gen 19:12–14).

**Tyre** — Babylon's commercial face. The merchant prince who says "I am a God, I sit in the seat of God" (Ezek 28:2). The luxury trade catalog of Ezekiel 27 becomes the template for Revelation 18's description of Babylon's merchants.

> *"And their dead bodies shall lie in the street of the great city, which **spiritually** is called **Sodom** and **Egypt**, where also our Lord was crucified."* — Rev 11:8

The word "spiritually" (G4153 *pneumatikos*) is the key. These are not geographic labels. They are functional identifiers. Wherever you find the pattern — self-deification, man-made systems replacing covenant, oppression of God's people, extraction before judgment — you have found Babylon, regardless of the name on the map.

### "I Am, and None Else Beside Me"

Every Babylon-face speaks the same sentence:

> *"Thou saidst, **I shall be a lady for ever** … thou that art given to pleasures, that dwellest carelessly, that sayest in thine heart, **I am, and none else beside me**; I shall not sit as a widow, neither shall I know the loss of children."* — Isa 47:7–8

> *"The king spake, and said, **Is not this great Babylon, that I have built** for the house of the kingdom by the might of **my** power, and for the honour of **my** majesty?"* — Dan 4:30

> *"Because thine heart is lifted up, and thou hast said, **I am a God, I sit in the seat of God**, in the midst of the seas"* — Ezek 28:2 (Tyre)

> *"Pharaoh king of Egypt … which saith, **My river is mine own, and I have made it for myself**"* — Ezek 29:3 (Egypt)

The claim is always the same: *I made this. I sustain this. I am the source.* This is the language of self-deification — and it stands in direct opposition to the God who says simply "**I AM**" (Exod 3:14). Babylon borrows the syntax of deity and applies it to human achievement. The tower was the architecture; the speech is the theology.

### Brick for Stone — The Babylon/Jerusalem Contrast

The Gen 11:3 substitution opens a series of contrasts that runs through the entire Bible. Babylon and $jerusalem are built on opposing foundations at every level:

| Babylon | Jerusalem |
|---------|-----------|
| Brick (man-made) | $rock (God-given covenant) |
| "Let us make us a $name" (Gen 11:4) | God gives the $name (Isa 62:2) |
| Man reaches up | God comes down (Rev 21:2) |
| "I am, and none else" (Isa 47:8) | "I AM" — the true God (Exod 3:14) |
| $harlot — "mother of harlots" (Rev 17:5) | Bride — "prepared as a bride" (Rev 21:2) |
| $merchants weep (Rev 18:11) | Worship and offerings (Isa 2:3) |
| Falls (Rev 18:2) | Descends (Rev 21:10) |
| $thorns — cursed produce | $fruit — covenant produce |
| Enslaves | Liberates |

> *"And upon her forehead was a **name** written, MYSTERY, **BABYLON** THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF **$[harlot]S** AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH."* — Rev 17:5

> *"And I John saw the holy city, new **$[jerusalem]**, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband."* — Rev 21:2

The $harlot and the bride are not two unrelated images. They are the same metaphor from opposite sides: who do you give your allegiance to? Babylon is the $harlot system — the covenant-breaker who seduces nations away from God's rule (Rev 17:1–2). $jerusalem is the bride — the faithful covenant community. Every person and nation is, functionally, living in one or the other.

### Wickedness Returns to Shinar

Zechariah 5 contains one of the most striking prophetic images in the Old Testament, and it maps the inner mechanics of Babylon's system:

> *"Then the angel … said, This is an ephah that goeth forth … This is **$[wickedness]**. And he cast it into the midst of the ephah; and he cast the weight of lead upon the mouth thereof."* — Zech 5:6–8

> *"Then said I to the angel … Whither do these bear the ephah? And he said unto me, To build it an house in the land of **Shinar**: and it shall be established, and set there upon her own base."* — Zech 5:10–11

$wickedness — lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2, 2 Thess 2:7–8) — is personified as a woman sealed inside a commercial container (an ephah, a unit of trade measurement). She is transported to Shinar, Babylon's ancestral land, where a *house* is built for her. She is "established" and "set upon her own base."

The imagery suggests that $wickedness has a home, and that home is Babylon. The system generates lawlessness, packages it, and establishes it as a permanent institution. The commercial container (ephah) hints at what Revelation makes explicit: Babylon's $wickedness travels through trade.

### The Merchants Weep — Babylon's Commercial Engine

The most detailed portrait of Babylon in Scripture is not about armies or thrones. It is about *commerce*:

> *"And the **merchants** of the earth shall weep and mourn over her; for no man buyeth their merchandise any more."* — Rev 18:11

The inventory that follows in Rev 18:12–13 is exhaustive — gold, silver, precious stones, fine linen, silk, ivory, vessels of wood and brass, spices, wine, oil, flour, wheat, cattle, sheep, horses, chariots — and ends with the most chilling item: "slaves, and **souls of men**."

This entire passage draws directly on Ezekiel 27, the lamentation over Tyre. The same maritime destruction, the same catalog of luxury goods, the same merchants weeping at the shore:

> *"The merchants among the people shall hiss at thee; thou shalt be a terror, and never shalt be any more."* — Ezek 27:36

Tyre's prince claimed to sit "in the seat of God" (Ezek 28:2). His covering cherub was "perfect in beauty" until "iniquity was found in thee by the multitude of thy traffick" (Ezek 28:15–16, H7404 *rekullah* — trade, traffic). Commerce itself did not corrupt — but commerce became the vehicle through which self-deification spread. Trade *without covenant* is Babylon's native language.

### Fire from Within — Babylon Consumes Itself

The judgment pattern across all five Babylon-faces shares a startling feature: the system burns itself.

> *"I will bring forth a **$[fire]** from the midst of thee, it shall devour thee, and I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth."* — Ezek 28:18

The $fire that destroys Tyre does not come from outside. It comes *from the midst* — from within the system's own iniquity. This is not merely poetic. The same self-consuming pattern appears across every Babylon-figure:

> *"For **$[wickedness]** burneth as the **$[fire]**: it shall devour the **$[thorns]** and briers, and shall kindle in the thickets of the forest, and they shall mount up like the lifting up of smoke."* — Isa 9:18

$wickedness burns like $fire. It devours the $thorns — the worldly people characterized by "cares, riches, and pleasures" (Lk 8:14) who make up Babylon's population. The smoke rises exactly as it rises in Revelation:

> *"And the kings of the earth … shall see the smoke of her burning."* — Rev 18:9

Isaiah 10:17 adds: "the light of Israel shall be for a fire … and it shall burn and devour his thorns and his briers **in one day**." Compare Rev 18:8: "Therefore shall her plagues come **in one day**, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire."

The "one day" destruction. $thorns as fuel. Smoke rising. $wickedness as the fire. The prophetic template is identical across seven centuries of Scripture. Babylon generates its own $wickedness, populates itself with $thorns, and then the very lawlessness it produced becomes the fuel for its own consumption.

> *"Behold, they shall be as stubble; the **$[fire]** shall burn them; they shall not deliver themselves from the power of the flame: there shall not be a coal to warm at, nor fire to sit before it."* — Isa 47:14

Babylon's own fire will leave nothing — not even a coal to warm by. The system that manufactures warmth (false comfort, false security, false $wine) will be consumed until no warmth remains.

### The Nations Drink Her Wine

> *"For all nations have drunk of the **$[wine]** of the wrath of her **$[fornication]**, and the kings of the earth have committed **$[fornication]** with her, and the merchants of the earth are waxed rich through the abundance of her delicacies."* — Rev 18:3

$wine in Scripture is covenant blood — life poured out (Gen 49:11, Mat 26:28). But Babylon has her own $wine. It is the $wine of $fornication — the counterfeit covenant cup, the idolatrous allegiance that replaces devotion to God. Nations drink it and become intoxicated — unable to see clearly, unable to recognize that they have exchanged the true covenant for a substitute.

> *"**Babylon** hath been a golden cup in the LORD'S hand, that made all the earth drunken: the nations have drunken of her **$[wine]**; therefore the nations are mad."* — Jer 51:7

The golden cup is beautiful — Babylon's system is attractive. But the $wine inside produces madness. $fornication — idolatry, the act of serving other masters (Deut 31:16, Hos 1:2) — is how nations enter Babylon's system. They drink, they become intoxicated, they forget the covenant, and they serve the substitute.

> *"And there followed another angel, saying, **Babylon** is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the **$[wine]** of the wrath of her **$[fornication]**."* — Rev 14:8

### Come Out of Her, My People

Across every Babylon-face, the same command precedes judgment:

> *"And I heard another voice from heaven, saying, **Come out of her, my people**, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues."* — Rev 18:4

Lot is told to leave Sodom before $fire falls (Gen 19:12–14). Israel is called out of Egypt before plagues descend. Jeremiah commands: "Flee out of the midst of Babylon" (Jer 51:6). The pattern is consistent: God's people are *extracted* from the system before it consumes itself.

This command implies something uncomfortable: God's people can be *inside* Babylon. They can be participating in the system, entangled in its commerce, drinking its $wine. The call to "come out" would be meaningless if they were never in. Babylon is not merely a foreign empire across the river — it is a system that God's own covenant people can inhabit without recognizing it.

### Babylon the Mountain

> *"Behold, I am against thee, O destroying **$[mountain]**, saith the LORD, which destroyest all the earth: and I will stretch out mine hand upon thee, and roll thee down from the **$[rock]s**, and will make thee a burnt **$[mountain]**."* — Jer 51:25

God calls Babylon a $mountain — a kingdom, a seat of ruling authority (Rev 17:9, Dan 2:35). But this $mountain *destroys* all the earth. It is a counterfeit kingdom — a high place of power that exists by consuming rather than governing.

The seven $mountains on which the $harlot sits (Rev 17:9) are seven kingdoms — seats of worldly authority hosting the Babylon system across history. And when Daniel sees the stone cut "without hands" strike the statue's feet and grow into a great $mountain filling the earth (Dan 2:35, 44–45), he sees God's kingdom *replacing* every Babylon-mountain with the one true $mountain built on $rock.

## Patterns

1. **Substitution is the core action.** Brick for $rock. Man's $name for God's $name. Self-deification for worship. $fornication for faithfulness. Counterfeit $wine for covenant blood. Babylon never creates something entirely new — it replaces something God already established with a human imitation.

2. **Five names, one system.** Babel, Shinar, Egypt, Sodom, Tyre — each emphasizes a different dimension (origin, oppression, moral corruption, commerce) but all share: self-deification, $fornication, extraction of God's people, sudden $fire-judgment. Rev 11:8 proves the names are functional, not geographic.

3. **Babylon self-destructs.** The $fire comes from within (Ezek 28:18). $wickedness burns as $fire (Isa 9:18). $thorns are consumed by the system that grew them. Babylon does not need an external enemy — its own lawlessness is the fuel. "There shall not be a coal to warm at" (Isa 47:14).

4. **Commerce is the delivery mechanism.** The ephah carries $wickedness to Shinar (Zech 5:5–11). Trade corrupted the covering cherub (Ezek 28:16). Merchants weep at Babylon's fall (Rev 18:11). The system traffics in "souls of men" (Rev 18:13). Babylon's reach is commercial before it is military.

5. **The "I am" claim.** Every Babylon-figure speaks self-deification: Isa 47:8, Dan 4:30, Ezek 28:2, Ezek 29:3. This is the theological center — man claiming the authority that belongs to "I AM" alone (Exod 3:14).

6. **"Come out" precedes "fall."** Lot extracted, then Sodom burns. Israel called out, then Egypt judged. "Come out of her, my people" (Rev 18:4), then "Babylon is fallen" (Rev 18:2). The people of God are always separated before the system collapses.

7. **Babylon falls; $jerusalem descends.** The $harlot is judged (Rev 17–18) and the bride appears (Rev 21). The two cannot coexist. Babylon's collapse is not merely destruction — it clears the way for the covenant city to be established.

## Connections

- **$jerusalem** — Babylon's direct opposite. $harlot vs. bride. Falls vs. descends. Man builds up vs. God sends down. "Mother of harlots" (Rev 17:5) vs. "mother of us all" (Gal 4:26). Every attribute of Babylon is inverted in $jerusalem.

- **$rock** — What Babylon replaces. Brick *for stone* (Gen 11:3). The covenant foundation that man cannot manufacture. The stone cut without hands (Dan 2:34) that becomes the $mountain crushing every Babylon-kingdom.

- **$name** — What Babylon fabricates. "Let us make us a name" (Gen 11:4) vs. the $name God gives and places (2 Sam 7:13, 2 Chr 6:6). Taking the $name in vain — making the covenant nothing — is the essence of living in Babylon (Exod 20:7).

- **$mountain** — Babylon *as* a kingdom. Jer 51:25 explicitly calls Babylon a "destroying mountain." Rev 17:9 identifies seven mountains as seven kingdoms. God's $mountain replaces them all (Dan 2:35, Isa 2:2).

- **$harlot** — Babylon's identity. The great $harlot sits on many $waters (Rev 17:1). $fornication — idolatry, divided allegiance — is Babylon's export. The nations "commit fornication" with the system by serving its substitute covenant.

- **$wine** — Babylon's intoxicant. The $wine of $fornication (Rev 14:8, 18:3) is the counterfeit covenant cup. Nations drink and go mad (Jer 51:7). The golden cup looks beautiful; the contents produce blindness.

- **$fire** — Babylon's judgment. $fire from within (Ezek 28:18). $wickedness burns as $fire (Isa 9:18). Babylon = stubble (Isa 47:14). "In one day" (Rev 18:8, Isa 10:17). The system is consumed by its own fuel.

- **$wickedness** — Babylon's payload. $wickedness personified is carried to Shinar and established there (Zech 5:5–11). Lawlessness (G458 *anomia*) is what Babylon generates, exports, and is ultimately consumed by.

- **$thorns** — Babylon's population. $thorns = worldly people defined by "cares, riches, and pleasures" (Lk 8:14). $wickedness devours $thorns (Isa 9:18). The smoke rises (Isa 9:18, Rev 18:9). Babylon produces and then consumes its own people.

- **$sea** — Babylon's domain. The $harlot sits on many waters = "peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues" (Rev 17:15). The $sea is the chaotic mass of nations from which Babylon draws power and over which it exercises control. Beasts rise from the $sea (Dan 7:2–3, Rev 13:1).

- **$fornication** — Babylon's method. $fornication = idolatry, the act of turning from God to serve other masters (Deut 31:16). Babylon does not merely exist as a system — it actively seduces nations into divided allegiance.

## For Further Study

- **$jerusalem** — Babylon's opposite. If Babylon is man's substitute kingdom, Jerusalem is the covenant community God builds. How does the bride-city reverse every feature of the harlot-city?
- **$rock** — What Babylon replaces with brick. The unchanging covenant that man cannot manufacture. The stone cut without hands that crushes every Babylon-kingdom.
- **$fire** — Babylon's appointed end. The same $fire refines the faithful and consumes the counterfeit. How does the dual function of fire illuminate Babylon's destruction from within?
- **$wickedness** — Babylon's defining export. Lawlessness personified, sealed in a commercial container, and carried to Shinar. What does it mean that wickedness has a *home*?
- **$mountain** — Both Babylon and Jerusalem are called mountains. What distinguishes the destroying mountain from the holy mountain?
