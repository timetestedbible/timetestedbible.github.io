# Grass

**Mortal people — flourishing briefly, withering under the $wind of the LORD, and sorted by their $fruit at harvest.**

> *"The voice said, Cry. And he said, What shall I cry? All flesh is **grass**, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field: The **grass** withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people is **grass**."* — Isa 40:6–7

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture does not leave this symbol to inference. Isaiah states it twice in two verses: "All flesh is **grass**" and then, in case the reader missed it, "surely the people is **grass**."

The equation is explicit. Grass = people. Not metaphorically "like" people — *is* people. Peter quotes it into the New Testament unchanged (1 Pet 1:24), confirming the identification holds across testaments.

Once you see it, the entire landscape of Scripture shifts. When Revelation says "all green grass was burnt up" (Rev 8:7), that is not an environmental report — it is a statement about judgment on people. When Jesus says God clothes the grass of the field (Mat 6:30), he is talking about how God provides for mortal, temporary human beings. When wheat and tares grow together until harvest (Mat 13:25–30), those are two kinds of *grass* — two kinds of people — indistinguishable until the sorting comes.

And against this temporary, withering, wind-swept grass stands the one thing that does not wither: the $bread — the Word of God.

> *"The **grass** withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever."* — Isa 40:8

**Symbolizes:** Mortal people — temporary, flourishing briefly, subject to the wind/Spirit

**Opposite:** The Word of God — eternal, enduring, never withering (Isa 40:8, 1 Pet 1:25)

**Defining verses:** Isa 40:6–8, 1 Pet 1:24–25, Ps 103:15–16, Rev 8:7, Rev 9:4

**The surprise:** Wheat and tares are both grasses — both types of *people*. They grow in the same $field, look identical, and can only be distinguished at harvest by their $fruit. The sorting of grasses is the judgment of humanity.

**Connected:** $wind, $tree, $fruit, $bread, $name, $mark

## Grass Across Scripture

### "Surely the People Is Grass"

Isaiah 40 is the defining text. The prophet is told to cry, and asks what he should cry. The answer comes in a three-part structure:

> *"All flesh is **grass**, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field."* — Isa 40:6

> *"The **grass** withereth, the flower fadeth: because the spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it: surely the people is **grass**."* — Isa 40:7

> *"The **grass** withereth, the flower fadeth: but the word of our God shall stand for ever."* — Isa 40:8

Three layers: (1) All flesh = grass. (2) The Spirit/wind causes the grass to wither — and the people *are* the grass. (3) Grass withers; the Word endures.

Peter quotes this passage directly:

> *"For all flesh is as **grass**, and all the glory of man as the flower of **grass**. The **grass** withereth, and the flower thereof falleth away: But the word of the Lord endureth for ever."* — 1 Pet 1:24–25

The repetition across testaments establishes the equation beyond dispute. And notice what Peter adds: "the glory of man" is the *flower* of grass — the finest, most impressive display of human achievement is still just the blossom on a plant that is about to be cut. The flower is explored further below, but the core point is that grass and its flower together represent human beings at every level — common and elite, anonymous and glorious. All of it withers.

### The Wind That Withers

What causes the grass to wither? Isaiah 40:7 is specific: "the spirit (H7307 *ruach*) of the LORD bloweth upon it." The $wind — invisible power, the Spirit of God — passes over the grass, and it is gone.

Psalm 103 echoes the same mechanism:

> *"As for man, his days are as **grass**: as a flower of the field, so he flourisheth. For the $[wind] passeth over it, and it is **gone** (H369 *ayin*); and the place thereof shall know it no more."* — Ps 103:15–16

The Hebrew word *ayin* (H369) — "gone," nothing, no longer existing — is striking. The $wind passes over the grass and the grass becomes *nothing*. The place where it stood no longer recognizes it.

The same pattern appears in Elijah's departure: the Spirit (H7307 *ruach*) of the LORD takes Elijah up (2 Ki 2:16), and the search party cannot find him. Spirit acts on a person — the person is *gone*. The $wind that withers grass is the same invisible divine power that removes people from the earth. On the surface it looks like mortality. At a deeper level it is the sovereign action of God upon human beings.

### A Lifetime in a Single Day

> *"In the morning they are like **grass** which groweth up. In the morning it flourisheth, and groweth up; in the evening it is cut down, and withereth."* — Ps 90:5–6

The psalmist compresses an entire human life into one day of grass: sprouts in the morning, blooms by midday, cut down by evening. The image captures what every funeral confronts — the brevity of a life that seemed, while it was green, like it would last forever.

> *"Fret not thyself because of evildoers, neither be thou envious against the workers of iniquity. For they shall soon be **cut down** like the **grass**, and wither as the green herb."* — Ps 37:1–2

> *"For he remembered that they were but flesh; a $[wind] that passeth away, and cometh not again."* — Ps 78:39

> *"Man that is born of a woman, which is made as **grass**"* — Isa 51:12 (paraphrase of the pattern)

The cutting-down of grass = the death of people. This is not merely poetic comparison. It is the consistent biblical shorthand: when you see grass cut, burned, or withered, you are seeing people under judgment or mortality.

### The Flower of Grass — The Glory of Man

Within the grass symbol, the *flower* represents the finest expression — the glory, wealth, or prominence of human life. Even this fades:

> *"Let the brother of low degree rejoice in that he is exalted: But the rich, in that he is made low: because as the flower of the **grass** he shall pass away. For the sun is no sooner risen with a burning heat, but it withereth the **grass**, and the flower thereof falleth, and the grace of the fashion of it perisheth: so also shall the rich man fade away in his ways."* — Jas 1:9–11

The rich man *is* the flower of the grass. Not a different symbol — just the most impressive bloom on the same temporary plant. His wealth, his beauty, his "grace of fashion" — all of it is the flower that falls when the heat comes. James draws a direct line: "so also shall the rich man fade away."

Isaiah uses the same image: "all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field" (Isa 40:6). The word "goodliness" (H2617 *chesed* in some readings, though the context emphasizes external splendor) points to human achievement at its peak — cultural, military, economic glory. Still grass. Still temporary.

### "All Green Grass Was Burnt Up"

> *"The first angel sounded, and there followed hail and fire mingled with blood, and they were cast upon the earth: and the third part of $[tree]s was burnt up, and all green **grass** was burnt up."* — Rev 8:7

If $tree = nation/people group, and grass = mortal people, this verse reads: a third of the nations were destroyed, and *all* the common people suffered the judgment. The scale shifts — a fraction of the organized structures, but universal impact on the people within them.

The distinction between $tree and grass here is the distinction between collective entities and individual persons. A $tree can survive partial burning; grass cannot. Nations may endure in some form after judgment; the people themselves bear the full weight of it.

### "Hurt Not the Grass"

> *"And it was commanded them that they should not hurt the **grass** of the earth, neither any green thing, neither any $[tree]; but only those men which have not the seal of God in their foreheads."* — Rev 9:4

This verse is extraordinary. The locust-army from the abyss is told to spare the grass — and to hurt only the *unsealed* people. If grass = people, then "hurt not the grass" = protect God's people. The unsealed are the exception — those without the $mark of God.

Compare Ezek 9:4–6, where the man with the writer's inkhorn sets a $mark (H8420 *tav*) on the foreheads of those who grieve over the abominations, and the slayers are told: "come not near any man upon whom is the $[mark]." The pattern is the same: marked people are protected; unmarked people are exposed. The grass that is spared in Rev 9:4 is the grass that bears the seal — the people whose $mark identifies them as belonging to God through visible covenant practice.

Reading Rev 8:7 and Rev 9:4 together raises a productive tension. In Rev 8:7 all green grass burns; in Rev 9:4 the grass is protected. Different judgments, different purposes — but the symbol remains consistent. The grass is always *people*. What changes is which people and what judgment.

### Two Kinds of Grass — The Wheat and the Tares

Wheat, barley, and tares are all grasses. This botanical fact is the key to one of Jesus' most important parables:

> *"The kingdom of heaven is likened unto a man which sowed good seed in his field: But while men slept, his enemy came and sowed tares among the wheat."* — Mat 13:24–25

> *"The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one."* — Mat 13:38

Jesus interprets his own parable. The wheat = children of the kingdom. The tares = children of the wicked one. Both are grasses. Both grow in the same field. Both look nearly identical during the growing season. You cannot tell them apart until the harvest — when the $fruit reveals what they truly are.

> *"Let both grow together until the harvest: and in the time of harvest I will say to the reapers, Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn."* — Mat 13:30

The harvest sorts the grasses. Tares are bundled and burned (judgment); wheat is gathered and stored (salvation). The distinguishing factor is $fruit — works and deeds. Wheat produces nourishing grain; tares produce nothing useful. The people who look alike in life are separated by their output at the end.

Barley fits the same framework. Ruth arrives in Bethlehem "in the beginning of barley harvest" (Ruth 1:22) — the firstfruits harvest, the earliest grain. Barley = early harvest people, the firstfruits of God's redemption. The harvest of Rev 14:15–16 echoes this: "Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe."

All of these are subtypes of grass — subtypes of people — distinguished by timing and $fruit.

### God Clothes the Grass

> *"Wherefore, if God so clothe the **grass** of the field, which to day is, and to morrow is cast into the oven, shall he not much more clothe you, O ye of little faith?"* — Mat 6:30

> *"If then God so clothe the **grass**, which is to day in the field, and to morrow is cast into the oven; how much more will he clothe you, O ye of little faith?"* — Lk 12:28

Jesus' argument from lesser to greater depends on the grass being *temporary* and *insignificant* relative to his audience. God gives beauty and provision even to mortal, passing human life in general — how much more to those who trust him? The grass is "cast into the oven" — burned, consumed, ended. Yet even this temporary existence receives divine attention.

The phrase "O ye of little faith" is pointed. The grass doesn't worry about its provision. The people Jesus addresses — who *are* grass in the broader symbol — are anxious about what the grass receives without asking.

### The Great Tree Reduced to Eating Grass

> *"They shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat **grass** as oxen."* — Dan 4:25

Nebuchadnezzar's dream presents him as a great $tree (Dan 4:10–12) — a mighty nation/empire sheltering all peoples. When that $tree is cut down in judgment, the king is reduced to eating grass like a beast. If grass = people, the image suggests the empire that once contained and organized peoples is now reduced to the level of common humanity — consuming what it once ruled over, reduced from $tree-status to grass-status.

The progression is: great $tree (empire) → cut down → eats grass (lives among common people as a beast). The symbolic demotion mirrors the spiritual reality: Nebuchadnezzar loses his reason and his throne until he acknowledges that "the most High ruleth in the kingdom of men" (Dan 4:32).

## Patterns

1. **Grass = people is stated, not inferred.** Isaiah 40:7 says it outright: "surely the people is grass." Peter confirms it (1 Pet 1:24). No other symbolic identification in Scripture is more explicit.

2. **The $wind withers the grass.** Isa 40:7, Ps 103:16 — the Spirit/wind of the LORD passes over people, and they are gone. The invisible divine power acts on mortal humanity, and mortality is the result.

3. **Grass withers; the $bread endures.** The defining antithesis: people are temporary, the Word of God is eternal (Isa 40:8, 1 Pet 1:25). Every grass reference implicitly invokes this contrast.

4. **The flower = the glory of the grass.** The most impressive human achievement is still just the bloom on a withering plant (Isa 40:6, Jas 1:10–11). Elite and common alike are grass.

5. **Types of grass = types of people.** Wheat = children of the kingdom. Tares = children of the wicked one. Barley = firstfruits. All grasses, all people, distinguished only at harvest by their $fruit.

6. **Burned grass = judged people.** Rev 8:7 (all green grass burnt), Ps 37:2 (cut down like grass), Mat 13:30 (tares burned). The destruction of grass is consistently the judgment of people.

7. **Protected grass = sealed people.** Rev 9:4 — the grass is spared; only the unsealed are hurt. The $mark of God distinguishes which grass is protected and which is exposed.

8. **$tree reduced to grass = empire reduced to common humanity.** Dan 4:25–33 — Nebuchadnezzar's fall from great $tree to grass-eater enacts the demotion from national power to mortal frailty.

## Connections

- **$wind** — The invisible power that withers the grass: "the spirit of the LORD bloweth upon it" (Isa 40:7). $wind = Spirit / invisible power. The Spirit acts on mortal people, and mortality is the result. The same *ruach* that gives life (Ezek 37:9) also takes it — the grass flourishes or withers at the Spirit's passage.

- **$tree** — Grass and $tree appear together in Rev 8:7 and Dan 4:10–33. $tree = nation / people group; grass = individual mortal people within or beneath those nations. A third of the $trees burn, but *all* the grass burns — the collective structures partially survive, but the people bear the full weight. Nebuchadnezzar as $tree, cut down and eating grass, enacts the same distinction.

- **$fruit** — What distinguishes one grass from another. Wheat and tares are both grasses, indistinguishable until the $fruit — works and deeds — reveals their nature at harvest (Mat 13:38). Good $fruit = children of the kingdom; no $fruit = children of the wicked one (Mat 7:16–20).

- **$bread** — The antithesis of grass. Grass withers; the $bread — the Word of God, the covenant terms — endures forever (Isa 40:8, 1 Pet 1:25). People are temporary; God's Word is not. The entire Isa 40 passage sets mortal humanity against the eternal Word.

- **$name** — In Rev 14:1, those with the Father's $name in their foreheads are the protected ones — contrasted with those bearing the beast's $mark. The grass that survives judgment (Rev 9:4) is the grass in covenant relationship ($name) with God.

- **$mark** — The seal that separates protected grass from exposed grass. Rev 9:4 spares the grass but hurts the unsealed. Ezek 9:4–6 marks the grieving remnant for protection. The $mark — the observable practice that identifies whose authority a person lives under — determines which grass is cut and which is spared.

## For Further Study

- **$wind** — The invisible power that withers the grass and raises the dead. What does it mean that the same *ruach* scatters and gathers, kills and resurrects?
- **$tree** — If grass is individual people and trees are nations, how does Scripture use their interaction — burning, sheltering, cutting down — to map the relationship between persons and peoples?
- **$fruit** — The only thing that distinguishes wheat from tares. How does Scripture define the works and deeds that reveal what kind of grass you are?
- **$bread** — The eternal counterpart to temporary grass. If grass = people and $bread = the Word of God, what does it mean to "eat" the Word — and what happens to the grass that does?
- **$mark** — The seal that separates protected grass from exposed grass. What are the observable practices that identify which authority's grass you are?
