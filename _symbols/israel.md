# Israel

**God's covenant people — defined from the beginning by character and covenant, not by bloodline alone.**

> *"Thy name shall be called no more Jacob, but **Israel**: for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed."* — Gen 32:28

---

## The Key Insight

Strong's own definition flags it: H3478 = "a **symbolical** name of Jacob; also (**typically**) of his posterity." The dictionary itself marks this name as carrying symbolic weight beyond the literal.

The name is given for CHARACTER: persistence in contending with God, prevailing despite injury, and refusing to let go until blessed (Gen 32:24-28). Hosea adds that the prevailing included weeping and supplication (Hos 12:3-4) — not combat victory, but persistent prayer through tears.

What often goes unnoticed: the Torah builds an inclusion mechanism into the very first Passover. A "mixed multitude" left Egypt with Israel (Exo 12:38). The stranger who keeps Passover and is circumcised becomes "as one born in the land" — under "one law" for homeborn and stranger (Exo 12:48-49, Num 15:15). This isn't a later accommodation. It's foundational.

**Symbolizes:** God's covenant people — those who contend with God and prevail; an identity defined by covenant, joinable from the beginning

**Opposite:** The nations outside covenant; Esau, who despised his birthright (Gen 25:34)

**Connected:** $jacob, $church-ekklesia, $tree, $rock, $name

## Israel Across Scripture

### Inclusion from the Beginning

The pattern of non-descendants joining Israel runs from the Torah through the NT:

- Exo 12:38 — mixed multitude leaves Egypt WITH Israel
- Exo 12:48-49 — stranger who keeps Passover = "as one born in the land"; ONE LAW
- Num 15:15 — "as ye are, so shall the stranger be before the LORD"
- Ruth 1:16 — Moabitess: "thy people my people, thy God my God" — enters the messianic line (Mat 1:5)
- Josh 14:14 — Caleb the Kenizzite inherits in Israel for covenant faithfulness
- Isa 56:3,6-7 — stranger who joins the LORD and keeps sabbath receives a $[name] "better than sons and daughters"
- Ezek 47:22-23 — strangers receive inheritance BY LOT among the tribes, "as born in the country"
- Esth 8:17 — "many of the people of the land became Jews"
- Rom 11:17 — Gentiles grafted into Israel's olive $[tree]

This is not a NT innovation. The Torah provides the mechanism at the founding event.

### God's Firstborn Son — and the Man Child

> *"**Israel** is my son, even my firstborn."* — Exo 4:22

> *"When **Israel** was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt."* — Hos 11:1

God identifies Israel as His firstborn son — relational, covenantal language. Not merely a political entity but a child God taught to walk (Hos 11:3), a son called out of Egypt. Compare Jer 31:9: "I am a father to Israel, and $ephraim is my firstborn" — the firstborn-within-the-firstborn.

This "son" language connects to a striking image in Isaiah and Revelation. Isa 66:7-8 describes Zion delivering a $man-child (H2145 *zakar*) BEFORE travail comes — and then immediately interprets: "shall a **nation** be born at once? for as soon as Zion travailed, she brought forth her children." The $man-child = a nation born from Zion. Rev 12:5 picks up the same image: a $man-child "who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron" and is "caught up unto God, and to his throne." The rod-of-iron promise also appears in Ps 2:7-9 ("Thou art my Son... I shall give thee the nations") and is given to the overcomers in Rev 2:26-27.

The thread: Israel = God's $firstborn son (Exo 4:22) → the $man-child born from Zion = a nation (Isa 66:7-8) → rules with rod of iron (Rev 12:5, Ps 2:9) → the same authority promised to overcomers (Rev 2:26-27). The "son" is both collective (a nation) and embodied (in the one who fulfills it). The connection between "Israel is my son" and the $man-child of Rev 12 is worth tracing carefully.

### The Servant — Israel at Two Levels

Isa 49:3 — "Thou art my servant, O **Israel**, in whom I will be glorified." But Isa 49:5-6 says the servant's mission is "to bring $[jacob] again to him" and "to raise up the tribes of $[jacob]." The servant IS Israel AND is sent TO Israel.

This resolves naturally once you see that Israel operates on multiple levels simultaneously. The covenant identity (Israel) can be embodied in a faithful remnant — or ultimately in one person — who then restores the rest of the people to what the name means. The servant IS the identity and carries the mission of calling the people back to that identity. It's not a contradiction — it's how covenant identity works: the faithful within Israel restore the whole to what "Israel" was always meant to be.

### Jacob and Israel in Parallel

Isaiah consistently pairs the two names in Hebrew poetic parallelism:

- "Israel my servant, $[jacob] whom I have chosen" (Isa 41:8)
- "$[jacob] my servant, Israel whom I have chosen" (Isa 44:1)
- "For $[jacob] my servant's sake, and Israel mine elect" (Isa 45:4)

Note: in Isa 41:8, Israel = servant and Jacob = chosen. In Isa 44:1, the assignments swap. This confirms synonymous parallelism — the two names are used interchangeably, not to mark a consistent flesh/spirit distinction. (See the $jacob study for the full evidence.)

### "Not All Israel Which Are of Israel"

Paul's most direct statement on the identity question:

> *"They are not all **Israel**, which are of Israel: neither, because they are the seed of Abraham, are they all children: but, In Isaac shall thy seed be called. That is, they which are the children of the flesh, these are not the children of God: but the children of the promise are counted for the seed."* — Rom 9:6-8

Paul's examples (Isaac/Ishmael, $[jacob]/Esau) are WITHIN the lineage — God choosing which descendants. The text clearly states: flesh alone does not equal Israel; the promise is what counts. Compare Rom 2:28-29: "he is not a Jew which is one outwardly... but he is a Jew which is one inwardly."

### The Tree Is Israel's

> *"Thou bearest not the root, but the root thee."* — Rom 11:18

Paul's olive $tree metaphor (Rom 11:17-24): Gentile believers are grafted INTO Israel's tree. Natural branches can be broken off; wild branches grafted in. But the tree remains Israel's. Gentiles join it — they don't replace it with a new one.

Eph 2:12 confirms: Gentiles were "aliens from the COMMONWEALTH of Israel, strangers from the covenants of promise." The remedy: brought near by Christ's blood (v.13) — not given a separate structure.

Since the $church-ekklesia IS the qahal (Acts 7:38), the "church" Gentiles enter is Israel's assembly. (See the Church/Ekklesia study for the full word-chain.)

### An Israelite Indeed

> *"Behold an **Israelite** indeed, in whom is no guile!"* — Jn 1:47

Jesus sees Nathanael and praises him as a genuine Israelite. The marker: "no guile" (G1388 *dolos* = deceit). Recall: $jacob's defining trait before the name change was guile — deception, supplanting (Gen 27:35-36). An "Israelite indeed" is one who has completed the Jacob-to-Israel transformation. Jesus defines genuineness by character, and says it of a man who is also a physical Israelite.

### The Permanent Covenant

> *"If those ordinances depart from before me, saith the LORD, then the seed of **Israel** also shall cease from being a nation before me for ever."* — Jer 31:36

God ties Israel's permanence to the sun, moon, and stars. The covenant cannot be revoked (Rom 11:1-2: "God hath NOT cast away his people"). But the question of WHO constitutes "the seed of Israel" is addressed throughout the NT — children of promise, not flesh alone (Rom 9:8).

### Twelve Tribes: Permanent Architecture

The twelve-tribe structure persists from Elijah to Revelation:

- 1 Ki 18:31 — Elijah builds an altar with twelve stones "according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob, unto whom the word of the LORD came, saying, Israel shall be thy name"
- Mat 19:28 — disciples sit on twelve thrones "judging the twelve tribes of Israel"
- Rev 7:4 — 144,000 sealed from "all the tribes of the children of Israel"
- Rev 21:12 — New Jerusalem's twelve gates bear the names of the twelve tribes

The framework is structural, not merely historical. Ezek 47:22-23 places strangers' inheritance WITHIN this tribal framework — the architecture includes non-descendants by design.

## Connections

- **$jacob** — The pre-transformation name. Isaiah uses both names interchangeably in poetic parallelism. Both appear in positive and negative contexts. God permanently identifies Himself as "God of Jacob" (Exo 3:15).

- **$church-ekklesia** — G1577 ekklesia IS the LXX rendering of H6951 qahal. Acts 7:38 calls Israel at Sinai "the ekklesia in the wilderness." The "church" is Israel's assembly — same word, same inclusion mechanism (Exo 12:48), same one-law principle (Num 15:15).

- **$ephraim** — The scattered firstborn within Israel (Jer 31:9). His seed = "fullness of nations" (Gen 48:19). Separated from $judah; reunited in Ezek 37.

- **$judah** — The sceptre and lawgiver line (Gen 49:10). After exile, "Jew" (from Judah) became the identity term for all Israelites. Complementary to Ephraim: Judah rules, Ephraim inherits fruitfulness.

- **$tree** — Israel IS the olive tree in Rom 11. Nations are trees (Ezek 31). Israel's tree is the one into which Gentiles graft.

- **$man-child** — "Israel is my son, my firstborn" (Exo 4:22) connects to Isa 66:7-8, where the man child born from Zion IS a nation. Rev 12:5's man child rules with a rod of iron — the same promise given to overcomers (Rev 2:26-27). The firstborn son and the man child may trace the same thread.

- **$firstborn** — Israel = God's firstborn (Exo 4:22). $ephraim = God's firstborn within Israel (Jer 31:9). The ekklesia = "ekklesia of the firstborn" (Heb 12:23). Firstborn-status layers across all three.

- **$egypt** — The bondage-system Israel is called OUT of. "When Israel was a child... called my son out of Egypt" (Hos 11:1). Jesus walks the same path (Mat 2:15). The Exodus is Israel's origin story — and the pattern repeats in Rev 18:4 ("come out of her, my people"). Egypt's root (H6696) also produces $rock — same confinement, opposite function.
