# Moment

**The sudden falling of divine judgment — the instant God's evaluation results in swift, irreversible action.**

> *"In a **moment** shall they die, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away: and the mighty shall be taken away without hand."* — Job 34:20

---

## The Key Insight

Read every verse in Scripture where "moment" appears, and notice what *happens* in the moment: people are consumed, cities are overthrown, the wicked are destroyed, nations fall into desolation. The verbs that accompany "moment" are not neutral. They are judgment verbs — consume, destroy, die, overthrow, desolate. The word does not merely describe a unit of time. It imports the *event* of divine reckoning into whatever passage it enters.

Even where "moment" describes something brief and passing — God's anger, a temporary forsaking — it is still *judgment* that is brief. The contrast is always the same: the moment of judgment is short; God's mercy is everlasting. The secondary use does not undermine the meaning. It depends on it.

**Symbolizes:** Sudden divine judgment — the instant God's evaluation falls as swift action

**Opposite:** Everlasting — the enduring character of God's covenant mercy, which outlasts every moment of reckoning

**Defining verses:** Num 16:21, Job 34:20, Ps 73:19, Isa 47:9, 1 Cor 15:52

**The surprise:** The one New Testament use that seems unrelated — "in a **moment**, in the twinkling of an eye" (1 Cor 15:52) — is not an exception. It is the ultimate moment of judgment: the dead are raised, the living are changed, and the corruptible puts on incorruption. Judgment falls — but for those in Christ, the verdict is transformation, not destruction.

**Connected:** $rock, $sand, $truth, $sleep

## Moment Across Scripture

### Consumed in a Moment — The Dominant Pattern

The first appearance of "moment" in Scripture sets the tone for every one that follows:

> *"And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying, Separate yourselves from among this congregation, that I may **consume** them in a **moment**."* — Num 16:21

Korah's rebellion. The earth opens. Fire comes from the LORD. The judgment is total and instant. And the same phrase is repeated when the congregation murmurs against Moses and Aaron:

> *"Get you up from among this congregation, that I may **consume** them as in a **moment**."* — Num 16:45

The word is H7281 *rega* — a wink, an instant, a twinkling. But notice what fills the instant: *consumption*. The moment is not an empty interval. It is loaded with divine action.

This pattern repeats with striking consistency across the Old Testament:

> *"In a **moment** shall they **die**, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away: and the mighty shall be taken away without hand."* — Job 34:20

> *"How are they brought into **desolation**, as in a **moment**! they are utterly consumed with terrors."* — Ps 73:19

> *"But these two things shall come to thee in a **moment** in one day, the **loss of children**, and **widowhood**: they shall come upon thee in their perfection."* — Isa 47:9

> *"My tabernacles are spoiled, and all my cords are broken… for all the land is **spoiled**: suddenly are my tents spoiled, and my curtains in a **moment**."* — Jer 4:20

> *"For the punishment of the iniquity of the daughter of my people is greater than the punishment of the sin of Sodom, that was **overthrown** as in a **moment**, and no hands stayed on her."* — Lam 4:6

Consumed. Die. Desolation. Loss. Spoiled. Overthrown. The verbs tell you what "moment" means. A secular reader sees "brief duration." Scripture sees *the falling of judgment*.

### Sodom's Moment and Zion's Moment

Lam 4:6 makes a comparison worth pausing over. The punishment of Jerusalem is said to be *greater* than the punishment of Sodom — "that was overthrown as in a moment, and no hands stayed on her." Sodom's destruction was instantaneous. No human hands participated; no siege, no army, no prolonged suffering. Fire fell, and Sodom was gone.

Jerusalem's judgment, by contrast, was prolonged — famine, siege, the horrors described throughout Lamentations. The moment of Sodom's overthrow is held up as the *merciful* version of judgment. Swift and total. The absence of a moment — drawn-out suffering — is worse.

This suggests something about the nature of divine judgment in Scripture: the moment is not cruelty. It is finality. It is God's reckoning arriving without delay, without negotiation, without the slow erosion of drawn-out consequence. When the evaluation is complete, the verdict executes instantly.

### The Wicked Prosper — Until the Moment

Psalm 73 is the clearest window into how "moment" functions symbolically. Asaph wrestles with the prosperity of the wicked:

> *"For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked."* — Ps 73:3

Their ease seems permanent. Their arrogance seems untroubled. But then Asaph enters the sanctuary and understands their *end*:

> *"Surely thou didst set them in slippery places: thou castedst them down into destruction. How are they brought into **desolation**, as in a **moment**! they are utterly consumed with terrors."* — Ps 73:18–19

The word "slippery places" evokes $sand — shifting ground that cannot hold when the weight of judgment arrives. The wicked stand on what seems solid but is not. And when the moment comes, the collapse is not gradual. It is instantaneous. Desolation *as in a moment*. The "as in" (H7281 preceded by the comparative *ke*) treats the moment as a *category* of event. Their ruin belongs to the class of things that happen in the blink of an eye.

This is the symbolic import: the moment is not about duration. It is about the *kind* of event — sudden divine reckoning that exposes what was always true but hidden.

### Elihu's Verdict — Without Hand

Job 34:20 adds a critical detail:

> *"In a **moment** shall they die, and the people shall be troubled at midnight, and pass away: and the mighty shall be **taken away without hand**."*

"Without hand" — no human agency. The moment of judgment is divine in origin. No army, no executioner, no earthly cause. This echoes Lam 4:6 ("no hands stayed on her") and Daniel's stone "cut out without hands" (Dan 2:34). When the moment falls, it is God acting directly.

The timing is also significant: "at midnight." The people are in $sleep — unaware, unconscious, spiritually dead to what is about to happen. The moment arrives when those under judgment are least prepared. $sleep represents death and spiritual unawareness; the moment is what breaks that unawareness with finality.

### Babylon's Moment — Isa 47:9

> *"But these two things shall come to thee in a **moment** in one day, the loss of children, and widowhood: they shall come upon thee in their perfection for the multitude of thy sorceries, and for the great abundance of thine enchantments."* — Isa 47:9

Babylon — the enthroned harlot who says "I shall not sit as a widow" (Isa 47:8) — receives both childlessness and widowhood "in a moment in one day." The doubling ("in a moment in one day") emphasizes the compression: what should take a lifetime of loss arrives all at once.

"In their perfection" — the losses come *complete*. Not partial. Not gradual. The judgment arrives fully formed, as though it had been building unseen and was released in a single instant.

This is how judgment works throughout Scripture. It accumulates invisibly while mercy waits, and then it falls. The moment is the falling.

### "For a Small Moment" — Judgment Contrasted with Mercy

Two passages use "moment" to describe God's own actions toward His people, and both explicitly contrast the moment with something everlasting:

> *"For his anger endureth but a **moment**; in his favour is life: weeping may endure for a night, but joy cometh in the morning."* — Ps 30:5

> *"For a small **moment** have I forsaken thee; but with great mercies will I gather thee. In a little wrath I hid my face from thee for a **moment**; but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on thee, saith the LORD thy Redeemer."* — Isa 54:7–8

These are not exceptions to the pattern — they are its most profound application. The moment is still *judgment*: anger, forsaking, hidden face, wrath. But the judgment is measured — "a small moment," "for a moment" — while the mercy that follows is **everlasting**.

The structure is deliberate: **moment** stands opposite **everlasting**. This is the defining contrast. God's judgment is real but bounded. God's mercy is without end. The moment falls and passes. The covenant endures.

This is where $rock enters the picture. The $rock is the unchanging covenant — what outlasts every moment of judgment. What is built on the $rock survives the moment. What is built on $sand does not. Ps 30:5 and Isa 54:7–8 are the testimony of those standing on the $rock after the moment has passed: the judgment was brief; the mercy is forever.

### The Last Moment — 1 Cor 15:52

> *"In a **moment**, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed."* — 1 Cor 15:52

The Greek is G823 *atomos* — literally, indivisible. The smallest possible unit. From *a-* (not) + *temnō* (to cut). This is the moment that *cannot be divided further*. It is paired with "the twinkling of an eye" — the time it takes for light to flash across a pupil. Two expressions of irreducible brevity, stacked for emphasis.

And what happens in this indivisible instant? The dead rise. The living are changed. The corruptible puts on incorruption. The mortal puts on immortality. This is judgment — the final evaluation that separates the perishable from the imperishable.

But for those in Christ, the moment of judgment is not destruction. It is *transformation*. The same sudden divine action that consumed Korah, overthrew Sodom, and brought Babylon to widowhood — in this final instance, it *changes* the faithful into what they were always meant to be. The moment falls on the $rock, and what is built there does not collapse. It is perfected.

Paul frames this moment against the same contrast found throughout the Old Testament:

> *"For our light affliction, which is but for a **moment**, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory."* — 2 Cor 4:17

Light affliction → for a moment. Glory → eternal. The pattern holds: the moment is the judgment; the everlasting is the covenant mercy beyond it. Paul is not minimizing suffering. He is placing it in the symbolic framework Scripture has built from Num 16 forward: the moment of divine reckoning is real, but it is bounded. What follows it — for those who endure — is without measure.

## Patterns

1. **Judgment verbs fill the moment.** Consume (Num 16:21, 45), die (Job 34:20), desolation (Ps 73:19), loss and widowhood (Isa 47:9), spoiled (Jer 4:20), overthrown (Lam 4:6), changed (1 Cor 15:52). The moment is not empty time — it is divine action.

2. **The moment falls "without hand."** Job 34:20, Lam 4:6 — no human agency, no earthly mechanism. The moment is God acting directly.

3. **The moment arrives during $sleep.** Job 34:20 — "at midnight." The spiritually unaware are overtaken. Those who watch and are sober (1 Thess 5:6) are not caught in the moment as in destruction.

4. **Moment stands opposite everlasting.** Ps 30:5 (moment of anger vs. lifetime of favour), Isa 54:7–8 (small moment vs. everlasting kindness), 2 Cor 4:17 (momentary affliction vs. eternal glory). The contrast defines both terms: judgment is bounded; covenant mercy is not.

5. **What is built on $rock survives the moment.** The $rock is the unchanging covenant. What is built on $sand — the multitudes at the covenant boundary who never enter — is swept away when the moment falls. The parable of Mat 7:24–27 dramatizes the same event: the storm comes suddenly; only the house on the $rock stands.

6. **The final moment transforms rather than destroys.** 1 Cor 15:52 — the same pattern of sudden divine action, but the verdict for those in Christ is resurrection, not consumption. The moment is always judgment; the *outcome* depends on one's standing in the covenant.

## Connections

- **$rock** — The unchanging covenant that outlasts every moment of judgment. Ps 30:5 and Isa 54:7–8 are the testimony of the $rock-dweller: the moment passed, but the covenant held. What endures beyond the moment is what was built on the $rock.

- **$sand** — The multitudes at the covenant boundary who have not been bound to the $rock. When the moment falls, what is built on $sand collapses instantly (Mat 7:26–27). Ps 73:18 — "slippery places" that give way "as in a moment." $sand = what does not survive the moment of reckoning.

- **$truth** — God's commandment, the standard by which the moment evaluates. The moment is not arbitrary destruction — it is the execution of divine $truth. Babylon falls "for the multitude of thy sorceries" (Isa 47:9). Jerusalem is overthrown for iniquity (Lam 4:6). The $truth is the measuring line; the moment is the verdict.

- **$sleep** — Death and spiritual unawareness, the state of those overtaken by the moment. Job 34:20 — judgment falls "at midnight." 1 Thess 5:2–6 connects the day of the Lord coming "as a thief in the night" to those who $sleep. The moment is not a surprise to those who watch; it is a surprise to those who $sleep.
