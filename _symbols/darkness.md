# Darkness

**Concealed disobedience — the state of hiding one's conduct from scrutiny.**

> *"And this is the condemnation, that $[light] is come into the world, and men loved **darkness** rather than $[light], because their deeds were evil. For every one that doeth evil hateth the $[light], neither cometh to the $[light], lest his deeds should be reproved."* — Jn 3:19–20

---

## The Key Insight

The passage above is not about ignorance. The people in it *know* the $light is there. They have seen it. They are not in the dark because they cannot find a lamp — they are in the dark because they do not want to be seen.

That distinction — between *not knowing* and *not wanting to be known* — is the key to every appearance of darkness in Scripture. Darkness is not primarily a lack of information. It is a state of **concealment**. It is what happens when a person, a people, or a power hides its conduct from the scrutiny that $light brings.

The very next verse confirms this by describing the opposite: "But he that doeth $[truth] cometh to the $[light], that his deeds may be **made manifest**" (Jn 3:21). The one who keeps God's standard ($truth = His commandments) *comes into the open*. He wants his deeds visible. He is not hiding anything.

Darkness hides. $light reveals. That is the axis on which every other use of the word turns.

**Symbolizes:** Concealed disobedience — hiding one's conduct, doing in secret what one would not do in the open

**Opposite:** $light — visible obedience, example that can be seen and followed. Where darkness conceals, $light makes manifest.

**Defining verses:** Jn 3:19–21, Eph 5:11–13, 1 Jn 1:5–7, Job 34:22, 1 Cor 4:5

**The surprise:** The most common assumption is that darkness means ignorance or abstract evil. But Scripture consistently defines it by what it *does* — it **hides**. Even God's judgment on those in darkness is described as *exposure*: "The Lord will bring to light the hidden things of darkness" (1 Cor 4:5). The remedy for darkness is not education. It is uncovering.

**Connected:** $light, $truth, $wickedness, $way, $fruit, $name, $night

## Darkness Across Scripture

### What God Is Not

> *"This then is the message which we have heard of him, and declare unto you, that God is $[light], and in him is no **darkness** at all."* — 1 Jn 1:5

The letter opens with a definition. God is $light — visible obedience, the perfect example of His own standard. And there is no darkness in Him *at all*. No hidden agenda, no concealed motive, no gap between what He commands and what He does.

This is not a statement about luminosity. It is about transparency. God operates entirely in the open. His $truth — His commandments, His standard — is fully visible. Nothing is concealed.

The next two verses draw out the implication for human beings:

> *"If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in **darkness**, we lie, and do not the $[truth]: But if we walk in the $[light], as he is in the $[light], we have fellowship one with another."* — 1 Jn 1:6–7

Walking in darkness while *claiming* fellowship with God is called a **lie**. And notice the parallel: walking in darkness = "do not the $truth." Since $truth throughout Scripture points to God's commandments (Ps 119:142 — "Thy law is the truth"), this passage equates darkness with concealed failure to keep God's standard. It is not merely being mistaken about God. It is *claiming* to walk with Him while hiding disobedience.

### The Mechanism: Darkness Hides, Light Exposes

The clearest passage on how darkness functions is Eph 5:11–13:

> *"And have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of **darkness**, but rather reprove them. For it is a shame even to speak of those things which are done of them in secret. But all things that are reproved are made manifest by the $[light]: for whatsoever doth make manifest is $[light]."* — Eph 5:11–13

Three details reveal darkness's nature:

1. **"Works of darkness"** — Darkness produces $fruit (works/deeds). These are called *unfruitful* — not because they produce nothing, but because they produce nothing of value. They are the opposite of the "$fruit of the Spirit" and the "good works" that $light makes visible (Mat 5:16).

2. **"Done of them in secret"** — The defining characteristic of these works is that they are *hidden*. It is shameful even to describe them openly. Darkness is the environment in which shameful things can exist precisely because no one is looking.

3. **"Made manifest by the light"** — The remedy is not instruction but *exposure*. $light does not teach darkness a lesson; it makes what was hidden *visible*. This is the functional relationship: darkness conceals, $light reveals.

Paul's instruction is active: "reprove them." The command is not "avoid" or "ignore" but *bring them into the open*. The act of exposure itself is an act of $light.

### No Shadow Deep Enough

> *"There is no **darkness**, nor shadow of death, where the workers of iniquity may hide themselves."* — Job 34:22

Elihu makes the point absolute: darkness cannot ultimately accomplish its purpose. Those who work iniquity *seek* darkness as cover, but no darkness is opaque enough to conceal them from God. The verse assumes that hiding is what darkness is *for* — and declares that it fails before the divine eye.

Paul echoes this in 1 Cor 4:5:

> *"Therefore judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who both will bring to $[light] the hidden things of **darkness**, and will make manifest the counsels of the hearts."* — 1 Cor 4:5

The "hidden things of darkness" are not demonic mysteries. They are the concealed counsels of human hearts — motives, intentions, deeds kept in the dark. The Lord's coming is described as an act of *uncovering*. The darkness that hides human conduct will be stripped away.

Daniel 2:22 adds a further dimension: "He revealeth the deep and secret things: he knoweth what is in the **darkness**, and the $[light] dwelleth with him." God is not baffled by darkness. He sees through it because $light — visible, transparent, open obedience — *dwells* with Him. His nature is antithetical to concealment.

### David's Secret — The Logic of Darkness and Exposure

A narrative example makes the pattern concrete. After David's sin with Bathsheba, the prophet Nathan delivers God's verdict:

> *"For thou didst it secretly: but I will do this thing before all $[israel], and before the sun."* — 2 Sam 12:12

David operated in darkness — "secretly" (H5643 *sether* — in the hiding place, covertly). The adultery, the murder of Uriah, the cover-up — all done where they could not be seen. God's response is not private correction but *public exposure*: "before all Israel, and before the sun." The sun — the most visible source of $light in the physical world — becomes the instrument of judgment against concealment.

The same pattern appears in 2 Ki 17:9, describing Israel's decline: "And the children of Israel did **secretly** those things that were not right against the LORD their God." The nation's descent into judgment began not with open rebellion but with *concealed* departure from the covenant. They hid their $wickedness (covenant transgression) — and that hiding *is* the darkness.

### The Way of the Wicked

> *"The $[way] of the wicked is as **darkness**: they know not at what they stumble."* — Prov 4:19

The $way represents conduct — one's manner of life. The $way of the $wicked is *as darkness*: their entire pattern of conduct is characterized by concealment. And here we see darkness's secondary effect: blindness. "They know not at what they stumble."

This is important. Darkness does produce ignorance — but ignorance is the *result*, not the meaning. The person who hides long enough loses the ability to see. First they choose not to come into the $light; eventually they cannot find the $light. The concealment comes first; the confusion follows.

The contrast makes this clear:

> *"But the path of the just is as the shining $[light], that shineth more and more unto the perfect day."* — Prov 4:18

The just person's $way grows brighter — increasingly visible, increasingly an example. The wicked person's $way grows darker — increasingly hidden, increasingly disoriented. Two trajectories, defined by the willingness to be seen.

### "Your Hour, and the Power of Darkness"

> *"Then Jesus said unto the chief priests and captains of the temple, and the elders, which were come to him, Be ye come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves? When I was daily with you in the temple, ye stretched forth no hands against me: but this is your hour, and the power of **darkness**."* — Lk 22:53

Jesus identifies what is happening at His arrest with precision. These men did not confront Him in the temple, in daylight, when the crowds could see. They came at $night, with weapons, in secret. The concealment *is* the darkness. He names it: "the power of darkness" — the authority that operates by hiding, that does in secret what it would not do in the open.

This is why darkness is consistently associated with Satan's domain (Acts 26:18, Col 1:13). The adversary's fundamental strategy is concealment — not ignorance, not mere evil, but the *hiding* of evil so that it cannot be identified, confronted, or reproved. The authority structures of $wickedness rely on darkness — on keeping their operations out of $light.

### From Darkness to Light — The Turning

> *"To open their eyes, and to turn them from **darkness** to $[light], and from the power of Satan unto God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins."* — Acts 26:18

Paul's commission, received from the risen Christ, is described as a *turning*. Not merely learning new information, but reorienting from concealment to visibility, from hidden disobedience to open obedience.

The parallel structure equates:
- **Darkness** ↔ power of Satan
- **$light** ↔ God

Turning from darkness to $light is not gaining new knowledge. It is coming out of hiding. It is the move from concealed $wickedness (lawlessness) to visible $truth (keeping God's commandments).

Colossians 1:13 describes the same event from God's side: "Who hath delivered us from the power of **darkness**, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son." Darkness is a *domain* — a jurisdiction characterized by concealment. Salvation is a transfer of jurisdiction: from the hidden to the visible, from the covered to the open.

### The Command: Cast It Off

> *"The $[night] is far spent, the day is at hand: let us therefore cast off the works of **darkness**, and let us put on the armour of $[light]."* — Rom 13:12

The works of darkness are something worn — like clothing. And the instruction is to *strip them off*. This is active. Darkness is not just a condition that happens to a person; it is something a person *does*, something they *carry*, and something they can *remove*.

The same passage continues with what putting on $light looks like: "Let us walk honestly, as in the day" (Rom 13:13). "Honestly" (G2156 *euschemonos*) = decently, becomingly — in a $way that can withstand being seen. Walking in the day means conducting oneself as if everyone is watching — because in God's economy, they are.

### Darkness Blinds

> *"But he that hateth his brother is in **darkness**, and walketh in **darkness**, and knoweth not whither he goeth, because that **darkness** hath blinded his eyes."* — 1 Jn 2:11

John describes a progression: *is* in darkness → *walks* in darkness → *does not know* where he is going → *has been blinded* by darkness. The concealment that begins as a choice becomes a condition. The hiding calcifies into blindness.

Psalm 82:5 describes the same trajectory on a corporate scale: "They know not, neither will they understand; they walk on in **darkness**: all the foundations of the earth are out of course." When those in authority walk in darkness — operating by concealment, hiding their $way from scrutiny — the very foundations of social order collapse. Concealed disobedience at the top produces instability for everyone.

> *"Then Jesus said unto them, Yet a little while is the $[light] with you. Walk while ye have the $[light], lest **darkness** come upon you: for he that walketh in **darkness** knoweth not whither he goeth."* — Jn 12:35

There is urgency here. The $light — the visible example of God's $truth — is present but will not remain indefinitely. The time to walk openly is *now*. Darkness, once it settles, disorients. The person in the dark does not merely do wrong — they lose the ability to navigate at all.

## Patterns

1. **Darkness hides; $light reveals.** This is the defining axis. Jn 3:19–21, Eph 5:11–13, 1 Cor 4:5, Job 34:22, Dan 2:22. Every passage about darkness involves concealment; every passage about $light involves making manifest.

2. **Darkness is a choice before it is a condition.** Jn 3:19 — men *loved* darkness. 1 Jn 2:11 — darkness *blinded* his eyes. The sequence matters: first the preference for hiding, then the inability to see. Blindness is the fruit, not the root.

3. **Darkness produces shame.** Eph 5:12 — "It is a shame even to speak of those things which are done of them in secret." The works of darkness are not just hidden; they are things that *cannot withstand* being spoken of openly. Shame is the signature of darkness.

4. **Darkness is the opposite of $truth-doing.** 1 Jn 1:6 — walking in darkness = "do not the truth." Since $truth = God's commandments, darkness = the concealment of failure to keep those commandments. Not abstract evil, but hidden covenant transgression.

5. **Darkness operates through authority structures.** Lk 22:53 — "the power of darkness." Acts 26:18 — "the power of Satan." Col 1:13 — "the power of darkness." Darkness is not just personal concealment; it is an organized domain — a jurisdiction that maintains itself by keeping disobedience hidden.

6. **Darkness cannot ultimately conceal.** Job 34:22, 1 Cor 4:5, 2 Sam 12:12. Every act of hiding will be uncovered. The Lord brings "to $light the hidden things of darkness." What was done in secret will be declared before all Israel and before the sun.

7. **Walking in darkness parallels the broad $way.** Prov 4:19 — "the $way of the wicked is as darkness." The broad $way (Mat 7:13) and the path of darkness describe the same reality: a pattern of life that avoids scrutiny, avoids the narrow standard of $truth, and ends in destruction.

8. **$night is the season; darkness is the state.** Gen 1:5 — God called darkness $night. $night is the temporal dimension of darkness — the season when concealment prevails, when men sleep, when the thief comes. Darkness is the condition; $night is when it dominates.

## Connections

- **$light** — The defining opposite. $light = visible obedience, the example that can be seen and followed. Darkness conceals; $light makes manifest (Eph 5:13). God is $light with no darkness at all (1 Jn 1:5). Every command to leave darkness is a command to *be seen* — to let one's works be visible before others (Mat 5:16).

- **$truth** — God's commandments, His standard. Walking in darkness = "do not the $truth" (1 Jn 1:6). Doing $truth = coming to the $light (Jn 3:21). Darkness is the concealment of failure to keep God's standard; $truth is that standard itself.

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness, covenant transgression. Darkness and $wickedness overlap but are distinct: $wickedness names the *act* (transgressing the covenant); darkness names the *concealment* of that act. Workers of iniquity seek to hide in darkness (Job 34:22). The "mystery of iniquity" (2 Th 2:7) is $wickedness operating in secret — which is to say, $wickedness wrapped in darkness.

- **$way** — Conduct, manner of life. "The $way of the wicked is as darkness" (Prov 4:19). Darkness describes the *character* of a particular conduct: hidden, disoriented, unable to see where it leads. The opposite is the path of the just, which shines as the $light (Prov 4:18).

- **$fruit** — Works, deeds. The "unfruitful works of darkness" (Eph 5:11) are deeds that produce nothing of covenant value — $fruit that cannot withstand the $light. Darkness produces works; but they are works of shame, hidden by design.

- **$name** — Covenant relationship. Those who claim the $name (the covenant) while walking in darkness take the covenant in vain (compare Exod 20:7 with 1 Jn 1:6). Invoking the $name while hiding disobedience is precisely the lie John identifies: claiming fellowship while concealing unfaithfulness.

- **$night** — The temporal expression of darkness. God called darkness $night (Gen 1:5). Where darkness is the *state* of concealment, $night is the *season* in which it prevails — when men sleep, when the thief comes, when opportunity closes (Jn 9:4, 1 Th 5:2–7).

## For Further Study

- **$light** — The defining opposite. If darkness is concealed disobedience, what exactly does $light look like when Scripture calls believers "the light of the world"? The answer transforms a vague compliment into a specific commission.

- **$truth** — The standard that darkness hides from. Understanding $truth as God's commandments (not just "factual accuracy") reframes what it means to "do the truth" and "come to the light" (Jn 3:21).

- **$night** — The season of darkness. How does Scripture distinguish the *state* of darkness from the *time* of $night — and what does it mean that the $night "is far spent"?

- **$wickedness** — The act that darkness conceals. If $wickedness is covenant transgression and darkness is the concealment of it, what happens when the mystery of iniquity is finally *revealed* (2 Th 2:7–8)?

- **$way** — The conduct that darkness characterizes. "The way of the wicked is as darkness" (Prov 4:19). How does the $way symbol map the trajectories of those who hide versus those who walk openly?
