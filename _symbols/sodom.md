# Sodom

**The judgment of unrighteous abundance — ultimate moral decay, the name Scripture assigns to any covenant community that has become indistinguishable from $babylon.**

> *"Behold, this was the iniquity of thy sister **Sodom**, pride, fulness of $[bread], and abundance of idleness was in her and in her daughters, neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy."* — Ezek 16:49

---

## The Key Insight

Ask most readers what Sodom's sin was and they will name a sexual one. Scripture names four others first.

Ezekiel 16:49 lists them in order: (1) pride, (2) fulness of bread, (3) abundance of idleness, (4) neglect of the poor. Only *then* does verse 50 add: "and committed abomination before me." The sexual sin was the *fruit* of a deeper root — a community that had everything and gave nothing, that was full and at ease and therefore rotted from within.

This matters because it makes Sodom a *pattern*, not merely a historical event. A city can be sexually restrained and still be Sodom if it is proud, overfed, idle, and indifferent to the vulnerable. That is exactly how the prophets use the name — and it is exactly why Jesus says it will be "more tolerable" for Sodom than for the towns that heard His teaching and yawned.

The Hebrew name itself encodes the fate: H5467 *Cedom* derives from an unused root meaning "to scorch." Sodom is the city whose name *is* its judgment. And that judgment — $fire from heaven — becomes Scripture's defining image of what happens when covenant abundance meets covenant indifference.

**Symbolizes:** Prosperous community at ease in covenant violation — fullness that becomes fuel for sudden heavenly $fire

**Opposite:** $jerusalem — the covenant community under God's rule, the bride where righteousness dwells

**Defining verses:** Ezek 16:49–50, Gen 19:24–25, Lk 17:28–30, Rev 11:8

**The surprise:** Jesus does not describe Sodom's sin as sexual. He describes it as *ordinary*: "They did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded" (Lk 17:28). Six perfectly normal activities. The sin was not what they did — it was their obliviousness to what was coming.

**Connected:** $fire, $babylon, $egypt, $jerusalem, $bread, $harlot, $wickedness

## Sodom Across Scripture

### The Garden and the Furnace

> *"And Lot lifted up his eyes, and beheld all the plain of Jordan, that it was well watered every where, before the LORD destroyed **Sodom** and Gomorrah, even as the garden of the LORD, like the land of $[egypt]."* — Gen 13:10

The first description of Sodom compares it to two things: the garden of the LORD, and $egypt. Paradise and bondage in a single sentence. The land looked like Eden — lush, abundant, effortless provision. It also looked like $egypt — the system that enslaves through provision, that feeds you until you belong to it.

Lot's progression tells the story of gradual absorption. He *beheld* (Gen 13:10). He *chose* (Gen 13:11). He *dwelled in the cities of the plain* (Gen 13:12). He *pitched his tent toward Sodom* (Gen 13:12). By chapter 19, he *sat in the gate* (Gen 19:1) — the seat of civic authority. The trajectory is directional: toward abundance, into the system, absorbed by the culture.

> *"But the men of **Sodom** were wicked and sinners before the LORD exceedingly."* — Gen 13:13

Moses tells us this *before* any sexual sin is narrated. The wickedness — the covenant transgression, H7563 *rasha* — was already present when the land still looked like Eden. The garden appearance masked the furnace reality.

### Abraham Refuses Sodom's Bread

> *"And the king of **Sodom** said unto Abram, Give me the persons, and take the goods to thyself. And Abram said to the king of **Sodom**, I have lift up mine hand unto the LORD... That I will not take from a thread even to a shoelatchet... lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich."* — Gen 14:21–23

After rescuing Lot and the captives, Abraham is offered Sodom's wealth. He refuses absolutely — not even a thread or a sandal strap. His reason: "lest thou shouldest say, I have made Abram rich."

Abraham understood what Lot did not: Sodom's $bread comes with strings. To eat from Sodom's table is to become indebted to Sodom's system. This is the same logic that governs $egypt — provision as the mechanism of bondage. Abraham's refusal preserves his identity as one whose prosperity comes from the LORD alone, not from the city whose name means "scorched."

### The Cycle Ezekiel Names

Ezekiel 16 is the defining passage — not Genesis 19. In it, God compares $jerusalem to her "sister" Sodom and declares that Jerusalem is *worse*:

> *"Behold, this was the iniquity of thy sister **Sodom**, pride (H1347 *ga'own*), fulness of $[bread] (H3899 *lechem*), and abundance of idleness (H8252 *sha'anan* — quiet ease, careless security), was in her and in her daughters, neither did she strengthen the hand of the poor and needy."* — Ezek 16:49

> *"And they were haughty, and committed abomination (H8441 *to'evah*) before me: therefore I took them away as I saw good."* — Ezek 16:50

This is Sodom's cycle, given in sequence:

1. **Pride** (H1347) — self-exalting confidence
2. **Fulness of bread** — $bread means covenant terms / the Word of God. Fulness of bread suggests abundance of provision — having every covenant benefit without the covenant obligation
3. **Abundance of idleness** — careless ease, the security of those who feel no urgency
4. **Neglect of the poor** — refusal to give what was freely received
5. **Abomination** — the final expression of a society that has abandoned all covenant restraint

The sexual sin enters *last*, as the visible eruption of invisible rot. The root is prosperity without righteousness — being full and at ease and proud and indifferent. The abomination is what grows in that soil.

Notice what this implies: any community can become Sodom. It is not a geographic location — it is a moral trajectory. And Ezekiel's point is that $jerusalem had walked it:

> *"Neither hath Samaria committed half of thy sins; but thou hast multiplied thine abominations more than they, and hast justified thy sisters in all thine abominations which thou hast done."* — Ezek 16:51

Jerusalem, the covenant community, was more Sodom than Sodom. She had more $bread — more covenant revelation — and did less with it.

### "Rulers of Sodom" — The Prophets Apply the Name

> *"Except the LORD of hosts had left unto us a very small remnant, we should have been as **Sodom**, and we should have been like unto Gomorrah. Hear the word of the LORD, ye rulers of **Sodom**; give ear unto the law of our God, ye people of Gomorrah."* — Isa 1:9–10

Isaiah addresses Israel's leaders as "rulers of Sodom." Not because they live in the plain of Jordan — because they exhibit Sodom's pattern. Only a *remnant* prevents Israel from literally becoming Sodom. The name has become transferable: any leadership presiding over covenant abundance with covenant indifference earns the title.

> *"And the prophets of $[jerusalem]... they are all of them unto me as **Sodom**, and the inhabitants thereof as Gomorrah."* — Jer 23:14

Jeremiah extends the application: Jerusalem's prophets *are* Sodom. What makes them so? The context specifies: they "commit adultery, and walk in lies: they strengthen also the hands of evildoers" (Jer 23:14). They enable $wickedness — covenant transgression — through false assurance. They tell people they are safe when they are not.

> *"They declare their sin as **Sodom**, they hide it not."* — Isa 3:9

The final stage: sin becomes public identity. Not hidden, not ashamed — declared. This is what follows the cycle of Ezek 16:49–50: when pride, fulness, ease, and neglect have done their work, the abomination no longer needs to hide. It *presents* itself.

### Fire From Heaven — What Sodom's Judgment Looks Like

> *"Then the LORD rained upon **Sodom** and upon Gomorrah brimstone and $[fire] from the LORD out of heaven."* — Gen 19:24

> *"And Abraham... looked toward **Sodom** and Gomorrah, and toward all the land of the plain, and beheld, and, lo, the $[smoke] of the country went up as the $[smoke] of a furnace."* — Gen 19:28

Sodom's judgment has four distinguishing features:

**It comes from above.** The $fire is heaven-sent — external, not self-generated. This distinguishes Sodom from Tyre, where $fire comes "from the midst of thee" (Ezek 28:18). Tyre's commercial corruption breeds its own internal destruction; Sodom's moral corruption summons judgment *from God*.

**It is total.** "He overthrew those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and that which grew upon the ground" (Gen 19:25). The word "overthrew" is H2015 *haphak* — to turn upside down, to overturn completely. This is not partial discipline; it is total reversal.

**It is sudden.** "The same day that Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven" (Lk 17:29). The judgment falls in a $moment — one day, no warning sequence, no escalating plagues. Compare this with $egypt, where judgment comes through ten progressive plagues with opportunities for repentance between each. Sodom receives no such mercy. The cycle has run to completion.

**It leaves smoke as testimony.** Abraham sees the smoke rising (Gen 19:28). This image becomes the template for $babylon's fall: "the smoke of her burning" seen by kings and merchants from afar (Rev 18:9, 18:18).

### Extraction — Getting Lot Out

> *"And while he lingered, the men laid hold upon his hand, and upon the hand of his wife, and upon the hand of his two daughters; the LORD being merciful unto him: and they brought him forth, and set him without the city."* — Gen 19:16

Lot *lingers*. The angels physically *pull* him out. Even then, his wife looks back and becomes a pillar of salt.

This extraction pattern — reluctance to leave, physical intervention required, looking back as fatal — recurs throughout the Sodom-$babylon complex:

- "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues" (Rev 18:4)
- "Remember Lot's wife" (Lk 17:32) — Jesus' shortest and most urgent sermon

The difficulty of extraction is proportional to the comfort of the system. Sodom looked like the garden of the LORD. Leaving paradise requires a force stronger than attraction — and even then, the backward glance kills.

### "As It Was in the Days of Lot" — Jesus' Use of Sodom

> *"Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot; they did eat, they drank, they bought, they sold, they planted, they builded; But the same day that Lot went out of **Sodom** it rained $[fire] and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all."* — Lk 17:28–29

Jesus pairs the days of Noah with the days of Lot as twin templates for the coming of the Son of Man. Both share the same structure:

| Days of Noah | Days of Lot |
|---|---|
| Eating, drinking, marrying | Eating, drinking, buying, selling, planting, building |
| Flood took them all | Fire destroyed them all |
| Noah entered the ark → water came | Lot went out → fire came |
| $water judgment | $fire judgment |

The activities Jesus lists for Sodom are *not sinful*. Eating, drinking, buying, selling, planting, building — these are the ordinary transactions of a functioning economy. The sin is not the activity; it is the obliviousness. They were doing business as usual on the day $fire fell.

This is Sodom's deepest danger: it does not look dangerous. It looks *normal*. It looks prosperous. It looks like the garden of the LORD. And that is precisely why Jesus warns about it — not as a historical curiosity, but as the pattern for how judgment will arrive in the last days.

> *"Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed."* — Lk 17:30

### "More Tolerable for Sodom"

> *"And thou, Capernaum, which art exalted unto heaven, shalt be brought down to hell: for if the mighty works, which have been done in thee, had been done in **Sodom**, it would have remained until this day. But I say unto you, That it shall be more tolerable for the land of **Sodom** in the day of judgment, than for thee."* — Mat 11:23–24

Sodom had abundance and rejected covenant restraint. Capernaum had abundance *and* direct revelation — Jesus' miracles performed in its streets — and still rejected. The principle: greater light refused = greater judgment received.

This statement shatters any reading that isolates Sodom's sin as uniquely terrible. Jesus says Sodom's judgment is *lighter* than what awaits those who had more and did nothing with it. Sodom becomes a measuring stick — the baseline of covenant violation against which others are measured and found wanting.

### "Spiritually Called Sodom and Egypt"

> *"And their dead bodies shall lie in the street of the great city, which **spiritually** is called **Sodom** and $[egypt], where also our Lord was crucified."* — Rev 11:8

This is the capstone verse. The "great city" — $babylon — receives three names in Revelation, and each names a *function*, not a location:

- **$babylon** — the whole system of man's substitute for God's covenant rule
- **Sodom** — its moral face: abundance without righteousness, inviting fire from heaven
- **$egypt** — its bondage face: provision that enslaves, judged by plagues but healable

The word "spiritually" (G4153 *pneumatikos*) signals that these are symbolic identifications. The city where the Lord was crucified is Jerusalem — yet it is *spiritually* Sodom and $egypt. The covenant community can become everything it was supposed to oppose. This is the Ezekiel 16 principle carried to its final expression: $jerusalem *becomes* Sodom when it walks Sodom's cycle.

The three names converge on a single reality: any city, any community, any system that replaces covenant faithfulness with self-sufficient prosperity wears Sodom's face.

### The Vine of Sodom — Good Soil, Toxic Fruit

> *"For their vine is of the vine of **Sodom**, and of the fields of Gomorrah: their grapes are grapes of gall, their clusters are bitter."* — Deut 32:32

Moses speaks this in his final song over Israel — warning that Israel's vine will become Sodom's vine. Good soil. Good climate. The appearance of productivity. But the fruit is poison.

The image works because Sodom's land was "well watered as the garden of the LORD" (Gen 13:10). The problem was never the soil. The problem was what grew in it when covenant faithfulness was absent. Abundance + no righteousness = toxic fruit. The vine looks healthy; the grapes are gall.

### Even Sodom Will Be Restored

> *"When I shall bring again the captivity of **Sodom** and her daughters... then shalt thou bear thine own shame."* — Ezek 16:53, 55

One of the most overlooked details in the Sodom narrative: Ezekiel promises *restoration* even for Sodom. The purpose is to shame $jerusalem — if God restores Sodom, how much more will Jerusalem bear the weight of her own superior guilt?

> *"Neither was thy sister **Sodom** mentioned by thy mouth in the day of thy pride."* — Ezek 16:56

Jerusalem was too proud to even *mention* Sodom — too proud to recognize the family resemblance. Yet they were sisters. And the restoration promise covers both.

## Patterns

1. **Sodom's cycle is progressive.** Pride → fulness → ease → neglect → abomination → judgment. Each stage grows from the last. Skip the early stages, and the final one seems to appear from nowhere. Read them in order, and it becomes inevitable.

2. **Sodom is transferable.** Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Revelation all apply the name to cities and peoples who are not geographically Sodom. The name is a diagnosis, not an address.

3. **The sin is not the sin you expect.** Jesus lists six ordinary activities (Lk 17:28). Ezekiel lists four conditions before naming abomination (Ezek 16:49–50). The obvious sin is the *symptom*. The disease is prosperous indifference.

4. **Fire from heaven — not from within.** Sodom's $fire comes from above; Tyre's comes from within (Ezek 28:18). Both are faces of $babylon, both end in $fire, but the source differs. Sodom's judgment is God's direct intervention; Tyre's is self-consumption.

5. **Extraction is simultaneous with destruction.** "The same day that Lot went out" (Lk 17:29). There is no gap between rescue and ruin. The righteous exit and the $fire falls on the same calendar day.

6. **Water then fire.** The days of Noah (water) and the days of Lot ($fire) are paired by Jesus (Lk 17:26–30) and by Peter (2 Pet 3:5–7). The present world is "reserved unto $fire" — we are in the post-water, pre-fire era. Sodom's judgment is the prototype of the final one.

7. **Greater revelation, greater judgment.** Capernaum's judgment exceeds Sodom's (Mat 11:23–24). The standard is proportional to the light received. Sodom had abundance; Capernaum had abundance *and* miracles. Having more $bread and doing nothing with it makes the fire hotter, not cooler.

## Connections

- **$fire** — God's holy standard in active contact: consuming what violates covenant, refining what keeps it. Sodom receives $fire from heaven — the covenant standard applied to a community that had every provision and no faithfulness. The $fire does not refine Sodom; there is no covenant material left to preserve.

- **$babylon** — Man's substitute system for God's covenant rule. Sodom is $babylon's *moral face* — the dimension of self-indulgent abundance that invites external judgment. Rev 11:8 names the great city as both Sodom and $egypt, revealing two faces of one anti-covenant system.

- **$egypt** — $babylon's bondage face — provision that enslaves. Gen 13:10 compares Sodom's land to $egypt's land: both look abundant, both confine those who enter. But $egypt is healable (Isa 19:22); Sodom receives sudden total destruction. The faces share the mechanism of provision-as-trap but differ in how judgment falls.

- **$jerusalem** — The covenant community, the bride. Sodom's opposite — yet Ezekiel makes them sisters (Ezek 16:46) and declares $jerusalem *worse* (Ezek 16:51). The covenant community becomes Sodom when it walks Sodom's cycle. Rev 11:8 identifies the city where the Lord was crucified as "spiritually" Sodom.

- **$bread** — The Word / covenant terms. Sodom's "fulness of bread" (Ezek 16:49) is fulness of provision without covenant obligation — having every resource and using none of it for righteousness. Abraham refuses Sodom's $bread (Gen 14:22–23) to avoid entanglement.

- **$wickedness** — Covenant transgression / lawlessness. "The men of Sodom were wicked (H7563 *rasha*) and sinners before the LORD exceedingly" (Gen 13:13). $wickedness burns like $fire (Isa 9:18) — Sodom's covenant transgression becomes its own fuel.

- **$harlot** — The covenant breaker. Sodom's cycle parallels the $harlot's pattern: divided loyalty, abundance spent on self-gratification rather than covenant faithfulness. Jer 23:14 links Sodom-identity with adultery and lies among $jerusalem's prophets.

## For Further Study

- **$fire** — Sodom's judgment is the prototype for all fire-from-heaven passages. How does the same $fire that destroys Sodom refine the sons of Levi (Mal 3:2–3)? The difference is not the fire — it is the material.

- **$babylon** — Sodom is one face of $babylon's larger system. How do the moral face (Sodom), the bondage face ($egypt), and the commercial face (Tyre) compose the single "great city" of Revelation?

- **$jerusalem** — Sodom's opposite and, terrifyingly, Sodom's sister. How does the covenant community become "spiritually" Sodom — and what does restoration look like for both?

- **$egypt** — The other name applied to the great city in Rev 11:8. $egypt enslaves through provision; Sodom corrupts through abundance. How do these two mechanisms work together in the same system?

- **$bread** — "Fulness of bread" is Sodom's second-listed sin. If $bread represents covenant terms / the Word of God, what does it mean to be *full* of bread and yet utterly empty of righteousness?
