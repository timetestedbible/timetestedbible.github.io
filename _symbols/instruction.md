# Instruction

## Approved Definition

**The law's discipline and correction, received and kept by the [wise](/research/symbols/wise/) but despised by [fools](/research/symbols/fool/).**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Fool and the Wise](/books/symbolic-language/the-fool-and-the-wise/).

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The law's discipline and correction, received and kept by the [wise](/research/symbols/wise/) but despised by [fools](/research/symbols/fool/).**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **instruction** begins: “The act of instructing, teaching, or furnishing with knowledge; information.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The discipline of the law, received and kept — what only fools despise.

> *“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction.”* — Proverbs 1:7

> *“Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding.”* — Proverbs 23:23

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **instruction** begins:

> “In*struc”tion, n. Etym: [L. instructio: cf. F. instruction.] 1. The act of instructing, teaching, or furnishing with knowledge; information.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *instruction*. The English-form sweep found **33 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Proverbs 1:7 and 23:23 clearly connect instruction with wisdom, acquisition, retention, and the fool’s rejection, but neither excerpt explicitly identifies instruction as 'the discipline of the law' or directly states obedience to law. That narrowing depends on lexical or wider contextual reasoning beyond the printed citations.

- One could argue the book's gloss materially narrows instruction to specifically legal/Torah discipline, excluding broader consensus senses such as wisdom teaching, parental admonition, or apostolic doctrine, which would push toward REFINED with BOOK_NARROWS; additionally, the printed citations support the 'despised by fools' and 'to be bought and kept' elements but do not themselves establish the 'law' component.

- Consensus regularly treats instruction as a broader lexical-theological range (teaching + discipline + Torah + parental/apostolic forms) rather than exclusively the received-and-kept discipline of the law.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
