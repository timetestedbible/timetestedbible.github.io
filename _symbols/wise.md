# Wise

**Obedient — one who hears God's instruction and does it.**

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that **do** his commandments."* — Ps 111:10

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture defines its own terms. Three times — in the Law, the Psalms, and the Gospels — it tells us exactly what "wise" means. Not once does the definition involve intelligence, education, or cleverness.

**Deuteronomy 4:6** — *"Keep therefore and **do** them; for this is your **wisdom** and your understanding in the sight of the nations."*

Moses does not say "understand them" or "analyze them." He says *keep and do*. That is your wisdom.

**Psalm 111:10** — *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of **wisdom**: a good understanding have all they that **do** his commandments."*

The Psalmist equates wisdom directly with doing the commandments. Not contemplating them. Doing them.

**Matthew 7:24** — *"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and **doeth** them, I will liken him unto a **wise** man, which built his house upon a $[rock]."*

Jesus says it plainly: the one who *hears and does* is the wise man. The one who hears and does *not* is the fool. The dividing line is not comprehension — both hear. It is action.

A secular dictionary defines "wise" as having good judgment, deep knowledge, or sound discernment. Scripture defines it as obedience. The distinction matters because it reframes every occurrence of the word. When Proverbs commends wisdom, it is not commending intellectual achievement — it is commending responsive obedience to God's word.

**Symbolizes:** Obedient — one who hears God's commandments and keeps them

**Opposite:** The fool — disobedient, one who hears but does not do

**Defining verses:** Ps 111:10, Deut 4:6, Mat 7:24

**The surprise:** The Greek word translated "wise" in the parable of the ten virgins and the two builders (G5429 *phronimos*) means *practically sensible* — oriented toward action, not abstract thought. And the Greek for "harmless as doves" (G185 *akeraios*, Mat 10:16) is paired with this same word: "**wise** as serpents" uses G5429. Jesus pairs practical responsiveness with unmixed devotion — both are action words.

**Connected:** $rock, $sand, $oil, $light, $faith, $truth

## Wise Across Scripture

### The Wise Man and the Fool — Hearing Is Not Enough

> *"Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and **doeth** them, I will liken him unto a **wise** man, which built his house upon a $[rock]: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell not: for it was founded upon a $[rock]."* — Mat 7:24–25

> *"And every one that heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them **not**, shall be likened unto a **foolish** man, which built his house upon the $[sand]: And the rain descended, and the floods came, and the winds blew, and beat upon that house; and it fell: and great was the fall of it."* — Mat 7:26–27

Both men hear. Both experience the same storm. The *only* variable is whether they do what they heard. The wise man's house stands because it is built on $rock — the unchanging covenant. The foolish man's house collapses because it is built on $sand — multitudes without covenant binding.

The parable reveals that *hearing* without *doing* is functionally the same as not hearing at all. The storm does not discriminate between those who never heard and those who heard but ignored. The house falls either way.

Notice what is *not* in the parable: there is no middle category. No "partially wise" builder. No house built on gravel. Jesus divides all hearers into two groups — those who obey and those who do not. This absolute binary runs through every wise/fool contrast in Scripture.

### Proverbs — The Wise Heart Receives Commandments

> *"The **wise** in heart will receive commandments: but a prating fool shall fall."* — Prov 10:8

The wise heart is defined by a single capacity: it *receives* commandments. The Hebrew H3947 *laqach* (receive, take, accept) implies active acceptance — reaching out and taking hold. The fool, by contrast, does not reject commandments through careful analysis. He simply talks (*prating* — H8193 *saphah*, lips) and falls.

> *"The way of a fool is right in his own eyes: but he that hearkeneth unto counsel is **wise**."* — Prov 12:15

The fool's defining characteristic is self-direction — "right in his own eyes." The wise man's defining characteristic is that he *hearkeneth* (H8085 *shama'* — hears and obeys). The same Hebrew word *shama'* is used in the Shema: "Hear, O Israel" (Deut 6:4). To hear, in Hebrew thought, *is* to obey. The wise man listens and responds; the fool listens only to himself.

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but **fools** despise **wisdom** and instruction."* — Prov 1:7

"Despise" is H936 *buz* — to hold in contempt. The fool does not lack access to wisdom. He despises it. He holds instruction in contempt. This is not an intellectual failure but a posture of refusal — the opposite of the wise heart that *receives* commandments.

### The Psalm That Closes the Definition

> *"The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God."* — Ps 14:1

Compare with the beginning of wisdom:

> *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of **wisdom**: a good understanding have all they that do his commandments."* — Ps 111:10

The wise man fears the LORD and does His commandments. The fool says "no God" — not necessarily denying God's existence, but denying His authority. The Hebrew H5036 *nabal* (fool) carries the sense of someone morally senseless, willfully closed. The fool says *in his heart* — not with his mouth — "no God." He lives as though there is no one to obey. His life declares what his lips may not.

This is the deepest layer of the wise/fool contrast: it is not about capacity but about posture. The wise man orients toward God's command and acts. The fool orients away from it and refuses.

### Ten Virgins — Wisdom Has Oil

> *"Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom. And five of them were **wise**, and five were **foolish**."* — Mat 25:1–2

> *"They that were **foolish** took their lamps, and took no $[oil]: But the **wise** took $[oil] in their vessels with their lamps."* — Mat 25:3–4

All ten virgins have lamps. All ten go out to meet the bridegroom. All ten fall asleep while waiting. The *only* difference is $oil.

$oil in the pipeline represents proven works — the product of the covenant life pressed and tested through trial. It cannot be borrowed: "Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you: but go ye rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves" (Mat 25:9). Works cannot be transferred. No one else's obedience counts as yours.

The wise virgins are wise because they *prepared* — they acted in advance, accumulating $oil through faithful obedience over time. The foolish had lamps (commandments, the instrument) but no $oil (no proven track record of keeping them). When the bridegroom came, the lamps without $oil could produce no $light.

The parable ends with a warning that echoes the wise/fool division:

> *"Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour."* — Mat 25:13

"Watch" is not passive awareness. It is the active vigilance of one who stays ready — the same readiness the turtledove shows in keeping her appointed times (Jer 8:7). The wise are those who are *doing* when the master arrives.

### Becoming Wise by Becoming Foolish

> *"Let no man deceive himself. If any man among you seemeth to be **wise** in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be **wise**."* — 1 Cor 3:18

Paul inverts the world's categories. To be G4680 *sophos* in the world's estimation means to be self-sufficient — "right in his own eyes" (Prov 12:15). To become a fool in the world's eyes is to submit to God's commandments, which the world considers absurd.

The substitution clarifies it: "If any man among you seems to be *obedient to the world's standard*, let him become disobedient *to the world's standard*, that he may become *obedient to God's standard*." The wise/fool label depends entirely on *whose* commandments you are keeping. One master's wisdom is another master's foolishness.

### Walk as Wise

> *"See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as unwise, but as **wise**, redeeming the time, because the days are evil."* — Eph 5:15–16

"Walk" — the consistent biblical metaphor for daily conduct, how one lives. Paul does not say *think* as wise or *believe* as wise but *walk* as wise. The wisdom is in the walking — the active, step-by-step conduct of life according to God's standard.

"Redeeming the time" (G1805 *exagorazo*) — buying up, making the most of. The wise make active use of the time given. The connection to the ten virgins is striking: the wise prepared while there was time. The foolish did not. Both parables — the builders and the virgins — turn on what was done *before* the crisis arrived.

## Patterns

1. **Wise is always defined by doing, never by knowing.** Ps 111:10, Deut 4:6, Mat 7:24 — all three definitions include explicit action verbs: *do*, *keep*, *doeth*. No verse in Scripture defines wisdom as understanding alone.

2. **The wise/fool contrast is always a contrast of response, not ability.** Both hear (Mat 7:24–26). Both have lamps (Mat 25:3–4). Both have access to counsel (Prov 12:15). The fool's problem is not that he cannot obey — it is that he will not.

3. **Wisdom is visible after the test.** The storm reveals which foundation was chosen (Mat 7:25–27). The bridegroom's arrival reveals who had $oil (Mat 25:10). Wisdom is proven by endurance — the same pressing that produces $oil from olives.

4. **"Fear of the LORD" is the entry point.** Prov 1:7, Ps 111:10 — the fear of the LORD is the *beginning* of wisdom. Fear (H3374 *yir'ah*) is reverence that produces compliance. Without the posture of submission, there is no obedience and therefore no wisdom.

5. **The fool speaks; the wise acts.** Prov 10:8 — "prating fool." Ps 14:1 — "fool has *said*." The fool's characteristic activity is speech without action. The wise man's characteristic activity is receiving and doing.

6. **Whose standard determines whose "wise."** 1 Cor 3:18 — the world's wise must become the world's fool to become God's wise. The labels reverse depending on which master's commandments define the standard. This is why $truth (God's commandments) must be established before wisdom can be measured.

## Connections

- **$rock** — The wise man builds on $rock — the unchanging covenant (Mat 7:24–25). Obedience anchors life to the covenant that does not shift. The fool builds on $sand and falls. The $rock is not chosen for its appearance but for its permanence: the covenant endures the storm that destroys everything else.

- **$sand** — The fool's foundation. $sand represents multitudes at the covenant boundary — people who have not yet been bound by covenant to the $rock. Building on $sand is building on human effort, numbers, or descent without the binding covenant. Sand becomes living stone only through union with Christ, the $rock (1 Pet 2:4–5). The wise man's house stands because he has crossed from $sand to $rock — from hearing to doing.

- **$oil** — The wise virgins had $oil; the foolish did not (Mat 25:3–4). $oil represents proven works — the fruit of obedience pressed through trial. It cannot be borrowed because no one else's track record of obedience substitutes for your own. The wise accumulate $oil by consistent faithful action *before* the crisis.

- **$light** — $oil fuels the lamp that produces $light — visible obedience (Mat 5:14–16). The wise man's life produces $light because his obedience is visible. "Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works" (Mat 5:16). Without $oil (proven works), there is no $light (visible example). The chain is: wisdom (obedience) → $oil (proven works) → $light (visible testimony).

- **$faith** — $faith represents trusting obedience — active response to God's word. Wisdom and $faith describe the same posture from different angles: $faith emphasizes the trust that motivates the action; wisdom emphasizes the action itself. James 2:17 — "faith without works is dead" — parallels the wise/fool division exactly: both categories hear, but only one acts.

- **$truth** — $truth represents God's commandments — the standard the wise man obeys. Wisdom cannot exist apart from $truth because wisdom *is* the response to $truth. "Thy word is truth" (Jn 17:17) provides the content; wisdom provides the compliance. The fool despises instruction (Prov 1:7) — he rejects $truth — and that rejection *is* his foolishness.

- **$rock** — The wise man's foundation. What does the $rock represent, and why does building on it mean survival through every storm?
- **$sand** — The fool's foundation. How do multitudes at the covenant boundary become living stones — and what keeps them as shifting sand?
