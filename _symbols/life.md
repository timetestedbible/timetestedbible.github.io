# Life (the life)

## Approved Definition

**[Covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) existence — the life lived walking in the [Way](/research/symbols/way/) according to God's law.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Way, the Truth & the Life](/books/symbolic-language/the-way-the-truth-and-the-life/).

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**[Covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) existence — the life lived walking in the [Way](/research/symbols/way/) according to God's law.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **life** begins: “The state of being which begins with generation, birth, or germination, and ends with death; also, the time during which this state continues; that state of an animal or plant in which all or any of its organs are capable of performing all or any of their functions; -- used of all animal and vegetable organisms.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

Covenant existence — the life the law produces.

> *“I call heaven and earth to record this day against you, that I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life, that both thou and thy seed may live:”* — Deuteronomy 30:19

> *“That thou mayest love the LORD thy God, and that thou mayest obey his voice, and that thou mayest cleave unto him: for he is thy life, and the length of thy days: that thou mayest dwell in the land which the LORD sware unto thy fathers, to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, to give them.”* — Deuteronomy 30:20

> *“Ye shall therefore keep my statutes, and my judgments: which if a man do, he shall live in them: I am the LORD.”* — Leviticus 18:5

> *“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”* — John 14:6

> *“Because strait is the gate, and narrow is the way, which leadeth unto life, and few there be that find it.”* — Matthew 7:14

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *life*. The English-form sweep found **409 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.

## Evidence by Sense

The approved entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Approved core:** [Covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) existence — the life lived walking in the [Way](/research/symbols/way/) according to God's law.

3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **Christ Himself, and the eternal life imparted to believers.**

- Deuteronomy 30:19-20 and Leviticus 18:5 support a connection between covenant obedience and life, but they do not by themselves establish that the law produces a distinct entity called “covenant existence.” More importantly, John 14:6 directly predicates “the life” of Christ, and Matthew 7:14 mentions a way leading to life; neither excerpt alone identifies that life as law-produced.

- One could argue REFINED: Response B explicitly lists covenant flourishing (Deut 30:15-20) as a common alternative, so the book merely promotes a recognized secondary sense; and the book might intend Christ as the true embodiment or fulfillment of covenant-law life, harmonizing rather than contradicting the christological reading. However, the book's own common-view note signals deliberate departure from 'Christ Himself / eternal life imparted,' and 'the life the law produces' inverts the consensus's law-versus-grace framing, so the contradiction is material.

- NT usage (esp. Johannine 'I am the life' and statements that life is in the Son, not the law—cf. Gal 3:21; Rom 10:5 vs. 10:6-10) makes the law-product identification incompatible with the dominant sense of 'the life'.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
