## The Short Case

The sun identifies the Father as the original source of light, while the righteous shine derivatively like the sun in His kingdom.

> *“For the LORD God is a sun and shield: the LORD will give grace and glory: no good thing will he withhold from them that walk uprightly.”* — Psalms 84:11

> *“And he dreamed yet another dream, and told it his brethren, and said, Behold, I have dreamed a dream more; and, behold, the sun and the moon and the eleven stars made obeisance to me.”* — Genesis 37:9

> *“Then shall the righteous shine forth as the sun in the kingdom of their Father. Who hath ears to hear, let him hear.”* — Matthew 13:43

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

The entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Core sense:** The Father, the source of light, from which every lesser light is drawn; the righteous shall shine as the sun in His kingdom.

3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Because the blind consensus repeatedly makes Christ—not merely God generically—the principal identification, one could view the book’s Father-specific identification as replacing the central referent and therefore as divergent. However, the consensus expressly includes God, and Psalm 84:11 directly identifies the LORD God as a sun, making refinement the better classification. The printed texts support God as sun and the righteous shining, but do not by themselves establish the Father as the exclusive source from which every lesser light is drawn; Genesis 37:9 also concerns familial or governing figures rather than that source claim.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: in Trinitarian terms the Father and the Son are distinct referents, and every consensus response names Christ as the paradigmatic meaning; a book that reserves the sun for the Father and relegates 'every lesser light' to derived status arguably replaces the consensus's central christological referent rather than merely narrowing it, especially if the referenced chapter 29 assigns Christ to the moon.

- Consensus primary rests on Malachi 4:2 and NT Christ-glory texts that the book's entry and printed citations entirely omit, so the shift to 'the Father' may be viewed as an idiosyncratic demotion of the traditional Sun-of-Righteousness identification.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
