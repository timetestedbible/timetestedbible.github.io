# Return of Christ

**The Covenant King's arrival to execute covenant judgment and establish covenant rule — the decisive $moment of total accountability.**

> *"For as the **lightning** cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the **coming** of the Son of man be."* — Mat 24:27

---

## The Key Insight

Three Greek words describe this event. Not one of them means "second coming."

G3952 *parousia* — the **arrival** of a king. A royal visit. When the emperor arrived at a province, his *parousia* meant inspection, accountability, and the settling of accounts. The province had better be in order.

G2015 *epiphaneia* — a **shining forth**. The visible, undeniable manifestation of what was previously hidden. The LXX uses this word for God's visible interventions in history — moments when the invisible God becomes unmistakably present.

G602 *apokalypsis* — an **uncovering**. Not revelation of information, but the stripping away of every covering. What was concealed is laid bare. The Hebrew equivalent, H1540 *galah*, means to expose, to make naked.

Three lenses on the same event: political (the King arrives), optical (the hidden one shines forth), judicial (everything is uncovered). Together they describe not an escape plan but a **reckoning** — the moment the Covenant King steps into full view and every covenant obligation comes due simultaneously.

This is why Jesus compares it to $lightning: not because it is fast, but because it is **total exposure**. Lightning illuminates everything at once, from horizon to horizon. Nothing hides. The Return of Christ is the ultimate $day — the time of exposure and accountability that $night and $sleep cannot survive.

**Symbolizes:** The Covenant King's decisive arrival-as-exposure — total accountability for covenant faithfulness or unfaithfulness

**Opposite:** $night / $sleep — hiddenness, unawareness, the condition of those who do not recognize what is coming

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:27, 1Th 4:15–16, 2Th 2:8, 1Cor 15:23, Rev 1:7, Titus 2:13

**The surprise:** The Hebrew concept beneath *parousia* is H6485 *paqad* — "to visit." In the Old Testament, when God *visits* (H6485), it always means He is settling accounts. Every divine "visit" is a reckoning. The Greek dressed it in royal-arrival language, but the Hebrew reveals the older pattern: God comes to *audit*.

**Connected:** $day, $light, $darkness, $sleep, $moment, $earthquake, $birth-pains, $sudden-destruction, $fall-of-babylon, $the-end, $faith, $fruit, $oil, $virgin, $harlot, $mountain, $clouds, $trumpet, $days-of-noah, $wickedness

## Return of Christ Across Scripture

### Three Words, One Arrival

The New Testament never settles on a single term for this event. It rotates between three — and the rotation matters, because each word illuminates a different face of the same reality.

**Parousia** (G3952) appears 24 times. It is the political word — the royal arrival. When Paul tells the Thessalonians about the *parousia* of the Lord (1Th 4:15), they would have heard what any Greek-speaking subject of Rome heard: *the Emperor is coming.* The word carried weight: roads were repaired, debts were collected, prisoners were judged, and the province was expected to present itself favorably. A *parousia* was not a casual visit. It was an inspection.

> *"For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of rejoicing? Are not even ye in the presence of our Lord Jesus Christ at his **coming** (G3952)?"* — 1Th 2:19

> *"Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the **coming** (G3952) of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious $[fruit] of the earth, and hath long patience for it, until he receive the early and latter rain."* — Jas 5:7

James pairs the *parousia* with a farmer waiting for $fruit — works, deeds, the harvest of covenant obedience. The King arrives to inspect the crop.

**Epiphaneia** (G2015) appears 6 times. It is the optical word — the visible manifestation, the shining forth. The LXX uses it for God's dramatic, undeniable interventions (2Sam 7:23 LXX). Every New Testament use is paired with a demand for covenant obedience:

> *"Keep this commandment without spot, unrebukeable, until the **appearing** (G2015) of our Lord Jesus Christ."* — 1Ti 6:14

> *"Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that **day**: and not to me only, but unto all them also that love his **appearing** (G2015)."* — 2Ti 4:8

Those who receive the crown are those who *love* His appearing — not those who merely await it. The word *epiphaneia* tests affection: do you want to be seen, or do you want to stay hidden?

**Apokalypsis** (G602) appears 18 times, with several pointing directly to this event. It is the judicial word — the uncovering, the exposure. What was covered is laid bare:

> *"That the trial of your $[faith], being much more precious than of $[gold] that perisheth, though it be tried with $[fire], might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the **appearing** (G602 *apokalypsis*) of Jesus Christ."* — 1Pe 1:7

> *"When the Lord Jesus shall be **revealed** (G602) from heaven with his mighty angels, in flaming $[fire] taking vengeance on them that know not God, and that obey not the gospel."* — 2Th 1:7–8

The *apokalypsis* strips the covering off everything — off the faithful (whose tested $faith is found worthy) and off the unfaithful (whose $wickedness is exposed). The uncovering is indiscriminate. It reveals *all*.

Titus 2:13 fuses two of these words into a single phrase:

> *"Looking for that blessed hope, and the glorious **appearing** (G2015 *epiphaneia*) of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ."* — Titus 2:13

And 2Th 2:8 fuses *epiphaneia* and *parousia* together:

> *"Then shall that $[wickedness] be revealed, whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the brightness (G2015 *epiphaneia*) of his **coming** (G3952 *parousia*)."* — 2Th 2:8

The *shining forth* of His *arrival* destroys the lawless one. The exposure is the weapon. The $light of the King's presence incinerates $wickedness — covenant transgression cannot survive total visibility.

### Lightning and the Day

> *"For as the **lightning** cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the **coming** of the Son of man be."* — Mat 24:27

Jesus does not compare His coming to a thunderbolt (impact) but to lightning (illumination). Lightning exposes the entire horizon in an instant. It cannot be localized — "lo, here" or "lo, there" (Mat 24:23, 26). You cannot point to it as if it were in one room. It is everywhere at once.

This is the $day arriving: exposure, the time of accountability, when every hidden thing is made visible (1Cor 4:5). The Return of Christ *is* the Day of the Lord — not a separate event that triggers the Day, but the Day itself. Paul moves seamlessly from the *parousia* in 1Th 4:15 to the "Day of the Lord" in 1Th 5:2 without any break, because they are the same reality:

> *"For yourselves know perfectly that the **$[day]** of the Lord so cometh as a thief in the **$[night]**."* — 1Th 5:2

> *"But ye, brethren, are not in **$[darkness]**, that that **$[day]** should overtake you as a thief. Ye are all the children of **$[light]**, and the children of the **$[day]**: we are not of the **$[night]**, nor of **$[darkness]**."* — 1Th 5:4–5

The entire symbolic cluster activates at once: $day (exposure/accountability), $light (visible obedience), $darkness (concealed disobedience), $night (the season of unawareness), $sleep (spiritual death/unawareness). Paul's point is not chronological — *when* it happens — but relational: which category are you in? Those who belong to the $day will not be surprised by the $day. Those who belong to the $night will be destroyed by the very exposure they tried to avoid.

### The Days of Noah — Normal Life Concealing Covenant Violation

> *"But as the **days of Noe** were, so shall also the **coming** of the Son of man be. For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark, and knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."* — Mat 24:37–39

The emphasis is not on the wickedness of Noah's generation — Jesus does not mention violence or corruption (though Gen 6:5–11 does). He emphasizes the *normalcy*: eating, drinking, marrying. Ordinary life continuing as though nothing is wrong. "They knew not" — the defining failure is not moral depravity but **unawareness**. They were in $sleep while the covenant deadline approached.

Peter makes explicit what Jesus implies:

> *"Knowing this first, that there shall come in the last days scoffers, walking after their own lusts, and saying, Where is the promise of his **coming** (G3952)? for since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue as they were from the beginning of the creation."* — 2Pe 3:3–4

> *"For this they willingly are ignorant of, that by the word of God the heavens were of old, and the earth standing out of the water and in the water: Whereby the world that then was, being overflowed with water, perished."* — 2Pe 3:5–6

The scoffers' argument is uniformitarianism: "all things continue as they were." They deny that God intervenes. They mistake patience for absence. And Peter's answer links two judgments — the flood ($days-of-noah) and the fire to come (2Pe 3:7) — as expressions of the same pattern: God's word creates the conditions, God's word destroys the conditions, and between creation and judgment there is a window that people mistake for permanence.

The $days-of-noah are not about what the generation *did* but about what the generation *assumed*: that normal life would continue indefinitely, that the covenant had no deadline.

### Birth Pains and Sudden Destruction

> *"All these are the beginning of **sorrows** (G5604 *odin* — birth pains)."* — Mat 24:8

> *"For when they shall say, **Peace and safety**; then **$[sudden-destruction]** cometh upon them, as **travail** (G5604) upon a woman with child; and they shall not escape."* — 1Th 5:3

Jesus calls the preliminary signs — wars, famines, $earthquakes across places — the *beginning* of $birth-pains. Not the crisis itself, but the onset of contractions. The pain signals that a new order is being born through the death of the old. $birth-pains are covenant judgment producing new covenant order — the agonizing transition between ages.

Paul's use is sharper: the $sudden-destruction that falls on the unaware comes *as* birth pains — inescapable and intensifying. The person saying "peace and safety" is in $sleep. They are declaring the normalcy that the $days-of-noah generation declared. And the judgment falls on them in a $moment — sudden, without warning, without remedy.

The combination — $birth-pains producing a new order, $sudden-destruction falling on those who refused to watch — suggests that the *parousia* is simultaneously a birth and a death. The same event that delivers the faithful destroys the unfaithful. One woman's labor produces a child; the same contractions are lethal to the lie that nothing was going to change.

### The Counterfeit Parousia

> *"Even him, whose **coming** (G3952 *parousia*) is after the working of Satan with all power and signs and lying wonders."* — 2Th 2:9

The lawless one has his own *parousia*. This is not incidental — it confirms that *parousia* means royal arrival, because Satan counterfeits royal arrivals. The man of sin presents himself as the arriving king, complete with signs and wonders. The word choice matters: this is not merely an "appearance" but a *parousia* — a full-scale mimicry of the Covenant King's inspection visit.

The true *parousia* destroys the counterfeit by sheer exposure: "whom the Lord shall consume with the spirit of his mouth, and shall destroy with the *epiphaneia* of his *parousia*" (2Th 2:8). The counterfeit cannot survive the brightness of the genuine. When $light arrives, $darkness does not coexist — it is annihilated.

### The Trumpet That Transfers the Kingdom

> *"In a **$[moment]**, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last **$[trumpet]**: for the **$[trumpet]** shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed."* — 1Cor 15:52

> *"For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the **trump** of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first."* — 1Th 4:16

> *"And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a **$[trumpet]**, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other."* — Mat 24:31

> *"And the seventh angel sounded; and there were great voices in heaven, saying, The **$[kingdom]s** of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever."* — Rev 11:15

These are not four different trumpets. They are four accounts of the same $trumpet — the authoritative signal of God's sovereign action that initiates kingdom transfer. Compare the content:

- 1Cor 15:52 — "last trump" → dead raised, living changed
- 1Th 4:16 — "trump of God" → Lord descends, dead rise, saints caught up in $clouds
- Mat 24:31 — "great trumpet" → angels gather elect
- Rev 11:15 — "seventh angel sounded" → kingdoms transferred

And at the seventh trumpet in Revelation, everything converges: "the nations were angry, and thy wrath is come, and the time of the dead, that they should be judged, and that thou shouldest give reward unto thy servants the prophets, and to the saints... and shouldest destroy them which destroy the earth" (Rev 11:18). Kingdom transferred. Dead judged. Saints rewarded. Destroyers destroyed. Ark of the covenant revealed (Rev 11:19). $earthquake.

This is the Jubilee $trumpet (Lev 25:9) — the sound that cancels all debts, frees all slaves, and returns all land to its original owner. The same vocabulary fills Jer 50–51's oracle against $babylon: redeemer, captives freed, debts cancelled, trumpet, "come out of her." The fall of $babylon and the sounding of the $trumpet are one event: the cosmic Jubilee.

### He Comes with Clouds

> *"Behold, he cometh with **$[clouds]**; and every eye shall see him, and they also which pierced him: and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him."* — Rev 1:7

The $clouds are not weather. They are the visible manifestation of God's presence — the same medium through which He descended at Sinai (Exo 19:16), led Israel through the wilderness (Exo 13:21), filled the temple (1Ki 8:10–11), authenticated His Son at the Transfiguration (Mat 17:5), and received Jesus at the Ascension (Acts 1:9).

Daniel saw it first:

> *"I saw in the night visions, and, behold, one like the Son of man came with the **$[clouds]** of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and there was given him dominion, and glory, and a **$[kingdom]**."* — Dan 7:13–14

The Son of Man rides the $clouds — God's throne-vehicle — and receives the $mountain (kingdom). The $clouds simultaneously conceal His person and announce His arrival. They are the medium of encounter between the unapproachable God and the world that must face Him.

The angels at the Ascension said: "This same Jesus, which is taken up from you into heaven, shall so come in like manner as ye have seen him go into heaven" (Acts 1:11). He left in a cloud. He returns in a cloud. The medium is unchanged because the Presence is unchanged.

### The Olive Mountain Splits

> *"And his feet shall stand in that day upon the **$[mountain]** of Olives, which is before Jerusalem on the east, and the **$[mountain]** of Olives shall cleave in the midst thereof toward the east and toward the west, and there shall be a very great valley."* — Zec 14:4

Zechariah connects the return to a specific geography and a specific action: the $mountain splits. The $mountain is kingdom — ruling authority. The Mount of Olives, the place of the Ascension (Acts 1:12), becomes the place of return. And it does not remain as it was. It cleaves. The old order — even the holy mountain of Jerusalem's eastern approach — is broken open by the Covenant King's arrival.

This connects to the $earthquake pattern. The shaking removes what can be shaken so that what cannot be shaken remains (Heb 12:26–27). The $mountain splits not to destroy the kingdom but to remake it — the valley created between the halves becomes a path of escape (Zec 14:5). Even the $earthquake serves the covenant purpose: judgment for the rebellious, rescue for the faithful.

### Epiphaneia Spans Both Advents

One anomaly demands attention. Paul uses *epiphaneia* not only for the future return but also for the first appearing:

> *"But is now made manifest by the **appearing** (G2015 *epiphaneia*) of our Saviour Jesus Christ, who hath abolished death, and hath brought life and immortality to light through the gospel."* — 2Ti 1:10

The incarnation was an *epiphaneia* — a shining forth. The same word. This suggests that the "appearing" is not a one-time future event but a quality of Christ's relationship to the world: He *manifests*. The first appearing manifested grace; the second manifests judgment. But the word is the same because the *person* is the same. The light that shone in a manger is the light that will incinerate the lawless one.

### The Parousia Separates

The *parousia* is not merely an arrival — it is the event that separates. The same moment that gathers the $elect scatters the unfaithful:

> *"Then shall two be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Two women shall be grinding at the mill; the one shall be taken, and the other left."* — Mat 24:40–41

The $virgin with $oil (proven works of tested obedience) enters the feast; the $virgin without $oil is shut out (Mat 25:1–12). The servants who multiplied their $fruit are rewarded; the servant who buried his talent is cast out (Mat 25:14–30). The sheep are separated from the goats (Mat 25:31–46). In every case, the *parousia* is the mechanism of division — the event that makes visible who belonged to $day and who belonged to $night, who was awake and who was in $sleep, who was the $virgin and who was the $harlot.

This is the consistent reading across every occurrence: the Return of Christ is not simply a future calendar event. It is the covenant mechanism that enforces accountability. Every exhortation to watch, to be sober, to keep $faith, to bear $fruit, to store up $oil, to walk in $light — all of these derive their urgency from the fact that the Covenant King *will* arrive, and when He does, everything hidden becomes visible.

### The Order of Resurrection

> *"For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive. But every man in his own order: Christ the firstfruits; afterward they that are Christ's at his **coming** (G3952). Then cometh **$[the-end]** (G5056 *telos*), when he shall have delivered up the **$[kingdom]** to God, even the Father."* — 1Cor 15:23–24

Paul establishes a sequence: Christ the firstfruits → those who are Christ's at His *parousia* → then the *telos*. The $the-end (G5056 *telos*) is not mere cessation but the goal arriving — covenant purpose fulfilled. The *parousia* precedes and enables the *telos*. The resurrection of the dead at His coming is the penultimate act; the delivery of the kingdom to the Father is the final one.

The *telos* arrives when testimony is complete (Mat 24:14 — "this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the *telos* come") and when the kingdom is fully transferred (Rev 11:15). The *parousia* is the hinge between the incomplete and the fulfilled, between the age of witness and the age of reign.

## The Old Testament Beneath the Greek

The Greek vocabulary — *parousia*, *epiphaneia*, *apokalypsis* — sits atop an older Hebrew foundation. Three concepts converge:

**H935 בּוֹא (bo)** — "to come, to enter." The standard OT word for God's arrival. Malachi uses it:

> *"Behold, I will send my messenger, and he shall prepare the way before me: and the Lord, whom ye seek, shall suddenly **come** (H935) to his temple, even the messenger of the covenant, whom ye delight in: behold, he shall come, saith the LORD of hosts. But who may abide the **$[day]** of his coming? and who shall stand when he **appeareth**? for he is like a refiner's $[fire], and like fullers' soap."* — Mal 3:1–2

The Lord comes *suddenly* to His temple. His coming is indistinguishable from the $day of His coming. And His appearance is refining $fire — exposure that purifies the faithful and consumes the unfaithful. This is the same event the New Testament calls *parousia* + *epiphaneia*.

**H1540 גָּלָה (galah)** — "to uncover, to make naked, to expose." The Hebrew beneath G602 *apokalypsis*. When God *reveals*, He strips the covering away. The same root describes exile (Israel "uncovered" from the land) and exposure of nakedness. The *apokalypsis* of Jesus Christ is the ultimate *galah* — every covering removed, every pretense stripped, every hidden thing brought to $light.

**H6485 פָּקַד (paqad)** — "to visit, to attend to, to muster, to reckon." When God *visits*, He audits. He counts what is there and what is missing. The LXX translates this with G1984 *episkope* (visitation) — the same word family Peter uses: "glorify God in the day of visitation" (1Pe 2:12). The *parousia* is the divine *paqad* — the King visits His province, inspects the accounts, and settles every debt.

## Patterns

1. **The arrival IS the judgment.** The *parousia* does not trigger a separate judgment event — the arrival itself is the reckoning. The *epiphaneia* of His *parousia* destroys the lawless one (2Th 2:8). The $lightning that shines east to west is both the coming and the exposure. The King's arrival is the audit.

2. **Three Greek words, one event, three angles.** *Parousia* (political: the King arrives), *epiphaneia* (optical: the hidden one shines forth), *apokalypsis* (judicial: everything is uncovered). No passage suggests these describe different events. They are lenses.

3. **The Return activates the $day/$night separation.** 1Th 4:15–5:8 moves seamlessly from *parousia* to "Day of the Lord" to children of $light vs. children of $darkness. The event separates humanity into two categories that were always present but hidden.

4. **$sleep is the defining danger.** Not $wickedness — the $days-of-noah generation was eating, drinking, marrying. The fatal condition is unawareness: "they knew not" (Mat 24:39). Every exhortation surrounding the Return is about waking up (Rom 13:11, Eph 5:14, 1Th 5:6).

5. **The counterfeit confirms the pattern.** The lawless one has his own *parousia* (2Th 2:9). Satan counterfeits the royal arrival. This confirms that *parousia* is a structural category — the arrival of a ruling authority — not just a name for one specific event.

6. **The $trumpet is the hinge-point.** 1Cor 15:52, 1Th 4:16, Mat 24:31, Rev 11:15, Isa 27:13 — all converge on one trumpet, one moment, one kingdom transfer. The $trumpet does not announce the Return; it initiates the irreversible transition the Return accomplishes.

7. **$fruit / $oil / $faith determine outcome.** James 5:7 (farmer waits for $fruit until the *parousia*), Mat 25:3–4 ($oil distinguishes wise $virgins from foolish), 2Ti 4:8 (crown to those who love the *epiphaneia*), 1Pe 1:7 ($faith tried by fire found to praise at the *apokalypsis*). The Return rewards covenant $faithfulness — works, proven obedience, trusting loyalty.

8. **$babylon falls when the King arrives.** Rev 11:15–18 (7th $trumpet: kingdom transferred, destroyers destroyed), Rev 16:17–19 (7th bowl: "It is done," $babylon remembered), Jer 50–51 (Jubilee vocabulary at $babylon's fall). The collapse of man's self-ruling system and the arrival of the Covenant King are simultaneous.

## Connections

- **$day** — The Return *is* the ultimate Day: exposure and accountability made total. Paul treats the *parousia* and the "Day of the Lord" as one reality (1Th 4:15–5:2). The Covenant King's arrival is the $day arriving.

- **$light** — The *epiphaneia* is a shining forth; the $lightning of Mat 24:27 illuminates everything. $light = visible obedience; the Return exposes who walked in it and who did not.

- **$darkness** — Concealed disobedience cannot survive the *apokalypsis*. The Return strips the covering off hidden $wickedness (1Cor 4:5). Those in $darkness are overtaken as by a thief (1Th 5:4).

- **$sleep** — Death and spiritual unawareness. The condition the *parousia* terminates. "Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall give thee $light" (Eph 5:14). The exhortations to watch are exhortations to leave $sleep.

- **$moment** — Sudden judgment. The *parousia* arrives "in a $moment, in the twinkling of an eye" (1Cor 15:52). The Return is the ultimate $moment — divine reckoning falling instantly.

- **$earthquake** — God shaking every established order to judge what is unfaithful. The *parousia* brings the ultimate shaking (Heb 12:26–27, Rev 11:19, Rev 16:18). What cannot be shaken remains — the Covenant King's $kingdom.

- **$birth-pains** — Covenant judgment producing new covenant order. The Return is what the birth pains labor to produce — the new age born through the death of the old (Mat 24:8, 1Th 5:3).

- **$sudden-destruction** — Inescapable covenant judgment on those saying "peace and safety." The Return IS the $sudden-destruction for the unfaithful (1Th 5:3) — the same event that is deliverance for the watchful.

- **$fall-of-babylon** — The collapse of man's self-ruling system. The 7th $trumpet simultaneously transfers the kingdom and destroys the destroyer (Rev 11:15–18). The Return and $babylon's fall are one event.

- **$the-end** — Covenant purpose fulfilled. The *parousia* precedes and enables the *telos* (1Cor 15:23–24). The $the-end is the goal the Return achieves — testimony complete, kingdom delivered.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience. What is tested and found worthy at the *apokalypsis* (1Pe 1:7). The Return rewards $faith and exposes its absence.

- **$fruit** — Works and deeds. The farmer waits for the precious $fruit of the earth until the *parousia* (Jas 5:7). The Return is harvest day.

- **$oil** — Proven works. The wise $virgins who had $oil enter the wedding feast at the King's arrival; the foolish are shut out (Mat 25:1–12). $oil cannot be borrowed at the last $moment.

- **$virgin** — Faithful, exclusively devoted. The *parousia* separates the wise $virgin from the foolish — those whose exclusive devotion produced $oil from those whose devotion was nominal.

- **$harlot** — Idolater, covenant breaker. The $harlot ($babylon the great) is destroyed by the *epiphaneia* of the *parousia* (2Th 2:8, Rev 17–18). The Return is the $virgin's vindication and the $harlot's ruin.

- **$mountain** — Kingdom, ruling authority. The Son of Man comes on $clouds to receive the $kingdom (Dan 7:13–14). Every $mountain-kingdom is shaken; God's $mountain alone endures (Heb 12:28).

- **$clouds** — God's presence-vehicle. He left in a cloud (Acts 1:9), He returns in $clouds (Rev 1:7, Mat 24:30). The medium of divine encounter that simultaneously conceals and reveals.

- **$trumpet** — The authoritative signal initiating kingdom transfer. The "last trump" (1Cor 15:52), "trump of God" (1Th 4:16), "great trumpet" (Mat 24:31), and the seventh trumpet (Rev 11:15) all mark the Return as the irreversible transition between ages.

- **$days-of-noah** — The pattern of sudden, separating judgment on a generation that continued normal life while rejecting God's covenant. Jesus makes this the primary analogy for the *parousia* (Mat 24:37–39).

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness, covenant transgression. The "lawless one" is destroyed specifically by the *epiphaneia* of the *parousia* (2Th 2:8). The Return is the end of $wickedness — not by reform but by incineration.

## For Further Study

- **$trumpet** — The authoritative signal that initiates kingdom transfer. How does the convergence of 1Cor 15:52, 1Th 4:16, Mat 24:31, and Rev 11:15 illuminate the timing and nature of the Return?

- **$fall-of-babylon** — The collapse of man's self-ruling system. How does the Jubilee vocabulary in Jer 50–51 connect Babylon's fall to the cosmic debt-cancellation the King's arrival accomplishes?

- **$days-of-noah** — Jesus' primary analogy for the *parousia*. What does the pattern of Noah's generation reveal about the defining danger — not depravity, but unawareness?

- **$day** — Exposure and accountability. How does the Day of the Lord function as the symbolic reality the Return of Christ enacts?

- **$clouds** — The visible manifestation of God's presence. From Sinai to Ascension to Revelation, how does the cloud function as the medium of divine encounter — and what does it mean that Christ returns through it?

- **$virgin** — Faithful, exclusively devoted. The parable of the ten virgins (Mat 25:1–12) is set explicitly at the *parousia*. What distinguishes the wise from the foolish, and why can $oil not be shared?

- **$the-end** — Covenant fulfillment. How does the *telos* differ from mere cessation, and what does 1Cor 15:23–24 reveal about the *parousia*'s place in the sequence of fulfillment?
