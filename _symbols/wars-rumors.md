# Wars and Rumors of Wars

**Covenant conflict — divine judgment manifesting as fracture within and between covenant peoples, the sign that covenant dissolution is underway but not yet complete.**

> *"And ye shall hear of **wars** and **rumours of wars**: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

---

## The Key Insight

Jesus says: "the $[the-end] is not yet."

That single phrase reshapes everything. Wars and rumors of wars are not the climax. They are not the catastrophe. They are not even the main event. They are what happens *before* the main event — and Jesus tells His disciples not to be troubled by them.

This is counterintuitive. War is the worst thing most people can imagine. But in Scripture's covenant framework, war is a *symptom*, not a cause. It is the visible surface of something deeper: the disintegration of covenant order. When peoples who should be unified turn on each other, when cities fracture and kingdoms collapse from within, that fracture is not random political violence — it is the signature of God's judgment on covenant unfaithfulness.

The root of the Hebrew word for war (H4421 *milchamah*) reveals the connection. It comes from H3898 *lacham* — which means both **to fight** and **to eat/devour**. The same consonants give us *lechem* — $bread. War and bread share a root. Covenant conflict is the dark mirror of covenant sustenance: where $bread represents the Word that nourishes covenant life, war represents the devouring that happens when that Word is rejected.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant conflict — divine judgment producing visible fracture among peoples, a sign of covenant dissolution in progress

**Opposite:** $peace — *shalom*, covenant wholeness and fulfillment. Where peace is the wholeness of covenant kept, war is the fracture of covenant broken

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:6, 2 Chr 15:5–6, Jer 51:46, Dan 9:26, Judg 5:8

**The surprise:** War in Scripture is not primarily about human aggression — it is about divine discipline. And the Hebrew root that produces "war" also produces "bread." The same root that means *to eat* means *to fight*. Covenant nourishment and covenant conflict spring from the same place.

**Connected:** $nation-against-nation, $birth-pains, $famine, $earthquake, $pestilence, $the-end, $deceive, $babylon

## Wars and Rumors of Wars Across Scripture

### The Old Testament Template Jesus Quoted

Most readers encounter "wars and rumors of wars" as if Jesus coined the phrase on the Mount of Olives. He did not. He was quoting a pattern His audience already knew — and the original context is essential.

> *"And in those times there was no peace to him that went out, nor to him that came in, but great vexations were upon all the inhabitants of the countries. And **nation was destroyed of nation, and city of city**: for God did vex them with all adversity."* — 2 Chr 15:5–6

This is the prophet Azariah speaking to King Asa. The subject is Israel's past — a period when covenant unfaithfulness produced internal dissolution. Notice: **God** is the agent. "God did vex them." The fracture is not the result of geopolitical accident. It is divine discipline for covenant abandonment.

When Jesus says "nation shall rise against nation, kingdom against kingdom" (Mat 24:7), He uses the identical Greek phrasing that the Septuagint uses for 2 Chr 15:6. His Jewish listeners would have heard the echo immediately: *this is the same pattern as before — God Himself is the troubler.*

The same formula appears in Isaiah's oracle against Egypt:

> *"And I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians: and they shall fight every one against his brother, and every one against his neighbour; **city against city**, and **kingdom against kingdom**."* — Isa 19:2

Egypt's internal civil war is not blamed on Egyptian politics. "**I** will set" — the LORD is the agent. The fracture is judgment. The $nation-against-nation pattern, wherever it appears, signals that God is actively dissolving the unity of a people who have abandoned covenant order.

### When New Gods Are Chosen

> *"They chose new gods; then was **war** in the gates."* — Judg 5:8

Deborah's song contains what may be the most compressed statement of the war-pattern in all of Scripture. One verse. Two clauses. The causal chain is explicit: idolatry → war. When covenant loyalty shifts to "new gods," the consequence is war — specifically, war *in the gates*, which is where justice was administered, where elders sat, where the community's covenant order was visible. War in the gates is the dissolution of the covenant's governing center.

This is not a unique insight from Judges. It is the logic undergirding every instance of war in Scripture's prophetic framework. The cycle is always the same:

1. **Idolatry** — covenant people turn to other gods ($deceive)
2. **Fracture** — internal unity dissolves ($nation-against-nation)
3. **War** — the fracture becomes violent
4. **Judgment** — the war IS the judgment, not merely a prelude to it
5. **Restoration** — if repentance comes, $peace returns

Leviticus 26 and Deuteronomy 28 establish this sequence as the covenant's own terms. "I will bring a sword upon you, that shall avenge the quarrel of my covenant" (Lev 26:25). The sword avenges the *covenant's quarrel* — war is covenant enforcement.

### The Root That Tells the Story

H4421 *milchamah* (war, battle) derives from H3898 *lacham*, a verb with two semantic fields that should not coexist but do:

- **To fight, to wage war** — "the LORD shall *fight* for you" (Exod 14:14)
- **To eat, to devour** — "they shall be as bread (*lechem*) for us" (Num 14:9, literally: "they are *lacham* for us")

*Lechem* — $bread — comes from the same root. The word for war and the word for bread share a common origin. This is not an accident of etymology; it is a window into the symbolic logic of Scripture. $bread represents the Word of God that sustains covenant life (Deut 8:3, "man doth not live by $[bread] only, but by every word"). War represents the devouring that occurs when that Word is withdrawn.

When $famine comes — the absence of God's Word (Amos 8:11) — war follows. The peoples who should be fed by the covenant Word instead devour each other. *Lechem* becomes *milchamah*. Bread becomes battle.

### "Not Yet the End" — War as Preliminary Sign

> *"And ye shall hear of **wars** and **rumours of wars**: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but $[the-end] is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

> *"For **nation** shall rise against **nation**, and **kingdom** against **kingdom**: and there shall be $[famine]s, and $[pestilence]s, and $[earthquake]s, in divers places. All these are the beginning of $[birth-pains]."* — Mat 24:7–8

Jesus places wars and rumors of wars within a specific sequence, and what He says about that sequence is striking: **be not troubled**. The word for $the-end here is G5056 *telos* — not cessation but *goal*, the covenant purpose arriving at fulfillment. Wars are not the *telos*. They are early labor contractions — $birth-pains that signal transition is underway but delivery has not come.

The sequence matters:

- First: $deceive — false messiahs and false representation of God's word (Mat 24:4–5)
- Then: wars and rumors of wars — but "the $[the-end] is not yet"
- Then: $nation-against-nation — the 2 Chr 15:6 pattern of internal fracture
- Then: $famine, $pestilence, $earthquake — escalating covenant judgments
- All of this together = "the beginning of $[birth-pains]"

Wars are *beginning*. They are the first visible sign that the covenant order is fracturing. But $birth-pains are not death — they are painful transition preceding deliverance. Something new is being born through the agony. The covenant is not simply ending; it is being fulfilled and reconstituted.

The critical word is "rumors" (G189 *akoē* — literally *hearing*, report). Wars *and the hearing of wars*. Not just conflict itself but the anxiety produced by reports of conflict — the social destabilization that precedes and surrounds the actual violence. The atmosphere of disintegration matters as much as the disintegration itself.

### Wars Decreed Until the End

> *"And after threescore and two weeks shall Messiah be cut off, but not for himself: and the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary; and the end thereof shall be with a flood, and unto the end **wars** and desolations are determined."* — Dan 9:26

Daniel's prophecy covers the entire span between Messiah's cutting off and the consummation: "unto $[the-end], wars and desolations are *determined*" — literally *decreed* (H2782 *charats* — to cut, decide sharply). This is not a prediction that "bad things will happen." It is a covenantal decree: the inter-advent period will be characterized by ongoing covenant conflict because the covenant transition is ongoing.

The Hebrew H7093 *qets* ("end") in Daniel shares its root with H7112 *qatsats* — to cut off. The end is a *cutting*. And between the cutting off of Messiah and the cutting-point of the covenant's consummation, the decreed condition is war. Not peace. Not resolution. War — covenant conflict — as the sustained background noise of an age in transition.

> *"And a **rumour** shall come one year, and after that in another year shall come a **rumour**, and violence in the land, ruler against ruler."* — Jer 51:46

Jeremiah describes $babylon's fall with the same dual pattern: rumors first, then violence. Ruler against ruler echoes the $nation-against-nation formula. $babylon — man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — collapses from within through the same covenant-fracture mechanism.

### YHWH the Man of War

> *"The LORD is a man of **war**: the LORD is his name."* — Exod 15:3

There is an anomaly in the pattern that demands attention. War is not always a sign of covenant *violation*. Sometimes YHWH Himself wages war — and this is a sign of covenant *enforcement*.

The Exodus is the paradigm. Pharaoh oppresses God's covenant people; YHWH makes war against Egypt. The plagues are military operations. The Red Sea is a battlefield. "The LORD shall *fight* for you, and ye shall hold your peace" (Exod 14:14) — the same root *lacham*. When God fights, His people are at $peace. When God's people abandon covenant, *they* fight each other.

This means war in Scripture operates on two axes:

- **Horizontal war** (people vs. people) = sign of covenant fracture, divine discipline
- **Vertical war** (God vs. covenant-breakers) = covenant enforcement, divine intervention

The book of Revelation brings both axes together. The beast wages war against the saints (Rev 13:7) — horizontal, covenant oppression. But heaven wages war against the dragon (Rev 12:7) — vertical, covenant enforcement. And at the end, the Lamb defeats the gathered kings (Rev 19:19) — God's *polemos* against the world's *polemos*.

James locates the source with surgical precision:

> *"From whence come **wars** and fightings among you? come they not hence, even of your lusts that **war** in your members?"* — Jas 4:1

The external fracture originates in the internal fracture. Wars between peoples come from wars within persons. Covenant conflict at the social level mirrors covenant conflict at the individual level — desire turned away from the covenant master toward other allegiances.

### War's Cessation — The Return of Shalom

> *"He maketh **wars** to cease unto the end of the earth; he breaketh the bow, and cutteth the spear in sunder; he burneth the chariot in the fire."* — Ps 46:9

> *"Scatter thou the people that delight in **war**."* — Ps 68:30

If war = covenant fracture, then the cessation of war = covenant restoration. $peace — *shalom* — is not merely the absence of conflict. It is the presence of covenant wholeness. When God makes wars to cease, He is not simply imposing a ceasefire. He is restoring what war's existence indicated had been broken.

This is why Isaiah's messianic vision culminates not in victory over enemies but in the end of war itself:

> *"And he shall judge among the nations, and shall rebuke many people: and they shall beat their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruninghooks: **nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more**."* — Isa 2:4

The reversal of $nation-against-nation. The same formula — nation against nation — now negated. What was fracture becomes unity. What was devouring becomes cultivation. *Milchamah* returns to *lechem* — war becomes bread again.

## Patterns

1. **Idolatry is the cause; war is the effect.** Judg 5:8 — "new gods chosen, then war in the gates." 2 Chr 15:5–6 — covenant abandonment produces national fracture. Isa 19:2 — God sets Egyptians against Egyptians as judgment. The sequence never reverses: covenant unfaithfulness always precedes the violence.

2. **God is the agent, not merely the observer.** "God did vex them" (2 Chr 15:6). "I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians" (Isa 19:2). "Wars and desolations are *determined*" (Dan 9:26). War is not random political upheaval noticed and then interpreted theologically — it is *decreed* as covenant discipline.

3. **War is preliminary, not final.** "The $[the-end] is not yet" (Mat 24:6). Wars are the *beginning* of $birth-pains (Mat 24:8), not the consummation. They indicate a process underway, not a goal achieved.

4. **Internal fracture, not foreign invasion.** The defining pattern is *self*-destruction: city against city, kingdom against kingdom, brother against brother. The fracture comes from within the covenant community, not from outside it.

5. **The root links war to bread.** H3898 *lacham* = fight AND eat. *Milchamah* (war) and *lechem* ($bread) share the root. When covenant sustenance ($bread / God's Word) is withdrawn ($famine), peoples devour each other instead of being nourished.

6. **Rumors precede violence.** Mat 24:6 pairs wars with *hearing* of wars. Jer 51:46 describes rumors arriving year after year before the collapse. The destabilization of information and trust precedes the destabilization of order.

7. **War ceases when covenant is restored.** Ps 46:9, Isa 2:4. The end of war is not military victory but covenant wholeness — $peace / *shalom*. Nation shall not lift sword against nation when the covenant order is fully established.

## Connections

- **$nation-against-nation** — The specific manifestation of wars in the Olivet Discourse: covenant peoples fracturing under divine discipline. Jesus quotes 2 Chr 15:6 verbatim through the Septuagint. Nation-against-nation is what wars *look like* — internal dissolution, not foreign conquest.

- **$birth-pains** — Wars, $famine, $earthquake, and $pestilence together constitute the "beginning of sorrows" (Mat 24:8). $birth-pains represent covenant judgment producing new covenant order — painful transition preceding deliverance. Wars are early labor: agonizing but purposeful, pointing toward something being born.

- **$famine** — The absence of God's Word (Amos 8:11). Famine and war share the root H3898 *lacham*: when the covenant Word ($bread) is withdrawn, peoples devour each other in war. $famine is the spiritual condition; war is its social manifestation.

- **$earthquake** — God shaking established order to remove what is unfaithful (Heb 12:26–27). While war fractures covenant community horizontally, $earthquake destabilizes the entire structure — political, social, cosmic. Both are signs, not the $the-end itself.

- **$pestilence** — God's direct lethal judgment for covenant violation. Where war is peoples destroying *each other*, $pestilence is God's own hand striking directly. The prophetic triad — sword, $famine, $pestilence (used ~30 times in Jeremiah alone) — represents escalating covenant curses, with war as the outermost ring.

- **$the-end** — The covenant purpose arriving at fulfillment (G5056 *telos*). Wars are explicitly *not* the $the-end (Mat 24:6). They are the preliminary tremors that indicate the $the-end is approaching — but the goal has not yet arrived. Dan 9:26 declares wars will persist "unto the $[the-end]" — they characterize the entire period before consummation.

- **$deceive** — In the Olivet Discourse, deception *precedes* wars (Mat 24:4–5 before 24:6). False representation of God's commands leads covenant people astray, which produces the fracture that becomes war. $deceive is the cause behind the cause: idolatry misdirects loyalty, fracture follows, war is the visible result.

- **$babylon** — Man's substitute system for God's covenant rule. $babylon both *produces* war (as the system that displaces covenant order) and *collapses through* war (Jer 51:46 — ruler against ruler). War is both $babylon's weapon and its self-destruction: the system devours itself with its own violence (Ezek 28:18).

- **$bread** — The symbolic opposite hidden in the etymology. How does *lechem* illuminate what *milchamah* destroys — and what $peace restores?

- **$babylon** — The system that both produces war and is consumed by it. How does Babylon's self-devouring judgment (Jer 51:46, Rev 18) fulfill the war-pattern?
