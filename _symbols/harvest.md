# Harvest

## Approved Definition

**The end of the age, when the field of mankind is reaped — the wheat gathered, the chaff burned.**
> *"The **harvest** is the end of the world; and the **reapers** are the angels."* — Mat 13:39

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The end of the age, when the field of mankind is reaped — the wheat gathered, the chaff burned.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **harvest** begins: “The gathering of a crop of any kind; the ingathering of the crops; also, the season of gathering grain and fruits, late summer or early autumn.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

The Hebrew word for harvest (H7105 *qatsir*) comes from H7114 *qatsar* — to **cut**, to sever. The Hebrew word for "the end" (H7093 *qets*) comes from H7112 *qatsats* — to **cut off**. Both words grow from the same semantic root: cutting.

Hebrew embeds what Jesus states plainly: the harvest **is** $the-end. Not a metaphor for the end — the mechanism of it. "The harvest is the end of the world" (Mat 13:39) is not a simile. The Greek is *estin* — "is." An equation.

But harvest adds something $the-end alone does not carry. $the-end is the covenant purpose arriving — the goal reached. Harvest tells you *how* it arrives: through ripeness, through cutting, through gathering, through separation into two destinations. The end is the what. The harvest is the how.

And the how matters, because Scripture does not present one harvest. It presents two — separated by season, by crop, and by outcome. One goes to the barn. The other goes to the $wine press. Both are reaped by the same sickle. Both are gathered by the same angels. The difference is not in the reaper but in what was sown.

**Symbolizes:** The appointed separation — when accumulated works reach fullness and are gathered to their outcome (barn or winepress)

**Opposite:** Seedtime / Sowing (Gen 8:22) — the beginning of the process whose end is harvest

**Defining verses:** Mat 13:39, Rev 14:14–20, Joel 3:13, Gal 6:7–9, Jer 51:33

**The surprise:** The two harvests are not an invention of Revelation. They are calendared in the Torah feasts — the Feast of Harvest (Pentecost, wheat) and the Feast of Ingathering (Tabernacles, grape). The liturgical calendar *is* the harvest timetable.

**Connected:** $the-end, $fire, $grass, $tree, $fruit, $wine, $wickedness, $babylon, $angel, $sowing

## Harvest Across Scripture

### The Cutting and the Cutting Off

The etymological connection between harvest and the end is not incidental. It is structural.

H7105 *qatsir* (harvest) ← H7114 *qatsar* (to cut, to sever, to reap)
H7093 *qets* (the end) ← H7112 *qatsats* (to cut off)

Both derive from the act of cutting. When the Hebrew mind heard "harvest," it heard the same consonantal family as "the end." And when Jesus said "the harvest *is* the end of the age" (Mat 13:39), He was not importing a foreign metaphor. He was drawing out what was already buried in the language.

But $the-end (G5056 *telos*) means the covenant purpose arriving — the goal reached, not mere cessation. So harvest is the *enactment* of that covenant fulfillment. It is not an event added to the end; it is how the end works. The cutting. The gathering. The separating. The storing or the burning.

This means every harvest passage — agricultural, proverbial, prophetic, apocalyptic — participates in the same pattern. Each one is a small-scale enactment of the principle that will one day operate at cosmic scale.

### Two Harvests, Two Feasts, Two Outcomes

Scripture does not present a single harvest. It presents two, and the distinction appears to be woven into the very calendar of Israel.

> *"Thou shalt keep the feast of **harvest**, the firstfruits of thy labours, which thou hast sown in the field: and the feast of ingathering, which is in the end of the year, when thou hast gathered in thy labours out of the field."* — Exo 23:16

Two feasts. Two crops. Two seasons:

1. **Feast of Harvest** (Weeks / Pentecost) — the spring wheat harvest. Firstfruits brought in. The grain is gathered.
2. **Feast of Ingathering** (Tabernacles / Sukkot) — the autumn grape harvest. The vintage is pressed.

Revelation 14 separates these into two sequential actions:

> *"And I looked, and behold a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown, and in his hand a sharp **sickle**. And another angel came out of the temple, crying with a loud voice to him that sat on the cloud, Thrust in thy **sickle**, and **reap**: for the time is come for thee to **reap**; for the **harvest** of the earth is ripe."* — Rev 14:14–15

This is the grain harvest. The Son of man reaps. The earth is "ripe" (G3583 — dried, ready for cutting). No wrath is mentioned.

Then the second harvest:

> *"And another angel came out from the altar, which had power over $[fire]; and cried with a loud cry to him that had the sharp **sickle**, saying, Thrust in thy sharp **sickle**, and gather the clusters of the vine of the earth; for her grapes are fully ripe."* — Rev 14:18

The grapes are "fully ripe" (G187 *akmazo* — at peak). The angel with authority over $fire issues the command. The grapes go not to a barn but to "the great winepress of the wrath of God" (Rev 14:19), and blood flows to the horse bridles for 1,600 furlongs.

Same sickle. Same earth. Different crops. Different destinations.

The duality is not Revelation's invention. Joel merged them into a single verse — Revelation expanded what Joel compressed:

> *"Put ye in the **sickle**, for the **harvest** is ripe: come, get you down; for the press is full, the fats overflow; for their $[wickedness] is great."* — Joel 3:13

Joel names both instruments in one breath: the sickle (grain harvest) and the winepress (grape harvest). The fullness language — "press is full, fats overflow" — echoes Rev 14:18's "fully ripe." And the trigger is explicit: "their wickedness is great." $wickedness — covenant transgression, lawlessness — reaches its fullness, and the harvest begins.

The pattern suggests that the feasts are not merely commemorative. They are prophetic timetables. Pentecost (Acts 2) appears to be the firstfruits of the grain harvest — the Spirit poured out, the first gathering of the faithful. The grape harvest at Tabernacles remains future, the final ingathering that separates the vintage of wrath from the vintage of joy.

### Ripeness — Why God Waits

Harvest cannot be rushed. A farmer does not thrust in the sickle before the grain is ready. This principle is not agricultural advice in Scripture — it is theological architecture.

> *"For afore the **harvest**, when the bud is perfect, and the sour grape is ripening in the flower, he shall both cut off the sprigs with pruning hooks, and take away and cut down the branches."* — Isa 18:4–5

God watches the ripening. He acts "afore the harvest" to prune, but the harvest itself waits for fullness.

> *"And he said, So is the kingdom of God, as if a man should cast seed into the ground; And should sleep, and rise night and day, and the seed should spring and grow up, he knoweth not how. For the earth bringeth forth $[fruit] of herself; first the blade, then the ear, after that the full corn in the ear. But when the $[fruit] is brought forth, immediately he putteth in the **sickle**, because the **harvest** is come."* — Mk 4:26–29

The kingdom grows through stages — blade, ear, full corn. The sickle appears only "when the fruit is brought forth." The trigger is ripeness, not an arbitrary date.

This pattern connects to a much earlier divine statement:

> *"But in the fourth generation they shall come hither again: for the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full."* — Gen 15:16

God delays Abraham's inheritance because the Amorites' $wickedness has not yet reached fullness. The harvest of judgment waits for the crop to ripen. This is not divine hesitancy — it is the harvest principle operating: accumulated works must reach their fullness before they are gathered to their outcome.

Joel 3:13 confirms: "their wickedness is great" — the fats overflow because the measure is full. Rev 14:15 confirms: "the harvest of the earth is ripe." Rev 14:18 confirms: "her grapes are fully ripe." Every apocalyptic harvest text carries the same prerequisite: **fullness**.

### Sowing and Reaping — The Creation Ordinance

> *"While the earth remaineth, seedtime and **harvest**, and cold and heat, and summer and winter, and day and night shall not cease."* — Gen 8:22

Seedtime and harvest are placed alongside day and night, cold and heat, summer and winter — irreversible creation ordinances. The sow-reap cycle is as fixed as the rotation of the earth. It is not a principle that can be negotiated.

> *"Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also **reap**. For he that soweth to his flesh shall of the flesh **reap** corruption; but he that soweth to the Spirit shall of the Spirit **reap** life everlasting. And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall **reap**, if we faint not."* — Gal 6:7–9

"God is not mocked" — this law is immutable. The harvest *will* come. The only variables are what was sown and when the "due season" (G2540 *kairos* — the appointed time) arrives. The outcome is not in question; only the timing.

And the harvest amplifies the sowing:

> *"For they have sown the wind, and they shall **reap** the whirlwind."* — Hos 8:7

Wind sown, whirlwind reaped. The return exceeds the investment. This is why harvest is simultaneously the righteous person's joy and the wicked person's terror:

> *"They that sow in tears shall **reap** in joy."* — Ps 126:5

> *"They have sown wheat, but shall **reap** thorns."* — Jer 12:13

The same principle, opposite outcomes. $fruit — the works and deeds produced — determines everything. What the $tree of each $nation has produced, the harvest reveals.

### The Wheat and the Tares

Jesus' most detailed harvest parable is the wheat and tares of Matthew 13 — and He provides His own interpretation, leaving no ambiguity about the symbolic identities:

> *"He that soweth the good seed is the Son of man; The field is the world; the good seed are the children of the kingdom; but the tares are the children of the wicked one; The enemy that sowed them is the devil; the **harvest** is the end of the world; and the **reapers** are the angels."* — Mat 13:37–39

The field is the world. The wheat ($grass — mortal people, children of the kingdom) and the tares ($grass — mortal people, children of the wicked one) grow together, indistinguishable until the harvest. The servants want to uproot the tares early, but the master forbids it:

> *"Let both grow together until the **harvest**: and in the time of **harvest** I will say to the **reapers**, Gather ye together first the tares, and bind them in bundles to burn them: but gather the wheat into my barn."* — Mat 13:30

Two crops. Two destinations. The tares go to $fire — the holy standard that consumes what violates covenant. The wheat goes to the barn. The same field, the same growing season, the same sun and rain — but the nature of the seed determines the destination.

The tares are gathered **first** and burned. Then the wheat is stored. The separation is the point. Harvest is not merely gathering — it is the appointed separation that reveals what each plant always was.

### The Fields White to Harvest

Not every harvest text is apocalyptic. Some describe a present reaping:

> *"Say not ye, There are yet four months, and then cometh **harvest**? behold, I say unto you, Lift up your eyes, and look on the fields; for they are white already to **harvest**."* — Jn 4:35

> *"The **harvest** truly is plenteous, but the labourers are few; Pray ye therefore the Lord of the **harvest**, that he will send forth labourers into his **harvest**."* — Mat 9:37–38

Here people are the crop. The $grass of the field — mortal humanity — is ripe for gathering. The laborers are sent to reap what others have sown:

> *"I sent you to **reap** that whereon ye bestowed no labour: other men laboured, and ye are entered into their labours."* — Jn 4:38

One sows, another reaps. The harvest of people into the kingdom follows the same pattern as the eschatological harvest: accumulated readiness ("white already") triggers the gathering. The ripeness of hearts, not the preference of the laborer, determines the timing.

Yet even this gathering-of-people is not disconnected from the final harvest. John 4:36 adds: "he that **reapeth** receiveth wages, and gathereth $[fruit] unto life eternal: that both he that soweth and he that **reapeth** may rejoice together." The $fruit gathered is "unto life eternal" — the same category as the grain harvest of Revelation 14, the same barn destination as the wheat of Matthew 13.

### Babylon's Threshing Floor

> *"The daughter of $[babylon] is like a threshingfloor, it is time to thresh her: yet a little while, and the time of her **harvest** shall come."* — Jer 51:33

$babylon — man's substitute system for God's covenant rule — has its own harvest. But it is not Babylon reaping others; it is Babylon *being* reaped. The threshing floor is where grain is beaten to separate kernel from chaff. Babylon becomes the threshing floor upon which God's judgment operates.

> *"Cut off the sower from $[babylon], and him that handleth the **sickle** in the time of **harvest**."* — Jer 50:16

The sickle is removed *from* Babylon. She who reaped nations will no longer reap. Her own accumulated $wickedness — covenant transgression — reaches its fullness, and she herself is gathered to the winepress. The same principle that governs Gen 15:16 (the iniquity of the Amorites reaching fullness) governs Rev 14:8 ("Babylon is fallen"). The $fire that consumes Babylon comes from within her own system (Ezek 28:18) — $wickedness itself becomes the fuel.

### Gleaning — Mercy Within the Separation

Even in harvest — even in judgment — God mandates mercy:

> *"And when ye **reap** the **harvest** of your land, thou shalt not wholly **reap** the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy **harvest**. . . . thou shalt leave them for the poor, and for the stranger."* — Lev 19:9–10

> *"When thou cuttest down thine **harvest** in thy field, and hast forgot a sheaf in the field, thou shalt not go again to fetch it: it shall be for the stranger, for the fatherless, and for the widow."* — Deut 24:19

The corners are not reaped. The forgotten sheaf is not retrieved. The gleanings are left. Even in the appointed separation, there is provision for those outside the main gathering — the poor, the stranger, the widow.

Ruth enacts this principle. A Moabite — a Gentile, an outsider — gleans in the barley and wheat harvest of Boaz (Ruth 2:3–23). The harvest of Israel becomes her entry point into the covenant community, the lineage of David, and ultimately the lineage of Christ. The gleaning law is not a footnote to harvest — it is the mercy that operates *within* judgment, the remnant provision embedded in the separation itself.

### The Harvest Is Past

Perhaps the most haunting harvest verse is the one that describes a missed season:

> *"The **harvest** is past, the summer is ended, and we are not saved."* — Jer 8:20

The harvest has a window. The appointed time arrives, the crop is ready, and the gathering occurs. Miss it, and the season is gone. The turtledove knows her appointed times (Jer 8:7); God's people do not. The harvest waits for ripeness, but it does not wait forever.

> *"The harvest of the Nile is the revenue of Tyre."* — Isa 23:3 (ESV)

> *"In the day of the desperate grief and incurable sorrow, the **harvest** shall be a heap."* — Isa 17:11 (paraphrased)

When the wrong works are sown, the harvest becomes grief. When the appointed season passes, the opportunity closes. The harvest principle is as certain as day and night (Gen 8:22) — but so is the consequence of sleeping through it:

> *"He that sleepeth in **harvest** is a son that causeth shame."* — Prov 10:5

## Patterns

1. **Harvest = The End enacted.** Both derive from "cutting" roots (H7114 → H7105 ‖ H7112 → H7093). Mat 13:39 equates them. But harvest adds what $the-end alone does not carry: ripeness prerequisite, dual outcome, gathering process, and the role of reapers ($angel).

2. **Two harvests = two feasts = two outcomes.** Grain / Pentecost / Rev 14:14–16 → barn. Grape / Tabernacles / Rev 14:17–20 → winepress. The Torah calendar encodes the eschatological separation.

3. **Ripeness = fullness of works.** Joel 3:13, Rev 14:15, Rev 14:18, Gen 15:16. God waits for accumulated $fruit — whether of faith or of $wickedness — to reach fullness. Harvest is triggered by ripeness, not by arbitrary timing.

4. **Sow-reap = creation ordinance.** Gen 8:22 places it alongside day/night. Gal 6:7 declares it immutable. What is sown determines what is reaped. The only variable is when.

5. **Amplification.** Wind → whirlwind (Hos 8:7). Sparingly → sparingly; bountifully → bountifully (2 Cor 9:6). The harvest exceeds the sowing in both directions.

6. **Gleaning = mercy within judgment.** Lev 19:9, 23:22, Ruth 2. Even in the appointed separation, corners are left unharvested. There is provision for the outsider within the judgment process.

7. **Joel 3:13 → Rev 14 chain.** Joel merges grain and grape harvest into one verse. Revelation expands them into two sequential actions. The OT template governs the apocalyptic fulfillment.

8. **$babylon as threshing floor.** Jer 51:33. Babylon's own accumulated covenant transgression reaches fullness and she is harvested — threshed by the same principle she imposed on others.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$the-end** — Harvest is the mechanism of $the-end — the covenant purpose reaching fulfillment through cutting, gathering, and separating. Mat 13:39 equates them: "the harvest IS the consummation of the age." Harvest enacts what $the-end names.

- **$fire** — The destination of the tares (Mat 13:40–42), the authority behind the grape harvest (Rev 14:18 — the angel "which had power over fire"), and the testing standard of all works (1 Cor 3:13). $fire — God's holy standard in active contact — is what meets the harvest at its conclusion: refining the wheat, consuming the chaff.

- **$grass** — People — the crop that is harvested. Isa 40:6–7 defines flesh as $grass; Mat 13:38 identifies the wheat and tares as "children of the kingdom" and "children of the wicked one." The $grass flourishes briefly, then is cut. Harvest is the cutting.

- **$tree** — Nations and people groups that bear $fruit. Mat 7:19 connects the $tree to the harvest principle: "Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire." The harvest separates nations by their accumulated produce.

- **$fruit** — Works and deeds — what the harvest actually gathers. Gal 5:19–22 equates fruit with works. The harvest does not separate people arbitrarily but by what they have produced. $fruit is the content of the harvest; harvest is the gathering of $fruit.

- **$wine** — Covenant blood — the substance of the grape harvest. Rev 14:19–20 sends the grapes to "the great winepress of the wrath of God." The grape harvest produces $wine — blood poured out. In covenant, wine is joy; outside covenant, wine is wrath. The harvest reveals which.

- **$wickedness** — Covenant transgression whose fullness triggers the harvest. Joel 3:13: "their wickedness is great." Gen 15:16: "the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full." $wickedness accumulates until it reaches the ripeness threshold, and then the sickle is sent.

- **$babylon** — The worldly system whose threshing floor is set (Jer 51:33). Babylon's own accumulated works become her harvest, her own $wickedness the fuel for her $fire. She is not merely judged — she is *reaped*.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **harvest** begins:

> “Har”vest, n. Etym: [OE. harvest, hervest, AS. hærfest autumn; akin to LG. harfst, D. herfst, OHG. herbist, G. herbst, and prob. to L. carpere to pluck, Gr. Carpet.] 1. The gathering of a crop of any kind; the ingathering of the crops; also, the season of gathering grain and fruits, late summer or early autumn. Seedtime and harvest . . . shall not cease. Gen viii. 22. At harvest, when corn is ripe. Tyndale.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Joel 3:13 emphasizes punitive judgment rather than righteous ingathering, and Matthew 13:39 alone identifies harvest with the end of the age without itself stating the wheat/chaff outcomes. Even so, the printed citations strongly support the entry's core identification, with Matthew 13:39 providing an explicit definition.

- One could argue the book implicitly narrows the symbol by omitting the very widespread evangelistic 'harvest of souls' sense (which Responses A and B call 'dominant' in popular usage), so the entry could be read as REFINED (BOOK_SUBSET). However, a short glossary stating the primary meaning without listing alternatives is not a material boundary claim, and the consensus itself ranks the eschatological sense as primary.

- The book omits the widely attested evangelistic sense that consensus lists as a dominant secondary use, but this does not alter the primary core match.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The end of the age, when the field of mankind is reaped — the wheat gathered, the chaff burned.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *harvest*, *reap*, *reaped*, *reapers*, *reapeth*, *sickle*, *harvestman*. The English-form sweep found **95 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
