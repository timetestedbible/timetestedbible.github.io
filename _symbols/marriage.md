# Marriage

**The covenant picture — God as husband, His people as wife, faithfulness as covenant obedience, adultery as idolatry.**

> *"I spread my $[wings] over thee, and covered thy nakedness: yea, I sware unto thee, and entered into a **covenant** with thee, saith the Lord GOD, and thou becamest mine."* — Ezek 16:8

---

## The Key Insight

When God wants to describe His relationship with Israel, He does not reach for the language of king and subject, or master and slave. He reaches for **marriage**.

> *"For thy Maker is thine **husband**; the LORD of hosts is his $[name]."* — Isa 54:5

> *"Turn, O backsliding children, saith the LORD; for I am **married** unto you."* — Jer 3:14

> *"I will **betroth** thee unto me for ever; yea, I will betroth thee unto me in righteousness, and in judgment, and in lovingkindness, and in mercies."* — Hos 2:19

> *"I have **espoused** you to one **husband**, that I may present you as a chaste $[virgin] to Christ."* — 2 Cor 11:2

> *"The **marriage** of the Lamb is come, and his **wife** hath made herself ready."* — Rev 19:7

This is not a metaphor borrowed from human experience and projected onto God. The direction runs the other way. Paul says human marriage exists *because* it pictures a prior reality: "This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church" (Eph 5:32). The human institution is the shadow. The covenant between God and His people is the substance.

Once this is seen, every piece of marriage language in Scripture maps onto covenant language — and several otherwise baffling passages (particularly the divorce texts) suddenly resolve.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant — the binding relationship between God and His people

**Opposite:** Divorce / covenant breaking — the termination of that relationship through $fornication (idolatry)

**Defining verses:** Isa 54:5, Jer 3:14, Hos 2:19, 2 Cor 11:2, Rev 19:7, Ezek 16:8

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word for "skirt" in Ruth's marriage proposal and Ezekiel's covenant-entry is H3671 *kanaph* — the same word translated "wings" throughout the Old Testament. Coming under a husband's "skirt" = coming under God's $wings = entering covenant. The marriage proposal and the covenant covering are the same word.

**Connected:** $fornication, $wings, $virgin, $harlot, $name, $jerusalem

## Marriage Across Scripture

### Wings and Skirts — The Same Covering

The connection between marriage and covenant is not merely thematic. It is embedded in a single Hebrew word that most English readers never see.

In Ruth 3:9, Ruth says to Boaz:

> *"Spread therefore thy **skirt** (H3671 *kanaph*) over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."* — Ruth 3:9

This is a marriage proposal. But the word H3671 *kanaph* is the same word translated "$wings" everywhere else — the $wings of the cherubim over the ark of the covenant (Exod 25:20), the $wings under which the psalmist takes refuge (Ps 91:4), the $wings of healing in Malachi (Mal 4:2), and the $wings of the hen that Jesus offers to $jerusalem (Mat 23:37).

It is also the word for the "borders" of a garment where the fringes (*tzitzit*) are placed: "Make them fringes in the borders (H3671 *kanaph*) of their garments... that ye may remember, and do all my commandments" (Num 15:38–40). The $wings of the garment carry the visible reminder of Torah — the covenant terms.

Ezekiel makes the equation explicit. God describes how He entered into covenant with Israel:

> *"I spread my **skirt** (H3671 *kanaph*) over thee, and covered thy nakedness: yea, I sware unto thee, and entered into a **covenant** with thee, saith the Lord GOD, and thou becamest mine."* — Ezek 16:8

Spreading the *kanaph* = entering a covenant. Ruth's request to Boaz uses the same word. Boaz himself had earlier said: "The LORD God of Israel, under whose **wings** (H3671 *kanaph*) thou art come to trust" (Ruth 2:12). Ruth came under God's covenant $wings, then asked Boaz for the same covering in marriage. The personal covenant mirrors the divine covenant — because both are covenant.

To come under someone's $wings is to come under their covenant. Marriage is how Scripture pictures that entry.

### The Husband's Name

When a woman marries, she takes her husband's $name. This is not merely a cultural convention — it maps directly onto the covenant pattern.

The $name in Scripture represents the covenant relationship itself. God placed His $name on Israel (Deut 28:10: "all people of the earth shall see that thou art called by the **name** of the LORD"). The $name is on the temple (2 Sam 7:13), on the city (Jer 25:29), on the people (2 Chr 7:14: "my people, which are called by my **name**"). To bear someone's $name is to be in covenant with them.

The third commandment — "Thou shalt not take the $name of the LORD thy God in vain" (Exod 20:7) — takes on new weight in the marriage frame. If taking God's $name is entering His covenant (as a bride takes the groom's name), then taking it "in vain" is not about careless speech. It is about living as though the covenant means nothing — claiming the $name while ignoring the terms. It is the spiritual equivalent of wearing the wedding ring and being unfaithful.

### The Faithful Wife and the Harlot — Two Women, Two Covenants

Scripture builds an entire symbolic framework on the two possible conditions of a wife:

The $virgin — faithful, exclusively devoted. "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste $[virgin] to Christ" (2 Cor 11:2). Those in Revelation who are "not defiled with women; for they are $[virgin]s" are those who "follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth" (Rev 14:4) — exclusive covenant loyalty.

The $harlot — unfaithful, serving other masters. "How is the faithful city become an $[harlot]!" (Isa 1:21). $jerusalem starts as a $virgin and becomes a $harlot — not through literal sexual sin, but through idolatry. The entire book of Hosea develops this: the prophet marries "a wife of whoredoms: for the land hath committed great whoredom, departing from the LORD" (Hos 1:2).

The two women of Revelation are the culmination:

- $babylon — "the great $[harlot]" (Rev 17:1), "MOTHER OF $[harlot]S" (Rev 17:5), with whom the kings of the earth commit $fornication (Rev 17:2). The false covenant community, the idolater.
- $jerusalem — "the bride, the Lamb's wife" (Rev 21:9), "prepared as a bride adorned for her husband" (Rev 21:2). The faithful covenant community, the $virgin.

Two women. Two cities. Two conditions of one covenant relationship. The marriage metaphor governs the entire contrast.

### What "Except for Fornication" Actually Means

This is where the marriage-as-covenant framework resolves a passage that has generated centuries of debate:

> *"Whosoever shall put away his wife, **except it be for $[fornication]**, and shall marry another, committeth adultery."* — Mat 19:9

If $fornication means sexual sin, a legal redundancy appears. Under Torah, sexual adultery carries the death penalty:

> *"The man that committeth adultery with another man's wife... the adulterer and the adulteress shall surely be put to death."* — Lev 20:10

If the adulterous spouse is executed, the marriage ends by death. There is no need for a divorce provision. The surviving spouse is free to remarry — not as a divorcee, but as a widow.

So what does "except for $fornication" mean?

$fornication in Scripture is consistently used for **idolatry** — serving other gods. The pattern is unmistakable:

> *"This people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land... and will forsake me, and break my covenant."* — Deut 31:16

> *"The land hath committed great whoredom, departing from the LORD."* — Hos 1:2

> *"Their heart... went a whoring after their idols."* — Ezek 6:9

The exception clause resolves cleanly once the marriage-covenant frame is applied:

- **Sexual adultery** under Torah → death penalty (Lev 20:10). The marriage ends.
- **$fornication (idolatry)** → divorce is permitted (Mat 5:32, Mat 19:9). The covenant partner who has served other gods may be put away.
- **Unequally yoked** → divorce is commanded (Ezra 10:3 — "put away" the foreign wives *and their gods*).

The three categories correspond to three conditions of covenant violation. The first is terminal. The second permits release. The third requires separation. All three make sense only when marriage = covenant and $fornication = idolatry.

### The Marriage of the Lamb

> *"Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the **marriage** of the Lamb is come, and his **wife** hath made herself ready."* — Rev 19:7

The entire arc of Scripture moves toward a wedding. The covenant that began at Sinai — "I am married unto you" (Jer 3:14) — reaches its consummation in Revelation. The $bride is $jerusalem, the covenant community: "I John saw the holy city, new $[jerusalem], coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a **bride** adorned for her **husband**" (Rev 21:2).

The angel says explicitly: "Come hither, I will shew thee the **bride**, the Lamb's **wife**" — and then shows John the city (Rev 21:9–10). The bride is not a building. The $bride is a people. $jerusalem is not stones; it is the covenant community made ready.

The betrothal has already happened — Paul says "I have espoused you to one husband" (2 Cor 11:2). The consummation awaits. The marriage of the Lamb is the final, complete union of God with His people — the covenant fulfilled, the bride made ready, the husband and wife dwelling together without separation.

This is why the entire drama of Scripture can be read as a marriage story: a betrothal (Sinai), an unfaithful wife ($israel going after other gods), a patient husband ("I am married unto you" despite everything), a redemption (the Lamb), and a wedding feast (Rev 19:7–9).

## Patterns

1. **God is always the husband.** Isa 54:5, Jer 3:14, Hos 2:19, Ezek 16:8 — the LORD initiates, proposes, and enters the covenant. The human partner is the wife, the one who responds.

2. **Faithfulness = covenant obedience.** The faithful wife is the $virgin — exclusively devoted, following the Lamb (Rev 14:4). Faithfulness is not emotional affection but covenant loyalty: keeping the terms, remembering the $name.

3. **Adultery = idolatry.** Every time Scripture describes Israel "playing the harlot," it means serving other gods. Ezek 16, Ezek 23, Hosea 1–3, Jer 3 — the metaphor is relentless. Spiritual unfaithfulness is the adultery that breaks the marriage.

4. **$wings = the marriage/covenant covering.** H3671 *kanaph* unifies the marriage proposal (Ruth 3:9), the covenant entry (Ezek 16:8), the Torah reminder (Num 15:38), and the divine shelter (Ps 91:4). Under the $wings is under the covenant is inside the marriage.

5. **The bride is a community, not an individual.** $jerusalem is the bride (Rev 21:2, 9–10). $babylon is the $harlot (Rev 17). The two women are two peoples — the faithful covenant community and the idolatrous counterfeit.

6. **Betrothal now, consummation later.** 2 Cor 11:2 (espoused) → Rev 19:7 (marriage come). The church is in the betrothal phase — legally bound, not yet united. The wedding feast is still ahead.

7. **Divorce provisions reveal what breaks the covenant.** The Torah's legal categories — death for adultery, divorce for $fornication, separation from foreign gods — map precisely onto the spiritual realities of covenant violation.

## Connections

- **$fornication** — Idolatry / serving other gods. This is the stated ground for divorce in the marriage-covenant framework. "Except for $fornication" (Mat 19:9) = except for idolatry. The wife who goes after other gods is the wife who may be put away.

- **$wings** — Covenant covering / Torah reminder. The same Hebrew word (H3671 *kanaph*) means both "wings" and "skirt." Spreading the $wings over someone = entering covenant with them (Ezek 16:8). Ruth's marriage proposal and God's covenant entry use the same term.

- **$virgin** — Faithful / exclusively devoted. The opposite of the $harlot. The $virgin is the bride who has not committed $fornication — she has kept her exclusive devotion to one husband (2 Cor 11:2, Rev 14:4).

- **$harlot** — Idolater / covenant breaker. The unfaithful wife. When $jerusalem becomes a $harlot (Isa 1:21), the marriage-covenant has been violated through the worship of other gods.

- **$name** — Covenant / covenant relationship. Taking the husband's $name = entering the covenant. Bearing the $name "in vain" (Exod 20:7) = living as though the marriage means nothing — claiming the covenant while ignoring its terms.

- **$jerusalem** — Bride / covenant community. The city is the wife. "Thy Maker is thine husband" (Isa 54:5) is spoken to $jerusalem. "The bride, the Lamb's wife" (Rev 21:9) is shown to be the holy city. $jerusalem is not geography — it is the covenant people in their faithful state, the bride made ready.
