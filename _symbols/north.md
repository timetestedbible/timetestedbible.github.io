# North

## Approved Definition

**The hidden quarter and the direction from which concealed invaders emerge. The north country gathers the nations that spoil [Babylon](/research/symbols/babel/); the enemy seeks his seat in its hidden recesses. It is also the direction of God's [mountain](/research/symbols/mountain/).**
> *"And the word of the LORD came unto me the second time, saying, What seest thou? And I said, I see a seething pot; and the face thereof is toward the **north**. Then the LORD said unto me, Out of the **north** an evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the land."* — Jer 1:13–14

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**The hidden quarter and the direction from which concealed invaders emerge. The north country gathers the nations that spoil [Babylon](/research/symbols/babel/); the enemy seeks his seat in its hidden recesses. It is also the direction of God's [mountain](/research/symbols/mountain/).**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **north** begins: “That one of the four cardinal points of the compass, at any place, which lies in the direction of the true meridian, and to the left hand of a person facing the east; the direction opposite to the south.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

The Hebrew word for north is H6828 *tsaphown*. It does not mean "the direction opposite south." It comes from H6845 *tsaphan* — **to hide, to conceal, to store up in secret, to lurk**. The Strong's definition reads: "properly, HIDDEN, i.e. dark; used only of the north as a quarter (gloomy and unknown)."

The north is not first a compass point. It is first a *quality*: the hidden quarter. The direction where things are stored out of sight. The quarter from which what you did not see coming arrives.

This single root illuminates a pattern that runs from Genesis to Revelation. God's throne-presence approaches from the north — a whirlwind out of concealment (Ezek 1:4). God's instruments of destruction approach from the north — nations that the target never anticipated (Jer 1:14). The adversary covets the north — because it is God's hidden seat of authority (Isa 14:13). And in the most striking pattern of all, the *same direction* that sends Babylon to destroy Judah later sends a destroyer against Babylon itself. The hidden quarter does not belong to any earthly power. It belongs to the God who operates from concealment.

> *"Verily thou art a God that hidest thyself, O God of Israel, the Saviour."* — Isa 45:15

**Symbolizes:** The hidden direction — the quarter from which what is concealed approaches: God's veiled glory, God's concealed judgment, and the $snare you never saw being set

**Opposite:** The south (H5045 *negeb* = parched, dry, visible; H8486 *teman* = right hand when facing east — the known, open, exposed direction)

**Defining verses:** Jer 1:13–14, Ezek 1:4, Ps 48:2, Isa 14:13, Jer 50:3

**The surprise:** The word family of H6845 *tsaphan* produces not only "north" but also H4710 *mitspun* (a secret thing), H6840 *tsaphiyn* (a treasure, as something hidden), and even the prophet's name Zephaniah (H6846) — "Yah has secreted/hidden." The entire cluster encodes concealment: hoarding, lurking, protecting, treasuring in darkness.

**Connected:** $lion, $bear, $serpent, $dragon, $fire, $snare, $babylon, $throne

## North Across Scripture

### The Throne in the Hidden Quarter

> *"Beautiful for situation, the joy of the whole earth, is mount Zion, on the sides of the **north**, the city of the great King."* — Ps 48:2

Mount Zion — God's throne-seat — is identified with the "sides of the north." Not the geographical north of Israel, but the *symbolic* north: the hidden quarter where the great King dwells. God's authority resides in concealment.

This makes the adversary's ambition in Isaiah 14 precise:

> *"I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation, in the sides of the **north**: I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the most High."* — Isa 14:13

The boast is not "I will go northward." It is "I will sit in *the hidden seat*" — the concealed place of divine authority. The north is contested territory because it is $throne territory. The adversary wants to occupy the quarter that belongs to God alone.

And Job 26:7 stretches this further into cosmology:

> *"He stretcheth out the **north** over the empty place, and hangeth the earth upon nothing."* — Job 26:7

The north — the hidden direction — is stretched over *tohu* (H8414), the same word used for the formless void of Gen 1:2. God's concealed authority covers the chaos. The hidden direction is a canopy over the abyss.

### The Whirlwind from Concealment

> *"And I looked, and, behold, a whirlwind came out of the **north**, a great cloud, and a $[fire] infolding itself, and a brightness was about it, and out of the midst thereof as the colour of amber, out of the midst of the $[fire]."* — Ezek 1:4

Ezekiel's throne-vision — the foundation of all biblical theophany — approaches from the north. The $fire, the cloud, the brightness, the living creatures, the wheels, the throne, the One seated upon it: all of it arrives from the hidden quarter.

This is not incidental geography. Ezekiel is in Babylon, by the river Chebar, and what meets him is the glory of God emerging from the direction of concealment. God's glory does not arrive from the visible south. It approaches from the quarter defined by its hiddenness — and when it arrives, it is overwhelming, blinding, impossible to look away from.

The north hides things. When what the north hides is God's own presence, the unveiling is cataclysmic.

### Evil from the North — Jeremiah's Drumbeat

No prophet uses the phrase "from the north" more relentlessly than Jeremiah. It becomes a drumbeat through the first half of the book — a warning that Judah will not heed because the threat is, by definition, *hidden*:

> *"Set up the standard toward Zion: retire, stay not: for I will bring evil from the **north**, and a great destruction."* — Jer 4:6

> *"A great nation and many people shall be raised up from the sides of the earth… they are cruel, and have no mercy; their voice roareth like the sea."* — Jer 6:22–23

> *"Behold, a noise of the bruit is come, and a great commotion out of the **north** country, to make the cities of Judah desolate, and a den of $[dragon]s."* — Jer 10:22

> *"Behold, I will send and take all the families of the **north**, saith the LORD, and Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant."* — Jer 25:9

Babylon is the instrument — but the emphasis is never on Babylon's identity. It is on the *direction*: from the north, from the hidden quarter, from the direction you cannot see. Nebuchadnezzar is called "my servant" — God's hidden agent, stored up in the north for the appointed time.

The connection to the $snare is structural. Proverbs 1:17 says, "Surely in vain the net is spread in the sight of any bird" — if you can see the trap, it fails. The north *is* the direction of concealment, and judgment from the north *is* judgment from concealment. It arrives as a $snare: unseen until it springs shut.

The Judah-destroyer even carries a $lion identity:

> *"The **$[lion]** is come up from his thicket, and the destroyer of the Gentiles is on his way; he is gone forth from his place to make thy land desolate."* — Jer 4:7

A $lion from concealment. A beast from the hidden quarter. The same image will return — reversed.

### The Boomerang — Same Direction, Reversed Target

This may be the most striking pattern in the north symbolism. The very same language Jeremiah uses for Babylon's destruction of *Judah* reappears — nearly word for word — for the destruction of *Babylon*:

| Judah's Destroyer | Babylon's Destroyer |
|---|---|
| "Out of the **north** an evil shall break forth" (Jer 1:14) | "Out of the **north** there cometh up a nation against her" (Jer 50:3) |
| "A great nation from the sides of the earth" (Jer 6:22) | "A people… and many kings, shall be raised up from the coasts of the earth" (Jer 50:41) |
| "The $[lion] is come up from his thicket" (Jer 4:7) | "He shall come up like a $[lion] from the swelling of Jordan" (Jer 50:44) |

And then the culmination:

> *"And the heaven and the earth, and all that is therein, shall sing for $[babylon]; for the spoilers shall come unto her **from the north**, saith the LORD."* — Jer 51:48

The hidden direction does not belong to Babylon. It belongs to God. When God stored Babylon in the north to destroy Judah, Babylon may have imagined itself as the hidden power. But the north stores judgment for *every* target. The instrument becomes the victim. The same concealed approach, the same $lion imagery, the same "from the north" — now turned against the one who wielded it.

The executor never sees itself becoming the executed. That is the nature of the hidden direction: what it conceals is revealed only at the moment of impact.

### The North Gate — Opposite Traffic, Same Door

Ezekiel's temple visions concentrate a remarkable tension at the north gate:

- **Ezek 8:3** — The "image of jealousy" (an idol) is installed at the north gate entrance.
- **Ezek 8:14** — Women weeping for Tammuz (a pagan fertility god) sit at the north gate.
- **Ezek 9:2** — Six executioners approach from "the way of the higher gate, which lieth toward the **north**," each with a slaughter weapon.

The north gate is where false worship installs itself — *and* where divine judgment enters. The same threshold carries opposite traffic. The abomination squats at the hidden door, imagining itself secure in the concealed quarter. The executioners enter through that very door.

This is the contested-throne pattern of Ps 48:2 and Isa 14:13 played out architecturally. The hidden quarter belongs to God. Whatever occupies it without authority will be met by what God sends through it.

### Gog from the North Parts

> *"And thou shalt come from thy place out of the **north parts**, thou, and many people with thee, all of them riding upon horses, a great company, and a mighty army."* — Ezek 38:15

> *"And I will turn thee back, and leave but the sixth part of thee, and will cause thee to come up from the **north parts**, and will bring thee upon the mountains of Israel."* — Ezek 39:2

Gog's invasion follows the same template as Jeremiah's "nation from the north" — from the hidden quarter, with "many people," against God's land. The language of Ezek 38:15 ("many people with thee") parallels Jer 50:41 ("many kings shall be raised up"). The structural pattern is consistent: the eschatological threat emerges from concealment, with overwhelming numbers, approaching from the direction you cannot see until it arrives.

But like every power that comes from the north, Gog does not own the direction. God *causes* him to come (Ezek 39:2). The hidden quarter is God's staging ground, not the invader's home territory.

### Daniel's King of the North

Daniel 11 unfolds an extended conflict between the "king of the south" and the "king of the north" — a sustained opposition that runs through history. The north consistently represents the hidden, threatening adversary:

> *"And the king of the **north** shall come against him like a whirlwind, with chariots, and with horsemen, and with many ships."* — Dan 11:40

The word "whirlwind" directly echoes Ezek 1:4 — the whirlwind from the north. In Ezekiel, it carries God's throne-glory. In Daniel, the king of the north mimics the approach: whirlwind, overwhelming force, sweeping from concealment. The adversary imitates the divine pattern — just as Isa 14:13 describes the attempt to sit in the sides of the north.

Yet even this final king of the north is subject to disruption from the same direction:

> *"But tidings out of the east and out of the **north** shall trouble him."* — Dan 11:44

No one controls the hidden quarter. Even the king who claims it receives news from it that he did not anticipate.

### The Horses That Quiet God's Spirit

> *"The black horses which are therein go forth into the **north** country; and the white go forth after them… Then cried he upon me, and spake unto me, saying, Behold, these that go toward the **north** country have quieted my spirit in the **north** country."* — Zech 6:6, 8

Zechariah's four chariots go in the four directions. The horses sent to the north **quiet** (H5117 *nuach* — to rest, settle, give rest) God's spirit (H7307 *ruach*). Something about the execution of judgment in the hidden quarter *satisfies* divine justice. The direction where wrath was stored now receives the agents that lay it to rest.

This is the resolution of the "north" tension: God's judgment, issued from concealment, returns to concealment once fulfilled. What was stored up is expended. What was hidden is completed. The spirit rests.

## Patterns

1. **The root IS the meaning.** H6828 *tsaphown* from H6845 *tsaphan* = to hide. North is not a direction that happens to be dark — it is *the hidden direction* by definition. Every use carries this quality of concealment.

2. **Dual function — glory and judgment.** God's throne-presence (Ezek 1:4, Ps 48:2) and God's destructive instruments (Jer 1:14, Jer 50:3) both approach from the north. Both operate from hiddenness because God Himself operates from hiddenness (Isa 45:15).

3. **The boomerang.** The same direction, the same language, the same $lion imagery — first used against Judah (Jer 4:6–7), then turned against $babylon (Jer 50:3, 41, 44). The hidden direction does not belong to any earthly instrument. The executor becomes the executed, and never sees it coming.

4. **The contested threshold.** Ps 48:2 — God's throne on the sides of the north. Isa 14:13 — the adversary covets the same seat. Ezek 8 — abomination at the north gate. Ezek 9 — executioners enter through the north gate. The hidden quarter is God's domain; whatever usurps it is met at the door.

5. **Concealment = $snare.** Judgment from the north is judgment from concealment — the trap that works because it is not seen (Prov 1:17). The $snare and the north share the same operative principle: hiddenness is what gives the mechanism its power.

6. **"Many people" — the northern invasion template.** Jer 50:41 "many kings" ‖ Ezek 38:15 "many people" ‖ Dan 11:40 "chariots and horsemen and many ships." The eschatological approach from the north consistently involves overwhelming numbers emerging from concealment.

7. **Obscuring from the north.** "From the north a smoke" (Isa 14:31). The hidden direction produces what obscures — the north is where the darkening originates. Concealment generates further concealment.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$lion** — The Judah-destroyer is "a lion from his thicket" (Jer 4:7); the Babylon-destroyer also comes "like a lion" (Jer 50:44) — both from the north. The $lion (kingly authority that devours) always approaches from the hidden quarter in Jeremiah's oracles. The concealment of the north is the thicket from which the lion springs.

- **$snare** — The north and the $snare share the same operative principle: hiddenness. A snare works because it is not seen (Prov 1:17). Judgment from the north works because the target does not see it coming. The north *is* the direction of the snare.

- **$fire** — Ezek 1:4 — $fire infolding itself comes from the north with God's throne-glory. The hidden direction conceals the $fire of God's presence until the moment of theophany. Fire stored in concealment, released at revelation.

- **$dragon** — The cities of Judah become "a den of dragons" when the north-invader strikes (Jer 10:22). The $dragon — chaos, desolation — follows in the wake of what the hidden direction sends.

- **$throne** — God's $throne is on the "sides of the north" (Ps 48:2), and the adversary covets that seat (Isa 14:13). The north is $throne territory — the hidden seat of divine authority.

- **$babylon** — $babylon is both the instrument God stores in the north to destroy Judah AND the target destroyed by a nation from the north. Babylon imagines it *is* the north. The boomerang reveals it was only a tool stored there temporarily.

- **$bear** — Among the beasts that come as judgment. The $bear (devouring power, overwhelming force) fits the "many people" pattern of the northern invasion.

- **$serpent** — The $serpent operates by cunning concealment — the same operative quality as the north. The hidden direction and the hidden adversary share a structural kinship.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **north** begins:

> “North, n. Etym: [AS. noredh; akin to D. noord, G., Sw., & Dan. nord, Icel. noredhr. Cf. Norman, Norse.] 1. That one of the four cardinal points of the compass, at any place, which lies in the direction of the true meridian, and to the left hand of a person facing the east; the direction opposite to the south.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Jeremiah supports northern invaders and Revelation supports ten kings turning against the whore, but the printed texts do not identify those groups with one another. Nor do Psalm 48:2 and Isaiah 14:13 establish that the north is itself the enemy’s seat; they more naturally locate the divine city or assembly there, with the enemy seeking to usurp it.

- One could argue the book's core is actually the Satanic-seat reading — a consensus-listed minority alternative promoted to primary — combined with an eschatological Rev 17 identification, which arguably contradicts the mainstream reading of Ps 48:2 (Zion as God's city) and replaces the historical foe-from-the-north referent; that would push toward DIVERGENT. However, the printed verses (Jer 1:14; 50:9, 41; 51:48) show the book still centering evil/invasion from the north, and the Rev 17 link, while a leap, is an added layer rather than a denial of the shared core.

- Consensus (and historical-geographic grounding) treats north primarily as the route of judgment on Judah via Babylon, not definitionally 'hidden' or as the precise vehicle of Rev 17's ten kings against the whore; the satanic reading of Isa 14:13 and the Jer–Rev fusion are minority/non-standard.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The hidden quarter and the direction from which concealed invaders emerge. The north country gathers the nations that spoil [Babylon](/research/symbols/babel/); the enemy seeks his seat in its hidden recesses. It is also the direction of God's [mountain](/research/symbols/mountain/).**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *north*, *northern*, *northward*, *north parts*, *north country*, *north side*, *north wind*, *the north country*. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.
