### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **peace** begins: “A state of quiet or tranquillity; freedom from disturbance or agitation; calm; repose; specifically: (a) Exemption from, or cessation of, war with public enemies. (b) Public quiet, order, and contentment in obedience to law. (c) Exemption from, or subjection of, agitating passions; tranquillity of mind or conscience. (d) Reconciliation; agreement after variance; harmony; concord.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

Peace is the restoration and completion of covenant order through righteousness, obedience, and the gospel; it is distinguished from worldly truce, subjective feeling, and counterfeit declarations of peace where covenant remains broken.

> *“And the work of righteousness shall be peace; and the effect of righteousness quietness and assurance for ever.”* — Isaiah 32:17

> *“Great peace have they which love thy law: and nothing shall offend them.”* — Psalms 119:165

> *“O that thou hadst hearkened to my commandments! then had thy peace been as a river, and thy righteousness as the waves of the sea:”* — Isaiah 48:18

> *“Wherefore say, Behold, I give unto him my covenant of peace:”* — Numbers 25:12

> *“And I will make with them a covenant of peace, and will cause the evil beasts to cease out of the land: and they shall dwell safely in the wilderness, and sleep in the woods.”* — Ezekiel 34:25

> *“Moreover I will make a covenant of peace with them; it shall be an everlasting covenant with them: and I will place them, and multiply them, and will set my sanctuary in the midst of them for evermore.”* — Ezekiel 37:26

> *“How beautiful upon the mountains are the feet of him that bringeth good tidings, that publisheth peace; that bringeth good tidings of good, that publisheth salvation; that saith unto Zion, Thy God reigneth!”* — Isaiah 52:7

> *“For he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us;”* — Ephesians 2:14

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- The case could be classified MATCH if 'not a feeling' is read only as denying that peace is essentially or merely a feeling, since the consensus likewise makes objective reconciliation primary. Nevertheless, the book's categorical boundary language materially narrows cases that the consensus expressly recognizes as authentic dimensions.

- One could argue MATCH: the consensus already defines peace as covenantal shalom-wholeness rather than absence of conflict, cites John 14:27 and Jer 6:14, and 'covenant made whole' could be read as just a compressed restatement of 'restored right relationship with God.' On that reading the exclusion of 'a feeling' merely echoes the consensus's own 'more than inner calm' caveat rather than genuinely rejecting the tranquility facet.

- Elevating “work of righteousness” and “love the law” as definitional risks sidelining the consensus NT center that peace with God is received by faith apart from works of the law (e.g., Rom 5:1), shifting the soteriological condition.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
