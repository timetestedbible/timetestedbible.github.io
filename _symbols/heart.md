# Heart

## Approved Definition

**Jerusalem — the navel and heart of the land.**
> *"Keep thy **heart** with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life."* — Prov 4:23

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**Jerusalem — the navel and heart of the land.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **heart** begins: “(Anat.) Defn: A hollow, muscular organ, which, by contracting rhythmically, keeps up the circulation of the blood.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

Scripture never treats the heart as a pump. It never treats it as the center of emotion. It treats it as the **interior chamber where allegiance is formed** — and everything else flows from there.

The Hebrew root tells the story. H3823 *labab* means "to be enclosed" — the heart is the enclosed interior, the locked room behind the $lips. What occupies that room determines the whole person's trajectory. Proverbs 4:23 makes this explicit: the word "issues" is H8444 *totsaoth* — outgoings, exits, sources. The heart is not where feelings are felt. It is where the *course of life originates*.

This is why God's fundamental command targets it: "Thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine **heart**" (Deut 6:5). Not "feel warmly toward God" — *pledge total allegiance to God*. The Shema is not an emotion command. It is a covenant command. The heart is where that covenant is either kept or broken, received or resisted, written or hardened against.

And this is why the single most important transformation in all of prophetic Scripture is a heart-change:

> *"A new **heart** also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony **heart** out of your flesh, and I will give you an **heart** of flesh."* — Ezek 36:26

Stone does not receive writing. Flesh does. The entire arc of redemptive history — from $egypt's Pharaoh to the $renewed-covenant — can be told as a story about what happens to the human heart.

**Symbolizes:** The seat of covenant allegiance — where loyalty, will, and response to God are held; determines whether a person receives or resists the word

**Opposite:** $lips / mouth — outward profession without inward allegiance. "This people draw near me with their mouth, and with their **lips** do honour me, but have removed their **heart** far from me" (Isa 29:13; quoted by Jesus in Mat 15:8, Mk 7:6)

**Defining verses:** Deut 6:5, Prov 4:23, Jer 17:9–10, Jer 31:33, Ezek 36:26, Mat 15:18–19

**The surprise:** The heart has six distinct *states* in Scripture — hardened, circumcised, broken, written-upon, fleshy, pure — and they form a trajectory. The same heart that began as Pharaoh's stone can become the tablet of the $renewed-covenant. The progression is: stone → circumcised → broken → inscribed → flesh → pure. Ezek 36:26 is the full arc in one verse.

**Connected:** $rock, $fire, $circumcision, $egypt, $law, $spirit, $faith, $peace-shalom, $alarmed-fear, $deceive

## Heart Across Scripture

### The Total Allegiance Command

> *"And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine **heart**, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might."* — Deut 6:5

This verse — the Shema — is repeated more than any other command in Scripture: Deut 10:12, Deut 11:13, Deut 30:6, Josh 22:5, Mat 22:37, Mk 12:30, Lk 10:27. Every repetition centers on the heart. The word "all" (H3605 *kol*) appears three times in the original command — *all* heart, *all* soul, *all* might. No remainder held in reserve.

The heart appears first in the sequence. The soul and strength follow from it. This suggests the heart is the *origin* of allegiance, not merely one of its expressions. If the heart is wholly oriented toward God, the rest follows. If the heart is divided, soul and strength are misdirected no matter how vigorously they are applied.

God's own response confirms this priority:

> *"O that there were such an **heart** in them, that they would fear me, and keep all my commandments always."* — Deut 5:29

God does not say "O that they would try harder." He identifies the root: the heart. The organ of covenant decision. If it were oriented rightly, obedience would follow naturally. The problem is never effort. The problem is allegiance.

### Pharaoh's Heart — The Hardening Cycle

No passage in Scripture explores the heart's covenant function more exhaustively than Exodus 4–14, where Pharaoh's heart is mentioned over **twenty times**. Three distinct agents harden it:

**Pharaoh hardens his own heart:** Exod 8:15, 8:32, 9:34 — after the plagues relent, Pharaoh re-hardens. Each time he sees relief, he re-entrenches. The Hebrew in 8:15 and 8:32 is H3513 *kabed* — to make heavy, to make burdensome. The heart accumulates weight.

**God hardens Pharaoh's heart:** Exod 4:21, 7:3, 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 10:27, 11:10, 14:4, 14:8, 14:17 — God uses H2388 *chazaq* (to strengthen, to make firm). God does not introduce something new into Pharaoh's heart. He *firms up* what is already there.

**The heart "was hardened" (passive):** Exod 7:13–14, 7:22, 8:19, 9:7, 9:35 — the result, without named agent.

The pattern is sequential: Pharaoh first hardens himself → God then confirms the hardening → the heart becomes locked. A heart that repeatedly refuses evidence becomes, eventually, a heart that *cannot* respond. The same $fire that should have melted him — the plagues — only baked the clay harder.

This is $egypt's signature. The heart of Pharaoh *is* the heart of the system: self-deifying authority that refuses to recognize God's hand, enslaves through provision, and hardens under judgment rather than yielding. Every subsequent reference to hardened hearts in Scripture echoes this archetype:

> *"Wherefore then do ye harden your **hearts**, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their **hearts**?"* — 1 Sam 6:6

> *"Harden not your **hearts**, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness."* — Ps 95:8 (quoted in Heb 3:8, 3:15, 4:7)

Hebrews applies the warning directly to its readers: "To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your **hearts**" (Heb 3:15). The Pharaoh cycle is not a historical curiosity. It is a standing warning that hearts harden *incrementally*, each refusal making the next refusal easier, until the stone is set.

### The Writing Surface — Stone Tablets to Flesh Tablets

If the heart is where covenant allegiance lives, then it must be *inscribable* — it must be a surface that receives the covenant terms. Scripture develops this explicitly:

> *"Write them upon the table of thine **heart**."* — Prov 3:3, 7:3

> *"The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron, and with the point of a diamond: it is graven upon the table of their **heart**."* — Jer 17:1

The heart is a tablet. And whatever is written there — whether mercy and truth (Prov 3:3) or sin (Jer 17:1) — becomes the operating program of the whole person. The writing metaphor bridges directly to the most important covenant promise in the prophets:

> *"I will put my $[law] in their inward parts, and write it in their **hearts**; and will be their God, and they shall be my people."* — Jer 31:33

This is the $renewed-covenant. The $law — God's covenant terms — moves from external stone tablets to the internal writing surface of the heart. The location changes; the content does not. What was carved on Sinai's stone is now inscribed on living tissue.

Paul makes the transfer explicit:

> *"Ye are our epistle written in our **hearts**, known and read of all men… written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not in tables of stone, but in fleshy tables of the **heart**."* — 2 Cor 3:2–3

The contrast — "not tables of stone, but fleshy tables of the heart" — points back to Ezekiel's defining verse. Stone cannot receive living inscription. Flesh can. The heart must be *changed in composition* before the law can be written on it.

### Stone to Flesh — The Heart Transformed

> *"And I will give them one **heart**, and I will put a new $[spirit] within them; and I will take the stony **heart** out of their flesh, and will give them an **heart** of flesh."* — Ezek 11:19

> *"A new **heart** also will I give you, and a new $[spirit] will I put within you: and I will take away the stony **heart** out of your flesh, and I will give you an **heart** of flesh."* — Ezek 36:26

The stony heart is the Pharaoh-heart — the one that hardens under $fire, that refuses evidence, that cannot be inscribed with the law because stone does not receive ink. The heart of flesh is the covenant-ready heart — responsive, inscribable, alive.

Notice: this is something **God does**, not something the person achieves. "I will give… I will put… I will take away… I will give." The same God who told Israel to circumcise their hearts (Deut 10:16) and to make themselves a new heart (Ezek 18:31) is the one who *performs the surgery* (Deut 30:6, Ezek 36:26). What He commands, He also provides. The human responsibility and the divine gift are not contradictions — they are two sides of the same covenant dynamic.

The connection to $fire is illuminating. The same $fire that hardens clay destroys stone but refines gold. A stony heart under $fire becomes harder — Pharaoh after each plague. But a fleshy heart under $fire becomes purer — "the trial of your $[faith], being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with $[fire]" (1 Pet 1:7). The material determines the outcome, not the fire. Ezek 36:26 changes the *material*.

### The Circumcised Heart — Removing the Covering

> *"Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your **heart**, and be no more stiffnecked."* — Deut 10:16

> *"And the LORD thy God will circumcise thine **heart**, and the **heart** of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine **heart**, and with all thy soul, that thou mayest live."* — Deut 30:6

$circumcision of the heart is the removal of what covers and obstructs — the barrier between the person's interior allegiance and God. The physical sign enacted on the body points to this interior reality. And again, both agents appear: "Circumcise yourselves" (Deut 10:16, Jer 4:4) and "God will circumcise" (Deut 30:6).

The purpose of heart-circumcision is stated explicitly: "to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart." Circumcision does not create the heart's capacity for allegiance. It *removes what blocks it*. The foreskin — the covering — must go before the heart can fulfill the Shema.

The prophets and apostles draw the line sharply between circumcised and uncircumcised hearts:

> *"Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in **heart** and ears, ye do always resist the Holy Ghost."* — Acts 7:51

> *"He is a Jew, which is one inwardly; and $[circumcision] is that of the **heart**, in the $[spirit], and not in the letter."* — Rom 2:29

The uncircumcised heart — still covered, still obstructed — resists the $spirit. It bars entry to the sanctuary (Ezek 44:7, 9). An uncircumcised heart in the temple is an abomination: covenant membership claimed without covenant allegiance given.

Jeremiah adds a terrifying urgency: "Circumcise yourselves to the LORD, and take away the foreskins of your **heart**, ye men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem: lest my fury come forth like $[fire], and burn that none can quench it" (Jer 4:4). The uncircumcised heart is fuel. The $fire of God's covenant standard contacts it and consumes it — not because the fire changed, but because the material is wrong.

### The Broken Heart — What God Values

> *"The sacrifices of God are a broken $[spirit]: a broken and a contrite **heart**, O God, thou wilt not despise."* — Ps 51:17

> *"The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken **heart**; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit."* — Ps 34:18

> *"For thus saith the high and lofty One that inhabiteth eternity, whose name is Holy; I dwell in the high and holy place, with him also that is of a contrite and humble spirit, to revive the spirit of the humble, and to revive the **heart** of the contrite ones."* — Isa 57:15

The broken heart appears paradoxical: how can the same God who wants a *whole* heart (H8535 *tam*, as with the $dove) also value a *broken* one? The answer lies in what is being broken. The stone must crack before it can be removed. The hardened crust that formed through self-will and repeated refusal — *that* is what breaks. Brokenness is not weakness. It is the shattering of resistance.

This is why Ps 51:10 pairs the two: "Create in me a clean **heart**, O God; and renew a right spirit within me." The broken heart of v. 17 is the prerequisite for the clean heart of v. 10. The old allegiance must shatter before the new one can be installed.

God dwells in two places: the high and holy place, and the broken heart (Isa 57:15). The highest heaven and the humblest interior. These are not different locations to God. They are the same: wherever resistance to His covenant has been removed.

### The Source of Everything — What Comes Out

> *"For out of the **heart** proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false witness, blasphemies."* — Mat 15:19

> *"For from within, out of the **heart** of men, proceed evil thoughts, adulteries, fornications, murders, thefts, covetousness, wickedness, $[deceive], lasciviousness, an evil eye, blasphemy, pride, foolishness."* — Mk 7:21–22

Jesus establishes the heart as the *source* of defilement — not what goes in through the mouth, but what comes out from the heart. The Pharisees' error was treating the exterior as primary: wash the hands, avoid contaminating food. Jesus reverses the direction. The heart contaminates the person, not the environment.

This echoes the oldest assessment of the human heart in Scripture:

> *"And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his **heart** was only evil continually."* — Gen 6:5

> *"The imagination of man's **heart** is evil from his youth."* — Gen 8:21

The flood did not fix the heart. Genesis 8:21 comes *after* the flood — the same assessment as before the waters. The exterior judgment (water) could not reach the interior problem (heart). This is why Ezekiel's promise is so radical: not another flood, not another external reset, but a *heart transplant*.

Jeremiah names the diagnosis:

> *"The **heart** is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it? I the LORD search the **heart**, I try the reins."* — Jer 17:9–10

The heart $deceives — it misrepresents its own allegiances. Only God can search it. The self-assessment of the heart is unreliable. This is why outward profession ($lips) and inward allegiance ($heart) can diverge so dramatically — the heart hides its own condition even from its owner.

### The Fainting Heart — Alarm When Allegiance Wavers

> *"Let not your **heart** be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me."* — Jn 14:1

> *"$[Peace] I leave with you, my $[peace] I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your **heart** be troubled, neither let it be afraid."* — Jn 14:27

The "troubled heart" (G5015 *tarasso* — agitated, roiled) is the heart of $alarmed-fear — the covenant-judgment panic response that belongs to those who do not recognize God's hand. Jesus prohibits it. The antidote is $peace-shalom — covenant-wholeness, not absence of conflict.

The Old Testament establishes the same pattern:

> *"And lest your **heart** faint, and ye fear for the rumour that shall be heard in the land; a ruler against a ruler."* — Jer 51:46

Jeremiah warns God's people inside $babylon: do not let your heart faint when you hear the rumours of war. The fainting heart is the one whose allegiance has been shaken by circumstances — not because the circumstances are trivial, but because the heart looks at events rather than at the covenant-keeper.

> *"What man is there that is fearful and fainthearted? let him go and return unto his house, lest his brethren's **heart** faint as well as his **heart**."* — Deut 20:8

The fainthearted are contagious. A wavering allegiance in one heart destabilizes the hearts around it. The Deuteronomy command removes the fainthearted soldier before battle — not as punishment, but as quarantine. The heart that trusts the covenant can stand. The heart that does not will infect others.

The resolution is Philippians 4:7: "$[Peace] of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your **hearts** and minds through Christ Jesus." The word "keep" is G5432 *phroureo* — to garrison, to guard with a military detachment. $peace-shalom — covenant-wholeness — does not merely comfort the heart. It *garrisons* it. Stations soldiers around it. The troubled heart is defended by the peace that comes from settled allegiance.

### Where Your Treasure Is

> *"For where your treasure is, there will your **heart** be also."* — Mat 6:21

The heart follows the treasure. Not: *the treasure follows the heart*. Jesus does not say your heart determines your treasure. He says your treasure determines your *heart*. Whatever you invest in most deeply will capture your allegiance. The heart is drawn to what it has committed to.

This reframes the purity command:

> *"Blessed are the pure in **heart**: for they shall see God."* — Mat 5:8

A pure heart (G2513 *katharos* — clean, unmixed) is a heart with *one* treasure — one allegiance, undivided. The word carries the same "unmixed" sense as G185 *akeraios* (the $dove word in Mat 10:16). The pure heart is the undivided heart, and the undivided heart sees God. Not because purity earns the vision, but because a divided heart cannot perceive what it is not fully oriented toward.

> *"But that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good **heart**, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience."* — Lk 8:15

The heart is the soil. Same seed, different hearts, different harvests. The word that falls on the good heart — the heart whose allegiance is rightly oriented — produces fruit. The heart that is hardened (the wayside), shallow (stony ground), or divided (thorny ground) does not. The parable is a heart-taxonomy.

## The Six States of the Heart

Scripture presents the heart in six distinct conditions. These are not random — they form a discernible trajectory from resistance to covenant-readiness:

### 1. Hardened / Stony — Locked Against Covenant

The Pharaoh-heart. Accumulates weight through repeated refusal. The same evidence that should soften instead solidifies. Stone cannot receive inscription. Exod 4–14, Ps 95:8, Heb 3:8, Isa 6:10, Jn 12:40.

### 2. Circumcised — Covering Removed

The obstructing barrier is cut away. What hid the heart is removed so that allegiance can be offered. Commanded by God (Deut 10:16, Jer 4:4), performed by God (Deut 30:6). Rom 2:29, Acts 7:51.

### 3. Broken / Contrite — Resistance Shattered

The stony self-will cracks. Not destruction but demolition — the removal of what was never supposed to be there. God draws near to this heart (Ps 34:18) and dwells with it (Isa 57:15). Ps 51:17.

### 4. Written Upon — Receives the Covenant Terms

The inscribable surface. The law moves from external tablets to interior tissue. The heart becomes the document of the covenant itself. Jer 31:33, Heb 8:10, 10:16, 2 Cor 3:3, Rom 2:15.

### 5. Fleshy / New — Alive and Responsive

The heart transplant of Ezek 36:26. Stone removed, flesh given. A new operating system — the heart responds to $fire by being refined rather than hardened. Ezek 11:19, 1 Sam 10:9 (Saul receives "another heart").

### 6. Pure — Undivided Allegiance

The covenant-ready heart. Single treasure (Mat 6:21), single allegiance (Mat 5:8), the law fully inscribed and operative. Ps 24:4 — "clean hands, and a pure heart" = those who ascend the Lord's hill. Ps 73:1 — "God is good to Israel, even to such as are of a clean heart." 1 Tim 1:5, 2 Tim 2:22, 1 Pet 1:22.

**The full arc:** Stone (Pharaoh) → circumcised (covering removed) → broken (resistance shattered) → inscribed (law written) → flesh (alive) → pure (undivided). Ezekiel 36:26 compresses the entire trajectory into one verse: stone out, flesh in, $spirit installed, law inscribed.

## Patterns

1. **The heart is the origin, not the response.** Gen 6:5, Prov 4:23, Mat 15:18–19 — what comes *out* of the heart determines the course of life. The heart is not shaped by circumstances; it shapes everything downstream.

2. **Hardening is incremental and irreversible without divine intervention.** Pharaoh self-hardens → God confirms → the heart locks. The sequence appears in every hardening passage. No one's heart hardens in a single moment.

3. **What God commands, God provides.** "Circumcise your hearts" (Deut 10:16) → "God will circumcise your heart" (Deut 30:6). "Make you a new heart" (Ezek 18:31) → "I will give you a new heart" (Ezek 36:26). Human responsibility and divine gift are not contradictions.

4. **The heart's opposite is the lips.** Isa 29:13 — lips near, heart far. Mat 15:8 — Jesus quotes it against the Pharisees. The heart/lips split is the defining diagnostic of false covenant membership. Outward profession without inward allegiance.

5. **The heart determines how $fire is experienced.** Stone under fire → harder (Pharaoh). Flesh under fire → refined (1 Pet 1:7). Gold under fire → purified (Mal 3:2–3). The $fire does not change. The heart's composition determines whether judgment destroys or refines.

6. **The heart is searchable only by God.** Jer 17:9–10 — it $deceives even its owner. Self-diagnosis is unreliable. Only "I the LORD search the heart" reaches the truth. This is why the psalmist prays: "Search me, O God, and know my heart" (Ps 139:23).

7. **Fainting hearts are contagious.** Deut 20:8 — remove the fainthearted before battle. Jer 51:46 — heart fainting at rumours inside $babylon. A heart whose allegiance wavers destabilizes those around it. $alarmed-fear is not a private experience; it spreads.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$rock** — The stony heart is the anti-$rock. Both involve stone, but the $rock — the covenant — is what the heart should be built *on*, not built *of*. The heart of stone (Ezek 36:26) resists the covenant; the $rock under the house (Mat 7:24) sustains it. When God replaces stone with flesh, the heart can finally rest on the $rock rather than imitate it.

- **$fire** — God's covenant standard in active contact. The heart of stone hardens under $fire (Pharaoh); the heart of flesh is refined by it (1 Pet 1:7, Mal 3:2–3). Jer 4:4 warns that the uncircumcised heart becomes fuel for $fire. The heart's material determines the outcome — same fire, opposite results.

- **$egypt** — Pharaoh's hardened heart *is* $egypt's signature: the system that enslaves through provision, ruled by self-deifying authority, locked against evidence. Every biblical reference to heart-hardening echoes the Pharaoh cycle. 1 Sam 6:6 makes the connection explicit: "Wherefore then do ye harden your hearts, as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts?"

- **$circumcision** — The heart's covering must be removed before allegiance can be rightly oriented. Heart-circumcision is the interior reality that physical circumcision signified. Without it, the external sign is empty (Rom 2:29). With it, even the Gentile fulfills the $law (Rom 2:15, 26–29).

- **$law** — The law's final destination is the heart. From Sinai's stone tablets to Jeremiah's interior inscription (Jer 31:33) to Paul's "fleshy tables of the heart" (2 Cor 3:3), the covenant terms move inward. A heart that has the $law written on it does not need external enforcement — allegiance is internal.

- **$spirit** — Every heart transformation in Ezekiel pairs "new heart" with "new spirit" (Ezek 11:19, 36:26). The $spirit is the agent who writes the law on the heart and animates the flesh. Without the $spirit, even a flesh heart would be inert.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience is the heart's proper output. "With the heart man believeth unto righteousness" (Rom 10:10). $faith is not mental assent — it is the heart's allegiance expressed in action. Acts 8:37 — "I believe with all mine heart." The "all" echoes the Shema's "all thine heart."

- **$peace-shalom** — Covenant-wholeness garrisons the heart (Phil 4:7). Where the heart's allegiance is settled in the covenant, $peace-shalom fortifies it against $alarmed-fear. Jn 14:27 pairs them: "Let not your heart be troubled" + "my peace I give unto you."

- **$alarmed-fear** — The covenant-judgment panic response that seizes the heart when allegiance wavers. Jer 51:46 — heart faints at rumours inside $babylon. Jn 14:1 — Jesus prohibits it. The alarmed heart is the diagnostic of a person who does not recognize God's hand in events.

- **$deceive** — The heart $deceives "above all things" (Jer 17:9). It misrepresents its own allegiances. Deut 11:16 — "Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and ye turn aside." The heart can be led away from the covenant by misrepresentation of God's commands — the serpent's original strategy.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **the grave or Hades, where Christ lay three days.**

- Ezekiel 5:5 and 38:12 support Jerusalem or its people being situated in the midst of lands or nations, but they do not call Jerusalem the “heart of the earth” or establish that Matthew 12:40 uses the phrase geographically. Matthew’s immediate Jonah analogy more directly suggests confinement associated with death and deliverance.

- Matthew 12:40's parallel with Jonah 'in the whale's belly' strongly suggests confinement in death, not residence in a city; Jonah's own prayer locates him 'in the belly of Sheol' (Jonah 2:2), and the Ezekiel texts say Jerusalem is in the midst of the nations/land but never use the phrase 'heart of the earth,' so the identification rests on an inferential bridge rather than direct lexical equivalence. Moreover, Jesus was already in Jerusalem before his arrest, straining the 'three days and nights' as time 'in' Jerusalem.

- In Matthew 12:40, the phrase is directly parallel to Jonah being in the 'belly of the great fish' for three days and three nights, an image that much more naturally typifies entombment, death, or being swallowed by the earth, rather than merely being present in a central city.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**Jerusalem — the navel and heart of the land.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *heart*, *hearts*, *hearted*, *brokenhearted*, *fainthearted*, *hardhearted*, *stiffhearted*, *heart of the earth*.
