# Milk

**The word *received* — the elementary, pre-digested first food by which new believers grow, meant to give way to $meat (the will *done*).**

> *"As newborn babes, desire the sincere **milk** of the word, that ye may grow thereby."* — 1 Pet 2:2

**Symbolizes:** the word *received / heard* — the first nourishment of new believers
**Opposite:** $meat — the will *done* (milk is fed *to* you; meat you must work and discern)
**Connected:** $meat, $bread

## The key insight — milk is pre-digested

The metaphor turns on a fact the text never states: **milk is food the mother has already digested.** The infant does no work — it receives nourishment broken down for it and simply absorbs it. So milk pictures **teaching processed for you and received passively** — the word handed over ready-to-swallow, before the hearer has begun to chew, practice, or discern.

That is why milk is paired with *infancy*, never with effort:

> *"every one that useth milk is **unskilful** in the word of righteousness: for he is a **babe**."* — Heb 5:13

The babe (G3516 *nḗpios*) has *received* the word but is not yet *skilled* in it. Milk is the word **heard**; $meat is that same word **done** (Jas 1:22, *"doers… not hearers only"*).

## "The sincere milk of the word" — given for growth

Peter names milk plainly: it is *"the sincere milk of the word"* (G3050 *logikós*, "of the word"). It is **pure and good** — *"sincere"* (G97 *ádolos*, unadulterated) — and its purpose is **growth**:

> *"…that ye may **grow** thereby."* — 1 Pet 2:2

So milk is not an error or a lesser truth. It is the *right first food*, the word delivered in a form a newborn can take — and it is meant to do one thing: make the babe grow up.

## Milk is meant to be outgrown

Because its whole job is growth, *staying* on milk is the problem. Paul could give the Corinthians only milk because they were still *"carnal… babes in Christ"* and *"not able to bear"* meat (1 Cor 3:1-2). And Hebrews rebukes arrested growth:

> *"when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again… and are become such as have need of **milk**, and not of strong meat."* — Heb 5:12

The Old Testament drew the same line — deeper doctrine is for those **weaned**:

> *"Whom shall he teach knowledge?… them that are **weaned from the milk**, and drawn from the breasts."* — Isa 28:9

Weaning is not loss; it is readiness. The child leaves milk *because* it has grown — ready now for $meat, the doing of the Father's will.

## Patterns

1. **Pre-digested = received** — milk pictures the word handed over ready-to-swallow, absorbed without the hearer's own chewing or discernment.
2. **Pure and necessary first** — *"sincere milk of the word,"* given *"that ye may grow."* Milk is good; it is the right starting food.
3. **Milk = heard, $meat = done** — the same word, two stages: received (milk) → practiced/finished (meat).
4. **Meant to be outgrown** — *weaning* (Isa 28:9) and *"not able… now"* (1 Cor 3:2) mark readiness; staying on milk is rebuked (Heb 5:12).

## Connections

- **$meat** — the opposite and the goal: once the word received (milk) is *done* (meat = the will of the Father, Jn 4:34), the believer has moved from babe to "full age."
- **$bread** — the staple word/food; *"man shall live… by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God"* (Mt 4:4).
