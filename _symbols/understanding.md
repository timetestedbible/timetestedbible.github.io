# Understanding

## Approved Definition

**Discernment of good from evil, gained by departing from evil — “a good understanding have all they that do his commandments”.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [The Fool and the Wise](/books/symbolic-language/the-fool-and-the-wise/).

## Definition Layers

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **understanding** begins: “Un`der*stand”ing, a.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

Understanding is specifically discernment of good from evil, gained or evidenced by departing from evil and obeying God’s commandments.

> *“And unto man he said, Behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding.”* — Job 28:28

> *“The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom: a good understanding have all they that do his commandments: his praise endureth for ever.”* — Psalms 111:10

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **understanding** begins:

> “Un`der*stand”ing, a. Defn: Knowing; intelligent; skillful; as, he is an understanding man.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *understanding*. The English-form sweep found **156 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.

## Evidence by Sense

The approved entry currently states one controlling sense:

1. **Approved core:** Discernment of good from evil, gained by departing from evil — “a good understanding have all they that do his commandments”.
2. **Defining witnesses:** Job 28:28; Ps 111:10
3. **Boundary:** the sense applies where the passage activates the same relationship; ordinary literal uses remain literal.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- A MATCH verdict is plausible because the consensus already treats understanding as moral discernment that enables right living, and both printed verses were cited in the consensus. However, the book’s exclusive formulation—linking understanding specifically to departure from evil and commandment-keeping—is narrower than the consensus’s broader inclusion of scriptural and theological comprehension.

- One could argue MATCH: the book's definition is a direct quotation-level paraphrase of Job 28:28 and Ps 111:10, both of which appear in the consensus's own primary basis, so the book may simply be foregrounding one strand of an already-shared meaning rather than genuinely excluding the others. Since the entry never explicitly denies the broader illumination sense, the narrowing might be emphasis rather than boundary disagreement.

- Consensus primarily frames understanding as a prior divine gift enabling discernment and obedience, so treating it as the result gained by departing from evil may invert or over-restrict the causal and semantic range found across the broader biblical data.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
