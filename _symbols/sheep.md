# Sheep

**Covenant people — those who belong to God, hear His voice, follow, and cannot guide themselves.**

> *"And ye my flock, the flock of my **pasture**, are men, and I am your God, saith the Lord GOD."* — Ezek 34:31

---

## The Key Insight

God does not compare His people to sheep because they are woolly and gentle. He calls them sheep because sheep cannot lead themselves.

Every other symbolic quality follows from this one. Sheep hear the shepherd's voice — because they must. They follow — because they cannot navigate alone. They go astray — because without a shepherd, wandering is all they know. They are vulnerable to wolves — because they have no fangs, no claws, no cunning of their own. They are scattered when the shepherd is struck — because the shepherd was the only thing holding them together.

The entire relationship between God and His people is encoded in the word. H6629 *tso'n* derives from a root meaning "to migrate" — a creature defined not by where it stands but by its movement *under direction*. The Greek G4263 *probaton* means literally "that which walks forward" — forward motion under another's guidance. The words themselves insist: a sheep is not a thing that *is*, but a thing that *goes where it is led*.

This is why Scripture never uses "sheep" to describe humanity in general. Sheep are not just any people — they are *covenant* people. Belonging is the precondition. The sheep are "his people" and "the sheep of his pasture" (Ps 100:3). When they stray, they are *lost* sheep — still His, but displaced. When they are scattered, God holds the shepherds accountable (Ezek 34:5–8). The covenant is never dissolved by the wandering. The shepherd goes after the one that is lost.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant people — those who belong to God by relationship, hear and follow (or fail to), and depend entirely on the shepherd

**Opposite:** $goat — self-willed, independent, self-sufficient; in the final judgment, separated to the left hand (Mat 25:32–33)

**Defining verses:** Ezek 34:31, Ps 100:3, Jn 10:27–28, Isa 53:6, Mat 25:32–33

**The surprise:** The sheep's core quality is not obedience — it is *dependence*. Obedience is what sheep do when the shepherd is present. Going astray is what sheep do when they follow their own $way. Both behaviors confirm the same identity: these are creatures who cannot lead themselves.

**Connected:** $animal, $faith, $way, $truth, $light, $name, $grass

## Sheep Across Scripture

### "We Are His People" — The Explicit Definition

Scripture does not leave the meaning of sheep to inference. It states it plainly, repeatedly, using synonymous parallelism — the Hebrew poetic structure where the second line restates the first:

> *"Know ye that the LORD he is God: it is he that hath made us, and not we ourselves; we are his **people**, and the **sheep** of his pasture."* — Ps 100:3

> *"For he is our God; and we are the **people** of his pasture, and the **sheep** of his hand."* — Ps 95:7

> *"So we thy **people** and **sheep** of thy pasture will give thee thanks for ever."* — Ps 79:13

> *"O God, why hast thou cast us off for ever? why doth thine anger smoke against the **sheep** of thy pasture?"* — Ps 74:1

Four psalms, the same equation: **sheep = God's people**. Not people in general — *His* people, defined by pasture (His provision) and hand (His guidance). The relationship is covenantal. They belong to Him because He made them (Ps 100:3) and because He chose them.

Ezekiel 34:31 closes the loop with an explicit divine declaration: "ye my flock, the flock of my pasture, **are men**, and I am your God." Flock = men. God = shepherd. The symbol is not metaphorical decoration. It is definitional.

### Hear, Follow, Know — The Marks of Belonging

> *"My **sheep** hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: and I give unto them eternal life; and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand."* — Jn 10:27–28

Jesus defines sheep by three actions: they **hear**, they **follow**, and they are **known**. Each action is relational, not autonomous:

- **Hear** (Jn 10:3–4) — "the sheep hear his voice: and he calleth his own sheep by $[name]." The shepherd calls each one by $name — by covenant (see $name = covenant relationship). Hearing is not passive reception. It is covenant recognition: the sheep know which voice carries their covenant bond.

- **Follow** (Jn 10:4) — "the sheep follow him: for they know his voice." Following is the outward expression of $faith — trusting obedience. The sheep do not understand the route. They understand the voice. $faith is not mental assent but action under direction, and the sheep embody this: they move because they hear and trust.

- **Know** (Jn 10:4–5) — "a stranger will they not follow, but will flee from him: for they know not the voice of strangers." True sheep distinguish the shepherd's voice from the stranger's. This is not instinct — it is covenant familiarity. The sheep who belong can tell the difference.

These three marks — hear, follow, know — are the behavioral portrait of $faith. Covenant people hear God's $truth (His commandments), follow His $way (conduct according to that standard), and recognize His voice over competing voices. When any of these fails, the sheep strays.

### "All We Like Sheep Have Gone Astray"

> *"All we like **sheep** have gone astray; we have turned every one to his own $[way]; and the LORD hath laid on him the iniquity of us all."* — Isa 53:6

This is the other side of the sheep's nature. The same creature that follows when the shepherd leads *wanders* when left to its own $way. "Turned every one to his own way" — each sheep choosing its own conduct (H1870 *derek* = manner of life; see $way). The straying is not rebellion in the goat's sense — it is not willful independence. It is the natural drift of a creature that *cannot navigate alone* attempting to do so.

Two causes emerge across Scripture:

**1. Their own nature.** Isa 53:6 places the blame squarely on the sheep themselves — "we have turned." The instinct to wander is endemic.

**2. Bad shepherds.** Jer 50:6 — "My people hath been **lost sheep**: their shepherds have caused them to go astray." The shepherds — the leaders responsible for guiding covenant people — drove them off course. Ezekiel 34:5–8 expands the indictment: the sheep were scattered "because there is no shepherd: and they became meat to all the beasts of the field." Without faithful leadership, $animal — the nations, the predatory peoples — devour the unprotected flock.

In both cases, the sheep's vulnerability confirms the symbol: these are people who depend on being led. When that leading is absent or corrupted, they are lost.

### The Shepherd Seeks the Lost One

> *"How think ye? if a man have an hundred **sheep**, and one of them be gone astray, doth he not leave the ninety and nine, and goeth into the mountains, and seeketh that which is gone astray? And if so be that he find it, verily I say unto you, he rejoiceth more of that **sheep**, than of the ninety and nine which went not astray."* — Mat 18:12–14

> *"What man of you, having an hundred **sheep**, if he lose one of them, doth not leave the ninety and nine in the wilderness, and go after that which is lost, until he find it?"* — Lk 15:4

The parable only works if the lost sheep is still *his* sheep. It has wandered, not defected. The shepherd searches not because the sheep will find its way back — it will not — but because the covenant bond persists despite the straying. The rejoicing at recovery (Lk 15:5–7) is covenant restoration, not new acquisition.

Peter confirms the pattern: "For ye were as **sheep** going astray; but are now returned unto the Shepherd and Bishop of your souls" (1 Pe 2:25). The return is to a relationship that already existed. The sheep were always His. They had merely wandered from the $way.

### Sheep and Goats — The Judgment Contrast

> *"And before him shall be gathered all nations: and he shall separate them one from another, as a shepherd divideth his **sheep** from the **goats**: and he shall set the **sheep** on his right hand, but the **goats** on the left."* — Mat 25:32–33

The final judgment scene in Matthew 25 depends entirely on the symbolic distinction between sheep and $goat. Both have been in the same flock. Both have grazed together. But the shepherd knows the difference, and now he separates them.

The sheep — those on the right — are defined by what they *did*: fed the hungry, gave drink to the thirsty, clothed the naked, visited the sick and imprisoned (Mat 25:34–40). Their actions reveal their nature. They followed the shepherd's $way. Their $faith produced visible $fruit.

The goats — those on the left — are defined by what they *did not do* (Mat 25:42–43). They were present in the flock but did not act according to the shepherd's voice. Self-willed. Independent. Content to graze near the flock without actually following the shepherd's direction. Their conduct ($way) revealed that they never truly heard.

> *"Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me."* — Mat 25:40

> *"Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of the least of these, ye did it not to me."* — Mat 25:45

The sheep obeyed without even realizing they were serving the King. Their $faith — trusting obedience — was so embedded in their conduct that it required no calculation. The goats failed without realizing they were neglecting the King. Their self-direction was so natural to them that they did not notice the shepherd's voice at all.

### Scattered When the Shepherd Is Struck

> *"Smite the **shepherd**, and the **sheep** shall be scattered."* — Zec 13:7

> *"Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the **shepherd**, and the **sheep** of the flock shall be scattered abroad."* — Mat 26:31

Jesus applies Zechariah's prophecy directly to Himself on the night of His arrest. When the shepherd falls, the flock disintegrates. This is not a flaw in the sheep — it is their nature. They are cohered by the shepherd's presence. Remove him, and what held them together is gone.

The pattern had already played out in Israel's history. Numbers 27:17 records Moses' plea: "that the congregation of the LORD be not as **sheep** which have no shepherd." Without a leader, the covenant people become directionless — a flock without structure, vulnerable to every predator. This is what Jesus saw when He looked at the crowds:

> *"But when he saw the multitudes, he was moved with compassion on them, because they fainted, and were **scattered abroad**, as **sheep** having no shepherd."* — Mat 9:36

The compassion is covenantal. These are not strangers — they are sheep without a functioning shepherd. Their scattering is a leadership failure, not a character failure.

### Wolves in Sheep's Clothing

> *"Beware of false prophets, which come to you in **sheep's** clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves."* — Mat 7:15

The disguise works because sheep-appearance means *covenant-people-appearance*. False prophets do not dress as wolves. They dress as members of the flock — they look like covenant people, speak covenant language, attend covenant gatherings. Their camouflage is belonging. Only the fruit reveals the wolf inside (Mat 7:16–20).

> *"Behold, I send you forth as **sheep** in the midst of wolves."* — Mat 10:16

The disciples' identity is sheep — covenant people, vulnerable, dependent on the shepherd. They are sent *as sheep*, not as wolves with better intentions. Their vulnerability is not a deficiency to overcome but a condition to navigate wisely ("wise as $[serpent]s") while remaining unmixed in their allegiance ("harmless as $[dove]s").

### Silent Before the Shearers

> *"He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth: he is brought as a **lamb** to the slaughter, and as a **sheep** before her shearers is dumb, so he openeth not his mouth."* — Isa 53:7

> *"He was led as a **sheep** to the slaughter; and like a **lamb** dumb before his shearer, so opened he not his mouth."* — Acts 8:32

The servant of Isaiah 53 embodies the sheep's submission. Silence before shearers is not weakness — it is the ultimate posture of the covenant person who trusts the shepherd's hand even when that hand permits suffering. Philip identifies this sheep as Jesus (Acts 8:35).

Paul extends the identity to all covenant people under persecution: "For thy sake we are killed all the day long; we are accounted as **sheep** for the slaughter" (Rom 8:36, quoting Ps 44:22). The sheep's vulnerability before enemies is not failure — it is the covenant people's lot in a hostile world, endured in dependence on the shepherd.

### "Feed My Sheep" — The Pastoral Command

> *"He saith unto him the second time, Simon, son of Jonas, lovest thou me? He saith unto him, Yea, Lord; thou knowest that I love thee. He saith unto him, **Feed my sheep**."* — Jn 21:16

Three times Jesus asks Peter about love. Three times He answers with a command about sheep: "Feed my lambs" (Jn 21:15), "Feed my sheep" (Jn 21:16), "Feed my sheep" (Jn 21:17). Love for the shepherd is expressed by caring for the shepherd's flock. The commission is not abstract ministry — it is covenantal stewardship of covenant people.

Peter later passes this charge forward: "Feed the flock of God which is among you, taking the oversight thereof, not by constraint, but willingly; not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind; neither as being lords over God's heritage, but being **ensamples** to the flock" (1 Pe 5:2–3). The shepherds are to be $light — visible examples ($light = visible obedience) — to the sheep. Not lords, but patterns. The flock follows what it can see.

The writer of Hebrews seals the image: "Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great **shepherd** of the **sheep**, through the $[blood] of the everlasting covenant" (Heb 13:20). The shepherd is identified. The covenant is named. The sheep are His covenant people, brought through death into life by covenant blood.

## Within the Flock — Fat Cattle and Lean

One of the more unsettling details in Ezekiel 34 is that the judgment falls not only between shepherds and sheep, or between sheep and goats, but *within the flock itself*:

> *"And as for you, O my flock, thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I judge between cattle and cattle, between the rams and the he goats."* — Ezek 34:17

> *"Seemeth it a small thing unto you to have eaten up the good pasture, but ye must tread down with your feet the residue of your pastures? and to have drunk of the deep waters, but ye must foul the residue with your feet? And as for my flock, they eat that which ye have trodden with your feet; and they drink that which ye have fouled with your feet."* — Ezek 34:18–19

> *"Therefore will I save my flock, and they shall no more be a prey; and I will judge between cattle and cattle."* — Ezek 34:22

Some within the covenant community consume the good pasture and foul what remains. The fat cattle oppress the lean. The rams push with horn and shoulder (Ezek 34:21). Not all who graze in the flock behave as true sheep. The shepherd's judgment distinguishes — even among those who bear the covenant $name.

## Patterns

1. **Sheep = covenant people, always.** Every occurrence resolves with "covenant people" — whether the context is pastoral (Ps 23, Ps 100), prophetic rebuke (Ezek 34, Jer 50:6), parable (Mat 18, Lk 15), judgment (Mat 25), or christological (Isa 53, Jn 10). Not "followers" generically. Not "obedient ones." Covenant people — with all the dependence, vulnerability, and belonging that implies.

2. **The sheep's defining quality is dependence, not obedience.** Obedience is what happens when dependence is properly directed (Jn 10:27). Straying is what happens when it is not (Isa 53:6). Both behaviors confirm the same identity: creatures who cannot navigate alone.

3. **Hearing precedes following.** Jn 10:3–5 — the sheep hear the voice *first*, then follow. They do not follow a stranger because they do not recognize the voice. $faith (trusting obedience) begins with hearing $truth (God's commandments).

4. **Straying is never permanent severance.** The lost sheep is still *his* sheep (Mat 18:12, Lk 15:4). The shepherd seeks it. The sheep who were "going astray" have "now returned" (1 Pe 2:25). Covenant identity persists through the wandering.

5. **Sheep without a shepherd = compassion, not condemnation.** Mat 9:36, Num 27:17. When the flock is scattered, the blame falls on absent or corrupt shepherds (Ezek 34:5–8, Jer 50:6, Zec 10:3), not on the sheep.

6. **The goat is the defining contrast.** Mat 25:32–33 separates them at the end of the age. The sheep obeyed without calculating; the goats neglected without noticing. Self-will ($goat) is the opposite of dependent following ($sheep).

7. **Vulnerability is identity, not flaw.** Mat 10:16 sends disciples *as* sheep among wolves — not despite being sheep, but *as* sheep. Rom 8:36 quotes "sheep for the slaughter" as the expected condition of covenant people. The shepherd does not promise to make the sheep into wolves. He promises that nothing shall pluck them from His hand (Jn 10:28).

## Connections

- **$animal** — Sheep are a specific subset of $animal (people / tribe / sub-state). While $animal broadly represents people-groups characterized by their nature — lions (royal), wolves (ravenous), serpents (cunning) — sheep represent the specific subset who are domestic, dependent, and covenantally bound. In Daniel 4, animals dwell *under* the tree (nations subordinate to empires); sheep dwell *in the pasture* under the shepherd. The distinction is governance: animals are characterized by nature; sheep are defined by relationship.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience — and sheep are the living portrait of it. They hear and follow (Jn 10:27). They do not understand the route; they trust the voice. When $faith fails, the sheep strays (Isa 53:6). When $faith operates, the sheep moves forward under direction. The Greek *probaton* ("that which walks forward") is nearly a definition of $faith in motion.

- **$way** — "We have turned every one to his own $[way]" (Isa 53:6). The sheep's straying is described as choosing its own $way — its own conduct, its own manner of life. "He leadeth me in the paths of righteousness" (Ps 23:3) — the shepherd's $way replaces the sheep's self-chosen path. The contrast between the shepherd's $way and the sheep's own $way is the hinge of the entire symbol.

- **$truth** — The shepherd's voice carries $truth — God's commandments, His standard. "My sheep hear my voice" (Jn 10:27). The sheep that recognizes the voice is the one who recognizes God's $truth. The stranger's voice — the false standard — the sheep instinctively rejects (Jn 10:5). "Feed my sheep" (Jn 21:16) is a command to nourish covenant people with $truth.

- **$light** — Shepherds are to be "ensamples to the flock" (1 Pe 5:3) — $light, visible obedience. The sheep follow what they can see. A shepherd who is a true $light leads the sheep in the $way. A shepherd who is $darkness scatters them (Ezek 34:5–8). The chain: $truth (the standard) → $light (the visible example) → $sheep (those who follow the example).

- **$name** — "He calleth his own sheep by $[name]" (Jn 10:3). The shepherd knows each sheep by $name — by covenant relationship. Being called by $name is being identified as belonging to the covenant. "The great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant" (Heb 13:20) — the sheep are defined by the covenant blood that binds them to the shepherd.

- **$grass** — $grass represents mortal people — all flesh that withers (Isa 40:6–7). Sheep feed on $grass: "He maketh me to lie down in green pastures" (Ps 23:2); "I will feed them in a good pasture" (Ezek 34:14). If $grass = people, the pasture imagery suggests that covenant people are sustained among and within the broader human community — but directed to the *good* pasture by the shepherd, not left to graze wherever they wander.

## For Further Study

- **$goat** — The sheep's opposite. What does the goat represent, and how does self-will contrast with dependent following in the judgment of Matthew 25?
- **$faith** — The sheep's core action. If $faith is trusting obedience, how does hearing the shepherd's voice connect to the broader biblical pattern of obedience as the substance of belief?
- **$way** — The sheep's straying and the shepherd's path. How does "every one to his own way" (Isa 53:6) relate to the two ways of Matthew 7:13–14?
- **$grass** — The sheep's pasture. If grass represents mortal people, what does it mean for the shepherd to lead His flock to "green pastures"?
- **$name** — The shepherd calls His sheep by name. How does calling by $name relate to covenant identity and belonging?
- **$dove** — Another creature representing covenant devotion. How do the dove's faithful return and the sheep's dependent following illuminate each other — and where do they differ?
