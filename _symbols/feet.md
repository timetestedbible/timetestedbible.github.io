## The Short Case

Feet mean the walk—a man's conduct or way; washed clean while the body stays bathed.

> *“Jesus saith to him, He that is washed needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit: and ye are clean, but not all.”* — John 13:10

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- John 13:10 directly supports the distinction between a bathed body and feet that still require washing, but by itself it does not clearly establish the broader symbolic equation of feet with moral conduct or one’s walk.

- The sole printed citation (John 13:10) directly supports only the cleansing clause, not the foundational equation feet = walk/conduct; texts like Psalm 119:105 or Proverbs 4:26-27 would be needed for that. One could also argue the book implicitly fixes a single meaning where the consensus insists feet are a context-dependent cluster, which might push toward REFINED—but the entry does not assert exclusivity, so MATCH stands.

- None.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
