## Summary

**The vineyard's reckoning — where the [fruit](/research/symbols/fruit/) a people has borne is pressed out and rendered to the Owner, in one of two vintages: presses that “burst out with new wine” when the grapes are good; the growers themselves trodden — “the great winepress of the wrath of God” — and blood for wine, when the vintage is wickedness.**

## The Short Case

The book identifies the winepress more broadly as the vineyard’s point of reckoning, where a people’s fruit yields one of two outcomes: abundant new wine for good fruit or the bloody crushing of the wicked under divine wrath.

> *“And he fenced it, and gathered out the stones thereof, and planted it with the choicest vine, and built a tower in the midst of it, and also made a winepress therein: and he looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes.”* — Isaiah 5:2

> *“For the vineyard of the LORD of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his pleasant plant: and he looked for judgment, but behold oppression; for righteousness, but behold a cry.”* — Isaiah 5:7

> *“So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy presses shall burst out with new wine.”* — Proverbs 3:10

> *“And the floors shall be full of wheat, and the fats shall overflow with wine and oil.”* — Joel 2:24

> *“Put ye in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe: come, get you down; for the press is full, the fats overflow; for their wickedness is great.”* — Joel 3:13

> *“The Lord hath trodden under foot all my mighty men in the midst of me: he hath called an assembly against me to crush my young men: the Lord hath trodden the virgin, the daughter of Judah, as in a winepress.”* — Lamentations 1:15

> *“I have trodden the winepress alone; and of the people there was none with me: for I will tread them in mine anger, and trample them in my fury; and their blood shall be sprinkled upon my garments, and I will stain all my raiment.”* — Isaiah 63:3

> *“And the angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, and gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the wrath of God.”* — Revelation 14:19

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Because the consensus responses already recognize abundance and vineyard accountability as established secondary meanings, one could classify the book as merely synthesizing the full consensus and thus as a MATCH. However, the book elevates those secondary cases into a single governing, two-vintage definition, materially broadening the dominant consensus core.

- One could argue this is merely a MATCH plus organizational packaging: the book affirms the same primary wrath meaning and simply appends the abundance sense that every consensus response already lists as an alternative, so no case is actually admitted or excluded that the consensus does not recognize. Against this, the book's claim that wrath and abundance are two outcomes of one symbol (the vineyard's reckoning of fruit rendered to the Owner), and that the trodden are the growers themselves, is a substantive structural reinterpretation the consensus does not make.

- The dual-reckoning synthesis may over-unify what the consensus treats as distinct primary (judgment) versus secondary (abundance) or incidental (vineyard furniture) senses, and it omits the common Christological/passion reading of the lone treader.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
