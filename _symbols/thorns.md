# Thorns

**Worldly people who choke fruitfulness.**

> *"He also that received seed among the **thorns** is he that heareth the word; and the care of this world, and the deceitfulness of riches, choke the $[bread], and he becometh unfruitful."* — Mat 13:22

---

## The Key Insight

This is one of the few symbols where Jesus gives an explicit definition. In the Parable of the Sower, He interprets His own imagery:

> *"And that which fell among **thorns** are they, which, when they have heard, go forth, and are choked with cares and riches and pleasures of this life, and bring no $[fruit] to perfection."* — Lk 8:14

Three things characterize thorns: **cares of this world**, **deceitfulness of riches**, and **pleasures of this life**. And they do one thing: *choke* — actively preventing fruitfulness.

Since $tree represents a nation or people group throughout Scripture, and thorns are a type of plant, the pattern suggests that thorns represent a *type* of people — those defined by worldly preoccupation.

**Symbolizes:** Worldly people — those characterized by cares, riches, and pleasures

**Opposite:** Good ground — "an honest and good heart" that hears the word and brings forth $fruit with patience (Lk 8:15)

**What they do:** Choke — actively prevent fruitfulness in others (and themselves)

**Connected:** $tree, $fruit, $bread, $myrtle, $grass

## Thorns Across Scripture

### Jesus Defines the Symbol

All four gospel accounts of the Parable of the Sower record the same verb:

> *"And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprung up, and **choked** them"* — Mat 13:7

> *"And some fell among thorns, and the thorns grew up, and **choked** it, and it yielded no fruit"* — Mk 4:7

> *"And some fell among thorns; and the thorns sprang up with it, and **choked** it"* — Lk 8:7

Four accounts, one consistent action: *choke*. The thorns don't merely coexist with the seed — they actively suppress it. The word (the $bread sown) is heard, but the cares, riches, and pleasures prevent it from producing $fruit.

Notice the opposite that Luke provides: *"that on the good ground are they, which in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience"* (Lk 8:15). Good ground = patient fruitfulness. Thorns = fruitless choking. The contrast defines both.

### The Original Curse

Thorns first appear in Scripture as part of the curse after the fall:

> *"**Thorns** also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee"* — Gen 3:18

Genesis introduces thorns but doesn't explain what they represent. The Parable of the Sower, spoken thousands of years later, may unlock what was planted symbolically at the beginning: the cursed ground produces a type of growth characterized by worldliness — cares, riches, pleasures — that chokes the fruitfulness God intended.

An intriguing detail from the Gen 3 context: the Hebrew word H8104 *shamar* ("to keep, to guard") used in Gen 2:15 (Adam's original commission to guard the garden) carries the lexical note "to hedge about, *as with thorns*." The very mechanism of guarding — hedging with thorns — becomes part of the curse when the garden is lost. What was meant to protect now chokes.

### Thorns Among the Nations

Ezekiel uses thorns for neighboring peoples who afflict Israel:

> *"And there shall be no more a pricking **brier** unto the house of Israel, nor any grieving **thorn** of all that are round about them, that despised them"* — Ezek 28:24

Here the thorns are explicitly identified as peoples — nations surrounding Israel that despise and afflict God's covenant community. This aligns with the $tree = nation pattern: thorns are a specific type of nation, characterized not by covenant faithfulness but by the same worldly hostility the parable describes.

### The Anti-Thorn

Isaiah provides the explicit replacement:

> *"Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of the brier shall come up the $[myrtle] tree: and it shall be to the LORD for a $[name], for an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off"* — Isa 55:13

The $myrtle is the anti-thorn. Where the cursed order produced worldly, choking growth, the $myrtle replaces it — humble, fragrant, evergreen. The result is permanent: "an everlasting sign." The contrast illuminates both symbols: thorns are the cursed order that will be uprooted; myrtle is the faithful order that replaces it.

### The Bramble's Shadow

In Judges 9:15, the bramble (a thorny plant) offers its $shadow to the other trees — but the offer comes with a threat: *"if not, let fire come out of the bramble, and devour the cedars of Lebanon."* A thorn-ruler offers false protection that produces destruction instead. Compare with $shadow: God's shadow is covenant protection; the bramble's shadow is a false covenant that ends in fire.

## Patterns

1. **Explicit definition.** Jesus directly interprets thorns as cares, riches, and pleasures (Mat 13:22, Lk 8:14). This is rare — most symbols require inference across many verses. Thorns have a definitive answer from the Interpreter Himself.

2. **One action: choke.** Every Sower account uses the same verb. Thorns don't just fail to produce — they actively *prevent* fruitfulness in what grows alongside them.

3. **A type of $tree.** Since trees represent nations and people groups, thorns represent a specific type: people characterized by worldliness rather than covenant faithfulness.

4. **Part of the curse, replaced by the faithful.** Thorns enter at the fall (Gen 3:18) and are explicitly replaced by $myrtle (Isa 55:13) — the faithful remnant who constitute the material of God's dwelling.

5. **Nations that afflict.** Ezek 28:24 identifies thorns as peoples who despise and grieve Israel — worldly nations hostile to the covenant community.

## Connections

- **$tree** — Thorns are a type of plant/tree. Since $tree = nation or people group, thorns = a worldly type of people (Mat 13:22, Ezek 28:24).

- **$fruit** — Thorns prevent $fruit (Mk 4:7 "yielded no fruit"; Lk 8:14 "bring no fruit to perfection"). The parable defines thorns by what they destroy: fruitfulness.

- **$bread** — The seed in the parable is the word ($bread). Thorns choke the word's effectiveness — cares, riches, and pleasures suppress what was sown.

- **$myrtle** — The explicit anti-thorn (Isa 55:13). Where thorns choke, myrtle is fragrant. Where thorns are cursed, myrtle is "an everlasting sign." The faithful remnant replaces the worldly order.

- **$grass** — Grass represents mortal people generally; thorns are a specific subset — people defined by worldly cares. Both are temporary (Isa 40:7–8), but thorns are distinguished by their choking action.
