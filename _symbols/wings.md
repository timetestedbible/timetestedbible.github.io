# Wings

**Covenant covering — the protective shelter of God's covenant and the Torah that reminds you whose you are.**

> *"Now when I passed by thee, and looked upon thee, behold, thy time was the time of love; and I spread my **skirt** over thee, and covered thy nakedness: yea, I sware unto thee, and **entered into a covenant** with thee, saith the Lord GOD; and thou becamest mine."* — Ezek 16:8

---

## The Key Insight

The English Bible hides something extraordinary. When you read "wings" in Psalm 91, "skirt" in Ruth 3, "borders" in Numbers 15, and "corners" in Isaiah 11 — you are reading **the same Hebrew word every time**.

H3671 *kanaph* (כָּנָף).

One word — translated four different ways — covering:
- The $eagle's wings that bore Israel out of Egypt
- The garment edge Ruth asked Boaz to spread over her
- The borders of clothing where God commanded Israel to place fringes as a Torah reminder
- The four corners of the earth

English fractures *kanaph* into separate concepts. Hebrew holds them together. And the thread that runs through every usage is not flight, not feathers, not fabric — it is **covenant**.

Ezekiel 16:8 makes the equation explicit. God spreads His *kanaph* over Jerusalem and says, in the same breath: "I entered into a covenant with thee." The spreading of the wing *is* the entering of covenant. The two are not metaphorically linked — they are identified as the same act.

Once you see this, every appearance of *kanaph* in Scripture begins to resonate with the same note.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant covering — God's protective covenant and the Torah that identifies the one who dwells under it

**Opposite:** Exposed / uncovered — outside the covenant, without protection or identity

**Defining verses:** Ezek 16:8, Ruth 2:12, Ruth 3:9, Num 15:38–40, Ps 91:4, Exod 25:20

**The surprise:** The fringes (tzitzit) that God commands Israel to wear are placed on the *kanaph* — the "wings" — of the garment. Their stated purpose: "that ye may remember, and do all my commandments, and be holy unto your God" (Num 15:40). The wing of the garment is where Torah meets daily life.

**Connected:** $rock, $sand, $sea, $marriage, $name, $mark, $eagle, $bread, $faith, $the-way

## Wings Across Scripture

### One Word, One Meaning — Four Translations

The KJV translates H3671 *kanaph* as:
- **"wings"** — Ps 91:4, Exod 19:4, Ruth 2:12, Mal 4:2
- **"skirt"** — Ezek 16:8, Ruth 3:9, Deut 22:30, 1 Sam 24:4
- **"borders"** — Num 15:38
- **"corners" / "quarters"** — Isa 11:12, Deut 22:12
- **"ends"** — Job 38:13

A concordance reveals these are not different words. They are one word. And the passage that defines it most directly is Ezekiel 16:

> *"I spread my **skirt** (H3671 *kanaph*) over thee, and covered thy nakedness: yea, I sware unto thee, and entered into a **covenant** with thee, saith the Lord GOD."* — Ezek 16:8

Spreading the *kanaph* = entering covenant. This is not a simile or a metaphor requiring interpretation. It is a direct statement: the act of covering with the *kanaph* **is** the act of entering covenant.

### Ruth — From God's Wings to Boaz's Skirt

The book of Ruth stages the connection in two acts using the same word:

> *"The LORD recompense thy work, and a full reward be given thee of the LORD God of Israel, under whose **wings** (H3671 *kanaph*) thou art come to trust."* — Ruth 2:12

> *"I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy **skirt** (H3671 *kanaph*) over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman."* — Ruth 3:9

Boaz tells Ruth she has come under God's *kanaph*. Ruth then asks Boaz to spread his *kanaph* over her. The same word, the same gesture — the divine covenant and the $marriage covenant using identical language.

Ruth does not ask for charity. She does not ask for romance. She asks for covenant covering — the act of a kinsman-redeemer taking a woman into the binding protection of his household. This is $marriage as $name — as covenant relationship enacted through the spreading of *kanaph*.

The resonance with Ezekiel 16:8 is unmistakable. God found Israel naked and uncovered; He spread His *kanaph* and entered covenant. Ruth came as a foreigner with nothing; she asked the redeemer to spread his *kanaph*. In both cases, the one who was outside the covenant is brought inside through the covering of wings.

### The Fringes on the Wings — Torah Made Visible

Here is where the symbolic meaning sharpens from "covenant" into "covenant *and* Torah reminder":

> *"Speak unto the children of Israel, and bid them that they make them fringes in the **borders** (H3671 *kanaph*) of their garments throughout their generations… that ye may look upon it, and **remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them**; and that ye seek not after your own heart and your own eyes, after which ye use to go a whoring: that ye may remember, and do all my commandments, and **be holy** unto your God."* — Num 15:38–40

> *"Thou shalt make thee fringes upon the four **quarters** (H3671 *kanaph*) of thy vesture, wherewith thou coverest thyself."* — Deut 22:12

The fringes (tzitzit) are placed on the *kanaph* of the garment. Their stated purpose is threefold: (1) remember the commandments, (2) do the commandments, (3) be holy. The wings of the garment become the location of Torah memory — $mark made visible on the very edge of the covering.

This means the *kanaph* is not only a covering. It is a covering that *reminds*. To come under the wings is to come under the covenant — and the covenant includes commandments. The $bread (Word / covenant terms) is embedded in the wings. The $the-way (Torah / God's commandments) is written on the border.

Notice, too, what the passage warns against: "seek not after your own heart and your own eyes, after which ye use to go a whoring." The alternative to the wings is the behavior of the $harlot — divided loyalty, wandering after foreign gods. The fringes on the *kanaph* stand as a guard against exactly that.

### Under His Wings — Psalm 91

> *"He shall cover thee with his feathers, and under his **wings** (H3671 *kanaph*) shalt thou trust: his truth shall be thy shield and buckler."* — Ps 91:4

The psalm does not say "under his wings shalt thou be safe" — though safety is implied. It says "under his wings shalt thou **trust**." This is the $faith word — trusting obedience. Dwelling under the covenant covering requires active trust, not passive shelter.

And the parallel line explains what that covering consists of: "his truth shall be thy shield and buckler." The wings and the truth are set in synonymous parallelism. What covers you is what is true — God's Word, God's commandments, God's covenant terms. The wings *are* the truth; the truth *is* the shield.

The wider context of Psalm 91 confirms this pattern. The one who dwells "in the secret place of the most High" (Ps 91:1) — under the $shadow of the Almighty — abides in the covenant's shelter. The psalm enumerates protection from pestilence, arrows, terrors, plague — but the foundation is not a magical force field. It is the relationship declared in verse 2: "He is my refuge and my fortress: my God; in him will I trust."

### Healing in His Wings

> *"But unto you that fear my $[name] shall the Sun of righteousness arise with healing in his **wings** (H3671 *kanaph*)."* — Mal 4:2

The promise is not healing in a generic sense. It is healing *in his wings* — healing embedded in the covenant covering itself. And it comes to those who "fear my $[name]" — those who revere the covenant relationship ($name = covenant). The covenant, when returned to, restores what was broken.

The woman with the issue of blood touched the "hem" (G2899 *kraspedon*) of Jesus' garment (Mat 9:20) — the fringe, the tassel, the tzitzit on the *kanaph*. She reached for the very place where Torah memory was attached. She reached for the wings. And she found healing there.

### The Ark — Wings Over the Covenant

> *"And the cherubims shall stretch forth their **wings** (H3671 *kanaph*) on high, covering the mercy seat with their **wings**, and their faces shall look one to another; toward the mercy seat shall the faces of the cherubims be."* — Exod 25:20

The cherubim on the ark of the covenant spread their *kanaph* over the mercy seat — the place where the blood was applied, where God met with His people. Wings *over* the covenant. The gesture is identical to Ezekiel 16:8: spreading wings = covenant covering.

Inside the ark are the tablets of the law ($bread — the covenant terms), the pot of manna ($bread — the daily provision of God's Word), and Aaron's rod that budded. The wings of the cherubim overshadow all of it. The covenant covering protects and shelters the covenant contents.

The $eagle, whose face appears on the cherubim (Ezek 1:10, Rev 4:7), bears *kanaph* as the agent of covenant authority — the $eagle is the messenger who carries and delivers under that authority. The wings belong to the covenant; the eagle is the one who bears them.

### A Hen Gathers Her Chicks

> *"O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them which are sent unto thee, how often would I have gathered thy children together, even as a hen gathereth her chickens under her **wings**, and ye would not!"* — Mat 23:37

Jesus uses the *kanaph* image directly. He has offered covenant covering — the same gesture as Ezekiel 16:8, the same protection as Psalm 91:4 — and Jerusalem has refused it.

The language is intimate: a mother hen spreading her wings over vulnerable chicks. But the covenant dimension is what gives the lament its weight. This is not merely a protective instinct being rejected. It is the covenant itself being refused. "I would have… and ye would not." The wings were spread; the $sand (multitudes at the covenant boundary) would not come under them.

What follows is devastating: "Behold, your house is left unto you desolate" (Mat 23:38). Refuse the wings, and the house has no covering. The opposite of wings is exposure — the covenant shelter removed.

### The Wings of the Earth

Two passages use *kanaph* for the earth's extremities:

> *"That it might take hold of the **ends** (H3671 *kanaph*) of the earth, that the wicked might be shaken out of it?"* — Job 38:13

> *"And he shall set up an ensign for the nations, and shall assemble the outcasts of Israel, and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four **corners** (H3671 *kanaph*) of the earth."* — Isa 11:12

The "wings" of the earth are its edges — the coasts, the boundaries, the places where land meets $sea (nations / chaotic peoples). This is where $sand (multitudes at the covenant boundary) exists: at the seashore, at the edge, at the place where covenant land meets the turbulence of the nations.

The chain is suggestive: the *kanaph* of the earth is the boundary zone where the covenant covering extends outward toward the nations. The fringes on a garment's *kanaph* remind the wearer of Torah. The *kanaph* of the earth is where the covenant meets those who have not yet come under it — the place of offer, the place of gathering.

Isaiah 11:12 gathers $israel's dispersed from the four *kanaph* — from the four wings. They have been scattered to the covenant's edge; they are brought back from there.

## Patterns

1. **Spreading *kanaph* = entering covenant.** Ezek 16:8 (God over Jerusalem), Ruth 3:9 (Boaz over Ruth), Mat 23:37 (Jesus over Jerusalem — refused). The act of spreading wings is the act of covenant-making. Every time.

2. **Under the wings = under the covenant.** Ps 91:4, Ruth 2:12, Exod 25:20. The consistent spatial image — "under" — places the one who trusts *inside* the covenant's protection.

3. **Wings carry Torah memory.** Num 15:38–40 and Deut 22:12 attach commandment-reminders to the *kanaph*. The wings are not empty shelter. They carry content — the terms of the covenant, the commandments of God.

4. **One word, hidden by translation.** The KJV renders H3671 as wings, skirt, borders, corners, quarters, ends — obscuring the unity of the symbol. Recognizing *kanaph* as a single concept reunites passages that English separates.

5. **Wings refused = desolation.** Mat 23:37–38. The consequence of rejecting the covenant covering is not merely danger — it is a house left desolate. The opposite of wings is not risk; it is exposure, nakedness, abandonment.

6. **The earth has wings.** Job 38:13, Isa 11:12. The *kanaph* of the earth marks the boundary where covenant meets nations — the edge-zone where the gathering happens.

7. **Healing is in the wings.** Mal 4:2. The covenant covering contains restoration. The woman who touched the fringe on Jesus' garment (the *kanaph*) found healing at the exact point where Torah memory was attached.

## Connections

- **$rock** — The $rock is the unchanging covenant itself; the wings are its covering extension. The cherubim spread their wings over the ark of the covenant (Exod 25:20) — wings overshadowing the $rock. The dove dwells "in the clefts of the $rock" (Song 2:14) and finds shelter "under his wings" (Ps 91:4). The covenant and its covering are inseparable.

- **$sand** — $sand represents multitudes at the covenant boundary — people at the edge between the $sea of nations and the $rock of covenant. The *kanaph* of the earth (Job 38:13, Isa 11:12) is precisely where $sand exists: the boundary zone. The covenant wings extend to the edge; the multitudes there either come under the covering or are swept into the $sea.

- **$sea** — The $sea represents nations and chaotic peoples. The *kanaph* of the earth borders the $sea — the wings mark the place where ordered covenant territory meets the disorder of the nations. The gathering in Isa 11:12 brings the scattered from the four *kanaph* — from the coastlands where land meets $sea.

- **$marriage** — $marriage is the covenant picture. Ruth 3:9 and Ezek 16:8 use the identical gesture (spreading *kanaph*) for both marital and divine covenant. Under the wings = under the $marriage canopy = inside the covenant relationship.

- **$name** — $name represents the covenant relationship itself. Mal 4:2 promises healing in the wings to those who "fear my $name." The wings are the covering; the $name is the relationship. To come under the wings is to take the $name — to enter into identified covenant belonging.

- **$mark** — $mark is visible practice / observance. The fringes on the *kanaph* (Num 15:38–40) are the $mark of covenant allegiance — the visible, wearable reminder of commandments. The wing of the garment is where the $mark is placed, making the covenant covering observable.

- **$the-way** — $the-way is Torah / God's commandments. The explicit purpose of the fringes on the *kanaph*: "remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them" (Num 15:40). The wings carry $the-way — they are not shelter without instruction but shelter *consisting of* instruction.

- **$eagle** — The $eagle is the messenger / angel who bears *kanaph*. God bore Israel "on eagles' wings" (Exod 19:4); the cherubim have eagle faces and spread their wings over the ark (Ezek 1:10, Exod 25:20). The $eagle is the agent; the wings are the covenant authority it carries.

- **$bread** — $bread is the Word / covenant terms. Inside the ark, beneath the cherubim's wings, are the tablets and manna ($bread). The wings cover the covenant terms. To come under the wings is to come under the $bread — to be sheltered by and nourished by God's Word.

- **$faith** — $faith is trusting obedience. Ps 91:4 — "under his wings shalt thou **trust**." The response to the covenant covering is not passivity but active trust — obedient reliance on the one who spreads the wings.
