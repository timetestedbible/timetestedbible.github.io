# Myrtle

**The faithful remnant — humble, hidden, fragrant under pressure — who replace the cursed order and constitute God's dwelling.**

> *"Instead of the brier shall come up the **myrtle tree**: and it shall be to the LORD for a $[name], for an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off."* — Isa 55:13

---

## The Key Insight

The myrtle appears in only [seven](/reader/numbers/7) verses. But every occurrence shares the same profile: *low, hidden, faithful, evergreen, fragrant when crushed* — and always positioned where God stations Himself.

The plant itself tells the story. The myrtle is a low-growing shrub (2–5 meters, modest next to a cedar), with glossy evergreen leaves whose fragrance *intensifies when crushed*. It thrives in valleys and shaded areas, not on exposed heights. This production chain — pressure releasing fragrance — runs through every context where the myrtle appears.

**Symbolizes:** The faithful remnant — covenant people in humble, hidden position whose works are fragrant to God

**Opposite:** $thorns / brier — the cursed order that myrtle explicitly replaces (Isa 55:13)

**Defining verse:** Isa 55:13 — "instead of the brier shall come up the myrtle tree"

**The surprise:** The myrtle builds the booth. Neh 8:15 uses myrtle branches to construct Sukkot shelters. The booth is "a tabernacle for a $shadow" (Isa 4:6). The $shadow is the appointed times (Col 2:17). So the faithful remnant literally *constitutes the material* of God's covenant shelter.

**Connected:** $thorns, $shadow, $tree, $fig-tree, $booths, $to-and-fro

## Myrtle Across Scripture

### The Anti-Thorn

Isa 55:13 is the defining verse: "Instead of the thorn shall come up the fir tree, and instead of the brier shall come up the myrtle tree." The myrtle is the *explicit replacement* for the cursed order. Thorns choke the word (Mat 13:22); myrtle replaces them. The result is permanent: "an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off." The myrtle is an evergreen — it does not lose its leaves. The plant mirrors the promise.

### Where God Stands

In Zechariah's night vision (Zec 1:8), a man on a red horse stands among the myrtle trees "in the bottom" (H4699 *metsullah* — shade, from H6751 *tsalal*, the same root as $shadow). The KJV "bottom" suggests a low-lying, shaded valley — not a seat of visible power.

The angel of the LORD doesn't stand on a mountain or in a temple. He stands *among the myrtle trees* (Zec 1:11). The myrtle grove is the location of God's hidden presence. Patrol agents walk "to and fro through the earth" (Zec 1:10) and report back to this grove. Their report: "all the earth sitteth still, and is at rest." The nations are comfortable. God's response (vv. 14–15): jealousy for Jerusalem and anger at the nations who are "at ease."

The spatial picture is striking: the faithful remnant (myrtles) are in the shade, in the low place. The world is comfortable. And God is *with the myrtles*, not with the comfortable nations.

The LXX renders "myrtle trees" as "shady mountains" — interpreting rather than translating, apparently equating the myrtle grove with nations or kingdoms. Whether or not that's warranted, it confirms the translators saw weight in the image.

### The Material of God's Dwelling

Neh 8:15 lists myrtle branches alongside olive, palm, pine, and "thick trees" as building materials for Sukkot booths during the post-exile restoration of the Feast of Tabernacles. The text notes this hadn't been done "since the days of Joshua son of Nun" (v. 17), and the result was "very great gladness."

This is the connection that ties the myrtle to the whole shadow-booth-appointed-times chain: the booth (*sukkah*) is "a tabernacle for a $shadow in the daytime from the heat" (Isa 4:6). The $shadow is the appointed times (Col 2:17). And the booth is built from myrtle branches. The faithful remnant *constitutes the structure* of God's covenant shelter.

### Hadassah — The Myrtle-Girl

Est 2:7 introduces Esther with her Hebrew name first: "Hadassah, that is, Esther." H1919 *Hadassah* — feminine of H1918, literally "myrtle-girl."

The details read like the plant's profile made flesh: orphaned (no root visible), raised in exile, hidden identity (Hebrew name replaced by Persian name for the rest of the book), beautiful (found favor before the king), and ultimately willing to die for her people. She is a Jewish girl concealed within a foreign empire — the faithful remnant embodied in a single person.

Her Hebrew name is mentioned exactly once and then hidden under a foreign covering. The myrtle-identity persists but is not visible. Her fragrance — faithfulness under pressure — is what ultimately saves her people.

### Planted in the Wilderness

Isa 41:19 describes God planting seven types of trees in the wilderness: cedar, shittah, myrtle, oil tree, fir, pine, and box. The myrtle appears among larger species. The purpose (v. 20): "that they may see, and know, and consider, and understand together, that the hand of the LORD hath done this." God plants His people-garden in desolate places — wilderness becoming Eden (cf. Isa 51:3).

## Patterns

1. **Anti-thorn.** Isa 55:13 explicitly positions myrtle as the replacement for the brier. Where the cursed plant grew, the myrtle now stands.

2. **Low and hidden.** The myrtle is not a towering tree. In Zec 1:8 it's in the shade/bottom. Esther is hidden within a foreign court. The myrtle consistently appears in positions that are not prominent or powerful.

3. **Fragrance through crushing.** The myrtle's most distinctive physical property — fragrance released when leaves are crushed — parallels the olive-to-oil sequence (fruit pressed to yield oil). Pressure produces something pleasing.

4. **Embodied in a person.** Hadassah is the only individual in Scripture named after the myrtle. Her story — orphaned, concealed, faithful, willing to die, effective in intercession — gives the symbol a narrative embodiment.

5. **Material of God's dwelling.** Myrtle branches build Sukkot booths (Neh 8:15). The faithful remnant constitutes the structure of God's covenant shelter.

6. **Location of God's presence.** The angel of the LORD stands among the myrtle trees (Zec 1:8, 1:11) — not on a mountain, not in a visible seat of power.

7. **Everlasting.** The myrtle is an evergreen. Isa 55:13 calls the myrtle-replacement "an everlasting sign that shall not be cut off."

## Connections

- **$thorns** — Myrtle is the explicit anti-thorn (Isa 55:13). Thorns choke the word (Mat 13:22); myrtle replaces them.

- **$shadow** — The myrtle trees in Zec 1:8 are in the *metsullah* (H4699, shade). The faithful remnant dwells *in* God's shade — Ps 91:1 enacted in prophetic vision.

- **$booths** — Myrtle branches build the Sukkot booth (Neh 8:15). The booth is "a tabernacle for a shadow" (Isa 4:6). The appointed times are "a shadow of things to come" (Col 2:17). The faithful remnant constitutes the shelter.

- **$fig-tree** — When the barren fig is cursed (Mat 21:19), its shadow fails — leaves without fruit produce a false covering. The myrtle replaces the thorn/brier (Isa 55:13) and provides material for the booth that gives true shadow (Neh 8:15).

- **$tree** — Myrtle is a specific type of tree. Trees throughout Scripture appear as images of people or nations (Ezek 31:6). The myrtle is a particular kind: evergreen, low, fragrant — not powerful, but permanent.

- **$to-and-fro** — The agents in Zec 1:10–11 walk "to and fro through the earth" while stationed among the myrtle trees. The myrtle grove is the base of operations for this patrol activity.

## All Seven Occurrences

### Restoration — replacement of the cursed order
- Isa 55:13 — myrtle replaces brier; everlasting sign not cut off
- Isa 41:19 — God plants myrtle in wilderness alongside cedar, shittah, oil tree

### God's hidden presence among His people
- Zec 1:8 — man on red horse stands among myrtle trees in the shade
- Zec 1:10 — man among myrtle trees explains the to-and-fro patrol agents
- Zec 1:11 — patrol agents report to the angel of the LORD standing among the myrtle trees

### Material for covenant dwelling
- Neh 8:15 — myrtle branches gathered for building Sukkot booths

### Personal embodiment
- Est 2:7 — Hadassah (= myrtle), that is Esther — orphan, beautiful, faithful, concealed

## For Further Study

- **$shadow** — The myrtle grove is the shaded location of God's standing presence. How does shadow function as a symbol of divine protection and covenant covering?
- **$fig-tree** — The vine-and-fig formula defines covenant peace. When the fig fails, what fills the gap? The myrtle-to-booth connection may offer one answer.
