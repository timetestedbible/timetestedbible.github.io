# Born Again

**Receiving God's nature from above through His word — becoming His child and heir by a birth sourced from heaven, not from human will.**

> *"Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be **born** **from above**, he cannot see the kingdom of God."* — Joh 3:3

---

## The Key Insight

The Greek word G509 *anōthen* does not primarily mean "again." It means **from above**.

Every other time John uses this word, it means "from above." "He that cometh **from above** (G509) is above all" (Joh 3:31). "Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee **from above** (G509)" (Joh 19:11). The temple veil was rent "from the **top** (G509) to the bottom" (Mat 27:51). Luke uses the same word for "from the very first" (Luk 1:3).

Nicodemus heard "again" — and imagined re-entering his mother's womb (Joh 3:4). Jesus corrected him: not a second birth of the same kind, but a first birth from a completely different source. "That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit" (Joh 3:6). Two births. Two sources. Two natures.

The question Jesus poses is not "have you been born a second time?" but "what are you born *from*?"

This reframes the entire concept. In popular usage, "born again" describes a moment — when you "got saved," when you said a prayer, when you accepted Jesus. But Jesus's actual teaching is about the **source** of birth. Flesh produces flesh. Spirit produces spirit. The wind blows where it wills, and you cannot control it — "so is every one that is born of the Spirit" (Joh 3:8). It is not initiated by human decision. It is initiated from above.

**Symbolizes:** Receiving God's nature from above — becoming His child through His word and Spirit, producing a new identity evidenced by how you live

**Opposite:** Born of flesh — lineage, human will, and fleshly effort producing only more flesh (Joh 1:13, Joh 3:6, Gal 4:23)

**Defining verses:** Joh 3:3–8, 1 Pe 1:23, 1 Jo 3:9, 1 Jo 5:4

**The surprise:** John 3:10 — Jesus rebuked Nicodemus: "Art thou a master of Israel, and knowest not these things?" A teacher of the Torah should have recognized this teaching. It comes straight from Ezekiel 36:25–27 — the promise of clean water, a new heart, and God's Spirit causing obedience. "Born of water and the Spirit" was not a new doctrine. It was Israel's oldest hope, and the expert missed it.

**Connected:** $firstborn, $water, $truth, $light, $fruit, $heart, $birth-pains

## Born Again Across Scripture

### "Art Thou a Master of Israel?"

Before examining what "born again" means, consider what Nicodemus should have already known.

Jesus told a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews that a man must be "born of water and the Spirit" to enter the kingdom (Joh 3:5). Then He said: "Art thou a master of Israel, and knowest not these things?" (Joh 3:10). This rebuke only makes sense if Jesus was referencing something a Jewish teacher *should* recognize from the Hebrew Scriptures.

The reference appears to be Ezekiel 36:

> *"Then will I sprinkle clean **$[water]** upon you, and ye shall be clean: from all your filthiness, and from all your idols, will I cleanse you. A new **$[heart]** also will I give you, and a new spirit will I put within you: and I will take away the stony **$[heart]** out of your flesh, and I will give you an **$[heart]** of flesh. And I will put my spirit within you, and **cause you to walk in my statutes**, and ye shall keep my judgments, and do them."* — Eze 36:25–27

Three elements: (1) Water for cleansing, (2) a new spirit placed within, (3) the result — obedience to God's statutes. This is precisely what Jesus describes as "born of water and the Spirit." And the critical detail is the outcome: God does not merely offer a new nature; He **causes** obedience. The new birth produces a life that walks differently.

Ezekiel 37 extends the picture. The valley of dry bones — "the whole house of Israel" (Eze 37:11) — receives breath (H7307 *ruach* = breath, wind, spirit) and lives again (Eze 37:5–10). "I shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live" (Eze 37:14). A nation that was dead receives breath from God and stands up. The prophet is told to prophesy to the *wind* — and the same Hebrew word *ruach* that fills the bones is the word for wind that Jesus uses in John 3:8: "The wind bloweth where it listeth."

A master of Israel should have heard "born of water and the Spirit" and immediately thought of Ezekiel. Moses promised the same thing in different language: "The LORD thy God will circumcise thine heart, and the heart of thy seed, to love the LORD thy God with all thine heart" (Deu 30:6). David prayed for it: "Create in me a clean heart, O God; and renew a right spirit within me" (Psa 51:10). The new birth was not Jesus introducing something foreign. It was God fulfilling something ancient.

### Not of Blood, Not of the Will of Man

John's Gospel introduces the concept before the Nicodemus conversation. At the very beginning:

> *"But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his $[name]: Which were **born**, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God."* — Joh 1:12–13

Three negations define what the new birth is *not*:

1. **Not of blood** — not by ethnic descent or family lineage. Being Abraham's children by blood does not make you children of God (compare Rom 9:8: "the children of the flesh, these are not the children of God").
2. **Not of the will of the flesh** — not by natural desire or bodily impulse.
3. **Not of the will of man** — not by human decision, plan, or initiative.

The source is "of God" — and only of God. This triple negation strips away every human mechanism. You do not decide to be born. You do not earn it, inherit it ethnically, or generate it by willpower. Birth happens *to* you. It is sourced from above.

### "His Seed Remaineth in Him"

If birth requires a father's seed, what is God's "seed" that produces this birth? Two passages identify it directly:

> *"Being **born again** (G313), not of corruptible seed, but of incorruptible, by the **$[word]** of God, which liveth and abideth for ever."* — 1 Pe 1:23

> *"Of his own will **begat** he us with the **$[word]** of **$[truth]**, that we should be a kind of firstfruits of his creatures."* — Jas 1:18

The seed is the word of God. Incorruptible seed produces an incorruptible nature. Corruptible seed — flesh — produces flesh that decays. But the word of God "liveth and abideth for ever," and so does what it generates.

This connects to the parable of the sower (Mat 13:19–23), where the seed IS the word sown in different soils. The soil that receives it and bears $fruit is the one where the word takes root. The same pattern holds: God's word is the seed, and what it produces depends on the reception.

John confirms this in the middle of his "born of God" passages:

> *"Whosoever is **born of God** doth not commit sin; for **his seed remaineth in him**: and he cannot sin, because he is **born of God**."* — 1 Jo 3:9

"His seed remaineth in him" — God's word abides within the one born of God. The continuing presence of the seed is what sustains the new nature. This is why the born-of-God person does not practice sin: the word that generated the new birth continues to live inside, shaping behavior from within. Compare Ezekiel's promise: God's Spirit within *causes* the walk.

### By This We Know — The Behavioral Definition

The most striking feature of John's first epistle is how consistently he defines "born of God" by *observable behavior* — never by a confession, a moment, or an experience. The pattern is relentless:

> *"Every one that doeth righteousness is **born of him**."* — 1 Jo 2:29

> *"Whosoever is **born of God** doth not commit sin."* — 1 Jo 3:9

> *"Every one that loveth is **born of God**, and knoweth God."* — 1 Jo 4:7

> *"Whosoever believeth that Jesus is the Christ is **born of God**."* — 1 Jo 5:1

> *"Whatsoever is **born of God** overcometh the world."* — 1 Jo 5:4

> *"Whosoever is **born of God** sinneth not; but he that is **begotten of God** keepeth himself, and that wicked one toucheth him not."* — 1 Jo 5:18

Six statements. Six behavioral markers: does righteousness, does not practice sin, loves, believes, overcomes the world, keeps himself from the wicked one. Not one of these defines the new birth by a prayer prayed, a date remembered, or an emotional experience had. Each one defines it by what the person *does* — by $fruit.

The same epistle makes the contrast explicit:

> *"In this the children of God are manifest, and the children of the devil: whosoever **doeth not righteousness** is not of God, neither he that **loveth not** his brother."* — 1 Jo 3:10

Two families. Two lineages. And the distinguishing mark is conduct. You know the tree by its $fruit. The children of God do righteousness and love. The children of the devil do not. No other test is offered.

This does not mean perfection — John himself says "if any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father" (1 Jo 2:1). The Greek tense in "doth not commit sin" (1 Jo 3:9) suggests habitual practice, not isolated failure. The one born of God does not *practice* lawlessness as a way of life, because the seed — God's word — remains within and resists it.

### Born After the Spirit — The Two Covenants

Paul uses the birth metaphor to distinguish two covenant systems:

> *"But he who was of the bondwoman was **born after the flesh**; but he of the freewoman was **by promise**."* — Gal 4:23

> *"As then he that was **born after the flesh** persecuted him that was **born after the Spirit**, even so it is now."* — Gal 4:29

Ishmael and Isaac represent two kinds of birth. Ishmael was born through natural human effort — Abraham and Hagar producing a child by the will of the flesh. Isaac was born through God's promise — from a barren, elderly woman, by power that overrode natural impossibility.

Paul maps this onto two covenants:

| | Born after the flesh | Born after the Spirit |
|---|---|---|
| **Son** | Ishmael | Isaac |
| **Mother** | Hagar (bondwoman) | Sarah (freewoman) |
| **Covenant** | Sinai | Promise / $renewed-covenant |
| **City** | Jerusalem below (in bondage) | $jerusalem above (free) |
| **Result** | Bondage | Freedom |

The "mother" of those born from above is "Jerusalem which is above" — the heavenly $jerusalem, "which is the mother of us all" (Gal 4:26). Just as anōthen means "from above" in John, the birth that Paul describes comes from the heavenly realm. And just as with Isaac, this birth is not produced by human effort. Sarah's womb was dead. The child came by divine intervention alone.

The connection to John 1:13 is direct: "not of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God."

### Children of the Resurrection

Peter anchors the new birth to a specific event:

> *"Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which according to his abundant mercy hath **begotten us again** (G313) unto a lively hope **by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead**, to an inheritance incorruptible, and undefiled, and that fadeth not away, reserved in heaven for you."* — 1 Pe 1:3–4

The mechanism of the new birth is the resurrection. God "begot us again" *by* raising Jesus from the dead. The new birth is not detached from history — it is grounded in a specific historical event: the moment when death was conquered and incorruptible life became available.

Luke makes the connection even more explicit:

> *"Neither can they die any more: for they are equal unto the angels; and are the **children of God**, being the **children of the resurrection**."* — Luk 20:36

"Children of God" = "children of the resurrection." The two phrases are equated. To be born of God is to belong to the resurrection order — the order that has passed through death into life that does not end.

Paul sees the same trajectory:

> *"And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the **adoption**, to wit, the **redemption of our body**."* — Rom 8:23

The adoption — full sonship — awaits the redemption of the body. The Spirit is the "firstfruits" (the down payment), but the full inheritance is bodily resurrection. Being born of God begins now in how we live (1 John's behavioral markers), but it culminates in what we become: incorruptible, like the incorruptible seed that generated us (1 Pe 1:23).

### The Regeneration — Cosmic Rebirth

The word G3824 *palingenesia* (from *palin* = again + *genesis* = origin/birth) appears only twice in the New Testament. Both uses are remarkable:

> *"Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the **regeneration** when the Son of man shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel."* — Mat 19:28

> *"Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to his mercy he saved us, by the **washing of regeneration**, and renewing of the Holy Ghost."* — Tit 3:5

The first is cosmic: the "regeneration" is the moment when Christ sits on His throne — the renewal of all things. The world itself is born again. The second is personal: salvation comes through the "washing of regeneration" — water and Spirit, echoing both John 3:5 and Ezekiel 36:25–27.

The same word covers both scales. The individual experience of being born from above is a foretaste of the cosmic rebirth. What happens in the believer — dead things made alive, old nature replaced by new, the word of God taking root — will happen to the entire creation. Isaiah saw it: "Shall a nation be born at once? for as soon as $[jerusalem] travailed, she brought forth her children" (Isa 66:8). Paul heard creation groaning for it: "the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now" (Rom 8:22) — $birth-pains producing a new order.

## Patterns

1. **The source is always God, never man.** Joh 1:13 negates blood, flesh-will, and man-will. Joh 3:8 — the Spirit blows where it wills. Jas 1:18 — "of his own will begat he us." 1 Pe 1:3 — "according to his mercy." No passage attributes the new birth to a human decision.

2. **The seed is the word of God.** 1 Pe 1:23 — "born again by the word of God." Jas 1:18 — "begat us with the word of truth." 1 Jo 3:9 — "his seed remaineth in him." The father's nature is transmitted through His word/instruction.

3. **The evidence is behavioral, not experiential.** Every "born of God" statement in 1 John comes with a behavioral marker: does righteousness, does not sin, loves, believes, overcomes. None defines it by a prayer, a feeling, or a moment.

4. **Water and Spirit work together.** Joh 3:5, Eze 36:25–27, Tit 3:5 — cleansing (water) and indwelling (Spirit) are paired. Neither alone constitutes the new birth.

5. **Born of God = heirs.** 1 Pe 1:3–4 — born again → inheritance incorruptible. Rom 8:17 — children → heirs → joint-heirs with Christ. Heb 12:23 — "church of the $firstborn." The new birth confers inheritance rights.

6. **Resurrection is the anchor.** 1 Pe 1:3 — "begotten again by the resurrection." Luk 20:36 — "children of God = children of the resurrection." The new birth begins in behavior and culminates in bodily resurrection.

7. **The concept was not new — Nicodemus should have known.** Eze 36:25–27, Eze 37:14, Deu 30:6, Psa 51:10. The new heart, new spirit, and water cleansing were already promised. Jesus was announcing fulfillment, not innovation.

## Connections

- **$firstborn** — Being born of God makes you a covenant heir. Rom 8:29: Christ is "firstborn among many brethren." Heb 12:23: believers are the "church of the firstborn." The new birth confers the same covenant-heir status that Israel held (Exo 4:22), now extended through Christ.

- **$water** — "Born of water and the Spirit" (Joh 3:5). Water appears in Ezekiel's promise ("I will sprinkle clean water upon you," Eze 36:25), in Titus's "washing of regeneration" (Tit 3:5), and in the baptism pattern of death-and-rising (Rom 6:4). Water is the cleansing component of the new birth.

- **$truth** — "Begat us with the word of truth" (Jas 1:18). The seed that produces the new birth is identified as $truth — God's word, His instruction. The born-again life is generated by and sustained by $truth.

- **$heart** — Eze 36:26 promises "a new heart... a heart of flesh" replacing the stony heart. The new birth begins with the $heart — the internal transformation that precedes and produces the external behavior.

- **$fruit** — 1 John's behavioral markers parallel Jesus's teaching: "by their $fruit ye shall know them" (Mat 7:20). The new birth is known by what it produces — righteousness, love, faith, overcoming — not by what was said or felt at its inception.

- **$light** — "He that doeth $truth cometh to the $light, that his deeds may be made manifest, that they are wrought in God" (Joh 3:21). This verse concludes the born-again discourse. The one born from above moves toward visible obedience, not away from it.

- **$birth-pains** — The travail that produces the new order. Isa 66:8 — "shall a nation be born at once? for as soon as Zion travailed, she brought forth her children." Rom 8:22 — creation itself groans in $birth-pains, awaiting the fullness of what the new birth inaugurates.

KJV translates G1080 variously as "born," "begotten," "conceived," "brought forth." G509 as "again" (Joh 3:3, 3:7), "from above" (Joh 3:31, 19:11), "from the top" (Mat 27:51, Joh 19:23), "from the beginning" (Luk 1:3, Acts 26:5). The dual rendering of G509 as both "again" and "from above" is a translation choice — the Greek reader would have heard both simultaneously.
