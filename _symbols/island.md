# Island

**Sanctified people — set apart from the $sea of nations, waiting for God's standard.**

> *"Surely the **isles** shall wait for me, and the ships of Tarshish first, to bring thy sons from far, their silver and their gold with them, unto the name of the LORD thy God, and to the Holy One of Israel, because he hath glorified thee."* — Isa 60:9

---

## The Key Insight

Scripture tells us plainly what the $sea represents. Revelation 17:15 decodes it without ambiguity: *"The waters which thou sawest … are peoples, and multitudes, and nations, and tongues."* The sea is the restless, chaotic mass of unsanctified humanity — the wicked are "like the troubled sea, when it cannot rest" (Isa 57:20).

Now consider: what is an island?

It is not sea. It is land — but land *within* the sea, *surrounded by* the sea, *distinguished from* the sea. An island exists by virtue of separation. It rises out of the waters and stands apart.

The Hebrew concept that describes this quality — being set apart from what surrounds you — is *qadosh*: holy, sanctified. Not morally superior by nature, but *separated unto God*. The island's meaning is not derived from what it physically is (solid, stable, rocky), but from its *relationship* to the sea. A secular reader would look at "island" and think of geography. Scripture asks you to see the relationship: set-apart land in the midst of chaotic peoples.

This single relational insight unlocks every occurrence.

**Symbolizes:** Sanctified people — those distinguished and set apart from the nations

**Opposite:** The $sea — chaotic, unsanctified masses of humanity (Rev 17:15)

**Defining verses:** Gen 10:5, Isa 42:4, Isa 51:5, Rev 6:14, Rev 16:20

**The surprise:** The isles don't just *exist* as set-apart peoples — they *wait for God's law* (Isa 42:4). And Scripture tells us that God's $truth — His word, His commandments — is the very means by which people are sanctified (Jn 17:17). The set-apart peoples await the standard that makes them set apart.

**Connected:** $sea, $mountain, $truth

## Island Across Scripture

### The Relationship That Reveals the Meaning

The method here matters. If $sea = nations (Rev 17:15), then the symbolic meaning of everything *in relation to* the sea is shaped by that equation. A beast rising from the sea = a kingdom arising from the nations (Dan 7:2–3, Rev 13:1). A ship on the sea = an entity navigating among the nations. And land set apart from the sea = a people set apart from the nations.

This is not a physical description. It is a relational one. The island does not symbolize "stability" or "isolation" in some abstract sense — it represents the *act of being distinguished from* the chaotic mass of peoples. And the biblical word for "set apart from" is *sanctified*.

Creation itself establishes this pattern:

> *"And God said, Let the $[waters] under the heaven be gathered together unto one place, and let the dry land appear: and it was so."* — Gen 1:9

The first act of ordering the world involves separating land from water. God draws a boundary. Dry land appears *out of* the gathered waters. This creation-pattern — order emerging from chaos, the set-apart distinguished from the mixed — runs through every subsequent appearance of land-and-sea imagery in Scripture.

### Divided Among the Nations

> *"By these were the **isles** of the Gentiles divided in their lands; every one after his tongue, after their families, in their nations."* — Gen 10:5

The first occurrence of "isles" in Scripture describes the post-flood distribution of peoples. The sons of Japheth spread into distinct groups — each with its own tongue, family, and national identity. They are not a mixed, undifferentiated mass. They are *divided*, *distinguished*, *set apart* from one another.

The word translated "isles" here is H339 *iy* — and its very first use establishes the meaning: peoples who are distinct, who have been separated out and given their own identity and territory. The isles of the Gentiles are the Gentile nations as *differentiated peoples*, not the Gentile nations as a chaotic swarm.

### The Isles Wait for His Law

Three times in Isaiah, the isles are described as *waiting* — and what they wait for is deeply revealing:

> *"He shall not fail nor be discouraged, till he have set judgment in the earth: and the **isles** shall wait for his law."* — Isa 42:4

> *"My righteousness is near; my salvation is gone forth, and mine arms shall judge the people; the **isles** shall wait upon me, and on mine arm shall they trust."* — Isa 51:5

> *"Surely the **isles** shall wait for me."* — Isa 60:9

The isles wait for God's law, for His arm, for Him. They are not passive — they are *expectant*. They look for the very thing that will define and complete their set-apart status.

Now consider John 17:17:

> *"Sanctify them through thy $[truth]: thy word is $[truth]."* — Jn 17:17

$Truth in Scripture is God's word, His law, His commandments — the divine standard (Ps 119:142: "Thy law is the truth"). And $truth is the *means of sanctification*. To be sanctified is to be set apart — and the instrument that sets apart is God's word.

The connection is striking: the isles (set-apart peoples) await God's law (Isa 42:4), and God's law is the very thing that *makes* people set apart (Jn 17:17). The symbol and the mechanism are perfectly aligned. The sanctified peoples long for the standard that sanctifies them.

### Sent to the Isles Afar Off

> *"And I will set a sign among them, and I will send those that escape of them unto the nations, to Tarshish, Pul, and Lud, that draw the bow, to Tubal, and Javan, to the **isles** afar off, that have not heard my fame, neither have seen my glory; and they shall declare my glory among the Gentiles."* — Isa 66:19

God sends messengers to the isles afar off — distant set-apart peoples who have not yet heard. The isles are not a single location; they are scattered throughout the nations, separated by distance but united by their character: they are peoples *ready to be reached*, peoples who will receive the declaration of God's glory.

The substitution reads naturally: "I will send those that escape unto … the *sanctified peoples* afar off, that have not heard my fame." The gospel goes to those who are set apart but do not yet know the One who set them apart.

### Islands and Mountains — Paired in Judgment

Two passages in Revelation pair islands and $mountains in the context of cosmic shaking:

> *"And every **$[mountain]** and **island** were moved out of their places."* — Rev 6:14

> *"And every **island** fled away, and the **$[mountain]s** were not found."* — Rev 16:20

If $mountain = kingdom / ruling authority (Dan 2:35, Rev 17:9), then the pairing becomes clear: *kingdoms and set-apart peoples* are both shaken under God's judgment. Mountains are seats of power — governments, empires, thrones. Islands are sanctified communities — peoples distinguished from the chaotic mass. Both represent *ordered structures* that rise above the $sea.

Under the final judgment, both are displaced. Every kingdom is toppled; every set-apart community is shaken. The $sea (chaotic nations) rages, and the structures that stood above it — whether political authority ($mountain) or covenant identity (island) — are moved out of their places.

This pairing also suggests a functional distinction: the $mountain *rules over* the sea from above; the island *stands apart from* the sea at its level. One is authority; the other is sanctification. Both are forms of order in the midst of chaos, and both are subject to God's sovereign shaking.

### No More Sea — The End of the Distinction

> *"And I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was **no more $[sea]**."* — Rev 21:1

If the $sea is the chaotic mass of unsanctified nations, then "no more sea" means the turbulent, rebellious condition of humanity is finally eliminated. In the renewed creation, there is no more restless, godless mass.

And if the $sea vanishes — what happens to the island? The island exists only in relation to the sea. When the sea is gone, the distinction dissolves — not because the sanctified peoples cease to exist, but because everyone is sanctified. The set-apart are no longer set apart from anything. The boundary disappears because the chaos is gone.

The island's meaning is inherently relational: it requires the contrast with the $sea to function. In eternity, the contrast is no longer needed. The whole earth is holy ground.

## Patterns

1. **The island is defined by what it is NOT.** Every island passage gains its meaning from the contrast with the $sea. The island is the part that is not submerged, not chaotic, not mixed. Remove the sea and the concept of "island" loses its force — which is precisely why Rev 21:1 works.

2. **The isles wait.** Three times in Isaiah (42:4, 51:5, 60:9), the isles are characterized by expectant waiting — for law, for salvation, for God Himself. Waiting suggests both current separation (they are already set apart) and incompletion (they have not yet received what they are waiting for).

3. **Islands and mountains pair as structures above the sea.** Rev 6:14 and 16:20 link them in judgment. $Mountain = ruling authority; island = sanctified people. Both are ordered forms rising from the chaotic waters; both are shaken when God acts.

4. **The creation pattern is the template.** Gen 1:9–10 — God separates waters from dry land. This act of divine distinction underlies every subsequent sea/land contrast. To be "land" in a world of "sea" is to be the product of God's separating work.

5. **Truth sanctifies, and the isles await it.** Jn 17:17 + Isa 42:4 form a recursive loop: God's word is $truth → $truth sanctifies → the sanctified (isles) wait for God's law → the law (= $truth) sets them apart. The symbol and the mechanism point to each other.

## Connections

- **$sea** — The island's defining opposite. The $sea is the chaotic mass of unsanctified peoples and nations (Rev 17:15). The island exists only in distinction from the sea — sanctified people standing apart from the turbulent nations. When there is "no more sea" (Rev 21:1), the island's reason for existence is fulfilled: all chaos is gone, all peoples are sanctified, and the boundary dissolves.

- **$mountain** — Paired with the island in Rev 6:14 and Rev 16:20. $Mountain = kingdom / ruling authority — the seat of power that governs the nations from above. The island = sanctified people — the community of the set-apart that stands within the nations. Both are structures of order in the midst of the $sea; both are shaken when God judges. The mountain *rules*; the island *is distinguished*.

- **$truth** — God's word / His commandments / the divine standard (Jn 17:17, Ps 119:142). The isles *wait for his law* (Isa 42:4) — and that law is the $truth through which God sanctifies His people (Jn 17:17). $Truth is the mechanism that creates what the island represents: sanctification, set-apartness, distinction from the world.
