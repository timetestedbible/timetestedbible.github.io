# Full Moon

**The day of fullness — when the king takes his $throne. The Hebrew word for "full moon" (H3677) and the word for "throne" (H3678) are the same consonants: כסא.**

> *"Blow up the **trumpet** in the new moon, in the **time appointed** (H3677 keseh = full moon), on our solemn feast day."* — Ps 81:3

---

## The Key Insight

The KJV hides this word completely. H3677 *keseh* appears only twice in Scripture, and both times the KJV translates it "time appointed" — the word "moon" never appears. But multiple other translations (SLT, JPS, DRB, DBT) correctly render it **"full moon."**

Strong's defines H3677 as: "properly, **fulness** or the **full moon**, i.e. its festival."

The root H3680 *kasah* means "to fill up hollows, to cover." The full moon is the filled-up moon — hollows filled with light, fullness achieved. From the SAME root comes H3678 *kissé* = **$throne** ("properly, covered, i.e. a canopied seat").

Without vowels, both words are **כסא** (kaf-samekh-aleph). The Masoretic pointing forces a choice between "full moon" and "throne" — but the unpointed consonantal text holds both simultaneously. The full moon IS the throne. The day of fullness is the day the king takes his seat.

**Symbolizes:** The appointed day of the king's return — the fullness when authority is fully revealed

**Opposite:** New moon (H2320 *chodesh*) — the concealed/renewed moon, the beginning of the cycle

**Connected:** $throne, $trumpet, $marriage, $man-child, $shadow

---

## The Two Occurrences

H3677 appears in only two verses. Both are significant.

### The Shofar at the Full Moon

> *"Blow up the $[trumpet] (H7782 shofar) in the new moon (H2320), in the **full moon** (H3677 keseh), on our solemn feast day."* — Ps 81:3

The command binds two things together: the $trumpet and the full moon. The solemn feast at the full moon = the 15th of the month — when Passover (15th Nisan) and Tabernacles (15th Tishri) begin.

Psalm 81 continues (vv.4-5): "For this is a statute for $[israel], and a law of the God of $[jacob]. This he ordained in Joseph as a testimony, when he went out through the land of $[egypt]."

The shofar at the full moon is a **statute**, a **law**, and a **testimony** — ordained since the Exodus. If the "last trump" (1 Cor 15:52, 1 Th 4:16) marks the return, the connection to the full moon is worth considering.

### The Husband Returns at the Full Moon

> *"He hath taken a bag of money (H3701 keseph = silver) with him, and will come home at the day of the **full moon** (H3677 keseh)."* — Prov 7:20

The legitimate husband departs with silver (the price paid, the covenant sealed), and the day of his return is the day of the full moon. The bride knows the season — the keseh — but not the exact day or hour (compare Mat 24:36).

The $marriage pattern: the bridegroom departs (Jn 14:2-3 "I go to prepare a place... I will come again"), and the bride watches for the appointed day. The keseh is the "time appointed."

## Celestial Phenomena Exclusive to Full Moon

Several prophetic passages describe events that can only occur at the full moon phase:

### Light from East to West

> *"For as the **lightning** (G796 astrape = bright shining, glare) cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be."* — Mat 24:27

Actual lightning strikes locally and randomly — it does not "come from the east and shine to the west." But the full moon does exactly this: it rises in the east at sunset and its light is visible all the way to the west. It is the only celestial body whose light shines from east to west throughout the night.

### At Evening It Shall Be Light

> *"At evening time it shall be **light**."* — Zech 14:7

Normally, evening brings $darkness. But the full moon rises precisely as the sun sets — filling the sky with light at the moment darkness should begin. "Not day, nor night" — the twilight of sunset combined with the rising full moon.

### Sun and Moon Both Visible

> *"Sun, stand thou still upon Gibeon; and thou, Moon, in the valley of Ajalon."* — Josh 10:12

For both luminaries to be simultaneously visible on opposite sides of the sky, the moon must be in opposition to the sun. Opposition = full moon. This is the only phase where sun and moon stand on opposite horizons.

### Moon Turned to Blood

> *"The moon shall be turned into **blood**."* — Joel 2:31, Acts 2:20, Rev 6:12

For the moon to appear blood-colored, it must be visible — which requires a full or near-full phase. Joel 2:30 specifies the mechanism: "blood, and $[fire], and pillars of smoke." Smoke from judgment-fire reddens the visible moon. A new moon or thin crescent cannot appear "as blood" because there is nothing to see.

## The Three Hebrew Words for Moon

| Strong's | Word | Meaning | Emphasis |
|----------|------|---------|----------|
| H3394 | *yareach* | the moon | General — the celestial body (~26× OT) |
| H3842 | *levanah* | the white one | Brightness — from H3835 *laban* "to be white" (3× OT) |
| H3677 | *keseh* | the full one | Fullness — from H3680 *kasah* "to fill" (2× OT) |

H3842 *levanah* appears three times, all significant:

- Song 6:10 — the bride is "fair as the *levanah*" — the bride reflects the Son as the moon reflects the sun
- Isa 24:23 — the *levanah* is confounded when the LORD reigns — even the bright moon is outshone
- Isa 30:26 — the *levanah* shall be as the light of the sun — eschatological brightening

The math of Isa 30:26 only works at full moon: the full moon reflects approximately 1/7th of the sun's brightness. If the sun becomes sevenfold brighter, the moon = 1/7 × 7 = current sun brightness. "The light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun" — exactly the math of a full moon when the sun is seven times brighter.

## The Moon as Faithful Witness

> *"It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a **faithful witness** (H539 *aman*) in heaven."* — Ps 89:37

The moon = a faithful witness. H539 *aman* = faithful, established, trustworthy — the root of "Amen." The moon faithfully reflects the sun's light. At full moon, the witness is at maximum — the entire face reflects. The bride "fair as the moon" (Song 6:10) is the faithful witness who reflects the Son's light.

> *"He appointed the moon for **seasons** (H4150 *mo'edim* = appointed times)."* — Ps 104:19

The moon exists to mark the $shadow — the appointed times. The full moon (15th) is when the great feasts occur. The moon is not decoration — it is a covenant clock.

## The Jericho Connection

H3405 *Yericho* (Jericho) is from H3394 *yareach* (the moon). Jericho = the **moon city**. The city that falls at the $trumpet blast (Josh 6:20) is named after the moon. The shofar at the full moon (Ps 81:3) destroys the moon city.

The Jericho pattern: moon city falls at the trumpet → $babylon falls at the last trump. The same shofar that marks the full moon feast destroys the system.

## David's Empty Seat at the Full Moon Feast

1 Samuel 20 contains an enacted parable of remarkable precision. David should sit at the king's table at the new moon (chodesh) feast — the כסא-day. Instead he hides in the field:

> *"Tomorrow is the new moon, and I should not fail to **sit with the king** at meat: but let me go, that I may **hide myself in the field** unto the **third day** at even."* — 1 Sam 20:5

> *"Thou shalt be missed, because thy **seat will be empty**."* — 1 Sam 20:18

David — the anointed king — hides. His seat at the כסא-feast is empty. For **two days** (1 Sam 20:27 — "the second of the chodesh"). On the third day evening:

> *"David arose out of a place toward the south."* — 1 Sam 20:41

What does a full moon do at evening? It rises from the field on the horizon. The anointed king rising from the field IS the כסא ascending from the horizon. Compare Zech 14:7: "at evening time it shall be light."

The enacted parable: the anointed king's seat is empty for two days at the moon-feast. On the third day at evening, he rises. Compare Hos 6:2: "After two days will he revive us: in the third day he will raise us up."

## Patterns

1. **כסא without vowels = both throne and full moon.** The language itself holds the connection. The day of fullness is the day the king takes his seat.

2. **Shofar + full moon bound together.** Ps 81:3 — statute, law, testimony since the Exodus. The trumpet and the fullness are inseparable.

3. **The husband returns at the keseh.** Prov 7:20 — the bride knows the season but not the exact day. The $marriage typology is built into the word.

4. **Every major celestial sign of the coming = full moon phenomenon.** Light east to west (Mat 24:27), evening light (Zech 14:7), sun+moon opposite (Josh 10:12), blood moon through smoke (Joel 2:31). These converge on one lunar phase.

5. **The bride IS the moon.** Song 6:10 — fair as the levanah. The faithful witness reflecting the Son's light.

6. **The moon city falls at the trumpet.** Jericho (H3405 from H3394) = moon city destroyed by shofar.

## Connections

- **$throne** — Same root (H3680 *kasah*), same consonants (כסא). The full moon and the throne are linguistically identical. The keseh is when the kissé is filled.

- **$trumpet** — Ps 81:3 binds the shofar to the full moon. 1 Cor 15:52, 1 Th 4:16 — the last trump. The connection is worth tracing.

- **$marriage** — Prov 7:20: the husband returns at the keseh. The bridegroom-return pattern is tied to the full moon day.

- **$shadow** — The appointed times ($shadow = Col 2:17) are marked by the moon (Ps 104:19). The full moon marks the great feasts (15th).

- **$man-child** — Rev 12:5: the man child is caught up to God and to His **throne** (כסא). If throne = keseh, the man child is caught up to the fullness.
