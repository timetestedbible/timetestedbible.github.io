# Alarmed / Do Not Be Troubled

**Covenant-judgment panic — the physical alarm response (heart-faint, hands-feeble, face-change, birth-pang anguish) that belongs to those who do NOT recognize God's hand. Prohibited for God's people, who discern what the shaking means.**

> *"And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not **troubled**: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

---

## The Key Insight

The same event produces two opposite responses. That is the entire pattern.

When $babylon's king hears the report of armies coming, his "hands waxed feeble: anguish took hold of him, and pangs as of a woman in travail" (Jer 50:43). Five verses earlier, God's people inside Babylon receive the same report — and are told: "Let not your heart faint, and fear not for the rumour that shall be heard in the land" (Jer 51:46).

Same rumor. Same armies. Same city. One group collapses. The other is commanded to stand.

This is not a personality difference. It is a *covenant* difference. The alarm-response — the cascading physical panic of faint hearts, feeble hands, pale faces, and birth-pang anguish — is what happens when you witness covenant judgment and do not know Whose hand is behind it. The prohibition against being troubled is not a call to stoicism. It is a call to *recognition*: these things "must come to pass." The one who discerns the purpose does not panic at the process.

Jesus compresses this entire Old Testament pattern into a single Greek word — G2360 *throeō* — and says: *don't*.

**Symbolizes:** The panic response that belongs to those outside covenant recognition — prohibited for those who discern God's hand in the shaking

**Opposite:** $peace (*shalom* / *eirēnē*) — covenant wholeness, not the absence of trouble but the presence of the one who governs it. "My $[peace] I give unto you: not as the world giveth" (Jn 14:27)

**Defining verses:** Mat 24:6, Jer 51:46, Jer 50:43, Jn 14:1, Jn 14:27, 2Chr 15:5–7

**The surprise:** The Hebrew vocabulary for this alarm is a *compound physiological collapse* — heart melting (H4549), hands going slack (H7503), face turning green (H3420), trembling (H2731), and birth-pang anguish (H6869) — and this exact compound formula gets recycled verbatim from one oracle to the next, first applied to Zion under $babylon, then applied *to $babylon itself*. The terror migrates. The covenant-keeper's job is to not let it migrate into them.

**Connected:** $birth-pains, $wars-and-rumors-of-wars, $nation-against-nation, $earthquake, $famine, $babylon, $deceive, $the-end, $sudden-destruction, $peace

## Alarmed Across Scripture

### The Body of Panic — What Fear Looks Like in Hebrew

English translations flatten the alarm-response into generic words like "afraid" or "troubled." The Hebrew texts are far more precise. They describe a *body shutting down* — a compound physiological collapse that appears with striking consistency across the prophets:

> *"Therefore shall all **hands be faint**, and every man's heart shall **melt**: And they shall be **afraid**: **pangs** and **sorrows** shall take hold of them; they shall be in pain **as a woman that travaileth**: they shall be amazed one at another; their faces shall be **as flames**."* — Isa 13:7–8

This single passage contains nearly the full vocabulary cluster:

- **Hands faint** — H7503 *raphah*, to slacken, go limp. The hands that should act simply drop.
- **Heart melt** — H4549 *masas*, to dissolve. The internal will liquefies.
- **Afraid** — H926 *bahal*, sudden alarm, dismay. Not chronic anxiety but acute shock.
- **Pangs and sorrows** — H6735 *tsir* + H2256 *chebel*. The $birth-pains vocabulary.
- **As a woman that travaileth** — H3205 *yalad*. The comparison to labor is not metaphorical decoration; it is a precise technical identification: this is the $birth-pains response.
- **Faces as flames** — faces flushed or changed. In Jer 30:6, the same syndrome includes H3420 *yeraqon* — a pallor, a greenish paleness. The face *changes* under judgment-shock.

This is not a list of synonyms. It is an anatomical sequence: the hands fail, the heart dissolves, alarm strikes, and the body responds as if in labor, with the face visibly transformed. The prophets use this cluster so consistently that it becomes a formula — a recognizable signature of covenant-judgment terror.

### The Recycled Formula

The most striking evidence that this panic-response functions as a prophetic formula is the verbatim recycling between Jer 6:24 and Jer 50:43:

> *"We have heard the fame thereof: our **hands wax feeble**: **anguish** hath taken hold of us, and **pain**, as of a woman in **travail**."* — Jer 6:24

This describes *Zion* hearing that Babylon is coming. The hands go slack (H7503 *raphah*), anguish grips (H6869 *tsarah*), and labor-pangs strike (H2427 *chil* + H3205 *yalad*).

Now compare:

> *"The king of Babylon hath heard the report of them, and his **hands waxed feeble**: **anguish** took hold of him, and **pangs** as of a woman in **travail**."* — Jer 50:43

The Hebrew is virtually identical. The same formula — the same hands-feeble, same anguish, same birth-pangs — has been *transferred* from Zion to Babylon's king. What once described God's people panicking at Babylon's approach now describes Babylon panicking at its own judgment.

The terror is portable. It moves from one subject to another based on who is under judgment. It is not inherent to any nation; it is inherent to the *position* of standing under covenant wrath without recognizing the covenant-keeper behind it.

### The Template Jesus Quotes

> *"And nation was destroyed of nation, and city of city: for God did vex them with all adversity. Be ye strong therefore, and let not your **hands be weak**: for your work shall be rewarded."* — 2Chr 15:5–7

The prophet Azariah speaks to King Asa. The conditions he describes — $nation-against-nation, city against city, God Himself troubling the peoples — are the same conditions Jesus identifies in the Olivet Discourse. And the instruction is the same: *do not let your hands go slack* (H7503 *raphah*).

Jesus' "see that ye be not troubled" (Mat 24:6) is not a new teaching. It is the compression of a prophetic tradition that runs through the entire Old Testament: when God shakes the nations, His covenant people are told to *stand firm and not panic*, because the shaking has a purpose.

2Chr 15:5–7 is the clearest template:
1. God Himself is the agent of the trouble ("God did vex them")
2. The trouble manifests as internal fracture ($nation-against-nation)
3. The response for covenant people is strengthened hands, not weakened ones
4. The reason is purposeful: "your work shall be rewarded"

### The Olivet Prohibitions

Jesus issues the alarm-prohibition three times in the Olivet Discourse, each time using a different Greek word — and each word illuminates a different dimension of the panic He forbids:

> *"See that ye be not **troubled** (G2360 *throeō*): for all these things must come to pass, but $[the-end] is not yet."* — Mat 24:6

G2360 *throeō* means to cry aloud in alarm, to be frightened by clamor. It is the panic caused by alarming *reports* — wars and rumors of wars. The word appears only three times in the New Testament (Mat 24:6, Mk 13:7, 2Th 2:2), always in the specific context of eschatological alarm. Jesus is not addressing general anxiety. He is addressing the specific panic that comes from hearing about wars, upheavals, and rumors of $the-end.

> *"But when ye shall hear of wars and commotions, be not **terrified** (G4422 *ptoēo*): for these things must first come to pass; but $[the-end] is not by and by."* — Lk 21:9

G4422 *ptoēo* means to startle, to be scared into flight — the instinct to *run*. Luke's version adds the flight-reflex dimension: do not be startled into fleeing. The same word appears in Lk 24:37 when the disciples see the risen Jesus and are "terrified" — startled by what they do not understand.

> *"Men's hearts **failing** them for **fear**, and for looking after those things which are coming on the earth: for the powers of heaven shall be **shaken**."* — Lk 21:26

Here the prohibition gives way to description: this is what *will* happen to those who lack covenant recognition. G674 *apopsychō* — literally, the soul departing — hearts failing from fear. G4531 *saleuō* — shaken, the same word Paul uses in 2Th 2:2 ("be not soon shaken"). The heavenly powers shake; the unrecognizing hearts collapse. But the disciples have already been told: you are not to be among them.

The structure is precise: the same events (wars, $earthquakes, $nation-against-nation) produce alarm in the nations and steadiness in the covenant-aware. The events are not the diagnostic. The *response* is.

### Paul Preserves the Tradition

> *"That ye be not soon **shaken** (G4531 *saleuō*) in mind, or be **troubled** (G2360 *throeō*), neither by spirit, nor by word, nor by letter as from us, as that the day of Christ is at hand."* — 2Th 2:2

Paul uses the exact same alarm-word (G2360 *throeō*) that Jesus used on the Mount of Olives. He is not loosely echoing Jesus — he is preserving the technical prohibition. The Thessalonians have been shaken by a report that the day of Christ has already come, and Paul applies the Olivet instruction directly: do not be *throeō*-troubled by alarming eschatological reports.

He then adds the diagnostic marker: before that day arrives, certain things must first occur — the falling away, the man of sin revealed (2Th 2:3). Paul's argument mirrors Jesus' exactly: *these things must come to pass, but $the-end is not yet.* The alarm-response is premature because the sequence has not completed. Covenant recognition means knowing where you are in the sequence.

### The Upper Room — A Different Word, the Same Prohibition

> *"Let not your heart be **troubled** (G5015 *tarassō*): ye believe in God, believe also in me."* — Jn 14:1

> *"$[peace] I leave with you, my $[peace] I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be **troubled** (G5015 *tarassō*), neither let it be afraid."* — Jn 14:27

Jesus shifts vocabulary here. G5015 *tarassō* is a broader word — to agitate, to stir up, to roil like water. It is the word used for the troubling of the water at Bethesda (Jn 5:4), for Herod troubled at the magi's news (Mat 2:3), for the disciples troubled at seeing Jesus walk on the sea (Mat 14:26).

The trigger in Jn 14 is not eschatological upheaval but *personal loss* — Jesus is leaving. Yet the prohibition is structurally identical: do not let your heart be agitated, because the departure has a *purpose* ("I go to prepare a place for you," Jn 14:2). The antidote is the same: recognition of the divine plan behind the troubling event.

And there is a profound anomaly: Jesus Himself experienced *tarassō*. "Now is my soul troubled" (Jn 12:27). "He was troubled in spirit" (Jn 13:21). The same word. The one who prohibits the alarm-response for His disciples *absorbed it Himself*. He took the agitation so they would not have to carry it. The peace He gives in Jn 14:27 is not advice — it is transfer. He bears the *tarassō*; they receive the *eirēnē*.

### Peter's Final Application

> *"And if ye suffer for righteousness' sake, happy are ye: and be not **afraid** (G5399 *phobeō*) of **their** terror (G5401 *phobos*), neither be **troubled** (G5015 *tarassō*)."* — 1Pe 3:14

Peter quotes Isa 8:12–13 and applies the pattern one final time. The emphasis falls on the word *their*: the terror belongs to the oppressor, not to the oppressed. Fear is the property of those who stand on the wrong side of the covenant. Peter's instruction makes the sorting mechanism explicit — the alarm-response has an *owner*, and it is not you.

### Jeremiah 30 — The Covenant-Keeper's Birth Pains

> *"We have heard a voice of **trembling** (H2731 *charadah*), of **fear** (H6343 *pachad*), and not of $[peace]. Ask ye now, and see whether a man doth **travail** with child? wherefore do I see every man with his hands on his loins, as a woman in **travail**, and all faces are turned into **paleness** (H3420 *yeraqon*)? Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; **but he shall be saved out of it**."* — Jer 30:5–7

Here the entire compound panic-vocabulary assembles in a single passage: trembling (H2731), dread (H6343), no $peace, the $birth-pains comparison, hands on loins, face-paleness (H3420 — a sickly greenish pallor). This is the maximum-intensity alarm.

And yet the passage ends with deliverance: "he shall be saved out of it." The alarm is real. The suffering is real. But the alarm is not the *final word*. This is Jacob's trouble — named, known, appointed — and it has an exit. The one who recognizes the covenant structure of the event can endure the alarm without being owned by it.

### The Sorting Mechanism

The deepest pattern in this symbol is not the prohibition itself but what the prohibition *reveals*: the alarm-response is a diagnostic tool. The same crisis separates two groups based on their response.

| Event | Covenant-Keeper | Covenant-Breaker |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| Armies approach Babylon | "Let not your heart faint" (Jer 51:46) | "Hands waxed feeble" (Jer 50:43) |
| Wars and upheavals | "Be not troubled" (Mat 24:6) | "Hearts failing for fear" (Lk 21:26) |
| Suffering for righteousness | "Be not afraid of their terror" (1Pe 3:14) | *Their* terror (1Pe 3:14) |
| Nations shaken | "Let not hands be weak" (2Chr 15:7) | "God vexed them with all adversity" (2Chr 15:5) |

The event is identical on both sides. The response splits. And the reason for the split is always the same: *recognition*. "These things must come to pass" (Mat 24:6) — the one who knows the plan does not panic at its execution. The $birth-pains hurt, but the one who knows a child is coming does not mistake labor for death.

## Patterns

1. **Same event, opposite responses.** This is the master pattern. Every prohibition against alarm appears alongside a description of others who *do* experience it. The alarm is not absent — it is *redirected* away from covenant-keepers and onto those who lack covenant recognition.

2. **The compound physiological formula is consistent across centuries.** Heart-melt (H4549) + hands-feeble (H7503) + face-paleness (H3420) + trembling (H2731) + anguish (H6869) + $birth-pains comparison (H3205) appears in Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Psalms — always describing the same type of event: covenant judgment arriving.

3. **The formula migrates between subjects.** Jer 6:24 applies it to Zion; Jer 50:43 applies the same words to Babylon's king. Isa 13:7–8 applies it to Babylon under God's wrath; Ezek 7:17 applies it to Israel under God's wrath. The terror attaches to whoever is currently under judgment — it is not ethnically fixed.

4. **Jesus compresses the Hebrew compound into single Greek words.** The two-word Hebrew pair in Jer 51:46 (heart H7401-faint + H3372-fear) becomes one word in Mat 24:6 (G2360 *throeō*). The physical collapse vocabulary (H7503, H4549, H3420) compresses into Luke's "hearts failing" (G674 *apopsychō*). Jesus is not creating a new concept; He is condensing an existing prophetic tradition.

5. **The prohibition always includes a reason.** "For all these things MUST come to pass" (Mat 24:6). "For God did vex them" (2Chr 15:5). "I go to prepare a place" (Jn 14:2). "Your work shall be rewarded" (2Chr 15:7). The command to not be alarmed is never bare — it always comes with an explanation of purpose that makes the alarm unnecessary.

6. **The alarm-response is linked to $birth-pains at every level.** Isa 13:8, Jer 6:24, Jer 50:43, Jer 30:5–7 — the physiological panic and the birth-travail comparison always appear together. The $birth-pains are the *content* of what the alarmed person is experiencing. To recognize the pains as labor is to be freed from the panic.

7. **G5015 *tarassō* and G2360 *throeō* are distinct.** *Throeō* is specific eschatological panic triggered by alarming reports (Mat 24:6, Mk 13:7, 2Th 2:2). *Tarassō* is broader personal agitation (Jn 14:1, 14:27). Both are prohibited, but they address different triggers — prophetic rumors versus personal loss. The antidote for both is the same: covenant recognition and the peace that comes from knowing the hand behind the event.

8. **Jesus absorbs what He prohibits.** He was *tarassō*-troubled (Jn 12:27, 13:21) so that His disciples need not be (Jn 14:1, 14:27). This is not hypocrisy — it is substitution.

## Connections

- **$birth-pains** — The alarm-response and the $birth-pains vocabulary are fused at the root. The same passages that describe hands-feeble and hearts-failing also describe "pangs as of a woman that travaileth." The alarm IS the experience of $birth-pains from the perspective of someone who does not recognize the labor for what it is.

- **$wars-and-rumors-of-wars** — The immediate trigger for the alarm-prohibition in the Olivet Discourse. "Ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled" (Mat 24:6). Wars are the *report* that activates G2360 *throeō* — clamor-fright at alarming news.

- **$nation-against-nation** — The first sign listed alongside the alarm-prohibition, echoing the 2Chr 15:5–6 template where God Himself fractures nations and the prophet says "let not your hands be weak." The internal fracturing of covenant order is the *content* of the alarm.

- **$earthquake** — "Earthquakes in divers places" accompany the alarm-prohibition (Mat 24:7). God shaking established order (Hag 2:6–7, Heb 12:26–27) is precisely what produces the panic in those who trusted the order rather than the one who governs it.

- **$famine** — Part of the $birth-pains sequence (Mat 24:7) that produces alarm. When the Word is withdrawn ($famine), the panic intensifies — those without covenant recognition lose both provision and understanding simultaneously.

- **$babylon** — The subject that most vividly demonstrates the sorting mechanism. God's people *inside* Babylon are told not to panic (Jer 51:46); Babylon's king *does* panic (Jer 50:43). Same city, same crisis, opposite responses.

- **$deceive** — "Take heed that no man $[deceive] you" is the *first* warning in the Olivet Discourse (Mat 24:4), preceding even the alarm-prohibition. Deception and alarm work together: false reports trigger premature panic, and panic makes one susceptible to false solutions.

- **$the-end** — "The $[end] is not yet" (Mat 24:6) is the reason given for not being alarmed. Wars, upheavals, and $nation-against-nation are *not* the $the-end — they are the beginning of $birth-pains. The alarm arises from mistaking the labor for the finish.

- **$sudden-destruction** — "When they shall say, Peace and safety; then $[sudden-destruction] cometh upon them" (1Th 5:3). The alarm belongs to those who declared premature peace. Those who recognized the sequence are "not in $[darkness], that that day should overtake you" (1Th 5:4).

- **$peace** — The direct antidote. "My $[peace] I give unto you: not as the world giveth" (Jn 14:27). The world's peace is the absence of conflict. Christ's $peace is covenant wholeness — the knowledge that the one governing the crisis is your covenant partner.

- **$peace** — The direct opposite and antidote. Christ's peace is not the world's peace — what makes covenant peace different from the mere absence of conflict?
- **$babylon** — The city where the sorting mechanism is most visible. God's people inside $babylon are told not to panic while $babylon's own king collapses. What does it mean to be in $babylon but not of it?
- **$earthquake** — God shaking all established order. The alarm-response is the human reaction to divine shaking — what is the shaking designed to *remove*, and what remains?
