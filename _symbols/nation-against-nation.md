# Nation Against Nation

**Covenant order fracturing from within — peoples who should be unified turning on each other under God's hand as a sign of divine judgment.**

> *"For **nation shall rise against nation**, and **kingdom against kingdom**: and there shall be $[famine]s, and pestilences, and $[earthquake]s, in divers places. All these are the beginning of $[birth-pains]."* — Mat 24:7–8

---

## The Key Insight

The phrase "nation against nation, kingdom against kingdom" is not a general description of war. It is a *fixed formula* — and Jesus is quoting it.

Over six hundred years before the Olivet Discourse, the prophet Azariah stood before King Asa and said:

> *"**Nation** was destroyed of **nation**, and city of city: for God did vex them with all adversity."* — 2 Chr 15:6

The subject is not foreign invasion. It is *internal* collapse. God Himself is the agent — "God did vex them." The fracturing is not a natural consequence of political tensions; it is a covenant curse. Peoples who belonged together are being torn apart because God is actively troubling them.

When Jesus uses the same formula in Mat 24:7, His audience would have heard Azariah's warning: this is not about Rome versus Parthia. It is about the covenant community itself splintering — the sign that God's hand of discipline is at work.

And the opposite confirms it. Isaiah envisions a future where "nation shall not lift up sword against nation, neither shall they learn war any more" (Isa 2:4) — because the nations are streaming *together* up the $highway to the $mountain of the LORD. The $highway is the covenant path of faithfulness; when peoples walk it, they converge. When the highway lies waste, they fracture.

**Symbolizes:** Covenant peoples in internal conflict — the sign that unified covenant order is breaking apart under divine discipline

**Opposite:** The $highway — the covenant path where nations stream together toward God (Isa 2:2–4). When the highway is walked, peoples unite. When it is abandoned, they devour each other.

**Defining verses:** 2 Chr 15:6, Mat 24:7, Isa 19:2, Isa 9:19–21

**The surprise:** Every Old Testament occurrence features *internal* fracture — brother against brother, tribe against tribe, city against city. It is never about foreign empires clashing. God sets the members of a unified body against one another.

**Connected:** $birth-pains, $earthquake, $famine, $mountain, $darkness, $highway

## Nation Against Nation Across Scripture

### God Is the Troubler

The usual reading of "nation against nation" treats it as a vague description of geopolitical conflict — wars happen, kingdoms clash, and this is an expected feature of history. But every occurrence in Scripture identifies a specific *agent* behind the fracturing.

> *"**Nation** was destroyed of **nation**, and city of city: for **God did vex them** with all adversity."* — 2 Chr 15:6

The Hebrew is emphatic. God *troubled* them (H926 *bahal* — to disturb, terrify, confuse). The fracturing is not incidental; it is purposeful. It is covenant discipline — the internal dissolution of a people who should be at peace with one another but who have abandoned the covenant that held them together.

Isaiah's oracle against Egypt uses the same structure:

> *"And **I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians**: and they shall fight every one against his brother, and every one against his neighbour; city against city, and **kingdom against kingdom**."* — Isa 19:2

"I will set" — YHWH is the subject. He is not merely allowing Egypt to collapse; He is orchestrating it. The Spirit of Egypt will "fail in the midst thereof" (Isa 19:3), their counsel will be swallowed up, and they will turn to idols and sorcerers — to $darkness, the concealment of disobedience — rather than to the living God. Internal war is the *symptom*; divine discipline is the cause.

Haggai sees the same pattern on a cosmic scale:

> *"And **I will overthrow** the throne of kingdoms, and I will destroy the strength of the kingdoms of the heathen; and I will overthrow the chariots, and those that ride in them; and the horses and their riders shall come down, **every one by the sword of his brother**."* — Hag 2:22

Brother slaying brother. $mountain thrones overturned — not by external conquerors, but by mutual destruction. This is preceded by the shaking of heavens and earth (Hag 2:21) — $earthquake, the violent upheaval that removes every unfaithful order. The $mountains (kingdoms and ruling authorities) collapse because their own members consume one another.

### The Covenant Curse Pattern

The formula "brother against brother" is not original to Chronicles or Isaiah. It echoes the covenant curses of Leviticus and Deuteronomy:

> *"And ye shall be slain before your enemies: they that hate you shall reign over you; and **ye shall flee when none pursueth you**."* — Lev 26:17

> *"And **five of you shall chase an hundred**, and **an hundred of you shall put ten thousand to flight**"* — Lev 26:8

The promise in obedience is that a few Israelites will rout thousands. The curse in disobedience is the reverse: Israel will flee in terror from no pursuer, devoured by internal panic and mutual suspicion. Leviticus 26:25 adds: "I will send the pestilence among you; and ye shall be delivered into the hand of the enemy." The triad of $famine, pestilence, and sword that accompanies "nation against nation" in both Ezekiel and the Olivet Discourse is *the covenant curse sequence* — the specific penalties announced at Sinai for covenant unfaithfulness.

Isaiah 9 makes the internal nature explicit:

> *"Through the wrath of the LORD of hosts is the land darkened, and the people shall be as the fuel of the $[fire]: **no man shall spare his brother**. And he shall snatch on the right hand, and be hungry… **Manasseh, Ephraim; and Ephraim, Manasseh**: and they together shall be against Judah."* — Isa 9:19–21

These are the tribes of Israel — brothers, sons of the same covenant — devouring each other. The $darkness descends: not merely the concealment of disobedience, but the blindness that results from it. Wickedness burns "as the fire" and consumes the very people who should be one body. The covenant community is eating itself alive.

This is what "nation against nation" *means*. Not empires at war across borders, but peoples who should be unified under covenant order turning their swords inward.

### The Highway Abandoned

The defining contrast illuminates the symbol from the other direction. When covenant faithfulness holds, the image is of peoples streaming *together*:

> *"And many nations shall come, and say, Come, and let us go up to the **$[mountain]** of the LORD, and to the house of the God of Jacob; and he will teach us of his ways, and we will walk in his paths… And he shall judge among many peoples, and rebuke strong nations afar off; and **they shall beat their swords into plowshares**, and their spears into pruninghooks: **nation shall not lift up a sword against nation**, neither shall they learn war any more."* — Mic 4:2–3

The $highway leads to the $mountain — the covenant path leads to the kingdom of God. When nations walk it, they converge. War ceases. Swords become agricultural tools. "Nation shall not lift up sword against nation" is the *exact negation* of "nation shall rise against nation."

But when the $highway lies waste, the opposite occurs:

> *"The **$[highway]s** lie waste, the wayfaring man ceaseth: he hath broken the **covenant**, he hath despised the cities, he regardeth no man."* — Isa 33:8

The covenant is broken → the highways are desolate → travellers cease → the social order collapses. The $highway is the covenant path of faithfulness; its abandonment is the precondition for nation rising against nation. The connection is not metaphorical — it is causal. When peoples stop walking the covenant path, they stop moving in the same direction. And peoples moving in opposing directions eventually collide.

### The First Birth Pain

> *"All these are the beginning of **sorrows**."* — Mat 24:8

The word "sorrows" is G5604 *odin* — $birth-pains. Jesus places "nation against nation" as the *first* sign in a sequence that leads to a new order. $birth-pains are not purposeless suffering; they are *transitional* pain — covenant judgment producing a new covenant order. The existing order must fracture before the new one can emerge.

The sequence matters: internal fracture ($nation-against-nation) → $famine (God's Word withdrawn) → $earthquake (established order shaken loose) → persecution → false prophets → the end. The fracturing of the covenant community is the *first* symptom, not the last. It is the initial sign that God's hand of discipline is at work — the earliest labor contraction, still far from delivery, but unmistakable.

This maps onto 2 Chr 15:6 exactly. Azariah told Asa that the nation-against-nation fracturing happened *because God troubled them* — it was a sign of His active intervention, not a random misfortune. Jesus tells His disciples the same thing. When you see the covenant community fragmenting — ethnos against ethnos, basileia against basileia — recognize the sign. God is at work. The labor has begun.

### Ethnos and Goy — The Ambiguity Is the Point

The Hebrew word for "nation" in these passages is H1471 *goy*. In the LXX (the Greek Old Testament), it is consistently translated G1484 *ethnos* — the same word Jesus uses in Mat 24:7.

Both words carry a deliberate ambiguity. *Goy* can mean "nation" in a neutral sense — Israel itself is called a *goy* in Gen 12:2 ("I will make of thee a great *goy*"). But it also becomes the standard term for the *gentile* nations — the non-covenant peoples. *Ethnos* carries the same dual sense in the New Testament.

The ambiguity is not a problem to be resolved; it is the symbol doing its work. When the covenant people fracture, they *become indistinguishable from the nations*. The internal conflict strips away covenant identity. Ephraim against Manasseh, both against Judah (Isa 9:21) — these are covenant tribes behaving as *goyim*. Egypt's internal collapse (Isa 19:2) makes Egyptians enemies to other Egyptians. The fracturing does not merely weaken a people; it dissolves the unity that made them a *people* at all.

A covenant community turning on itself has, in effect, abandoned the $highway. It is no longer walking the elevated covenant path. It has descended to the byways — the crooked paths of unfaithfulness — where there is no shared direction, no common destination, and no peace.

## Patterns

1. **God is the agent, not the bystander.** Every occurrence names God as the one who sets peoples against each other: "God did vex them" (2 Chr 15:6), "I will set the Egyptians against the Egyptians" (Isa 19:2), "I will overthrow" (Hag 2:22). This is not natural political entropy — it is active covenant discipline.

2. **The fracture is always internal.** Brother against brother, city against city, tribe against tribe, Egyptians against Egyptians. The formula never describes foreign wars. It describes *self-destruction* within a unified body.

3. **The covenant curse triad accompanies it.** Sword, $famine, pestilence — the Levitical covenant curses (Lev 26, Deut 28) — cluster around "nation against nation" in Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Olivet Discourse. The fracturing is one element in a recognized judgment sequence.

4. **It is the first birth pain.** In Mat 24:7–8, nation-against-nation is the *beginning* of $birth-pains — the earliest sign that the old order is being broken so a new one can emerge. First the community fractures, then the $famine (Word withdrawn), then the $earthquake (established order shaken), then the consummation.

5. **Its negation is the messianic age.** "Nation shall not lift up sword against nation" (Isa 2:4, Mic 4:3) defines the age when the $highway is restored and all peoples stream to God's $mountain. The presence of nation-against-nation is therefore a negative marker: the messianic peace has not yet arrived, or the covenant that produced it has been broken.

6. **Jesus quotes the OT formula verbatim.** The LXX of 2 Chr 15:6 reads *ethnos epi ethnos* — nation upon nation. Mat 24:7 reads *ethnos epi ethnos*. Identical phrasing. The Olivet Discourse is not offering a novel prophecy; it is applying Azariah's established diagnostic: when you see this, God is the troubler.

## Connections

- **$birth-pains** — Nation-against-nation is the first contraction in a labor sequence that produces a new covenant order. Mat 24:8 places it at the "beginning of sorrows" — the earliest sign that the painful transition from old order to new has begun. The pain is purposeful, not random.

- **$earthquake** — The shaking that follows the fracturing. Hag 2:21–22 moves directly from "I will shake the heavens and the earth" ($earthquake — the removal of every unfaithful established order) to "every one by the sword of his brother" (nation-against-nation). The shaking destabilizes; the mutual destruction finishes what the shaking begins.

- **$famine** — The withdrawal of God's Word accompanies the internal fracturing. In the Olivet Discourse sequence, $famine follows nation-against-nation. When the covenant community fragments, the Word that held it together is pulled back. Amos 8:11's "famine of hearing the words of the LORD" is the spiritual reality beneath the political collapse.

- **$mountain** — Kingdoms and ruling authorities. Hag 2:22 overthrows the throne of kingdoms ($mountains toppled) through brother's sword against brother. But Isa 2:2 and Mic 4:1 envision God's $mountain — His kingdom — *exalted above* all others, drawing nations together rather than tearing them apart. Nation-against-nation is what happens to $mountains that are not God's.

- **$highway** — The covenant path of faithfulness. When the $highway is walked, nations converge on Zion and learn war no more (Isa 2:2–4). When the highway lies waste, the covenant is broken (Isa 33:8), and peoples fracture. Nation-against-nation is the direct consequence of the abandoned $highway.

- **$darkness** — Isa 19:2–3 pairs Egypt's internal war with the failure of their counsel and their turn to idols — concealed disobedience producing blindness. Isa 9:19 says "the land is darkened" as brother ceases to spare brother. The $darkness of hidden disobedience, once it saturates a people, destroys their ability to recognize each other as covenant partners.
