# Renewed Covenant

**The Hebrew word behind "new covenant" comes from a root that means "to renew, repair, rebuild" — and every layer of the source languages confirms it. The everlasting covenant was not replaced. It was renewed.**

> *"Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a **new** covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah."* — Jer 31:31

---

## The Key Insight

The English word "new" carries an ambiguity the Hebrew does not. In English, "new" can mean *never before existing* (a new invention) or *freshly restored* (a new coat of paint on the same house). The Hebrew word in Jer 31:31 resolves the ambiguity.

The word translated "new" is H2319 *chadash*. It derives from the root verb H2318 *chadash*, which Strong's defines as:

> **"to be new; causatively, to rebuild"** — KJV translates: **"renew, repair"**

The root verb does not mean "to create something that never existed." It means **to restore, repair, rebuild**. And the KJV itself translates H2318 as "renew" or "repair" in every occurrence of the verb.

This matters because the adjective H2319 carries its root's freight. A *chadash* covenant is a *renewed* covenant — the same agreement, restored and refreshed.

**The pattern**: H2319 = renewed, restored, refreshed (from root "to renew, repair")

**Opposite**: Broken covenant — what Israel did (Jer 31:32), which made renewal necessary

**Defining verses**: Jer 31:31–33, Heb 12:24, Heb 13:20, Lam 3:23, Ps 51:10

**The surprise**: The "new covenant" passages in the NT use two *different* Greek words for "new" — and the one in Heb 12:24 literally means "regenerated"

**Connected:** $rock, $wine, $bread, $name, $new-moon

---

## The Hebrew Root — "Renew, Repair, Rebuild"

The verb H2318 *chadash* appears ten times in the Old Testament. In every case, it describes the restoration of something that already existed — never the creation of something that didn't:

> *"**Renew**{H2318} a right spirit within me."* — Ps 51:10

David is not asking for a spirit he never had. He is asking God to restore what sin had damaged.

> *"Joash was minded to **repair**{H2318} the house of the LORD."* — 2Ch 24:4

The temple stood. It had fallen into disrepair. H2318 is the word for restoring it — not building a different temple.

> *"They shall **repair**{H2318} the waste cities, the desolations of many generations."* — Isa 61:4

The cities existed. They were ruined. H2318 is the word for rebuilding them.

> *"Who satisfieth thy mouth with good things; so that thy youth is **renewed**{H2318} like the eagle's."* — Ps 103:5

> *"Thou sendest forth thy spirit, they are created: and thou **renewest**{H2318} the face of the earth."* — Ps 104:30

> *"**Renew**{H2318} our days as of old."* — Lam 5:21

Every occurrence follows the same pattern: something that existed is restored, repaired, refreshed, made young again. The word never means "replaced with something fundamentally different."

When Jer 31:31 uses the adjective form (H2319) to describe the covenant, it carries this same sense: a covenant *renewed* — not a covenant *replaced*.

---

## The Moon Witness — Same Body, Renewed Cycle

The same root H2318 gives us H2320 *chodesh* — the word translated "$[new-moon]" or "month" throughout the Old Testament. The $new-moon is not a different moon each month. It is the *same moon* appearing renewed in its cycle — the returning light of the same body after a period of darkness.

This is not a coincidence. The Hebrew language itself embeds the concept: *chadash* (renewed) → *chodesh* (the renewed moon). The moon is the physical witness to what "new" means in Hebrew: the same thing, made fresh again, on a recurring cycle.

When God says He will make a *chadash* covenant, the language itself says: the same covenant, appearing renewed — like the moon returning after its dark phase.

## The Greek Split — Two Words, One Direction

The New Testament translates the "renewed covenant" passages into Greek — but uses two *different* Greek words for "new." The distinction is revealing.

### G2537 *kainos* — "New in Freshness"

Most passages use G2537 *kainos*, which Strong's defines as: *"new, especially in **freshness**; while G3501 (neos) is properly so with respect to age."*

| Verse | Phrase |
|-------|--------|
| Heb 8:8 | "I will make a new{G2537} covenant" |
| Heb 8:13 | "In that he saith, A new{G2537} covenant" |
| Heb 9:15 | "the mediator of the new{G2537} testament" |
| Mat 26:28 | "my blood of the new{G2537} testament" |
| Mk 14:24 | "my blood of the new{G2537} testament" |
| Lk 22:20 | "This cup is the new{G2537} testament in my blood" |
| 1Co 11:25 | "This cup is the new{G2537} testament in my blood" |
| 2Co 3:6 | "ministers of the new{G2537} testament" |

*Kainos* means fresh in quality — not fresh in existence. And the compounds derived from *kainos* confirm this: G340 *anakainizo* = "to restore," and G341 *anakainoo* = "to renovate." The word family points toward renewal, not replacement.

The Septuagint translators made the same choice. In the LXX, Jer 38:31 (= MT 31:31) renders H2319 with G2537 *kainos*: **διαθήκην καινήν** — a covenant renewed in quality.

### G3501 *neos* — "Young, Regenerated"

One passage breaks the pattern. Heb 12:24 uses a different word entirely:

> *"And to Jesus the mediator of the **new**{G3501} covenant, and to the $[blood] of sprinkling, that speaketh better things than that of Abel."* — Heb 12:24

G3501 *neos* means "new in time, youthful, fresh; figuratively, **regenerate**." The Strong's definition itself says "regenerate" — literally, *born again*. The writer of Hebrews chose a word that means *made young again* — the covenant regenerated, given new life, not swapped out for a different one.

### Both Words Point the Same Direction

*Kainos* = renewed in quality. Its compounds mean "to restore, to renovate."
*Neos* = made young again. Its figurative sense is "regenerated."

Neither Greek word demands the sense "replaced by something that never existed before." Both are consistent with renewal.

## Jeremiah's Own Explanation — Same Law, New Location

The prophet does not leave the meaning to etymology alone. Jer 31:32–33 explains *what changed* and *what didn't*:

> *"Not according to the $[covenant] that I made with their fathers… which **my covenant they brake**."* — Jer 31:32

The fault was not with the covenant. It was with the people: "my covenant **they** broke" (H6565 *parar* — to break, violate, frustrate). The terms were sound. The parties defaulted.

> *"I will put my **law**{H8451} in their inward parts, and write it in their **hearts**."* — Jer 31:33

The word is H8451 *torah* — the same law, the same instruction, the same covenant terms. What changes is the *location*: from tablets of stone to the inward parts. From external inscription to internal transformation.

The content of the covenant is unchanged. The writing surface is new.

This is precisely what renewal means: the same house, repaired (H2318). The same altar, renewed (2Ch 15:8). The same spirit, restored (Ps 51:10). The same law — now written where it cannot be lost.

## The Everlasting Pattern — It Cannot Be "New" If It Is Everlasting

Scripture calls the covenant "everlasting" across every major covenant epoch:

| Passage | Covenant |
|---------|----------|
| Gen 9:16 | Noah — "the **everlasting** covenant" |
| Gen 17:7, 13, 19 | Abraham — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Lev 24:8 | Showbread — "by an **everlasting** covenant" |
| 2Sa 23:5 | David — "an **everlasting** covenant, ordered in all things, and sure" |
| 1Ch 16:17 / Ps 105:10 | Israel — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Isa 24:5 | Earth — "broken the **everlasting** covenant" |
| Isa 55:3 | David's mercies — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Isa 61:8 | Truth — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Jer 32:40 | Future — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Eze 16:60, 37:26 | Restoration — "an **everlasting** covenant" |
| Heb 13:20 | Christ — "the blood of the **everlasting** covenant" |

The Hebrew word is H5769 *olam* — perpetual, of long duration, forever. The Greek is G166 *aionios* — eternal.

If the covenant is everlasting, it cannot be replaced. A covenant that ends is not everlasting. A covenant that is superseded by a different one has, by definition, expired. But the text says it does not expire.

What an everlasting covenant *can* do is be renewed — renewed in administration, renewed in mediation, renewed in how its terms are internalized. Heb 8:6 says Christ obtained "a more excellent ministry" and is "mediator of a **better** covenant, established upon better promises." Better mediator. Better promises. Better blood. But the same everlasting covenant — now written on the heart instead of stone.

## "New Every Morning" — The *Chadash* of Faithfulness

One verse binds the entire study together:

> *"They are **new**{H2319} every morning: great is thy **faithfulness**{H530}."* — Lam 3:23

The word "new" is H2319 *chadash* — the same word used in Jer 31:31 for the "new" covenant. God's mercies are *chadash* every morning. They are renewed. They are not *different mercies* each day. They are the *same mercies*, refreshed.

And the word "faithfulness" is H530 *emunah* — the same word that describes the $rock in Deu 32:4: "He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of **truth**{H530}." The everlasting covenant ($rock) is built on H530 *emunah* (faithfulness), and the daily mercies of that covenant are H2319 *chadash* (renewed every morning).

The covenant does not change. It renews. Every morning, the same faithfulness appears fresh — like the $new-moon returning in its cycle, the same body, newly visible.

## Patterns

1. **The Hebrew root resolves the ambiguity.** H2318 *chadash* (verb) means "to renew, repair, rebuild." The KJV itself never translates it as "replace" or "create from nothing." Every verbal occurrence describes restoration of something that already existed.

2. **The moon is the physical witness.** H2320 *chodesh* (new moon/month), from the same root, demonstrates the Hebrew concept: the same body, appearing renewed in its cycle.

3. **The Greek confirms the Hebrew.** Both *kainos* (renewed in quality) and *neos* (made young again / regenerated) point toward renewal. Neither demands "replaced by something previously non-existent."

4. **Jeremiah explains what changed.** Same Torah, new location — from stone to heart (Jer 31:33). The content is unchanged; the writing surface is new.

5. **"Everlasting" and "new" must be compatible.** The same covenant is called both "everlasting" (Heb 13:20) and "new" (Heb 12:24). The only reading that makes both true: the everlasting covenant is renewed — not ended and restarted.

6. **The fault was with the people, not the terms.** Jer 31:32 — "my covenant **they** broke." Heb 8:8 — "finding fault **with them**." The covenant was not defective. The people defaulted. Renewal addresses the default without voiding the terms.

7. **Lam 3:23 ties the threads.** H2319 *chadash* (renewed) + H530 *emunah* (faithfulness = Rock's defining quality in Deu 32:4). The covenant's daily renewal is inseparable from the Rock's unchanging faithfulness.

## Connections

- **$rock** — If Rock = covenant (the central insight of the $rock study), then the rock is what was renewed. The same immovable foundation, now with a better mediator and blood that speaks better things (Heb 12:24). The Rock did not move; it was restored.

- **$wine** — "This cup is the renewed covenant in my $[blood]" (Lk 22:20). The $wine — covenant blood — is what ratifies the renewal. The struck $rock in the wilderness poured out water; Christ was struck and blood poured out (Jn 19:34). Better blood, same covenant.

- **$bread** — Breaking $bread = cutting covenant (Gen 14:18, 1Co 10:16). The renewed covenant retains the same terms ($bread = Word / covenant instructions). Jer 31:33 puts the same Torah in the heart — the covenant meal now internalized.

- **$name** — To invoke the $name is to invoke the covenant. The renewed covenant does not change the Name — it writes the Name on the heart. "I will be their God and they shall be my people" (Jer 31:33) — the same covenant formula, renewed.

- **$new-moon** — H2320 *chodesh* shares the same root H2318 as the "new" in "new covenant." The monthly renewal of the moon is the created witness to how *chadash* works: same body, renewed cycle, returning light.
