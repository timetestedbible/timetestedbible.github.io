### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **sapphire** begins: “(Min.) Defn: Native alumina or aluminium sesquioxide, Al2O3; corundum; esp., the blue transparent variety of corundum, highly prized as a gem. of rubies, sapphires, and of pearlés white.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The full moon in its enthroned and bridal form — the clear sapphire likeness at the place of God's throne, identified with Issachar.

> *“And they saw the God of Israel: and there was under his feet as it were a paved work of a sapphire stone, and as it were the body of heaven in his clearness.”* — Exodus 24:10

> *“Hast thou with him spread out the sky, which is strong, and as a molten looking glass?”* — Job 37:18

> *“And now men see not the bright light which is in the clouds: but the wind passeth, and cleanseth them.”* — Job 37:21

> *“His seed shall endure for ever, and his throne as the sun before me.”* — Psalms 89:36

> *“It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a faithful witness in heaven. Selah.”* — Psalms 89:37

> *“Thou hast made his glory to cease, and cast his throne down to the ground.”* — Psalms 89:44

> *“My beloved is white and ruddy, the chiefest among ten thousand.”* — Song of Solomon 5:10

> *“His hands are as gold rings set with the beryl: his belly is as bright ivory overlaid with sapphires.”* — Song of Solomon 5:14

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **sapphire** begins:

> “Sap”phire ( or ; 277), n. Etym: [OE. saphir, F. saphir, L. sapphirus, Gr. sappir.] 1. (Min.) Defn: Native alumina or aluminium sesquioxide, Al2O3; corundum; esp., the blue transparent variety of corundum, highly prized as a gem. of rubies, sapphires, and of pearlés white. Chaucer.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Corpus and Method

This study checks the glossary's defining citations first, then compares them with every KJV verse that prints *sapphire*. The English-form sweep found **12 verses**. It is exhaustive for those printed forms, but it is not a substitute for a Strong's-number sweep where several Hebrew or Greek words share one English translation.

## Twelve or Thirteen — A Calendar Count

The count itself follows the calendar pattern. In the standard Hebrew and Greek corpus, the underlying sapphire words occur **12 times**:

| Witness | Occurrences |
|---|---:|
| Hebrew Old Testament — *sappir* (H5601) | 11 |
| Greek Revelation — *sappheiros* (G4552) | 1 |
| **Standard total** | **12** |

The Hebrew Revelation text indexed on this site uses *sappir* twice: once for the enthroned figure whose appearance is like sapphire (Rev 4:3), and again for New Jerusalem's second foundation (Rev 21:19). Using that Hebrew witness in place of the Greek Revelation count produces **13** occurrences.

The two totals match the lunar calendar's structure: 12 months in an ordinary year and a thirteenth month when the year is intercalated. A numerical correspondence does not define the symbol by itself, but here it reinforces the independent chain joining sapphire to Issachar, the tribe that understood the times; the full moon; and the appointed circuit of the heavens.
