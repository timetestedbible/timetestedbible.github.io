# Jericho

**The moon-city — the prototype of fortified systems that fall not by human force but by obedient process under God's $cherem-ban. Always *down* from Jerusalem.**

> *"And the LORD said unto Joshua, See, I have given into thine hand **Jericho**, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour."* — Josh 6:2

---

## The Key Insight

Jericho is the first city Israel faces in the Promised Land — and God makes it the template for everything that follows.

No battering rams. No siege towers. No military strategy. Seven priests carry seven $trumpet|trumpets. The army walks in silence. For six days they circle the walls once per day. On the seventh day, seven circuits, a long blast, and a shout — and the walls fall flat.

Heb 11:30 records the method plainly: *"By faith the walls of **Jericho** fell down, after they were compassed about seven days."* Faith — not force — is the active agent. The weapon is obedient process.

This matters because Jericho is not just a city. Its name (H3405 *Yericho*) likely derives from H3394 *yareach* — **the moon**. Jericho is the moon-city: a system operating on borrowed light, fortified behind walls "straitly shut up" (Josh 6:1), sitting at the lowest point on earth, always *down* from Jerusalem. When the true light arrives through the $trumpet blast of God's own army, the borrowed-light fortress collapses — and the entire conquest of Canaan takes its cue from the pattern.

Every subsequent city's destruction is measured against Jericho (Josh 8:2, 10:1, 10:28, 10:30). What God did first, He does repeatedly. And the method never changes: the fortified system falls when God's people obey the process He prescribes.

**Symbolizes:** The fortified world-system operating on borrowed authority — the first stronghold that falls when God's prescribed process is faithfully executed

**Opposite:** Jerusalem — the city of God's throne, always *up*. Going to Jerusalem is ascent; going to Jericho is descent.

**Defining verses:** Josh 6:1–5, Josh 6:20, Josh 6:26, Heb 11:30, Lk 10:30, 1 Kgs 16:34

**The surprise:** Jericho is ~800 feet *below* sea level — the lowest city on earth. When Jesus tells a parable about "a certain man went *down* from Jerusalem to Jericho" (Lk 10:30), the geography enacts the theology. Descent from God's presence leads to vulnerability, robbery, and near-death. And the place where Israel's conquest *began* (Josh 6) is the same place where Israel's monarchy *ended* — Zedekiah, the last king, was captured "in the plains of Jericho" fleeing Babylon (2 Kgs 25:5).

**Connected:** $cherem, $trumpet, $sword, $fire, $harlot, $scarlet, $firstborn, $curse

## Jericho Across Scripture

### The Moon-City and Borrowed Light

The etymology is disputed but suggestive. Strong's notes that H3405 *Yericho* comes "perhaps from H3394 *yareach*" — the moon. An alternate derivation traces it to H7306 *ruach* ("to blow, smell") — yielding "city of fragrance," fitting its reputation as a lush oasis of palms and balsam (Deut 34:3, "the city of palm trees").

Both etymologies may be more complementary than competing. The moon-city is attractive — fragrant, green, prosperous — but its light is *reflected*, not its own. The moon borrows glory from the sun. Jericho borrows life from its springs in a desert valley. It looks impressive. Its walls are "straitly shut up" (Josh 6:1 — the verb H5462 *sagar* doubled for emphasis: shut, utterly shut). But what is sealed against the invader cannot survive the trumpet of the One who owns the land.

The $trumpet connection runs deep. The Jericho narrative is saturated with the shophar — seven priests bearing seven trumpets of rams' horns (Josh 6:4), blowing continuously as the procession circles the city. The $trumpet is the authoritative signal of God's sovereign action — initiating kingdom transfer, wall-breaking judgment, and the irreversible transition between ages. At Jericho, all three converge: the land transfers from Canaan to Israel, the walls break, and the age of conquest begins.

Compare Ps 81:3 — "Blow up the trumpet in the new moon." The shophar blown at the lunar cycle connects to Jericho, the moon-city that fell to the shophar. The $trumpet shatters the borrowed-light system.

### "I Have Given" — Perfect Tense Before the Battle

> *"And the LORD said unto Joshua, See, I **have given** into thine hand **Jericho**, and the king thereof, and the mighty men of valour."* — Josh 6:2

The verb is perfect tense — completed action. Before a single circuit of the walls, before the first trumpet blast, God speaks of Jericho's fall as *already accomplished*. The seven-day process is not a siege to wear down the enemy. It is the enactment of a verdict already rendered.

This is why the method seems absurd by human standards. Walking in circles accomplishes nothing militarily. Blowing trumpets does not weaken stone. But the point is precisely that human force is irrelevant — the battle belongs to the LORD (compare 1 Sam 17:47). Heb 11:30 places Jericho in the faith catalog for this reason: **faith** is the active agent, and faith acts by obeying a process that makes no sense apart from God's word.

The process itself is structured around sevens: seven priests, seven trumpets, seven days, seven circuits on the seventh day. The number of completion and covenant. God's prescribed order — not Israel's ingenuity — brings the walls down.

### Cherem — God's Total Claim

> *"And the city shall be accursed (H2764 **cherem**), even it, and all that are therein, to the LORD."* — Josh 6:17

> *"And ye, in any wise keep yourselves from the **accursed thing** (H2764 **cherem**), lest ye make yourselves accursed, when ye take of the accursed thing, and make the camp of Israel a curse, and trouble it."* — Josh 6:18

Jericho is the first city placed under $cherem — God's total enclosure, the irrevocable seizure of persons, animals, and goods into God's exclusive jurisdiction. Nothing can be redeemed from it. Nothing can be kept. $cherem is contagious: touching what God has claimed makes *you* claimed.

Josh 6:17–18 lays out the contagion in three steps: take $cherem → become $cherem → make the camp $cherem. The warning is almost redundant in its repetition — because the stakes are total. This is not a battlefield command about spoils. It is the establishment of a principle: *the first city belongs entirely to God*.

The parallel to the $firstborn principle is striking. Just as the firstborn of every womb belongs to God (Exod 13:2) and must be either redeemed or given over, so the first city of the conquest belongs wholly to God. Jericho is the "firstfruits" of the Promised Land — entirely the LORD's, not Israel's to profit from.

And the consequence of violating this principle arrives immediately:

> *"But the children of Israel committed a trespass in the **accursed thing** (H2764): for Achan… took of the accursed thing: and the anger of the LORD was kindled against the children of Israel."* — Josh 7:1

One man takes from the $cherem, and the entire nation becomes $cherem. Corporate liability. The contagion principle established at Jericho operates exactly as warned. Israel cannot take Ai — a far smaller city — until the breach is resolved (Josh 7:11–13). Jericho's protocol is not optional. It is the template.

### The Scarlet Cord — Rahab's Escape from the Condemned City

> *"Behold, when we come into the land, thou shalt bind this line of **$[scarlet]** thread in the window which thou didst let us down by."* — Josh 2:18

Inside the moon-city, a $harlot shelters God's spies. Rahab is a covenant-breaker by trade — and she lives in the very wall that is about to collapse. Yet she is the one person in Jericho who recognizes the true God:

> *"For the LORD your God, he is God in heaven above, and in earth beneath."* — Josh 2:11

Her faith produces action: she hides the spies, she binds the $scarlet cord in her window. The $scarlet cord functions as the blood-marker of covenant salvation — the same principle as the Passover blood on the doorposts (Exod 12:7). Those inside the marked house are saved when judgment falls on the surrounding system.

Josh 6:25 records the result: *"And Joshua saved Rahab the **harlot** alive, and her father's household, and all that she had; and she dwelleth in Israel even unto this day."* The Gentile $harlot, extracted from the condemned $cherem city by faith and the $scarlet blood-sign, is grafted into the covenant people. She appears in Christ's genealogy (Mat 1:5). The pattern is clear: even from inside the most fortified system under God's total ban, faith opens an exit.

### The Curse on Rebuilding — Firstborn and Youngest

> *"Cursed be the man before the LORD, that riseth up and buildeth this city **Jericho**: he shall lay the foundation thereof in his **$[firstborn]**, and in his youngest son shall he set up the gates of it."* — Josh 6:26

Joshua pronounces a $curse — a covenant penalty clause — on anyone who rebuilds what God has destroyed. The cost is specified: the $firstborn dies at the laying of the foundation, the youngest at the setting of the gates. The entire span of a man's children, from first to last, consumed by the attempt to reconstruct the condemned system.

Five centuries pass. Then:

> *"In his days did Hiel the Bethelite build **Jericho**: he laid the foundation thereof in Abiram his **$[firstborn]**, and set up the gates thereof in his youngest son Segub, according to the word of the LORD, which he spake by Joshua the son of Nun."* — 1 Kgs 16:34

The $curse activates exactly as declared — and the timing matters enormously. This occurs during the reign of Ahab, the peak of northern kingdom apostasy. Rebuilding what God has placed under $cherem is not a neutral construction project. It is defiance of God's ban, and it costs exactly what was promised.

The covenant penalty clause (see $curse) does not expire. Josh 6:26 is dormant for half a millennium, then executes precisely. Hiel's $firstborn and youngest die — the entire family line consumed, bookending the gates and foundation of a city that should not exist.

### First Victory, Last Defeat — The Jericho Inclusio

One of the most striking patterns in the Jericho data is its appearance at both ends of Israel's monarchy:

**First victory:** Josh 6 — Israel enters the land. Jericho falls. The conquest begins.

**Last defeat:** 2 Kgs 25:5 / Jer 39:5 / Jer 52:8 — Zedekiah, the last king of Judah, flees Jerusalem as Babylon breaches the walls. Where is he caught? *"In the plains of Jericho."*

The place where Israel's triumphant entry began is the place where Israel's humiliating exit is sealed. The conquest opened at Jericho; the exile closes at Jericho. This geographical inclusio frames the entire monarchy: what began with walls falling *down* by faith ends with a king falling *down* by faithlessness.

The irony deepens: at the first Jericho event, God's people obeyed a process and a fortified city fell before them. At the last Jericho event, God's people had broken covenant, and they themselves are caught in the open field — no walls to protect them, no process of faith, no trumpet blast on their behalf.

### Going Down to Jericho — The Descent Theology

> *"A certain man went **down** from Jerusalem to **Jericho**, and fell among thieves, which stripped him of his raiment, and wounded him, and departed, leaving him half dead."* — Lk 10:30

In the parable of the Good Samaritan, the directional language is precise. The man goes *down* — from Jerusalem (God's presence, ~2,500 ft elevation) to Jericho (~800 ft *below* sea level). This is a descent of over 3,000 feet in approximately 17 miles. The geography enacts the theology: leaving God's presence means going down, and going down leads to robbery, violence, and near-death.

The man is stripped, wounded, and left "half dead." Priest and Levite pass by — the covenant representatives fail. A Samaritan rescues him. The one outside the formal covenant system shows the covenant-faithfulness that the system's own representatives do not.

Jericho in the Gospels is consistently the place where Jesus encounters the broken and the outcast:

- **Blind Bartimaeus** at Jericho receives sight (Mat 20:29, Mk 10:46, Lk 18:35). The man in darkness at the moon-city is given true light.
- **Zacchaeus** in Jericho, a despised tax collector, hears Jesus say: *"This day is salvation come to this house"* (Lk 19:9). Salvation descends to the descent-city.

The pattern suggests that Jericho represents the furthest point of descent from God's presence — the lowest place, geographically and spiritually. Yet it is precisely there that rescue arrives. The walls that once fell to faith now witness faith restoring sight and saving the lost.

### Elijah's Departure — The Transitional City

> *"And Elijah said unto him, Elisha, tarry here, I pray thee; for the LORD hath sent me to **Jericho**. And he said, As the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, I will not leave thee. So they came to **Jericho**."* — 2 Kgs 2:4

Elijah's final journey passes through Jericho on the way to the Jordan, where he is taken up. The "sons of the prophets that were at Jericho" (2 Kgs 2:5) know what is about to happen — the old order ending, the new beginning. After Elijah's ascent, the sons of the prophets at Jericho witness Elisha's succession (2 Kgs 2:15) and send fifty men to search for the departed prophet (2 Kgs 2:18).

Jericho serves here as a transitional city — the place between the old dispensation and the new, the threshold at the border of the land where prophetic authority transfers. The same liminal quality it held at the conquest (threshold into the Promised Land) it holds at the prophetic succession.

## Patterns

1. **First city = prototype.** Jericho is the first Canaanite stronghold under $cherem. Every subsequent conquest is measured against it (Josh 8:2, 10:1, 10:28, 10:30). The method established at Jericho — obedient process, not human force — becomes the template for how God breaks fortified systems.

2. **Moon-city = borrowed authority.** H3394 *yareach* (moon) → H3405 *Yericho*. The moon has no light of its own; it reflects the sun. Jericho represents a system operating on reflected, derivative power. When the true authority arrives with the $trumpet, the borrowed-light fortress collapses.

3. **Process, not force.** Seven days of circling, seven priests with seven trumpets, silence until the appointed shout. No siege engines. The weapon is obedient ritual following God's prescribed order. Heb 11:30 names this weapon plainly: *faith*.

4. **Cherem contagion.** Jericho is the test case for the $cherem principle. Josh 6:17–18 establishes the triple contagion: take $cherem → become $cherem → make the camp $cherem. Achan's violation (Josh 7:1) proves the principle is active, not theoretical.

5. **Rahab exception.** A $harlot inside the condemned city escapes by faith and a $scarlet cord — the blood-marker of covenant. The condemned system falls; the one who trusts God's word is extracted. The pattern anticipates every "come out of her, my people" (Rev 18:4).

6. **Curse that activates across centuries.** Josh 6:26 → 1 Kgs 16:34. The $curse on rebuilding Jericho lies dormant for ~500 years, then executes precisely — $firstborn at the foundation, youngest at the gates. The covenant penalty clause does not expire.

7. **Descent = vulnerability.** Lk 10:30 — going *down* to Jericho = leaving God's presence. Jericho's position ~800 ft below sea level makes the descent literal. Geography enacts theology.

8. **First victory → last defeat.** Josh 6 (conquest begins) → 2 Kgs 25:5 / Jer 39:5 (exile sealed). The same location bookends the entire monarchy, forming a geographical inclusio of faithfulness and faithlessness.

## Connections

- **$cherem** — Jericho is the prototype of $cherem — God's total enclosure, the irrevocable seizure from which nothing can be redeemed. The $cherem principle is *established* at Jericho: its contagion (Josh 7:1), its totality (Josh 6:17), and its consequences (Josh 7:11–13) are all demonstrated here first.

- **$trumpet** — The $trumpet is the authoritative signal that initiates kingdom transfer, wall-breaking judgment, and irreversible transition between ages. At Jericho, the $trumpet does all three simultaneously: the land transfers, the walls break, and the conquest era begins. Seven priests with seven shopharim anticipate the seven trumpets of Revelation.

- **$sword** — The $sword — the desolation-maker — executes after the walls fall: "utterly destroyed all that was in the city… with the edge of the $[sword]" (Josh 6:21). The captain of the LORD's host appears at Jericho with $sword drawn *before* the battle (Josh 5:13) — God's instrument precedes Israel's action.

- **$fire** — Jericho is "burnt with $[fire]" after its destruction (Josh 6:24). $fire — God's holy standard in active contact — consumes what violates covenant. The $cherem city, once claimed by God, is reduced by $fire to nothing recoverable.

- **$harlot** — Rahab the $harlot dwells inside Jericho's wall — the covenant-breaker within the condemned system. Her faith reverses her identity: she who was an idolater becomes a faithful covenant partner, grafted into Israel and into the line of Christ (Mat 1:5).

- **$scarlet** — The $scarlet cord in Rahab's window is the bloodline of covenant — the same principle as Passover blood on the doorposts. $scarlet marks the house for salvation when destruction sweeps the city. The word for "cord" (H8615 *tiqvah*) also means "hope" — the scarlet cord *is* hope.

- **$firstborn** — The $curse on rebuilding Jericho costs the $firstborn — the covenant heir (Josh 6:26, 1 Kgs 16:34). As the first city of the conquest is God's "firstfruits," so the firstborn of the rebuilder becomes the price of defying God's $cherem claim.

- **$curse** — Joshua's $curse (Josh 6:26) is the covenant penalty clause that self-activates upon violation. It lies dormant for centuries, then executes in Ahab's reign — the era of peak apostasy. The $curse does not fade; it waits for the trigger.
