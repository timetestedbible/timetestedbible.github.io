# Net

**Indiscriminate enclosure — a mechanism that gathers everything within its sweep, without selecting. Separation happens after the catch, not during it.**

> *"Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a **net**, that was cast into the $[sea], and gathered of every kind: Which, when it was full, they drew to shore, and sat down, and gathered the good into vessels, but cast the bad away."* — Mat 13:47–48

---

## The Key Insight

The net does not choose what it catches. It encloses.

This is the single feature that unites every appearance of the net in Scripture — whether cast by God, by $babylon, by the wicked, or by fishermen of the kingdom. The net gathers indiscriminately. What happens to the catch is determined *afterward*, by someone other than the net itself.

In Matthew 13:47–48, Jesus makes this explicit. The kingdom-net (G4522 *sagene*) is cast into the $sea — the chaotic nations — and gathers "of every kind" (G1085 *genos*). No sorting occurs in the water. The net is hauled to shore, and *then* they sit down, separate good from bad, and dispose accordingly. The net's job is to enclose. The shore is where judgment happens.

This pattern — **catch first, sort later** — governs every context in which the net appears. God spreads His net over Zedekiah and drags him to $babylon (Ezek 12:13). $babylon spreads its net over nations and worships the net itself (Hab 1:15–17). The wicked hide nets in pits for the innocent (Ps 35:7–8). In every case, the net is an instrument of total enclosure. The question is never *whether* the net catches — it always does. The question is: **who cast it, and what happens to the catch?**

**Symbolizes:** Indiscriminate enclosure — gathering everything within reach, with separation occurring only after the catch

**Opposite:** Escape / Breaking free — "the $[snare] is broken, and we are escaped" (Ps 124:7); "pull me out of the **net** that they have laid privily for me" (Ps 31:4)

**Defining verses:** Mat 13:47–48, Hab 1:15–17, Ezek 12:13, Ecc 9:12, Ps 35:7–8

**The surprise:** The Hebrew word H2764 *cherem* means BOTH "net" and "ban" — the irrevocable devotion of something to God for destruction. The physical instrument of enclosure and the judicial act of divine seizure are *the same word from the same root*. When $babylon casts its $cherem-net over nations (Hab 1:15), and then God places His $cherem-ban on $babylon (Jer 50:21), the same word reverses direction. The hunter becomes the hunted — caught in its own mechanism.

**Connected:** $cherem, $sea, $snare, $babylon, $harlot, $fish

## Net Across Scripture

### Eight Words for One Mechanism

Scripture uses eight different words for nets, and each illuminates a different facet of the same concept — enclosure:

**H2764** *cherem* — from H2763 *charam* (to shut in). Both "net" and "ban." The root meaning is enclosure: a net physically shuts in fish; the *cherem*-ban judicially shuts persons into God's exclusive jurisdiction. Same word, same mechanism, different scales.

**H7568** *resheth* — from H3423 *yarash* (to possess, dispossess). A net as catching = *possessing*. The commonest "net" word (~22× OT). What the net encloses, it takes ownership of.

**H4685** *matsod* — from H6679 *tsud* (to hunt). A hunter's net. But the same word also means *fortress* or *stronghold*. Net and fortress share the underlying concept: **enclosure**. What protects from one side imprisons from the other.

**H4364** *makmar* — from H3648 *kamar* (to blacken by heat). A net darkened for concealment. The hidden net. 2× OT.

**H4365** *mikmereth* — feminine of H4364. The fisher's drag-net. Appears in the defining Habakkuk passage (Hab 1:15–16).

**G1350** *diktyon* — from "to cast." The workhorse NT net word (12×). The seine hauled by fishermen.

**G293** *amphiblestron* — from *amphi* (around) + *ballo* (throw). A casting-net thrown *around* fish. 2× NT (Mat 4:18, Mk 1:16). The word itself describes encirclement.

**G4522** *sagene* — a large dragnet. Appears only once (Mat 13:47) — the kingdom-net parable. Reserved for the definitive kingdom image.

Every root points to the same reality: shutting in, enclosing, taking possession. The net is the mechanism of total capture.

### The Net and the Ban — One Word, One Root

> *"They take up all of them with the angle, they catch them in their **net** (H2764 *cherem*), and gather them in their **drag** (H4365 *mikmereth*): therefore they rejoice and are glad. Therefore they sacrifice unto their **net** (H2764 *cherem*), and burn incense unto their **drag** (H4365 *mikmereth*); because by them their portion is fat, and their meat plenteous."* — Hab 1:15–16

This is the passage where the $cherem-net connection becomes inescapable. $babylon's imperial power is described as a *cherem* — a net cast over nations. And then $babylon *worships its own net*. The empire deifies its power to enclose. Its military, commercial, and political apparatus becomes the object of its devotion. The system that captures nations becomes the god of the system.

But H2764 *cherem* is also the word for God's irrevocable ban — the total seizure by which God claims something for Himself with no possibility of redemption (Lev 27:28–29). When God declares *cherem* on $babylon itself (Jer 50:21 — "Go up against the land of Merathaim... and against the inhabitants of Pekod: waste and utterly destroy [H2763 *charam*] after them"), He uses $babylon's own mechanism against it.

The hunter's net becomes God's ban. The instrument of imperial enclosure becomes the instrument of divine judgment. Same word. Same root. Reversed direction.

> *"I will also spread my **net** (H7568 *resheth*) upon him, and he shall be taken in my **snare** (H2764 *cherem*)"* — Ezek 32:3

Here God uses *both* net-words on Pharaoh simultaneously: *resheth* (the possessing-net) and *cherem* (the enclosing-ban-net). The two converge on one target. God's *resheth* = God's *cherem*. When God nets you, you are enclosed. When you are enclosed by God, you are devoted — seized irrevocably.

### God Casts Nets

One of the more unsettling patterns in Scripture is that God Himself uses nets — and not only on His enemies:

> *"My **net** (H7568 *resheth*) also will I spread upon him, and he shall be taken in my $[snare]: and I will bring him to $[babylon]"* — Ezek 12:13

> *"I will spread my **net** (H7568 *resheth*) upon him, and he shall be taken in my $[snare]"* — Ezek 17:20

Both passages describe God netting Zedekiah, the last king of Judah. God's own covenant king — caught in God's own net — dragged to $babylon. The net here is not the enemy's weapon but God's instrument of covenant discipline. Zedekiah broke his oath; God enclosed him.

> *"Thou broughtest us into the **net** (H4685 *matsod*)"* — Ps 66:11

The psalmist attributes the net directly to God. The congregation acknowledges that God Himself brought them into enclosure. Yet the psalm does not end in despair — it continues to praise: "thou broughtest us out into a wealthy place" (Ps 66:12). The net is not the end. God's enclosure has an exit.

> *"I will spread my **net** (H7568 *resheth*) upon them; I will bring them down as the fowls of the heaven"* — Hos 7:12

God nets Ephraim — the same Ephraim who is elsewhere called a "silly dove" enticed away from covenant loyalty. The dove that should have returned home is caught in God's own net and brought down. When the faithful partner wanders, the covenant God becomes the fowler.

### Babylon's Net — Empire as Enclosure System

> *"Shall they therefore empty their **net** (H2764 *cherem*), and not spare continually to slay the nations?"* — Hab 1:17

$babylon — the worldly kingdom that substitutes man's rule for God's covenant — operates at the scale of a net. Not a $snare (which catches individuals through concealment and sudden seizure) but a *net* — a system-level mechanism that sweeps across populations and gathers nations wholesale.

Habakkuk's complaint identifies the essential horror: $babylon *never stops casting*. The net is emptied and cast again. Nation after nation is enclosed. And the empire worships the mechanism itself (Hab 1:16) — the military apparatus, the trade system, the administrative machinery that gathers peoples into its grip.

The connection to Ecclesiastes is striking:

> *"For man also knoweth not his time: as the fishes that are taken in an evil **net** (H4685 *matsod*), and as the birds that are caught in the $[snare]; so are the sons of men **snared** in an evil time, when it falleth **suddenly** upon them."* — Ecc 9:12

Net for fish. $snare for birds. Evil time for men. Three parallel images of capture, all converging on *suddenness*. The net does not announce itself. The $snare does not reveal its mechanism. The evil time falls without warning. This is the signature of $sudden-destruction — covenant judgment arriving on those who did not see it coming.

### The Wicked's Net Catches the Wicked

A remarkable pattern runs through the Psalms: the net set for the innocent circles back to catch the one who set it.

> *"The wicked have hid a $[snare] for me: they have spread the cords of a **net** (H7568 *resheth*) by the wayside; they have set gins for me."* — Ps 140:5

> *"Without cause have they hid for me their **net** (H7568 *resheth*) in a pit, which without cause they have digged for my soul. Let destruction come upon him at unawares; and let his **net** (H7568 *resheth*) that he hath hid catch himself: into that very destruction let him fall."* — Ps 35:7–8

> *"They have prepared a **net** (H7568 *resheth*) for my steps; my soul is bowed down: they have digged a pit before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves."* — Ps 57:6

> *"In the **net** (H7568 *resheth*) which they hid is their own foot taken."* — Ps 9:15

> *"Let the wicked fall into their own **nets** (H4364 *makmar*), whilst that I withal escape."* — Ps 141:10

Five psalms, one principle: the net reverses. The mechanism of enclosure is morally reflexive. What you cast comes back. This is not karma — it is the covenant structure of reality. The net you weave for others becomes the net God uses on you. Ps 35:8 makes this explicit: "let *his* net that *he* hath hid catch *himself*."

This pattern converges with the $cherem reversal: $babylon casts its *cherem*-net over nations (Hab 1:15), and God casts His *cherem*-ban on $babylon (Jer 50:21). The self-entrapment principle operates at individual and imperial scale alike.

### The Harlot as Net

> *"And I find more bitter than death the woman, whose heart is **snares** (H4685 *matsod*) and **nets** (H2764 *cherem*), and her hands as bands: whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her; but the sinner shall be taken by her."* — Ecc 7:26

The $harlot — the idolater, the covenant-breaker — is described with both net-words simultaneously. Her heart is *matsod* (the hunter's net) and *cherem* (the enclosing-ban-net). She captures those drawn to her, and her mechanism is the net: total enclosure from which the sinner cannot extract himself.

The parallel to $babylon is unmistakable. The $harlot of Revelation sits upon the $sea — the chaotic nations (Rev 17:15) — and her system operates exactly like a net: drawing all peoples into her commercial and religious grip. Ecclesiastes' "woman whose heart is nets" anticipates Revelation's "BABYLON THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF HARLOTS" (Rev 17:5). The heart of the system is a *cherem*-net — an instrument of total enclosure that gathers indiscriminately and holds irrevocably.

But note the escape clause: "whoso pleaseth God shall escape from her." The net is not fate. Escape exists for those aligned with God — a principle echoed in Ps 31:4 ("pull me out of the net") and Ps 124:7 ("the snare is broken, and we are escaped").

### The Altar's Network — A Net That Receives Offering

> *"And thou shalt make for it a grate of **network** (H7568 *resheth*) of brass; and upon the **net** shalt thou make four brasen rings in the four corners thereof."* — Exo 27:4

A detail easily overlooked: the brazen altar in the tabernacle has a grate of *resheth* — network — beneath the surface where the burnt offering is consumed. The sacrificial fire burns on a net.

This is the one context where the net is neither predatory nor judicial. It is receptive. The altar-net receives what is voluntarily given to God. The offering descends through the network to ashes. If every other net in Scripture encloses what is *seized*, the altar-net holds what is *surrendered*.

The same mechanism — enclosure — serves opposite purposes depending on who is placing what upon it.

### The Kingdom Net — Every Kind, Then Separation

> *"Again, the kingdom of heaven is like unto a **net** (G4522 *sagene*), that was cast into the $[sea], and gathered of every kind (G1085 *genos*): Which, when it was full, they drew to shore, and sat down, and gathered the good into vessels, but cast the bad away."* — Mat 13:47–48

Jesus chose a unique word for this parable. G4522 *sagene* appears nowhere else in the New Testament. It is a large dragnet — the biggest net in a fisherman's arsenal — reserved for the definitive image of the kingdom's gathering work.

Four elements define the parable:

1. **The net is cast into the $sea** — into the nations, the chaotic mass of peoples.
2. **It gathers of every kind** — the catch is indiscriminate. Good and bad, clean and unclean, all swept in together.
3. **Separation happens on the shore** — not in the water, not in the net, but after the catch is complete and drawn to land.
4. **The timing is the end of the age** — "So shall it be at the end of the world: the angels shall come forth, and sever the wicked from among the just" (Mat 13:49).

The kingdom-net does not filter. It encloses. This is both its power and its scandal — the kingdom in its present form contains "every kind," and the sorting is deferred to the angels at the consummation. Anyone expecting the net to be selective in the water misunderstands the mechanism.

### Fishers of Men — Leaving Nets, Becoming Nets

> *"And Jesus, walking by the $[sea] of Galilee, saw two brethren, Simon called Peter, and Andrew his brother, casting a **net** (G293 *amphiblestron*) into the $[sea]: for they were fishers. And he saith unto them, Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men."* — Mat 4:18–19

> *"And going on from thence, he saw other two brethren, James the son of Zebedee, and John his brother, in a ship with Zebedee their father, mending their **nets** (G1350 *diktyon*): and he called them."* — Mat 4:21

The disciples leave literal nets to become metaphorical ones. "Fishers of men" is not merely a clever analogy — it places them inside the net mechanism. They are the ones who will cast the kingdom-*sagene* into the $sea of nations.

Two net-related scenes frame their calling:

In Luke 5:4–6, the net breaks under the weight of the catch. The abundance overwhelms the mechanism. Peter falls at Jesus' knees, overwhelmed.

In John 21:6–11, after the resurrection, the net holds. One hundred and fifty-three great fish, and the net is "not broken" (Jn 21:11). What changed? The pre-resurrection net breaks; the post-resurrection net holds. The kingdom-mechanism, empowered by the risen Christ, is able to contain what it gathers.

### The Net in Restoration

> *"And it shall come to pass, that the fishers shall stand upon it from En-gedi even unto En-eglaim; they shall be a place to spread forth **nets** (H2764 *cherem*): their fish shall be according to their kinds, as the fish of the great $[sea], exceeding many."* — Ezek 47:10

The same word — *cherem* — that meant destruction in Hab 1:15 and divine ban in Lev 27:28 now appears in the vision of the restored river flowing from the temple. Fishermen spread *cherem*-nets along living waters, and the catch is abundant, ordered by kind, like the great $sea's own diversity.

The net that judged now gathers for life. The mechanism of enclosure serves abundance in the restored world. This is the same trajectory as the $cherem itself: Mal 4:6 threatens the earth with *cherem*, but Zech 14:11 promises "no more *cherem*" when the LORD reigns as king. The net has a terminus. Enclosure is not forever.

### Visibility Defeats the Net

> *"Surely in vain the **net** (H7568 *resheth*) is spread in the sight of any bird."* — Prov 1:17

One verse reveals the net's vulnerability: a net that can be *seen* catches nothing. Visibility nullifies the net's power. This is the proverbial foundation for prophetic warning itself — the prophet's job is to make invisible nets visible, to shout from the watchtower so that the bird sees the *resheth* spread before it and flies another way.

This is why the $snare operates through concealment and the net through scope. The $snare's power is in its hiddenness — what you cannot see captures you. The net's power is in its breadth — what you cannot outrun encloses you. But *both* are defeated by the same antidote: watchfulness. The one who sees and the one who flees are the ones who escape.

## Patterns

1. **Net = Cherem = Ban.** H2764 means both "net" and "irrevocable devotion to God." Same root (*charam* — to shut in), same mechanism (enclosure), different scales. Hab 1:15 and Ezek 32:3 make the convergence explicit. The physical net and the judicial ban are not homonyms but expressions of one reality.

2. **Indiscriminate catch → post-catch separation.** Mat 13:47–48 is the defining instance, but the pattern appears everywhere. The net gathers ALL; sorting follows. Kingdom, judgment, and empire alike operate by gathering first, disposing after.

3. **Self-entrapment.** Ps 9:15, 35:7–8, 57:6, 141:10. The net set for others catches the one who set it. The mechanism is morally reflexive. This operates at both individual scale (Psalms) and imperial scale ($babylon's *cherem*-net becomes God's *cherem*-ban).

4. **God uses nets.** Ezek 12:13, 17:20, 32:3, Hos 7:12, Ps 66:11. The net is not inherently evil — God casts it over His own unfaithful people and over their enemies. The net is an instrument; its moral quality depends on who wields it.

5. **$babylon worships its net.** Hab 1:16 — sacrifice and incense to the *cherem* and *mikmereth*. The empire deifies its own power to enclose. The system of capture becomes the system's god.

6. **Hunter → hunted.** Hab 1:17 ($babylon's *cherem*-net on nations) → Jer 50:21 (God's *cherem*-ban on $babylon). Same word, reversed direction. The net always returns.

7. **Visibility defeats the net.** Prov 1:17. A net seen is a net escaped. Prophetic warning = making hidden nets visible. Watchfulness = the antidote to enclosure.

8. **The resurrection net holds.** Lk 5:6 (net breaks) → Jn 21:11 (net does not break). Before the resurrection, the kingdom-mechanism is overwhelmed. After, it contains the catch. 153 great fish, and the net is intact.

## Connections

- **$cherem** — The net's deepest identity. H2764 is *both* "net" and "ban" — God's total enclosure, irrevocable seizure from which nothing can be redeemed. $babylon casts its *cherem*-net over nations; God casts His *cherem*-ban on $babylon. The physical mechanism and the divine judgment are one word from one root.

- **$sea** — The net's domain. The $sea represents chaotic nations and peoples (Rev 17:15). The kingdom-net is cast *into the sea* (Mat 13:47) — into the mass of humanity — and gathers of every kind. $babylon's net sweeps the same waters (Hab 1:15). The net operates wherever the $sea churns.

- **$snare** — The net's companion mechanism at a different scale. The $snare captures individuals through concealment and sudden seizure; the net encloses populations through sheer scope. Ps 140:5 places them side by side: *pach* (snare) + *resheth* (net) + *moqesh* (gin). Both are defeated by watchfulness (Prov 1:17, Lk 21:36).

- **$babylon** — The net's imperial user. $babylon is man's substitute system for God's covenant rule, and it operates as a net at civilizational scale — sweeping nations into its commercial, military, and religious grip. Hab 1:15–17 portrays $babylon worshiping its own net. The system deifies its mechanism of enclosure.

- **$harlot** — The net personified. The $harlot — the idolater, the covenant-breaker — has a heart of *matsod* and *cherem* (Ecc 7:26). She is the net's animating spirit. What $babylon does at system-level, the $harlot embodies as personal seduction: drawing all in, holding fast, releasing none — except those whom God delivers.

- **$sudden-destruction** — The net's moment of closure. Ecc 9:12 parallels fish in an evil net with the evil time falling *suddenly* upon the sons of men. The net catches; $sudden-destruction is the moment it snaps shut. Covenant judgment without warning on those who could not see the net spread before them.
