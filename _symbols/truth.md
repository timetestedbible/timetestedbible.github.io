# Truth

## Approved Definition

**God's law — the standard.**
> *"Sanctify them through thy **truth**: thy word is **truth**."* — Jn 17:17

---

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

**God's law — the standard.**

### Bible literal sense

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **truth** begins: “The quality or being true; as: -- (a) Conformity to fact or reality; exact accordance with that which is, or has been; or shall be. (b) Conformity to rule; exactness; close correspondence with an example, mood, object of imitation, or the like.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

A secular reader would define "truth" as factual accuracy — the opposite of a false statement. But Scripture does something the dictionary cannot explain: it tells you to **do** truth.

> *"He that **doeth truth** cometh to the $[light]."* — Jn 3:21

You can verify a fact. You can believe a fact. But you cannot *do* a fact. You can, however, do a commandment. You can keep a standard.

Scripture defines its own term with unusual precision:

> *"Thy **law** is the **truth**."* — Ps 119:142

> *"Thy **word** is **truth**."* — Jn 17:17

The chain is explicit: TRUTH = WORD = LAW = God's commandments. And Jesus completes it by embodying the standard in His own person:

> *"I am $[the-way], the **truth**, and the life."* — Jn 14:6

If truth were merely factual accuracy, calling a person "the truth" would be strange. But if truth is God's standard — His commandments, His way of life — then a person who perfectly embodies that standard *is* the truth. The Word made flesh.

**Symbolizes:** God's commandments — the divine standard that defines right living and reality itself

**Opposite:** The lie — rejecting God's standard; man's way substituted for God's way

**Defining verses:** Jn 17:17, Ps 119:142, Jn 14:6, 1 Jn 2:4

**The surprise:** The test for whether someone possesses truth is not intellectual — it is behavioral. "He that saith, I know him, and keepeth not his commandments, is a **liar**, and the **truth** is not in him" (1 Jn 2:4). The opposite of truth is not error — it is $wickedness.

**Connected:** $light, $darkness, $the-way, $bread, $name, $wickedness, $faith

## Truth Across Scripture

### The Recursive Definition

Scripture does not leave the meaning of truth for readers to infer. It supplies a definitional chain — each link equating truth with the same thing:

> *"Thy **law** is the **truth**."* — Ps 119:142

> *"Thy **word** is **truth**."* — Jn 17:17

> *"I am $[the-way], the **truth**, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me."* — Jn 14:6

Law → Word → Christ. Each term in the chain resolves to the same reality: God's commandments — the standard He has spoken, written, and ultimately embodied in His Son. The $bread of God's mouth (Deut 8:3) is the same substance as the truth that sanctifies. The $the-way of righteousness is the same path that truth defines.

Notice what Jesus joins together in Jn 14:6. He does not say He *teaches* the truth — He says He *is* the truth. If truth means God's standard, then the one who perfectly keeps every commandment without deviation does not merely possess the standard; He is the standard. The Torah walks and breathes.

### You Can Do Truth — Because Truth Is a Standard

The behavioral language around truth in Scripture is the clearest evidence that truth is not mere factual accuracy. Consider the verbs:

> *"He that **doeth truth** cometh to the $[light]."* — Jn 3:21

> *"If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in $[darkness], we lie, and **do not the truth**."* — 1 Jn 1:6

> *"I have no greater joy than to hear that my children **walk in truth**."* — 3 Jn 1:3

> *"I rejoiced greatly that I found of thy children **walking in truth**, as we have received a commandment from the Father."* — 2 Jn 1:4

Truth is something you *do*. Something you *walk in*. And 2 John makes the connection unmistakable: walking in truth = walking "as we have received a commandment." The commandment *is* the truth. Walking in truth *is* keeping the commandment.

This behavioral quality is why the connection to $light works so precisely. $light represents visible obedience — example that others can see. Doing truth is the inward reality; coming to the $light is the outward manifestation. One who keeps God's standard (does truth) becomes a visible example of it ($light). And conversely, one who walks in $darkness — concealed disobedience — "does not the truth" (1 Jn 1:6). The lie and the darkness go together because both involve hiding from the standard.

### The Liar Test

First John provides the most direct equation between truth and commandments — and it comes through the negative:

> *"He that saith, I know him, and **keepeth not his commandments**, is a **liar**, and the **truth is not in him**."* — 1 Jn 2:4

The structure is transparent. Not keeping commandments = liar. Liar = truth is not in him. Therefore: truth = commandments. The liar is not someone who speaks false facts. The liar is someone who *claims to know God while not keeping His commandments*. The lie is the gap between profession and obedience.

This resonates with how Jesus describes the devil:

> *"He was a murderer from the beginning, and **abode not in the truth**, because there is no truth in him. When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own: for he is a liar, and the father of it."* — Jn 8:44

Satan "abode not in the truth" — he did not remain in God's standard. The lie he speaks is "of his own" — his own standard, his own way, substituted for God's. If truth is God's commandments, then the foundational lie is the rejection of that standard. This reaches back to the garden: the serpent's lie was not merely factual deception but an invitation to abandon God's commandment. "Ye shall not surely die" was a replacement standard — man deciding for himself what the consequences would be.

The opposite of truth, then, is not accidental error. It is $wickedness — lawlessness, covenant transgression (Deut 17:2). The lie is not a mistaken statement; it is a way of life that rejects God's authority to define right and wrong.

### Truth Makes Free — Because Sin Is Bondage

> *"And ye shall know the **truth**, and the **truth** shall make you free."* — Jn 8:32

> *"Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin."* — Jn 8:34

> *"If the Son therefore shall make you free, ye shall be free indeed."* — Jn 8:36

The logic of John 8 moves in a tight chain: sin produces bondage (8:34), truth produces freedom (8:32), and the Son produces freedom (8:36). If both truth and the Son produce the same result — freedom from sin — then the Son *is* the truth (which Jn 14:6 confirms), and truth is whatever resolves the sin problem.

How does knowing God's standard make you free? Because sin is transgression of the law (1 Jn 3:4), and knowing — truly knowing, in the Hebrew sense of intimate acquaintance and practice — the commandments is the path out of transgression. The one who keeps God's standard is not enslaved to the cycle of lawlessness.

This also explains why the Son makes free. He is the standard embodied. To know Him is to know the truth. To follow Him is to walk in truth. The freedom is not abstract — it is the practical liberation that comes from living by God's design rather than against it.

### Truth Sanctifies

> *"Sanctify them through thy **truth**: thy word is **truth**."* — Jn 17:17

> *"And for their sakes I sanctify myself, that they also might be sanctified through the **truth**."* — Jn 17:19

To sanctify is to set apart — to separate from the common and dedicate to God. Jesus prays that His followers would be sanctified *through truth*. And He defines truth in the same breath: "thy word is truth."

God's commandments — His word, His standard — are the instrument of sanctification. They are what separates the covenant people from everyone else. Not cultural customs, not intellectual doctrines, but the divine standard lived out. And Jesus sanctifies *Himself* through that same truth (Jn 17:19), so that the sanctification He models would flow to those who follow Him.

This is the same logic as the $bread connection. $bread represents the Word — the covenant terms that sustain spiritual life (Deut 8:3, Jn 6:63). Truth and $bread overlap because both point to the same reality: God's spoken and written standard. To eat the $bread is to receive the terms. To walk in truth is to live by them.

### The Truth–Light–Darkness Triangle

Several passages in John and First John triangulate truth, $light, and $darkness in ways that reveal their relationships:

> *"He that **doeth truth** cometh to the **$[light]**, that his deeds may be made manifest."* — Jn 3:21

> *"Every one that **doeth evil** hateth the **$[light]**, neither cometh to the $[light], lest his deeds should be reproved."* — Jn 3:20

> *"If we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in **$[darkness]**, we lie, and **do not the truth**: But if we walk in the **$[light]**, as he is in the $[light], we have fellowship one with another."* — 1 Jn 1:6–7

The pattern emerges clearly:

- **Truth** = God's standard (the commandments themselves)
- **$light** = the visible demonstration of keeping that standard
- **$darkness** = the concealment of breaking that standard

Doing truth → coming to the $light. Not doing truth → walking in $darkness. Truth is the standard; $light and $darkness describe the visibility of one's relationship to it. The person who keeps God's commandments has nothing to hide and comes into the open. The person who rejects them seeks cover — not because they lack information, but because their deeds would be exposed.

This is why 1 Jn 1:6 equates walking in $darkness with lying: the $darkness-walker claims fellowship with God (who *is* $light, 1 Jn 1:5) while not doing the truth. The claim is the lie. The concealment is the $darkness. The absent obedience is the missing truth.

## Patterns

1. **Truth is defined recursively in Scripture.** Law = truth (Ps 119:142). Word = truth (Jn 17:17). Christ = truth (Jn 14:6). Each link in the chain resolves to the same reality: God's spoken and written standard, ultimately embodied in His Son.

2. **Truth is behavioral, not merely intellectual.** You *do* truth (Jn 3:21). You *walk in* truth (2 Jn 1:4, 3 Jn 1:3). You *keep* truth by keeping commandments (1 Jn 2:4). Every passage treats truth as actionable.

3. **The liar is not the factually mistaken — the liar rejects God's standard.** Not keeping commandments = liar, truth not in him (1 Jn 2:4). Satan "abode not in the truth" — he rejected the standard (Jn 8:44). The lie is lawlessness disguised as freedom.

4. **Truth and the Son produce identical results.** Truth makes free (Jn 8:32). The Son makes free (Jn 8:36). Truth sanctifies (Jn 17:17). The Son sanctifies through truth (Jn 17:19). The convergence confirms Christ is the standard embodied.

5. **Truth occupies the middle position between $light and $darkness.** Truth is the standard. $light is its visible practice. $darkness is its hidden rejection. The three form a consistent system across John and First John.

6. **Truth connects to $faith as standard to response.** $faith is trusting obedience — the human response to hearing God's standard. Truth is what $faith obeys. "Obedience of faith" (Rom 16:26) = obedience to the standard. Without truth, $faith has nothing to act on. Without $faith, truth remains external.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$light** — Visible obedience — the outward demonstration of keeping God's standard. "He that doeth truth cometh to the $light" (Jn 3:21). Truth is the standard; $light is what it looks like when someone lives by it.

- **$darkness** — Concealed disobedience — the hiding of covenant-breaking. Walking in $darkness = "do not the truth" (1 Jn 1:6). $darkness is not ignorance of truth but active concealment of one's rejection of it.

- **$the-way** — Torah / God's commandments — the same reality approached from a different angle. Jesus is both the $the-way and the truth (Jn 14:6). The $the-way is the path; truth is the standard that defines the path. They are inseparable.

- **$bread** — Word / covenant terms — what sustains spiritual life. "Thy word is truth" (Jn 17:17) and "man shall not live by $bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God" (Mat 4:4). $bread and truth both resolve to God's spoken word.

- **$name** — Covenant relationship — the relational context in which truth operates. Those who take God's $name but reject His commandments are liars (1 Jn 2:4). Taking the $name "in vain" (Exod 20:7) is claiming the covenant while not living by its truth.

- **$wickedness** — Lawlessness / covenant transgression — truth's direct opposite in practice. $wickedness is what truth frees from (Jn 8:32–34). The "workers of lawlessness" (Mat 7:23) are those who invoke the $name but reject the truth.

- **$faith** — Trusting obedience — the human response to truth. $faith without works is dead (Jas 2:17) because $faith is the act of obeying the standard. Truth is what God speaks; $faith is what the hearer does with it.

| G225 | *alētheia* | truth, reality — from G1 *a-* (not) + *lēthō* (to escape notice, to be hidden) | Literally "un-hidden" — what is not concealed. Compare $darkness (concealment) |
| H8451 | *torah* | instruction, law, direction | Connected via Ps 119:142 (law = truth) |

The Greek etymology of G225 *alētheia* is suggestive: *a-lētheia* = the "un-hidden," that which is not concealed. This resonates with the truth–$light–$darkness pattern. Truth is the standard that comes into the open. $darkness is what hides from it. The very word carries the sense of disclosure — what is revealed rather than suppressed.

The Hebrew H571 *ʾemeth* shares its root with H530 *ʾemunah* ($faith). In Hebrew, the standard (truth) and the response to the standard (faithfulness/obedience) grow from the same soil. The connection is not coincidental — it reflects the Hebrew understanding that God's commandments and the faithful keeping of them are two sides of the same covenant reality.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **truth** begins:

> “Truth, n.; pl. Truths. Etym: [OE. treuthe, trouthe, treowpe, AS. treów. See True; cf. Troth, Betroth.] 1. The quality or being true; as: -- (a) Conformity to fact or reality; exact accordance with that which is, or has been; or shall be. (b) Conformity to rule; exactness; close correspondence with an example, mood, object of imitation, or the like. Plows, to go true, depend much on the truth of the ironwork. Mortimer. (c) Fidelity; constancy; steadfastness; faithfulness. Alas! they had been friends in youth, But whispering tongues can poison truth. Coleridge.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The approved definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

A common reading is: **Christ Himself, and God's word — the gospel that sets free.**

- The entry could reasonably be classified as REFINED if “God’s revealed reality/word” is treated as the consensus core category and God’s law as a narrower subset of it. Psalm 119:142 also makes that narrowing textually explicit. The DIVERGENT classification depends on giving controlling weight to the consensus’s repeated Christological center and reading the glossary entry as replacing that center rather than merely addressing one context.

- One could argue this is REFINED rather than DIVERGENT: the consensus itself lists 'the truth as Scripture/God's word' as a common alternative (with Ps 119:142/160 as proof texts), and 'God's law' could be read as a narrowing within that recognized category rather than a replacement of the consensus referent. On that view the book simply selects and sharpens an already-acknowledged layer.

- Ps 119:142 (shared with consensus alternatives) supports 'law is truth' but does not displace the dominant Christocentric consensus that the book itself acknowledges as commonly taught.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**God's law — the standard.**

This is the approved glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.
