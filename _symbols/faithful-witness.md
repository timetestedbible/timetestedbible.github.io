# Faithful Witness

## Approved Definition

**The Messiah, who bears the Father's light faithfully through the [night](/research/symbols/night/). The [moon](/research/symbols/moon/) writes this witness in the sky.**

The fuller book and notebook trail is available in [Sun, Moon & Stars](/books/symbolic-language/sun-moon-and-stars/).

## Definition Layers

### Bible literal sense

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry for **faithful** begins: “Full of faith, or having faith; disposed to believe, especially in the declarations and promises of God.”

These layers may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Short Case

The Messiah, who reflects the Father's light like the moon established in the heavens.

> *“It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a faithful witness in heaven. Selah.”* — Psalms 89:37

> *“And from Jesus Christ, who is the faithful witness, and the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth. Unto him that loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood,”* — Revelation 1:5

> *“And unto the angel of the church of the Laodiceans write; These things saith the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God;”* — Revelation 3:14

> *“Behold, I have given him for a witness to the people, a leader and commander to the people.”* — Isaiah 55:4

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Dictionary Comparison

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Revelation 1:5 and 3:14 directly support the messianic identification, and Psalm 89:37 supports the moon as a faithful celestial witness. However, the printed excerpts do not themselves establish that moonlight specifically represents the Messiah bearing the Father’s light unchanged; that is a further typological inference.

- One could argue the book's claim that 'the moon is that name written in the sky' materially adds a creational-symbolic layer (moon = Christ-symbol) that no consensus response asserts as the primary meaning — the consensus treats the moon reading as a separate minority interpretation of Psalm 89:37 rather than as a symbol of Christ — which might justify REFINED. But since the core identification (Christ as the Faithful Witness) is untouched and the lunar link rests on the same universally cited verse, this remains elaboration rather than a boundary shift.

- It could be argued that the book's direct equation of the Messiah with the moon's light-bearing function leans heavily on a minority astronomical reading of Psalm 89, but this does not displace the primary identification of the referent as the Messiah.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
