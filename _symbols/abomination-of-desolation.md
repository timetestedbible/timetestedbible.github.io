# Abomination of Desolation

**Counterfeit authority installed where God alone belongs — replacing true covenant worship and causing the emptying of His people from that place.**

> *"When ye therefore shall see the **abomination of desolation**, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:) Then let them which be in Judaea flee into the mountains."* — Mat 24:15–16

---

## The Key Insight

The phrase has three parts, and all three matter. Something is *removed*. Something counterfeit is *installed*. And the result is *desolation* — emptying.

Every time this pattern appears in Scripture, the same sequence unfolds:

1. **The daily** — true worship, the continual sacrifice, the legitimate covenant relationship — is **taken away**.
2. **The abomination** — an idolatrous counterfeit, a detestable substitute — is **set up** in the place that belongs to God alone.
3. **Desolation** — the place is **emptied**. The people flee. The glory departs. What was once full of God's presence becomes a wasteland.

This is not merely a single future event. It is a *recurring structural pattern* that intensifies across Scripture — from Solomon's high places to Manasseh's temple idols to Antiochus's Zeus altar to Rome's armies to the final "man of sin" who sits in the temple of God, "shewing himself that he is God" (2 Thess 2:4). Each cycle escalates. Each cycle follows the same three-step sequence.

And the pattern has an opposite. When God's glory *fills* the holy place, it draws His people *in* (2 Chr 7:1–3). When a counterfeit fills the holy place, it drives them *out*. The Shekinah gathers. The abomination scatters. The abomination of desolation is the anti-Shekinah.

**Symbolizes:** Counterfeit authority installed in the place reserved for God — displacing true worship and emptying the holy place of covenant people

**Opposite:** The Shekinah — God's glory filling the holy place, drawing His people in (2 Chr 7:1–3, Rev 21:3)

**Defining verses:** Dan 9:27, Dan 11:31, Dan 12:11, Mat 24:15, 2 Thess 2:3–4

**The surprise:** Mark's Gospel uses a *masculine* participle (G2476 *hestekota*) for the abomination — "when ye shall see the abomination of desolation… *standing* where *he* ought not" (Mk 13:14). The grammar shifts the abomination from a *thing* to a *person*. It is not merely an object in the holy place — it is a counterfeit *authority* claiming the place of God.

**Connected:** $idolatry, $wickedness, $deceive, $harlot, $fornication, $mark, $animal

## Abomination of Desolation Across Scripture

### The Three-Part Sequence

The pattern is consistent enough to function as a diagnostic:

> *"And **arms** shall stand on his part, and they shall pollute the sanctuary of strength, and shall **take away the daily** sacrifice, and they shall **place the abomination that maketh desolate**."* — Dan 11:31

> *"And from the time that **the daily sacrifice shall be taken away**, and **the abomination that maketh desolate set up**, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days."* — Dan 12:11

First the daily is removed. Then the abomination is installed. The result — desolation — is embedded in the name itself.

Jesus confirms the sequence and adds the third step explicitly:

> *"When ye therefore shall see the **abomination of desolation**, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place… **Then let them which be in Judaea flee** into the mountains."* — Mat 24:15–16

See the abomination → flee. The installation of the counterfeit produces the emptying. Luke's parallel replaces the phrase with an interpretive key:

> *"And when ye shall see Jerusalem **compassed with armies**, then know that the **desolation** thereof is nigh. Then let them which are in Judaea **flee** to the mountains."* — Lk 21:20–21

Luke identifies the armies of Rome as the abomination. The counterfeit authority — a pagan military power — surrounds the holy city. The result is the same: flight, scattering, desolation. And Luke adds a critical time-marker: "Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, **until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled**" (Lk 21:24). The desolation has an expiration date.

### What the Words Themselves Reveal

The Hebrew phrase carries more weight than most translations convey.

H8251 *shiqquts* (abomination) derives from H8262 *shaqats* — "to be filthy, to loathe, to detest." This is the same root used for unclean animals in Leviticus 11:11 — things that provoke visceral recoiling. A *shiqquts* is not merely "wrong" — it is *detestable*, something that causes revulsion in the presence of the holy. Throughout the Old Testament, the word is consistently applied to idols and idolatrous objects (Deut 29:17, 2 Ki 23:24, Ezek 20:7–8).

H8074 *shamem* (desolate) carries a double meaning that English obscures. It means both **to devastate/empty a place** and **to be stunned, stupefied, appalled**. The abomination simultaneously empties the holy place and stuns the observer. When Daniel sees the vision, he is himself *shamem* — devastated by what he sees. The word captures both the objective reality (the place is emptied) and the subjective horror (the faithful are appalled).

In Greek, G946 *bdelygma* (abomination) derives from G948 *bdelysso* — "to stink, to turn away from in disgust." And G2050 *eremosis* (desolation) from G2049 *eremoo* — "to make desolate, to lay waste." The Septuagint and New Testament preserve the same double force: a stinking, revolting counterfeit that produces emptiness wherever it stands.

### A Person, Not Just a Thing

Mark's account contains a grammatical signal that commentators have long noticed:

> *"But when ye shall see the **abomination of desolation**, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, **standing** where **he** ought not, (let him that readeth understand,) then let them that be in Judaea flee to the mountains."* — Mk 13:14

The participle *hestekota* is masculine — "standing where *he* ought not." Greek grammar expects a neuter participle to agree with the neuter noun *bdelygma*. Mark breaks this rule deliberately. The abomination is not merely an idol on an altar. It is a *person* — a counterfeit authority — standing in the place reserved for God.

Paul makes this explicit:

> *"Let no man $[deceive] you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that **man of sin** be revealed, the son of perdition; Who opposeth and exalteth himself above all that is called God, or that is worshipped; so that he **as God sitteth in the temple of God**, shewing himself that he is God."* — 2 Thess 2:3–4

The abomination is a *who*, not just a *what*. A man sits in the *naos* — the inner sanctuary — and claims to be God. This is the ultimate expression of the pattern: not just an idol placed in the temple, but a person *replacing* God in the place where God alone should dwell. The pattern of $idolatry reaches its fullest expression — self enthroned where God belongs.

### Daniel 8 — The Transgression of Desolation

Daniel 8:13 uses a different first word that illuminates the pattern further:

> *"How long shall be the vision concerning the daily sacrifice, and the **transgression** (H6588 *pesha*) **of desolation**, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot?"* — Dan 8:13

Here the "abomination" is called a *pesha* — a revolt, a rebellion, a willful transgression. This is not accidental contamination. It is insurrection against God's authority. The counterfeit does not stumble into the holy place — it storms it. It is an act of deliberate rebellion that installs itself where it has no right to stand.

Daniel 8:11–12 describes the mechanism:

> *"Yea, he magnified himself even to the prince of the host, and by him the **daily** sacrifice was **taken away**, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down. And an host was given him against the daily sacrifice **by reason of transgression**."*

The daily is removed *by reason of transgression* — covenant unfaithfulness opens the door. This is a consistent biblical pattern: the counterfeit does not simply overwhelm from without. It enters because the covenant people's own unfaithfulness has created the opening. Ezekiel 8 demonstrates this vividly — the elders of Israel are already practicing $idolatry *inside* the temple before any foreign power arrives.

### The Wing of Abominations

Daniel 9:27 contains a phrase that connects the abomination to the physical structure of the temple:

> *"And he shall confirm the covenant with many for one week: and in the midst of the week he shall cause the sacrifice and the oblation to cease, and for the overspreading (H3671 *kanaph*) of **abominations** he shall make it **desolate**, even until the consummation."* — Dan 9:27

The word *kanaph* means wing, edge, extremity — it is the word used for the wings of the cherubim in the Holy of Holies (1 Ki 6:24, 8:7), for the corners of a garment, and for the pinnacle or extremity of the temple. The abominations are associated with the *kanaph* — the very architectural feature that represents God's covenant $wings of protection. The counterfeit authority installs itself at the pinnacle of the sacred structure. Where the $wings of God should shelter, the abomination stands.

### The Pattern Across History

Scripture records a series of escalating fulfillments, each following the same three-step structure:

**Solomon's high places** (1 Ki 11:5–7): Solomon builds shrines for Ashtoreth, Chemosh, and Milcom — called *shiqquts* — on the mountain facing Jerusalem. True worship continues in the temple, but the counterfeit is installed *within sight* of the holy place. The pattern begins subtly: not yet *in* the temple, but encroaching.

**Manasseh's abominations** (2 Ki 21:4–7, Jer 7:30, 32:34): "He set a carved image of the grove that he had made **in the house**" — inside the temple itself. Jer 32:34: "they set their **abominations** in the house which is called by my name, to defile it." Now the counterfeit is inside. The escalation is spatial: from the hills near the temple to within its walls.

**Ezekiel's temple vision** (Ezek 8:3–17): God gives Ezekiel a tour of the abominations inside the temple — the image of jealousy at the gate (v. 3), seventy elders burning incense to images in hidden chambers (v. 11), women weeping for Tammuz (v. 14), twenty-five men with their backs to the temple worshipping the $sun toward the east (v. 16). Each room takes the prophet deeper. The abomination has saturated every layer of the holy space. And what follows? Ezekiel 10–11 records the glory of God *departing* the temple — the Shekinah leaves, moving from the cherubim to the threshold to the east gate to the mountain east of the city. The counterfeit fills; the glory empties. Installation produces desolation.

**Antiochus Epiphanes, 167 BC** (Dan 8, 11:31): The historical fulfillment that Daniel's vision most directly describes. Antiochus erected an altar to Zeus on the altar of burnt offering, sacrificed swine, and prohibited Torah observance. The daily sacrifice was taken away. The abomination was set up. The temple was emptied of true worship for approximately 2,300 evenings and mornings (Dan 8:14) — until the Maccabean cleansing, commemorated as Hanukkah. A foreign ruler installed his god where Israel's God belonged, and the holy place was devastated.

**Rome, AD 70** (Lk 21:20–24): Luke's identification of the armies surrounding Jerusalem as the moment to flee. The Roman standards — bearing the image of the emperor, who claimed divine status — entered the temple precincts. Titus's soldiers offered sacrifice to their standards in the temple courts (Josephus, *War* 6.6.1). Counterfeit authority. Holy place. Desolation — the temple destroyed, the city emptied, the people scattered. "Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled" (Lk 21:24).

**The mystery already at work** (2 Thess 2:7): Paul writes that "the mystery of $[wickedness] (G458 *anomia* — lawlessness) doth already work." The pattern is not dormant between historical peaks — it is continuous, restrained only by something (or someone) that will be "taken out of the way" (2 Thess 2:7). When the restraint is removed, the final form is revealed: the man of sin who sits in the temple as God, who is then "consumed with the spirit of his mouth, and destroyed with the brightness of his coming" (2 Thess 2:8).

Each cycle intensifies: from the *vicinity* of the temple (Solomon) to *inside* the temple (Manasseh) to *saturating* every chamber (Ezekiel's vision) to *replacing* the altar (Antiochus) to *destroying* the structure (Rome) to *sitting as God* in the sanctuary itself (the man of sin). The trajectory moves from proximity to occupation to impersonation.

### The Abomination and the Harlot

The connection between the abomination and $fornication / $harlot imagery is not incidental — it is structural. The $harlot is the covenant-breaker who opens the door for the counterfeit:

> *"And the woman was arrayed in purple and scarlet colour, and decked with gold and precious stones and pearls, having a golden cup in her hand full of **abominations** (G946 *bdelygma*) and filthiness of her **$[fornication]**."* — Rev 17:4

> *"And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, $[babylon-the-great], THE MOTHER OF **$[harlot]S** AND **ABOMINATIONS** OF THE EARTH."* — Rev 17:5

Babylon is the *mother* — the source — of the abomination pattern. Her cup is full of *bdelygmata*, the same word Jesus uses in Mat 24:15. She does not merely commit $fornication; she is the matrix from which all counterfeit authority systems emerge. The $harlot is the covenant-breaker; the abomination is what she births into the holy place.

This is why Ezekiel 8 shows Israel's own elders practicing $idolatry before the foreign power arrives. The counterfeit cannot simply be imposed from outside. The $harlot — covenant unfaithfulness — opens the gate from within. Daniel 8:12 confirms: "an host was given him against the daily sacrifice **by reason of transgression**."

### What the Abomination Is "Highly Esteemed"

Luke records a saying of Jesus that broadens the abomination concept beyond the temple:

> *"Ye are they which justify yourselves before men; but God knoweth your hearts: for that which is highly esteemed among men is **abomination** (G946 *bdelygma*) in the sight of God."* — Lk 16:15

The same Greek word. Whatever men exalt to the highest place — whatever human authority or system claims ultimate loyalty — is *bdelygma* before God. The abomination is not confined to a physical temple. It is the principle of exalting the counterfeit wherever the true should stand. Jesus speaks this to the Pharisees, who have installed their own tradition in the place of God's commandments (Mk 7:8–9). The pattern operates wherever human authority replaces divine authority as the object of ultimate allegiance.

### The Terminus — What Cannot Enter

> *"And there shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh **abomination** (G946 *bdelygma*), or maketh a lie: but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life."* — Rev 21:27

The new Jerusalem is the permanent reversal of the pattern. No counterfeit authority will ever stand in God's dwelling place again. The holy city is filled with God's glory (Rev 21:11, 23) and emptied of every *bdelygma*. What the abomination of desolation does — emptying the holy place of God's presence — is permanently undone. The Shekinah fills the city. The abomination is permanently excluded. The pattern reaches its resolution.

## Patterns

1. **Three-step sequence: removal → installation → emptying.** Every manifestation follows this structure. The daily is taken away, the counterfeit is set up, the place is made desolate. Dan 11:31, Dan 12:11, Mat 24:15–16, Lk 21:20–21. The three steps are diagnostic — if all three are present, the pattern is operating.

2. **Transgression opens the door.** The abomination does not simply overwhelm from without. Dan 8:12 — the host is given "by reason of transgression." Ezek 8 — Israel's own elders are already practicing $idolatry inside the temple. The $harlot opens the gate. Covenant unfaithfulness creates the opening for counterfeit authority to enter.

3. **The counterfeit intensifies across cycles.** Solomon's high places → Manasseh's temple idol → Ezekiel's saturated chambers → Antiochus's Zeus altar → Rome's destruction → the man of sin as God. Each cycle occupies the holy place more completely. The trajectory moves from proximity to impersonation.

4. **The abomination is personal, not merely material.** Mark's masculine participle (*hestekota* — "he standing"), Paul's "man of sin" who "sitteth in the temple of God as God" (2 Thess 2:4). The pattern culminates not in an object but in a *person* claiming divine authority. $idolatry at its root is self-rule; the abomination's final form is a self enthroned as God.

5. **The anti-Shekinah.** God's glory filling the temple draws the people in (2 Chr 7:1–3). The abomination filling the temple drives the people out (Mat 24:16). They are structural inverses. One gathers; the other scatters. One fills with presence; the other empties into desolation.

6. **The desolation has a terminus.** Lk 21:24 — "until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled." Dan 12:11 — 1,290 days. 2 Thess 2:8 — destroyed by "the brightness of his coming." Rev 21:27 — permanently excluded from the new Jerusalem. The pattern is devastating but not eternal.

7. **Babylon is the mother of the pattern.** Rev 17:5 — "mother of harlots and abominations of the earth." The system of counterfeit authority has a source, and that source is itself destined for destruction (Rev 18). The abomination is the *product* of Babylon; the fall of Babylon does not eliminate it but releases its final form (compare Rev 11:7 — the beast emerges from the abyss).

## Connections

- **$idolatry** — The root. $idolatry is self-rule — rejecting God's word and substituting one's own authority. The abomination of desolation is $idolatry *installed in the holy place*. Every abomination is idolatrous, but not every act of $idolatry is "the abomination of desolation." The distinction is *location*: where God alone belongs.

- **$wickedness** — G458 *anomia* (lawlessness) is the operating principle of the abomination. "The mystery of $[wickedness] doth already work" (2 Thess 2:7). $wickedness = covenant transgression (Deut 17:2); the abomination is its apex expression — lawlessness enthroned as law.

- **$deceive** — The abomination does not announce itself as counterfeit. "Take heed that no man $[deceive] you" (Mat 24:4) precedes the abomination warning. The man of sin comes "with all deceivableness" (2 Thess 2:10). $deceive redirects covenant allegiance through misrepresentation — the abomination is what gets installed when the redirection succeeds.

- **$harlot** — The covenant-breaker who opens the door from within. Rev 17:4–5 — the $harlot holds the cup *full of abominations*. She is the mother of the pattern. $fornication with foreign powers creates the conditions for the counterfeit to enter.

- **$fornication** — The act of turning to other gods/masters. The abomination is what $fornication produces in sacred space — the foreign god installed where the true God was worshipped.

- **$mark** — Rev 13:14–16 links the beast's image (a form of the abomination pattern) to the $mark on hand and forehead. The $mark is the visible practice of allegiance; the abomination is the authority that demands it. Those who refuse the $mark are those who refuse the counterfeit's claim to the holy place.

- **$animal** — The beast (G2342 *therion*) of Rev 13 rises to demand worship and install its image. The $animal represents people/powers characterized by their nature; the beast is the counterfeit power that produces the abomination's final form.
