# Tartarus (*tartaroō*)

## Approved Definition

**To cast sinning angels into Tartarus: confinement in chains of darkness while they await judgment. The word does not name the grave of the human dead or [Gehenna](/research/symbols/gehenna/)'s destructive verdict.**
Peter uses *tartaroō* once. The verse identifies its subjects, their present
condition, and its limit: sinning angels are delivered into chains of darkness
and reserved for judgment (2 Pet 2:4). Jude 6 supplies the close parallel:
angels who left their proper habitation are kept in chains under darkness
until the judgment of the great day.

## Definition Layers

### Bible symbolic sense

*Not applicable. This is a recovered biblical word, not a separate symbol.*

### Bible literal sense

**To cast sinning angels into Tartarus: confinement in chains of darkness while they await judgment. The word does not name the grave of the human dead or [Gehenna](/research/symbols/gehenna/)'s destructive verdict.**

### Webster's English sense

Webster's 1913 entry places Tartarus beneath Hades in Greek poetry and notes
its later use for the punishment of wicked spirits or, more generally, the
lower world. That literary history does not erase the narrower subject and
action stated in Peter.

## Corpus and Method

The New Testament uses G5020 once, in 2 Peter 2:4. Jude 6 is included as the
direct verbal and thematic parallel. The definition therefore stays within
what those passages actually identify.

## Competing Definitions Tested

English Bibles may print *hell* in 2 Peter 2:4, inviting the reader to merge
Tartarus with every other passage carrying that English label. The text itself
does not authorize that merger. It concerns angels confined before judgment;
it does not call Tartarus the grave of mankind or the Valley of Hinnom.

## Relationship to Other Symbols

[Hell](/research/symbols/hell/) is the ambiguous English lookup label.
[Sheol](/research/symbols/sheol/) holds the human dead, while
[Gehenna](/research/symbols/gehenna/) names the valley-image of destructive
judgment. These definitions may participate in one larger doctrine without
becoming aliases of one another.

## Conclusion

**To cast sinning angels into Tartarus: confinement in chains of darkness while they await judgment. The word does not name the grave of the human dead or [Gehenna](/research/symbols/gehenna/)'s destructive verdict.**
