# Virgin

## Summary

**The espoused — betrothed to one husband with the wedding still ahead: [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) standing, not attainment; the law counts a betrothed virgin already a wife, the prophets call the ten-tribe house re-espoused after her divorce “the virgin of Israel,” and virgins plural are the [bride](/research/symbols/marriage/)’s companions who follow her in.**
> *"For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste **virgin** to Christ."* — 2 Cor 11:2

---

## Definitions

### Symbolic Definition

**The espoused — betrothed to one husband with the wedding still ahead: [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) standing, not attainment; the law counts a betrothed virgin already a wife, the prophets call the ten-tribe house re-espoused after her divorce “the virgin of Israel,” and virgins plural are the [bride](/research/symbols/marriage/)’s companions who follow her in.**

### Literal Biblical Definition

*Not yet stated separately. This is a research gap: the literal sense must be derived from ordinary biblical uses, not copied from Webster.*

### Webster's Definition

Webster's 1913 entry for **virgin** begins: “A woman who has had no carnal knowledge of man; a maid.”

These definitions may agree, but they are independent evidence. A biblical passage may use the term literally, symbolically, or both at the same time; the literal properties remain part of the logic when Scripture uses the object as a symbol.

## The Key Insight

Scripture tells one story with two women.

One belongs to her husband alone. The other sells herself to every passing stranger. They are not two different characters — they are the same woman at different moments. The faithful city *becomes* a $harlot (Isa 1:21). The $harlot is called back to become a virgin again (Jer 31:21). The entire drama of covenant history runs along this single track: virgin → harlot → virgin restored.

The word "virgin" in English suggests physical purity — a condition that, once lost, cannot be recovered. But in Scripture the virgin is something a city, a nation, and a people can *become again*. "Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O **virgin** of Israel" (Jer 31:4). What was broken is restored. What went after other masters returns to the one true husband. This is not about anatomy. It is about allegiance.

The $harlot represents $idolatry — self-rule, serving other gods, rejecting God's Word as the governing standard. The virgin represents the opposite: exclusive devotion, covenant $faith, trusting obedience to the one who betrothed you.

These two women — one faithful, one fallen — frame the entire arc of Scripture, from Eden to Revelation. And the question posed to every reader is the same question posed to Israel: *Which woman are you?*

**Symbolizes:** Exclusive covenant devotion — faithfulness to one God, one master, one standard

**Opposite:** The $harlot — $idolatry, covenant breaking, divided allegiance, serving other gods

**Defining verses:** Rev 14:4, 2 Cor 11:2, Isa 1:21, Jer 31:4

**The surprise:** In Rev 14:4, the 144,000 are called "virgins" because they were "not defiled with women." The "women" are not literal — they are $babylon and her daughters, the false religious systems. Being a "virgin" means refusing spiritual $fornication. Men, women, cities, and nations can all be "virgins" or "harlots" in this framework.

**Connected:** $harlot, $fornication, $idolatry, $marriage, $faith, $truth, $babylon, $jerusalem

## Virgin Across Scripture

### The Marriage That Governs Everything

The virgin symbol only makes sense inside the $marriage metaphor that governs the entire Bible. Scripture presents a consistent picture:

- **God** = Husband
- **His people** = Wife
- **The covenant** = $marriage vows
- **Faithfulness** = virgin
- **Idolatry** = $fornication / $harlot

> *"For thy Maker is thine husband; the LORD of hosts is his name."* — Isa 54:5

> *"Turn, O backsliding children, saith the LORD; for I am married unto you."* — Jer 3:14

> *"I will betroth thee unto me for ever; yea, I will betroth thee unto me in righteousness, and in judgment, and in lovingkindness, and in mercies."* — Hos 2:19

The virgin is not a category of person. She is a *position* — the faithful wife who has not gone after other gods. And the $harlot is not a different woman. She is the same wife who has broken the covenant vows.

This is why Paul frames his ministry in $marriage language: "I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste **virgin** to Christ" (2 Cor 11:2). The goal of the apostolic mission is to keep the covenant community in exclusive devotion to one master. The church is already "espoused" — betrothed. Paul's anxiety is that she might not arrive at the wedding still faithful.

### The Faithful City Becomes a Harlot

> *"How is the faithful city become an **harlot**! it was full of judgment; righteousness lodged in it; but now murderers."* — Isa 1:21

This single verse contains the entire virgin-to-$harlot transformation. $jerusalem was "faithful" — a virgin, full of judgment and righteousness, the standard of $truth upheld. Then she *became* a harlot. Not a different city — the same city, with a different allegiance.

What changed? The verse answers: righteousness was replaced by murder. Judgment gave way to injustice. The city that once kept God's standard ($truth) began following another standard ($idolatry). The virgin committed $fornication and became what she was never meant to be.

This pattern repeats throughout the prophets. Ezekiel 16 tells the story at length: God found $jerusalem as an abandoned infant, raised her, entered $marriage covenant with her ("I spread my skirt over thee... and thou becamest mine," Ezek 16:8), lavished beauty and wealth on her — and she used every gift to "play the harlot" with every nation that passed by (Ezek 16:15). The virgin bride became the $harlot who outdid even Sodom (Ezek 16:48).

The horror of the prophetic message is not that $jerusalem was always a harlot. It is that she *was* a virgin — and chose to become otherwise.

### "These Are They Which Were Not Defiled"

> *"These are they which were not defiled with women; for they are **virgins**. These are they which follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth."* — Rev 14:4

This is arguably the defining verse for the symbol, and it is widely misunderstood. The 144,000 — who include both men and women from every tribe — are called "virgins" because they were "not defiled with women." If taken literally, this would exclude all married men, all women, and most of Scripture's heroes. The symbolic reading is the only one that makes sense of the passage.

The "women" are the same women from Rev 17 — $babylon "the great whore" and her daughters, the "MOTHER OF HARLOTS" (Rev 17:5). Being "defiled with women" means committing spiritual $fornication with false religious and political systems. Being a "virgin" means refusing to do so.

The parallel clause confirms this: "These are they which **follow the Lamb** whithersoever he goeth." The virgin is defined by what she follows. The $harlot follows $babylon, Egypt, Assyria — any master who offers power or security. The virgin follows the Lamb. One allegiance. One master. Undivided.

This is why Rev 14:4 pairs "virgins" with "follow the Lamb" — the two phrases are saying the same thing. Exclusive devotion (virgin) = trusting obedience ($faith). The virgin has not mixed her allegiance with other systems. She is, in the language Jesus used of the $dove, G185 *akeraios* — unmixed.

### The Virgin of Israel — A Nation Called Faithful

The prophets repeatedly personify $israel as "the virgin":

> *"The **virgin** the daughter of Zion hath despised thee, and laughed thee to scorn."* — 2 Ki 19:21 (parallel: Isa 37:22)

> *"Again I will build thee, and thou shalt be built, O **virgin** of Israel: thou shalt again be adorned with thy tabrets, and shalt go forth in the dances of them that make merry."* — Jer 31:4

> *"Turn again, O **virgin** of Israel, turn again to these thy cities."* — Jer 31:21

> *"What thing shall I liken to thee, O daughter of $[jerusalem]? what shall I equal to thee, that I may comfort thee, O **virgin** daughter of Zion?"* — Lam 2:13

> *"The **virgin** of Israel is fallen; she shall no more rise."* — Amos 5:2

Notice the range: in 2 Kings 19:21, the virgin daughter of Zion is defiant and triumphant — laughing at the Assyrian threat because her covenant faithfulness is intact. In Amos 5:2, the virgin of Israel has "fallen" and will "no more rise." Same title. Different spiritual state. The name "virgin" is aspirational — it describes what Israel *should be* and what God calls her back to, even when she has fallen.

The restoration promise in Jer 31:4 is remarkable: "thou shalt be *built*" — the same language used for constructing $jerusalem. The virgin is being *rebuilt*. A virgin who needs rebuilding is not a statement about physical purity. It is a covenant term — the faithful community, broken by $idolatry, being restored to exclusive devotion.

Jer 31:21 makes the call explicit: "Turn again, O virgin of Israel, turn again to these thy cities." The word "turn" (H7725 *shuv*) is the standard word for repentance — to return. The $harlot is called to *turn back* and become a virgin again. The $marriage metaphor allows what biology does not: full restoration of the covenant bond through repentance.

### Two Women, Two Cities, Two Kingdoms

The Book of Revelation sets the virgin and the $harlot side by side as the final contrast of history:

**The Harlot** — Rev 17:
> *"I will shew unto thee the judgment of the great **whore** that sitteth upon many waters: with whom the kings of the earth have committed $[fornication]."* — Rev 17:1–2

> *"And upon her forehead was a name written, MYSTERY, $[BABYLON] THE GREAT, THE MOTHER OF **HARLOTS** AND ABOMINATIONS OF THE EARTH."* — Rev 17:5

**The Bride** — Rev 19, 21:
> *"Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the $[marriage] of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready."* — Rev 19:7

> *"And I John saw the holy city, new $[Jerusalem], coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband."* — Rev 21:2

$babylon is the $harlot. $jerusalem is the bride — the virgin. One city is "the mother of harlots," the source and generator of $idolatry across the earth. The other city is the faithful wife who "hath made herself ready" for her husband. One is destroyed by $fire. The other descends from God, radiant and eternal.

Every nation, every institution, every individual stands in one city or the other. The question Scripture presses is not *Do you know about God?* but *Whom do you serve?* The $harlot knows God exists — she was married to Him. Her problem is not ignorance but infidelity. The virgin's distinction is not superior knowledge but exclusive allegiance.

### The Transformation Chain

The full symbolic chain runs in both directions:

**Descent:** VIRGIN → commits $$fornication ($idolatry) → becomes $$harlot (covenant breaker) → joins $$babylon (worldly kingdom)

**Restoration:** $$harlot → repents (turns) → becomes VIRGIN again → enters $$jerusalem (covenant community)

This is not theoretical. Hosea enacts it. God tells the prophet: "Go, take unto thee a wife of whoredoms... for the land hath committed great whoredom, departing from the LORD" (Hos 1:2). Gomer is the $harlot — $israel who has departed from her husband. The entire book traces her descent and God's relentless pursuit, ending in restoration: "I will betroth thee unto me for ever" (Hos 2:19).

The $harlot is not beyond redemption. She is a virgin who forgot whom she belonged to. And the virgin is not inherently secure. She is a wife who might yet forget.

This is why the apostles are anxious. Paul fears the Corinthians will be "corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ" (2 Cor 11:3) — that the chaste virgin will be lured away. The letters to the churches in Revelation carry the same warning: Ephesus has "left thy first love" (Rev 2:4). Thyatira tolerates "that woman Jezebel, which... teacheth and seduceth my servants to commit $[fornication]" (Rev 2:20). The virgin is always one act of spiritual adultery from becoming the $harlot.

### What Keeps the Virgin Faithful

If the virgin represents exclusive covenant devotion, what sustains that devotion? The co-occurring symbols provide the answer:

**$truth** — God's Word, His commandments, His standard. The virgin is faithful because she follows a specific standard — not her own judgment, not the world's wisdom, but God's revealed Word. "Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth" (Jn 17:17). The virgin stays pure by staying under the authority of the Word.

**$faith** — Trusting obedience. The virgin does not merely *know* the truth; she *acts* on it. James 2:17 says $faith without works is dead. The virgin's faithfulness is demonstrated in action — following the Lamb "whithersoever he goeth" (Rev 14:4).

The opposite chain confirms this. $idolatry at its root is self-rule — rejecting God's Word (1 Sam 15:23). $fornication is the *act* of turning from $truth to follow another standard. The $harlot is one who has replaced God's authority with her own or with another's. The virgin is defined precisely by *not* doing this — by maintaining submission to the covenant standard even when other options present themselves.

## Patterns

1. **Virgin and harlot are the same woman.** "The faithful city *become* an harlot" (Isa 1:21). $jerusalem does not *encounter* a harlot; she *becomes* one. The transformation is always possible in both directions.

2. **Virginity can be restored.** "Again I will build thee, O virgin of Israel" (Jer 31:4). Unlike physical virginity, covenant virginity is recoverable through repentance. The $marriage metaphor allows what biology does not.

3. **Nations, cities, and communities are called virgins — not just individuals.** "The virgin daughter of Zion" (2 Ki 19:21), "the virgin of Israel" (Amos 5:2). The symbol operates at the collective level: a people's corporate allegiance.

4. **The virgin follows one master; the harlot follows many.** Rev 14:4 defines the virgin as one who follows the Lamb. Hos 7:11 describes $ephraim going to Egypt and Assyria. The number of masters is the diagnostic: one = virgin, many = $harlot.

5. **"Not defiled with women" = not entangled with false systems.** Rev 14:4 uses $marriage-purity language for what is clearly $idolatry avoidance. The "women" are $babylon's daughters — rival systems of allegiance.

6. **The two-city framework mirrors the two-woman framework.** $babylon = $harlot city (Rev 17). $jerusalem = bride/virgin city (Rev 21). Every institution and individual inhabits one or the other.

7. **The virgin's defining quality is the same as $faith.** Exclusive devotion (virgin) = trusting obedience ($faith). $idolatry (harlot) = unbelief/disobedience. The virgin-harlot axis maps onto the faith-unbelief axis.

## Relationship to Other Symbols
- **$harlot** — The virgin's strict opposite. The $harlot is the covenant breaker — one who was married to God but committed spiritual adultery through $idolatry (serving other gods/masters). The same person or community can be virgin or harlot depending on the state of their allegiance. Isa 1:21 captures the entire transformation in a single verse.

- **$fornication** — The *act* that transforms a virgin into a $harlot. $fornication = idolatry, the act of serving other gods. "Go a whoring after other gods" is the prophets' repeated charge (Deut 31:16, Judg 2:17, Ezek 6:9). The virgin who commits $fornication has broken the $marriage covenant.

- **$idolatry** — The root condition beneath $fornication. $idolatry = self-rule, rejecting God's Word as the governing standard (1 Sam 15:23). The $harlot does not merely serve *different* gods — she serves *herself* as ultimate authority. The virgin, by contrast, submits to God's standard.

- **$marriage** — The governing metaphor for the virgin symbol. God = Husband, His people = Wife, Covenant = marriage vows. Without $marriage there is no virgin and no $harlot — both terms describe the status of a covenant partner. "I have espoused you to one husband" (2 Cor 11:2).

- **$faith** — The internal quality that keeps the virgin faithful. $faith = trusting obedience — not mere belief but active submission to God's Word. The virgin of Rev 14:4 "follows the Lamb," which is $faith in action. The $harlot's corresponding condition is unbelief — refusal to obey.

- **$truth** — God's commandments, His standard, His Word. The virgin is faithful *to* something — and that something is $truth. "Thy word is truth" (Jn 17:17). $idolatry replaces God's standard with a self-made one. The virgin holds to the original.

- **$babylon** — The $harlot city, the world system built on self-rule. "MOTHER OF HARLOTS" (Rev 17:5). $babylon is the institutional expression of everything the virgin refuses — $idolatry on a civilizational scale, the kingdom that seduces nations into $fornication.

- **$jerusalem** — The bride city, the covenant community. "New Jerusalem... prepared as a bride" (Rev 21:2). $jerusalem is the institutional expression of the virgin — God's people gathered in exclusive devotion, the community that maintains $faith and dwells in covenant.

## Dictionary Comparison

Webster's 1913 entry for **virgin** begins:

> “Vir”gin, n. Etym: [L. virgo, -inis: cf. OF. virgine, virgene, virge, vierge, F. vierge.] 1. A woman who has had no carnal knowledge of man; a maid.”

The dictionary supplies the ordinary sense. The definition above is narrower or figurative only where Scripture's own cross-references require that transfer.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Deuteronomy 22 and 2 Corinthians 11 strongly connect virginity with betrothal, but the remaining citations do not establish that espousal is the term’s defining meaning in every case. Psalm 45 identifies the virgins as the bride’s companions, Matthew 25 does not call the ten virgins betrothed, and the restoration or re-espousal of divorced Israel requires contextual argument beyond the cited verses. The references thus support the proposed pattern without conclusively proving its asserted boundary.

- One could argue MATCH: the consensus already frames 'virgin' as a betrothal metaphor (2 Cor 11:2 'betrothed to one husband'; brides awaiting the Bridegroom), so 'espoused' merely restates the bride motif, and the companion/ten-tribe details are immaterial elaboration rather than real boundary disagreement. Conversely, one might press for DIVERGENT on the ground that 'covenant standing, not attainment' rejects the consensus's core of purity as the defining feature; but the book still retains chastity-to-one-husband as the operative contrast with the harlot, so the referent is not replaced.

- A reader could claim the shared betrothal texts make the cores simply equivalent (MATCH) or that “not attainment” quietly contradicts the purity emphasis (DIVERGENT); the explicit redefinition of conditions and the narrowed cases nevertheless favor REFINED.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.

## Conclusion

**The espoused — betrothed to one husband with the wedding still ahead: [covenant](/research/symbols/covenant/) standing, not attainment; the law counts a betrothed virgin already a wife, the prophets call the ten-tribe house re-espoused after her divorce “the virgin of Israel,” and virgins plural are the [bride](/research/symbols/marriage/)’s companions who follow her in.**

This is the glossary conclusion; broader applications in the study remain subordinate to the defining texts and countertexts.
