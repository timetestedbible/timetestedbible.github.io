# Four winds

## Approved Definition

**The four spirits of heaven — God's executing agents, shown as the four horsemen. The same fourfold outgoing pattern appears in the river dividing from Eden.**

## The Short Case

The four spirits of heaven—God's executing agents, shown as the four horsemen.

> *“And a river went out of Eden to water the garden; and from thence it was parted, and became into four heads.”* — Genesis 2:10

> *“And the angel answered and said unto me, These are the four spirits of the heavens, which go forth from standing before the Lord of all the earth.”* — Zechariah 6:5

> *“And I saw when the Lamb opened one of the seals, and I heard, as it were the noise of thunder, one of the four beasts saying, Come and see.”* — Revelation 6:1

> *“And I saw, and behold a white horse: and he that sat on him had a bow; and a crown was given unto him: and he went forth conquering, and to conquer.”* — Revelation 6:2

> *“And when he had opened the second seal, I heard the second beast say, Come and see.”* — Revelation 6:3

> *“And there went out another horse that was red: and power was given to him that sat thereon to take peace from the earth, and that they should kill one another: and there was given unto him a great sword.”* — Revelation 6:4

> *“And when he had opened the third seal, I heard the third beast say, Come and see. And I beheld, and lo a black horse; and he that sat on him had a pair of balances in his hand.”* — Revelation 6:5

> *“And I heard a voice in the midst of the four beasts say, A measure of wheat for a penny, and three measures of barley for a penny; and see thou hurt not the oil and the wine.”* — Revelation 6:6

The definition stays with the relationship these passages establish. Literal appearances preserve the image; they do not require every occurrence to be symbolic.

## Competing Definitions Tested

- Zechariah 6:5 explicitly calls the figures “four spirits of the heavens” and describes them as going forth from before God, supporting the agent identification. However, the printed verse alone does not establish the added identification with “the four horsemen”; that depends on the surrounding chariot-and-horse vision and further interpretation.

- One could argue DIVERGENT: the consensus's central identification is a spatial merism (directions/totality), and the book replaces this with personal agents identified as the four horsemen, an identification absent from all responses; treating an impersonal directional idiom as four specific personal beings could be seen as replacing the referent rather than refining it.

- While Zechariah 6:5 supports the 'four spirits/winds' as executing agents, applying this definition universally across all biblical mentions of the 'four winds' ignores dozens of passages (like Matthew 24:31 or Jeremiah 49:36) where the phrase clearly functions as a geographical idiom for the four compass directions.



These tests identify the boundary the definition must explain. A shared phrase or nearby theme is not enough by itself; the cited passages must establish the proposed identity and account for the countertexts without changing senses whenever the reading becomes difficult.
